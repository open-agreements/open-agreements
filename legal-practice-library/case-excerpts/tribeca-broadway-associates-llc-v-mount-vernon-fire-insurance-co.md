> Nor does it appear in this case that GDM’s broker had the authority to bind the carrier, for which it did not act as agent, so that we would not on that basis estop the carrier from denying the existence of coverage (compare Bucon, Inc. v Pennsylvania Mfg. Assn. Ins. Co., 151 AD2d 207 [1989]).

### tribeca-broadway-associates-llc-v-mount-vernon-fire-insurance-co-nor-does-the-certificate-of-insurance-8026ffbb {#tribeca-broadway-associates-llc-v-mount-vernon-fire-insurance-co-nor-does-the-certificate-of-insurance-8026ffbb}

> Nor does the certificate of insurance in this case confer coverage. A certificate of insurance is only evidence of a carrier’s intent to provide coverage but is not a contract to insure the designated party nor is it conclusive proof, standing alone, that such a contract exists (Buccini v 1568 Broadway Assoc., 250 AD2d 466 [1998]).
