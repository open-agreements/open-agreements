### mason-v-fakhimi-109-nev-1153-865-p-2d-333-1993-as-distinguished-from-liquidated-damages-c44caae7 {#mason-v-fakhimi-109-nev-1153-865-p-2d-333-1993-as-distinguished-from-liquidated-damages-c44caae7}

> As distinguished from liquidated damages, the term “penalty,” as used in contract law, is a sum inserted in a contract, not as the measure of compensation for its breach, but rather as a punishment for default, or by way of security for actual damages which may be sustained by reason of nonperformance, and it involves the idea of punishment. . . . [The] distinction between a penalty and liquidated damages is that a penalty is for the purpose of securing performance, while liquidated damages is the sum to be paid in the event of non-performance.
