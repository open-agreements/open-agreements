### berger-v-shanahan-accordingly-such-a-provision-is-fded2165 {#berger-v-shanahan-accordingly-such-a-provision-is-fded2165}

> Accordingly, such a provision is ordinarily to be construed as one for liquidated damages if three conditions are satisfied: (1) The damage which was to be expected as a result of a breach of the contract was uncertain in amount or difficult to prove; (2) there was an intent on the part of the parties to liquidate damages in advance; and (3) the amount stipulated was reasonable in the sense that it was not greatly disproportionate to the amount of the damage which, as the parties looked forward, seemed to be the presumable loss which would be sustained by the contractee in the event of a breach of the contract.

### berger-v-shanahan-the-law-is-well-established-in-a926818d {#berger-v-shanahan-the-law-is-well-established-in-a926818d}
