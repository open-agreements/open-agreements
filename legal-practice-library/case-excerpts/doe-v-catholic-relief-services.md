### doe-v-catholic-relief-services-following-today-s-decision-religious-890514c5 {#doe-v-catholic-relief-services-following-today-s-decision-religious-890514c5}

> Following today’s decision, religious organizations maintain complete autonomy in the employment of those who communicate the organization’s religious teachings. And religious organizations may require employees to adhere to religious doctrine so long as such requirements do not discriminate on the basis of race, sex, or national origin. But for those employees who do not perform spiritual functions, the state may act to ensure equal opportunities to work free from discrimination.

### doe-v-catholic-relief-services-here-we-hold-that-for-lay-8fc58871 {#doe-v-catholic-relief-services-here-we-hold-that-for-lay-8fc58871}

> Here, we hold that for lay employees like Doe—those not performing a spiritual function, not involved in carrying out the mission of the organization, and not even a member of the church implicitly consenting to its authority—the church autonomy doctrine does not require that courts extend some lesser protection compared to employees of secular organizations.
