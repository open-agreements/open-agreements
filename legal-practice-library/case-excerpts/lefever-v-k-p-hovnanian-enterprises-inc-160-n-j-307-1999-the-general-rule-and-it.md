### lefever-v-k-p-hovnanian-enterprises-inc-160-n-j-307-1999-the-general-rule-and-it-the-general-rule-of-corporate-successor-0ec611d3 {#lefever-v-k-p-hovnanian-enterprises-inc-160-n-j-307-1999-the-general-rule-and-it-the-general-rule-of-corporate-successor-0ec611d3}

> The general rule of corporate-successor liability is that when a company sells its assets to another company, the acquiring company is not liable for the debts and liabilities of the selling company simply because it has succeeded to the ownership of the assets of the seller. Traditionally, there have been only four exceptions: (1) the successor expressly or impliedly assumes the predecessor’s liabilities; (2) there is an actual or de facto consolidation or merger of the seller and the purchaser; (3) the purchasing company is a mere continuation of the seller; or (4) the transaction is entered into fraudulently to escape liability.
