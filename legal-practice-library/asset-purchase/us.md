# Negotiating and Drafting Small-Business Asset Purchase Agreements in the United States[^about]

How the buyer and seller of a profitable small or online business negotiate, draft and carry out an asset purchase agreement, from the choice of structure and the assets sold to price, remedies, closing and the transfer of each asset.

## What should an APA cover when buying a small business? {#small-business-apa-overview}

**Short answer.** When a small business is sold through an asset purchase agreement (APA), federal tax guidance generally treats each asset as sold separately and requires both the buyer and the seller to allocate the price among the business assets transferred. [^p1-irs-sale-each-asset][^p1-irs-residual-method-both-parties]

This guide addresses purchases of profitable businesses with $1 million or less of earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA), such as online and software businesses, local service businesses and restaurants, and not pre-profit or venture-backed acquisitions; the EBITDA figure is a scope choice, not a legal threshold. The guide covers negotiating, drafting and carrying out the agreement and transferring the assets, not the investigation of the business before signing, for which the [OpenAgreements Due Diligence Request List](/templates/openagreements-due-diligence-request-list) lists documents and information a buyer can request.

This guide is not a 50-state survey; it uses Delaware statutes to illustrate corporate and Uniform Commercial Code rules because Delaware's Division of Corporations reports that over two-thirds of the Fortune 500 call Delaware their corporate home, [^p1-delaware-corporate-home] and it uses California and New York for decisions and statutes that change an asset buyer's exposure, such as California's product-line successor-liability decision [^p1-ray-product-line-example] and New York's advance notice for bulk sales of business assets. [^p1-ny-bulk-sale-notice-example]

The asset-by-asset structure matches federal tax guidance, under which the IRS generally treats each asset of a sold business as sold separately for determining the treatment of gain or loss. [^p1-irs-sale-each-asset]

For an operating business, the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) points a buyer to the contracts, leases, cash flow and inventory of the business. [^sba-operating-business-diligence] An online business also depends on assets such as its websites, domain names and social-media accounts, which one filed agreement for an online publishing business transferred as listed on a schedule, together with the website content and software code; [^d1-salem-websites-overview] this guide treats [domain names](#domain-closing), [provider accounts](#migration-timing), [code and hosting projects](#website-migration) and [app-store listings](#app-transfer) separately.

For licenses and permits, the SBA describes two routes: obtaining them from the current owner or applying for new ones. [^p1-sba-licenses-from-owner]

Transferring contracts in an asset purchase typically triggers an anti-assignment clause unless the clause has an express exception, [^p1-tr-asset-sale-anti-assignment] so the parties decide whether closing waits for a missing [consent](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-closing-and-handover/us#consent-failure), a lawful interim arrangement applies, or the affected asset is excluded with a price change.

The price allocation has tax consequences for each side, because the IRS requires an allocation of the purchase price to determine the buyer's basis in each acquired asset and the seller's gain or loss on each asset; the [allocation question](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#purchase-price-allocation) addresses the details. [^p1-i8594-allocation-basis-gain]

The underlying contracts, the issuing authorities and the applicable state or industry rules decide whether a particular asset, license or permit transfers; the SBA separately directs a buyer to identify the federal, state and local permits and licenses the business needs. [^p1-sba-permit-levels]

## How do we transfer the domain at closing? {#domain-closing}

**Short answer.** A domain transfer at closing can be constrained by ICANN's transfer rules, including the general rule that a domain cannot move to a new registrar within 60 days after a change to the registrant's contact information, although some registrars may offer an opt-out, which ICANN says registrars need not provide. [^registrar-confirmation-domain-closing]

The asset schedule lists each domain by exact name, registered holder, registrar and expiry date, because each registration is a contract between the registrant and a particular registrar [^p6-icann-registrant-contract] and, for a generic top-level domain such as .com, the registrar sends its expiration notices to the registered name holder. [^p6-icann-errp-notices]

The handover plan can say whether the closing needs a change of registrant, a transfer between registrars or both, because ICANN's transfer rules address a transfer from one registrar to another and a transfer from one registrant to another as separate moves. [^d3-icann-two-kinds] Only the registered name holder, the administrative contact or a person expressly authorized by either of them can start a transfer, [^d3-icann-who-initiates] and a transfer between registrars requires an authorization code, also called an AuthInfo or transfer code. [^d3-icann-authinfo] For a move between registrars, the process starts with the registrar that will receive the name, which must confirm the registrant's intent on ICANN's standardized form. [^d3-icann-gaining-registrar] Because registrants manage their domain settings through their registrar, the plan can also record evidence that the buyer controls the registrar account, including renewal and account recovery. [^d3-icann-registrant-settings]

Registrar administration and hosting are separate steps; in a hosting move, traffic moves when the domain's DNS settings are changed to point to the new hosting infrastructure. [^d3-google-dns-move] Completion is recorded against the scheduled domain in the buyer's registrar account rather than inferred from the public website staying online, because the registration is maintained under the registrant's contract with its registrar. [^d3-icann-registrar-maintains]

## How do we migrate accounts before closing? {#migration-timing}

**Short answer.** The steps and timing of an account migration depend on each provider's transfer process and on which accounts the agreement requires to move before or after closing, as Vercel's rule that a project's transferor be an owner of the sending team and a member of the receiving team shows. [^p6-vercel-transfer-roles]

Provider rules can require the receiving account to be ready before a transfer starts; Vercel requires the receiving team to have a valid payment method before a project is transferred, [^p6-vercel-payment-method] and a GitHub repository transfer to another personal account lapses if the new owner does not accept it within one day. [^p6-github-accept-window]

Not every account has to move before closing; a purchase agreement can let some assets keep transferring after a defined migration milestone, as one filed purchase agreement expressly anticipated. [^migration-migration-timing] Later deliveries can be made enforceable obligations, and the same agreement made either party's failure to complete its migration process a material breach. [^d3-jeffs-migration-breach] It also tied payment to the handover, releasing the price to the seller, net of the broker's commission, only after its inspection period expired. [^d3-jeffs-release-after-inspection]

Provider-specific prerequisites are covered under [Apple apps](#app-transfer), [website and app projects](#website-migration) and [shared accounts](#shared-accounts).

## How can the seller separate a shared account from its other businesses, and how is the separation documented? {#shared-accounts}

**Short answer.** A seller can separate a shared account by moving the sold business's project or sub-account out of its wider account through the provider's own transfer route, as AWS allows when a member account is removed from an organization and becomes a standalone account rather than being closed. [^p6-aws-remove-standalone]

Providers expressly anticipate this kind of separation; Google Cloud's documentation says projects may need to move between organizations because of acquisitions or the separation of business units. [^p6-gcp-separation] The route depends on how the sold business sits inside the seller's account, and a business that already has its own project can move as a unit; Google Cloud describes a project migration as something other than a data transfer, during which the project's services, databases and virtual machines stay active without downtime. [^d3-gcp-not-data-transfer] Resources mixed into a project that also serves the seller's other businesses are recreated in a new buyer account and their data migrated, or are served by the seller for a limited time under the [transition terms](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-after-closing/us#transition-and-completion); one filed online-service agreement, for example, capped the seller's transition assistance with customer accounts and billing at 90 days. [^d3-sitestar-transition-cap]

A separation can leave access behind; AWS states that an access role created for the organization's management account is not deleted automatically when a member account leaves the organization, [^p6-aws-iam-role-remains] so the separation checklist covers users, roles, API keys, billing, data, integrations and recovery methods on both sides.

The separation record can list what moved, what stayed with the seller and why, and the date on which control changed, and that date can also move the charges; AWS makes the departing account's owner responsible for all new costs from the moment the account leaves the organization. [^p6-aws-billing-moves] Ownership, billing and contact details are separate completion items, and AWS requires an account becoming standalone to have a support plan, verified contact information and a current payment method. [^d3-aws-standalone-requirements] An acceptance check tests the resulting service, because separation can change what the account keeps; AWS states that a departing account loses access to cost and usage data from its time in the organization [^d3-aws-cost-data] and that users in the account can no longer use an AWS service whose integration was enabled for the organization. [^d3-aws-integration-lost]

## How should we plan a website or app project migration? {#website-migration}

**Short answer.** A website or app migration plan can identify the actual hosting platform and include tests of the resulting service, because a platform's project transfer may leave work undone, as when Vercel requires a project's integrations to be added again after the transfer. [^migration-website-migration]

Before the switch, Google suggests uploading a copy of the site to the new host and testing all aspects of how users interact with it, [^d3-google-copy-and-test] which for an online business can include an ordinary customer journey, a payment or subscription test, required notifications, deployment access and recovery access.

For a hosting move that keeps the same web address, Google suggests lowering DNS time-to-live values at least a week before the move so the change propagates faster [^p6-google-hosting-ttl] and shutting down the old hosting only once no one, including Google's crawler, is still using it. [^p6-google-hosting-shutdown] A closing plan that ends the seller's hosting on the closing date can conflict with that sequence, because Google's guide waits for traffic to the old provider to reach zero before shutdown, so the agreement can keep the old hosting available for a short, defined period after the switch. [^d3-google-traffic-zero]

Repository and domain settings move on their own terms; for a GitHub Pages site published from a private repository with a custom domain, GitHub suggests removing or updating the domain's DNS records before transferring the repository to avoid the risk of a domain takeover. [^p6-github-pages-dns] The [domain transfer](#domain-closing) is scheduled separately from the hosting move.

Some hosted app builders let the buyer take an independent copy of a project instead of moving the original; Lovable, for example, distinguishes a remix, which creates a second, independent copy and leaves the original untouched, from a transfer of the one existing project. [^d3-lovable-remix-vs-transfer] A copy establishes control of that copy, not completion of the acquisition, because Lovable's remix leaves behind everything tied to the original's identity, such as its live URL, collaborators, secrets and app users. [^d3-lovable-left-behind] A remix is a snapshot taken when it is made, and later changes to the original do not reach the copy, [^d3-lovable-snapshot] so the copy can be compared with the sold version at closing.

## What must be ready before an Apple app can transfer? {#app-transfer}

**Short answer.** An Apple app transfer in App Store Connect requires the recipient Account Holder's Apple Account and the recipient account's Team ID, so the buyer's developer account has to exist before the transfer can be requested. [^apple-transfer-app-transfer]

Account enrollment and any organization verification belong early in the closing plan rather than at the moment of transfer, because an organization enrolling in Apple's developer program needs an Apple Account with two-factor authentication turned on, [^p6-apple-enroll-2fa] and, unless it is a government entity, a D-U-N-S Number that Apple uses to verify the organization's identity, legal entity status and address. [^p6-apple-enroll-duns]

Apple's transfer criteria then apply to both accounts and to the app; neither account can be in a pending or changing state, and both parties must have accepted the latest paid and free agreements; [^p6-apple-criteria-accounts] the app must also have at least one version released to the App Store. [^p6-apple-criteria-released] An app that is currently available for pre-order cannot be transferred, [^d3-apple-no-preorder] so a pre-order or a pending membership change is a concrete closing dependency, not a reason to exchange account passwords.

The recipient's acceptance is the evidence to record, because after the transfer is initiated the app keeps a Pending App Transfer status until the recipient accepts it or the request expires after 60 days. [^d3-apple-pending-60-days] The app leaves the seller's account on transfer, and Apple advises the transferor to back up the app's information first, [^d3-apple-backup] so the seller's records of metadata, pricing and sales can be settled before the request is made.

## Is a marketplace's standard purchase agreement enough, or should it be negotiated? {#marketplace-standard-form}

**Short answer.** A marketplace's standard purchase agreement may come without any assurance that it fits a particular deal, so whether it is enough depends on whether its terms cover that deal's price structure, assets and risks. [^p6-trustmrr-templates-convenience]

TrustMRR's terms, for example, describe its document templates as a convenience only and disclaim any representation about their legal sufficiency or appropriateness for a specific situation. [^p6-trustmrr-templates-convenience]

The standard agreement is also where the buyer's protection sits, because the platform stays outside it; TrustMRR's terms say it is not the buyer or seller and not a party to any acquisition agreement between users. [^d3-trustmrr-not-a-party] TrustMRR's terms also state that the acquisition agreement and asset transfer remain between the buyer and seller, [^p6-trustmrr-agreement-between-parties] and that transferring the startup's assets is solely the responsibility of the buyer and seller. [^p6-trustmrr-transfer-responsibility] Protection against inaccurate revenue figures, missing assets or failed transfers therefore comes from the purchase agreement's own terms, because the platform does not guarantee either party, the assets or the outcome; [^d3-trustmrr-no-guarantee] the questions on [revenue figures](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#screenshots), [recovery for inaccurate representations](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-after-closing/us#remedy-allocation) and [handover](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-closing-and-handover/us#digital-handover) describe those terms.

A price paid partly after closing is one sign that the default needs more; TrustMRR's FAQ says the platform supports only a single lump-sum payment through escrow and that other payment arrangements are handled outside it. [^p6-trustmrr-lump-sum] A deal that needs a holdback, deferred payment, earnout or seller note therefore needs its own terms, discussed under [deferred payment](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#escrow-holdback-and-deferred-payment) and [earnouts](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#price-mechanisms), and a route the platform's terms permit, which for TrustMRR means written approval before completing a transaction outside its standard escrow. [^d3-trustmrr-approval-route-sf]

A general asset description is another sign; TrustMRR's FAQ says escrow needs verifiable asset details and that missing or vague asset details can lead to rejection of the agreement. [^p6-trustmrr-asset-details] An itemized [asset schedule](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-what-transfers/us#schedules) serves the escrow review, because TrustMRR creates the escrow transaction from the APA's asset details, and also gives the buyer a fixed list against which delivery is later checked. [^d3-trustmrr-escrow-from-apa]

Assets that need third-party consent or a migration in several steps, customer obligations that straddle closing and a seller restraint on competing are other signs, covered under [consents](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-closing-and-handover/us#consent-failure), [account migration](#migration-timing), [customer obligations](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-after-closing/us#customer-obligation-cutoff) and [seller non-competes](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-after-closing/us#seller-covenants). Whether a particular platform form already covers them is a question for the text of the revision the parties will sign, because it is the executed document that may create binding obligations according to its terms. [^d3-trustmrr-executed-document]

## Can we use our own APA or an addendum when a marketplace runs the sale? {#marketplace-process}

**Short answer.** A separate purchase agreement or addendum in a marketplace sale can be constrained by the marketplace's terms, such as TrustMRR's current terms requiring deals initiated there to be completed through its platform and requiring a new revision and fresh signatures for any change after the first electronic signature. [^trustmrr-complete-through-platform][^marketplace-revision-marketplace-process]

Marketplace terms can distinguish the platform's completion process, the acquisition documents and a separate escrow service, and some describe an approval route for using an alternative process; TrustMRR's terms, for example, require contacting its support and obtaining its written approval before completing a transaction outside its standard escrow. [^d3-trustmrr-approval-route] An alternative process that requires the platform's prior approval is not permission to bypass the platform, and TrustMRR's terms prohibit circumventing it on pain of account suspension or termination. [^d3-trustmrr-no-circumvention] Nor does such an approval establish that the escrow agent will accept a separate document, because TrustMRR's terms treat Escrow.com as a separate, independent service governed by its own terms. [^p6-trustmrr-escrow-separate]

An addendum also has to satisfy the purchase agreement's own amendment clause; one filed agreement, for example, permitted amendment only by a written agreement executed by the party to be charged. [^amendment-document-priority] A clear addendum identifies the underlying agreement by parties, date and signed revision, names the changed sections, states which unchanged terms continue and is signed as the amendment clause requires; one marketplace-brokered agreement permitted amendment in writing only if both parties signed and dated it. [^d3-jeffs-amendment] An express priority clause resolves a conflict that document dates alone may leave unanswered, such as a later escrow instruction or a marketplace's standard form that covers the same subject; one filed agreement provided that its own provisions control over any other document in a conflict. [^d3-sitestar-agreement-controls]

TrustMRR's FAQ says it uploads the signed APA to the Escrow.com transaction. [^p6-trustmrr-signed-apa-upload] An addendum that is outside the signed revision, or that the escrow agent has not accepted, may therefore not control the release of funds, and TrustMRR's terms send disputes about escrow funds and disbursements to Escrow.com directly. [^d3-trustmrr-escrow-disputes] An email discussing a change is not an executed amendment under a clause that, like the one above, requires a written agreement executed by the party to be charged. [^amendment-document-priority]

Whether the standard form needs changing at all is covered under [the marketplace's standard agreement](#marketplace-standard-form).
