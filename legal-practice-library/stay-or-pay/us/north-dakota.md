# Stay-or-Pay and Employee Repayment in North Dakota[^about]

North Dakota has no statute aimed at stay-or-pay terms, but its Century Code voids contract penalties, allows agreed damages only when actual damage is impracticable or extremely difficult to fix, and voids restraints on a lawful profession, while chapter 34-14 limits collecting a repayment from wages.

## Are repayment terms enforceable in North Dakota? {#repayment-enforceability}

**Short answer.** North Dakota voids a contract term that fixes damages for a breach in advance, except that the parties may agree on an amount presumed to be the damage when it would be impracticable or extremely difficult to fix the actual damage [^nd-9-08-04-fixed-damages-void]. Penalties imposed by contract for nonperformance are also void [^nd-9-08-03-penalties-void]. In our review we found no North Dakota statute that bans or limits stay-or-pay, training-repayment or sign-on bonus repayment terms in private employment, so the conservative inference is that a court would test a fixed repayment amount under these two sections.
The employer bears the burden. In *City of Fargo v. Case Development Co.*, the North Dakota Supreme Court held that a party seeking to enforce a clause stipulating the damages recoverable upon breach must prove that the clause is valid as an exception to section 9-08-04, and listed three foundational questions: whether the damages were very difficult to estimate when the contract was made, whether the parties made a reasonable endeavor to fix compensation, and whether the amount bears a reasonable relationship to the damages reasonably to be anticipated [^nd-fargo-burden-on-enforcer][^nd-fargo-three-foundational-facts].
The court has applied that test to employees who left early. In *Bowbells Public School District No. 14 v. Walker*, it upheld a graduated amount a teacher owed for breaking her contract, reasoning that the law requires only that the clause's purpose be primarily to predetermine damages rather than to impose a penalty [^nd-bowbells-graduated-clause][^nd-bowbells-purpose-predetermine][^nd-bowbells-burden-sustained]. In *Bottineau Public School District No. 1 v. Zimmer*, it upheld a $500 replacement-cost charge against a teacher released from his contract, and held that a valid stipulation binds the parties regardless of the actual damages incurred [^nd-bottineau-500-not-unreasonable][^nd-bottineau-bound-by-stipulation].
A repayment triggered by going to work for a competitor faces a separate and stricter limit. Section 9-08-06 voids a contract restraining anyone from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business, to that extent, outside the sale-of-goodwill and owner exceptions [^nd-9-08-06-restraint-void], and the North Dakota Supreme Court has read it to invalidate employment terms that impose a penalty on an employee who works for a competitor after employment [^nd-earthworks-penalty-for-competing]. In our review we found no North Dakota appellate decision applying section 9-08-06 to a repayment triggered by leaving for any reason rather than by competing.

"Every contract by which the amount of damages to be paid, or other compensation to be made, for a breach of an obligation is determined in anticipation thereof is to that extent void, except that the parties may agree therein upon an amount presumed to be the damage sustained by a breach in cases in which it would be impracticable or extremely difficult to fix the actual damage."[^nd-9-08-04-fixed-damages-void]

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> A flat repayment amount that the employer cannot tie to any estimate of the cost of the employee's early departure risks being held void [^nd-fargo-foundational-facts-caution].

## Can a North Dakota employer claw back pay already received? {#clawback-earned-comp}

**Short answer.** The North Dakota Supreme Court has enforced an employment agreement that made an associate's shortfall in billed hours a debt owed at termination [^nd-llh-debt-at-termination][^nd-llh-summary-judgment-affirmed]. In *Larson Latham Huettl LLP v. Iversen*, the employment agreement provided that any shortfall between billed and required hours would be a debt the associate owed at the end of the calendar year or at the termination of employment [^nd-llh-debt-at-termination], and the North Dakota Supreme Court affirmed summary judgment for the firm, holding that the agreement was not substantively unconscionable [^nd-llh-summary-judgment-affirmed][^nd-llh-not-unconscionable].
The parties may also agree on when compensation becomes due. In *Johnson v. Peterbilt of Fargo, Inc.*, the court held that a contract denying a salesman commissions on units delivered after his employment ended, whether voluntarily or otherwise, was not against any stated public policy [^nd-johnson-commission-condition][^nd-johnson-no-public-policy].
An employer may not, however, condition money otherwise due on the employee's not competing [^nd-werlinger-void-restraint]. In *Werlinger v. Mutual Service Casualty Insurance Co.*, the court held void under section 9-08-06 a clause forfeiting money an agent would otherwise be paid if he competed, explaining that the important feature was the restraint on competition imposed to avoid the forfeiture [^nd-werlinger-forfeiture-restraint][^nd-werlinger-void-restraint].
In our review we found no North Dakota appellate decision on recovering a cash sign-on or retention bonus, so the conservative course is to assume a court will test a fixed repayment amount under section 9-08-04 [^nd-9-08-04-clawback-test]. Collecting the repayment separately is also safer than withholding wages, because section 34-14-04.1 limits withholding, apart from legally required or court-ordered amounts, to advances other than undocumented cash and deductions authorized in writing or, for damage or shortage, at the time of the deduction [^nd-34-14-04-1-clawback-withholding].

"The important feature of the contracts involved in this case and in Geiss was not the basis upon which either company’s terminated agent was to be paid, but the restraint on competition that the companies imposed upon their agents to avoid the forfeiture of money they would otherwise be paid."[^nd-werlinger-forfeiture-restraint]

## Can repayment come out of a North Dakota final paycheck? {#final-pay-deduction}

**Short answer.** A North Dakota employer may take a repayment out of wages, including final wages, only within the withholding categories of section 34-14-04.1, subject to its documentation and authorization requirements [^nd-34-14-04-1-withholding-limits]. Section 34-14-04.1 limits withholdings to advances other than undocumented cash, a recurring deduction authorized in writing, and a nonrecurring deduction authorized in writing when the source of the deduction is cited specifically; a nonrecurring deduction for damage, breakage, shortage or negligence must be authorized by the employee at the time of the deduction [^nd-34-14-04-1-withholding-limits].
When an employee is discharged or quits, unpaid wages become due at the regular paydays established in advance for the periods worked [^nd-34-14-03-final-pay-due]. In a dispute over wages, the employer must give written notice of the amount, less whatever the employee owes the employer, that the employee concedes to be due, and pay that amount without condition [^nd-34-14-04-conceded-wages].
A deduction that recovers a cost of the employer's own business, such as training the employer requires for its work, can also run into federal wage law. Under the federal rule, wage requirements are not met when the employee kicks back the wage, directly or indirectly, to the employer or for the employer's benefit, and an employer-required cost borne by the employee violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which it cuts into the minimum or overtime wages required [^nd-fed-531-35-kickback][^nd-fed-531-35-overtime-final-pay].

"3. A nonrecurring deduction authorized in writing, when the source of the deduction is cited specifically."[^nd-34-14-04-1-withholding-limits]

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> Netting a one-time training or bonus repayment that is not an advance or another permitted withholding against the last paycheck, without a written authorization that cites the deduction specifically, risks a wage claim on which the employee may recover the unpaid wages with interest [^nd-34-14-04-1-withholding-caution][^nd-34-14-09-1-interest-caution].

## Are North Dakota training or tuition repayment terms treated differently? {#training-repayment}

**Short answer.** No North Dakota statute or appellate decision located for this guide treats training, tuition or relocation repayment in private employment differently from other repayment terms. The conservative inference is that the same section 9-08-04 test applies, under which an agreed amount is void unless actual damage would be impracticable or extremely difficult to fix [^nd-9-08-04-training-test]. The employer enforcing the amount bears the burden of proving the clause valid [^nd-fargo-training-burden], including that the amount bears a reasonable relationship to the damages reasonably to be anticipated [^nd-fargo-training-foundational].
A separate statute matters when the training is required for the job. Section 34-02-01 requires an employer, except as section 34-02-02 provides, to indemnify an employee for all that the employee necessarily expends or loses in direct consequence of discharging the employee's duties or obeying the employer's directions [^nd-34-02-01-training-indemnity]. The duty does not cover losses from the ordinary risks of the business [^nd-34-02-02-training-ordinary-risks], or tools or equipment the employee also uses outside the scope of employment [^nd-34-02-01-training-equipment]. In our review we found no North Dakota decision applying section 34-02-01 to a training-repayment term, so the conservative inference is that a repayment recovering the cost of training the employer directed the employee to take for the employee's current duties carries more risk than one recovering the cost of optional education.
Two features of the teacher cases offer a drafting model. The *Bowbells* clause scaled the payment to the timing of the breach [^nd-bowbells-training-graduated], and the court did not treat use of a standard clause in many contracts as incompatible with a reasonable endeavor to estimate damages [^nd-bowbells-standard-clause].
A training repayment that becomes due only if the employee goes to work for a competitor, rather than on any early departure, risks being void under section 9-08-06, which the North Dakota Supreme Court has read to invalidate employment terms imposing a penalty on an employee who works for a competitor after employment [^nd-earthworks-training-penalty], and which voided a clause that made an agent purchase the freedom to compete by forfeiting money otherwise due [^nd-werlinger-training-void][^nd-werlinger-training-forfeiture][^nd-werlinger-purchase-freedom].

## What happens to a North Dakota employer that over-collects a repayment from pay? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** A North Dakota employee may file a claim with the Department of Labor and Human Rights for wages due under chapter 34-14 within two years from the date the wages are due, if the amount claimed is at least $125 and no more than $15,000 [^nd-34-14-09-claim-limits]. The labor commissioner must ensure compliance with the chapter and may hold hearings on the merits of a claim [^nd-34-14-05-commissioner-duties], and may consider any offset, deduction or counterclaim the employer asserts, which the employer must disclose [^nd-34-14-05-offsets].
On recovery, the employee is entitled to interest on the unpaid wages, and to double or treble the unpaid wages if, within the year preceding the date the unpaid wages were due, the employer was found liable on two, or three or more, previous wage claims [^nd-34-14-09-1-recovery]. When an employer that discharged the employee fails to pay wages on time, the employee may collect the agreed daily wage for each day of default, for up to thirty days [^nd-34-14-03-waiting-time]. An employer that willfully refuses to pay wages due when demanded is guilty of an infraction [^nd-34-14-07-infraction].

## What repayment structure is safest under North Dakota law? {#safest-structure}

**Short answer.** The safest North Dakota repayment term sets an amount the employer can show was a reasonable estimate of damages that were very difficult to estimate when the contract was made, because the employer bears the burden of proving the clause valid under those foundational facts [^nd-fargo-structure-burden][^nd-fargo-structure-foundational]. Limiting the repayment to optional education or training, rather than training the employer directs the employee to take for current duties, reduces the risk that the cost is one section 34-02-01 requires the employer to bear [^nd-34-02-01-structure-indemnity]. A schedule that scales the amount to the timing of the departure follows the graduated clause upheld in *Bowbells* [^nd-bowbells-structure-graduated][^nd-bowbells-structure-sustained].
A repayment triggered by going to work for a competitor risks being void, because section 9-08-06 invalidates employment terms that impose a penalty on an employee who competes after employment [^nd-earthworks-structure-penalty]. As an inference from that rule, a trigger tied to the employee's own early resignation, regardless of where the employee works next, is less exposed to section 9-08-06, though it still has to satisfy section 9-08-04.
Collecting the balance by separate demand is safer than withholding pay, because, apart from advances and legally required or court-ordered withholding, section 34-14-04.1 permits a one-time deduction under a written authorization that cites the source of the deduction specifically [^nd-34-14-04-1-structure-authorization], and a deduction that recovers a cost of the employer's own business should not cut into required minimum or overtime wages [^nd-fed-531-35-structure][^nd-fed-531-35-overtime-structure].
