# Stay-or-Pay and Employee Repayment in Michigan[^about]

Michigan's wage payment act bars an employer from demanding or receiving consideration from an employee as a condition of employment, and the Michigan Supreme Court held in Sands Appliance Services v. Wilson that a mandatory tuition-repayment contract was void under that rule; a 2023 amendment carves out optional education repayment agreements and law enforcement academy costs on a declining four-year schedule, and MCL 408.477 requires written consent for each wage payment from which an employer-benefit deduction is taken.

## Are repayment terms enforceable in Michigan? {#repayment-enforceability}

**Short answer.** Apart from a statutory exception for law enforcement academy costs, a Michigan repayment term is enforceable only if the employee did not have to accept it to get or keep the job. MCL 408.478(1) bars an employer from demanding or receiving, directly or indirectly, any fee, gift, tip, gratuity or other remuneration or consideration from an employee as a condition of employment or continuation of employment [^mi-sop-condition-of-employment-ban], except for listed categories that include academy costs a law enforcement agency collects under a signed agreement [^mi-sop-police-academy-exception-intro]. In *Sands Appliance Services v. Wilson* the Michigan Supreme Court held that a tuition contract requiring an appliance technician who left before six years to repay training costs was void and unenforceable under that section, because the employee had to sign it to be hired [^mi-sop-sands-tuition-contract-void].

"Plaintiff conditioned defendant’s employment on his agreement to pay $50 a week for the first three years, minus $50 a week for each week worked in the next three years. Thus, plaintiff required that defendant grant it a contractual right to financial recovery in the event he did not complete six years of employment. This repayment arrangement represents remuneration or consideration that ran from defendant to plaintiff."[^mi-sop-sands-repayment-is-consideration]

The court rejected the argument that the contract escaped the statute because the employee received training in return, reasoning that otherwise the slightest return from the employer would remove the employee from the statute's protection [^mi-sop-sands-peppercorn]. The same opinion stated that programs in which an employer offers to fund an employee's education, subject to repayment unless the employee stays for a specific period, do not violate the section because they are optional and not a condition of employment [^mi-sop-sands-optional-programs]. The Legislature codified that distinction in 2023 by excluding from the section consideration collected under an optional education repayment agreement [^mi-sop-optional-education-exception].

An optional education repayment agreement falls outside the statutory ban [^mi-sop-optional-education-exception]. Where such a term fixes an amount as damages for leaving early, the Michigan Court of Appeals sustains a liquidated damages provision if the amount is reasonable in relation to the possible injury and not unconscionable or excessive [^mi-sop-uawgm-liquidated-test]. No Michigan appellate decision located for this guide applies that test to an optional employee repayment agreement.

MCL 445.774a lets an employer obtain an agreement that prohibits an employee from engaging in employment or a line of business after employment ends only if the agreement is reasonable as to duration, geographical area and the type of employment or line of business [^mi-sop-noncompete-reasonableness]. No Michigan decision located for this guide decides whether a payment owed only if the employee joins a competitor is such an agreement, so the conservative course is to make any repayment depend on the timing of the departure and not on where the employee works next.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> Making signature of a repayment agreement a term of the offer letter or a condition of keeping the job, outside the law enforcement academy exception [^mi-sop-police-academy-exception-intro], risks leaving the employer with no enforceable repayment right, whatever the training or other benefit the employee receives in return [^mi-sop-sands-tuition-contract-void][^mi-sop-sands-peppercorn].

## Can a Michigan employer claw back pay the employee already earned? {#clawback-earned-comp}

**Short answer.** No Michigan appellate decision located for this guide decides whether a signing, retention or relocation bonus already paid can be recovered under a stay-or-pay term. The wage payment act treats bonuses due under a written contract or written policy as fringe benefits [^mi-sop-fringe-benefits-definition], which an employer must pay in accordance with the terms of that contract or policy [^mi-sop-fringe-benefits-terms]. Separately, a repayment obligation the employee must accept as a condition of employment is consideration barred by MCL 408.478(1) [^mi-sop-condition-ban-clawback], and the Michigan Supreme Court declined to exempt a repayment arrangement because the employee received something in return [^mi-sop-sands-peppercorn-clawback].

No Michigan decision located for this guide decides whether a promise to return a bonus paid in advance, made as a condition of receiving the job, is consideration under MCL 408.478(1) in the way the training-repayment promise in *Sands* was. The conservative course is to state the service condition in the written bonus terms so that the bonus is not due until it is earned and to make any bonus repayment agreement optional and separate from the offer of employment. A bonus that is due under the written contract or policy must be paid according to its terms [^mi-sop-fringe-benefits-terms], so it should not be cancelled or withheld once due.

## Can a Michigan employer take a repayment out of the final paycheck? {#final-pay-deduction}

**Short answer.** Unless law or a collective bargaining agreement requires or expressly permits the deduction, a Michigan employer needs the employee's full, free and written consent, obtained without intimidation or fear of discharge, to deduct a repayment from wages, including final wages [^mi-sop-deduction-written-consent]. Because a repayment deduction benefits the employer, the consent must cover each wage payment subject to the deduction, and cumulative deductions may not reduce gross wages below the state minimum wage [^mi-sop-employer-benefit-deduction-consent]. An employee who voluntarily leaves must be paid all wages earned and due as soon as the amount can with due diligence be determined [^mi-sop-final-pay-voluntary-quit].

"Except as provided in this subsection and subsections (4) and (5), a deduction for the benefit of the employer requires written consent from the employee for each wage payment subject to the deduction, and the cumulative amount of the deductions must not reduce the gross wages paid to a rate less than the minimum rate as prescribed in the improved workforce opportunity wage act, 2018 PA 337, MCL 408.931 to 408.945."[^mi-sop-employer-benefit-deduction-consent]

Because consent is required for each wage payment subject to the deduction [^mi-sop-employer-benefit-deduction-consent], a general repayment authorization that does not cover the particular final wage payment does not establish the consent the statute requires. The conservative course is to obtain a separate written consent that names the final wage payment and the amount, given freely at separation. The overpayment exception lets an employer deduct without consent only an overpayment that resulted from a mathematical miscalculation, typographical error, clerical error or misprint, and only if every statutory condition is met [^mi-sop-overpayment-exception]; a training or bonus repayment is not such an overpayment. Each deduction must also be substantiated in the employer's records and identified to the individual employee [^mi-sop-deduction-records].

The Michigan minimum wage is $13.73 an hour beginning January 1, 2026, and $15.00 an hour beginning January 1, 2027 [^mi-sop-state-minimum-wage]. Under the federal rule, wages count as paid only when paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear [^mi-sop-flsa-free-and-clear], and the federal wage requirements are not met where the employee kicks back part of the wage to the employer or for the employer's benefit [^mi-sop-flsa-kickback]. The conservative course is to keep any deduction from reducing a workweek's pay below the minimum wage or cutting into overtime pay.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A repayment clause that lets the employer take the balance from the final paycheck without a written consent covering that wage payment exposes the employer to a department order for the wages withheld plus a penalty of 10 percent a year from the date it is notified of the complaint, and exemplary damages of up to twice the amount due if the violation is flagrant or repeated [^mi-sop-employer-benefit-deduction-consent][^mi-sop-department-order-penalty][^mi-sop-exemplary-damages].

## Are training or relocation repayments treated differently in Michigan? {#training-repayment}

**Short answer.** Michigan bars training repayment that an employee must accept as a condition of employment [^mi-sop-sands-tuition-contract-void-training] but permits optional education repayment agreements [^mi-sop-optional-education-exception-training] and, on the statutory conditions, law enforcement academy repayment under a signed agreement [^mi-sop-police-academy-exception-intro-training]. The Michigan Supreme Court held a mandatory tuition contract void under MCL 408.478(1) [^mi-sop-sands-tuition-contract-void-training]. Since 2023, the section excludes consideration collected under an optional education repayment agreement, in which the employer offers to fund the employee's education on the understanding that the employee will repay the costs unless the employee stays for a specific period [^mi-sop-optional-education-exception-training]. A law enforcement agency may also recover academy costs under a signed agreement entered into on or after the effective date of the 2023 amendment [^mi-sop-police-academy-exception-intro-training], on a declining schedule, from 100 percent for a voluntary departure within one year after the academy ends to 25 percent in the fourth year, each capped at the officer's first-year salary [^mi-sop-police-academy-schedule].

"(c) Remuneration or consideration collected by an employer under an optional education repayment agreement in which the employer offers to fund an employee's education with the understanding that the employee will repay the costs incurred unless the employee remains with the employer for a specific period."[^mi-sop-optional-education-exception-training]

The statute does not define optional, education or specific period, and no Michigan appellate decision located for this guide applies the 2023 exception. *Sands* explained the line before the amendment: an education program is outside the section when it is optional and not a condition of employment or continued employment [^mi-sop-sands-optional-programs-training]. The conservative course is to offer the education as a separate choice the employee may decline without affecting the job, to fund education or credentials rather than ordinary on-the-job training, and to state the repayment amount and the period in writing before the employer pays.

The law enforcement agreement must state that the agency will pay the academy cost required for licensure, the conditions for payment and repayment, and that repayment is waived if the departing officer does not need a law enforcement license within one or two years after leaving, depending on when the officer left [^mi-sop-police-agreement-terms]. No Michigan statute located for this guide sets a separate rule for relocation repayment, so a relocation repayment required as a condition of employment is tested under the general bar in MCL 408.478(1) [^mi-sop-condition-ban-training].

## How are Michigan repayment and wage-deduction rules enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** A repayment contract that violates MCL 408.478(1) is unenforceable, and the employee can raise the statute as a defense when the employer sues. In *Sands* the employer sued for breach of the tuition contract [^mi-sop-sands-employer-suit], and the Michigan Supreme Court held the contract void and unenforceable [^mi-sop-sands-void-enforcement] and reinstated the lower-court judgments [^mi-sop-sands-holding-enforcement]. An employee who believes the employer violated the wage payment act, including by an unauthorized deduction, may file a written complaint with the department within 12 months [^mi-sop-complaint-twelve-months]. For a violation of sections 2 through 8, the department must order the wages and fringe benefits due plus a 10 percent annual penalty [^mi-sop-department-order-enforcement].

After a complaint, the department notifies the employer, investigates and attempts informal resolution [^mi-sop-complaint-investigation], and if the dispute is not resolved issues a determination within 90 days that either side may ask to have reviewed within 14 days [^mi-sop-complaint-procedure]. For flagrant or repeated violations the department may add exemplary damages of up to twice the amount due [^mi-sop-exemplary-damages-enforcement], and it may assess a civil penalty of up to $1,000 for any violation of the act [^mi-sop-civil-penalty-scope]. Where a repayment agreement outside MCL 408.478(1) fixes a stipulated amount, the amount is sustained only if it is reasonable in relation to the possible injury and not unconscionable or excessive [^mi-sop-uawgm-liquidated-test-enforcement].

## What repayment structure is safest under Michigan law? {#safest-structure}

**Short answer.** The safest Michigan structure is an optional education repayment agreement that fits the 2023 exception: the employer offers to fund the employee's education on the understanding that the employee will repay the costs unless the employee stays for a specific period [^mi-sop-optional-education-exception-safest]. As a drafting recommendation, not an assured result, the offer should be separate from the job offer and declinable without consequence, because, outside the law enforcement academy exception [^mi-sop-police-academy-exception-intro-safest], a repayment obligation the employee must accept to be hired or to stay is void under MCL 408.478(1) [^mi-sop-sands-tuition-contract-void-safest]. Tying the amount to the employer's documented cost and reducing it over the service period may reduce penalty risk, although the amount remains subject to the test that it be reasonable in relation to the possible injury and not unconscionable or excessive [^mi-sop-uawgm-liquidated-test-safest]. Any deduction needs the employee's written consent for that wage payment and may not reduce pay below the minimum wage [^mi-sop-employer-benefit-deduction-consent-safest], so where no consent covers the final paycheck, paying it in full and pursuing the balance separately avoids a department order for the withheld wages plus the 10 percent penalty [^mi-sop-department-order-safest].
