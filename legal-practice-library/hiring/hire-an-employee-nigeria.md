# Hire an employee in Nigeria[^about]

Plan a first Nigerian employee hire: Labour Act coverage, written terms, minimum wage, leave, PAYE tax, pension and payroll levies, employee data, IP ownership, medical checks, notice and restraints.

## What should a founder settle before hiring a first employee in Nigeria? {#prepare-the-hire}

**Short answer.** A Nigeria-resident employee's pay is chargeable to Nigerian tax wherever it arises, unless an exemption applies, such as the one for employment pay at or below the national minimum wage, so a first hire usually brings Nigerian payroll duties even when a foreign company is the employer. [^ng-resident-income-chargeable] [^ng-prepare-minimum-wage-pay-exempt] [^ng-foreign-employer-residence] [^ng-prepare-employer-deducts-tax] As a practical safeguard, a founder settles who the employer is, the role and written terms, and payroll registration before the start date.

The hiring file records the legal employer's name and address, the candidate, actual duties, reporting line, work location and State of residence, start date, pay and payment cycle, working hours, leave, and any fixed-term expiry. Each of those facts feeds a later question in this guide, from worker coverage to payroll tax and pension.

A startup with a U.S. or other foreign parent and no Nigerian company still has to decide who the employer is. This guide does not resolve whether a foreign company employing staff in Nigeria must incorporate locally, or how a foreign employer registers for each Nigerian payroll scheme. Those questions sit with the company's Nigerian counsel and payroll provider before the first salary run.

A foreign national hired to work in Nigeria is a separate immigration workstream. The Nigeria Immigration Service describes the CERPAC residence permit as the card for foreigners residing or employed in Nigeria. [^ng-immigration-cerpac-permit]

The [Nigeria hiring reviewer checklist](/checklists/hiring/nigeria) turns these points into review items.

## Does Nigeria's Labour Act cover every employee? {#labour-act-coverage}

**Short answer.** Nigeria's Labour Act does not cover every employee as a worker: section 91 excludes, among others, people exercising administrative, executive, technical or professional functions. [^nigeria-worker-definition] Coverage depends on actual duties and the listed exclusions, rather than a job title.

Section 91 brings manual and clerical work within the definition, and it covers both a contract of service and a contract personally to execute any work. [^nigeria-worker-definition] A software engineer or senior manager therefore needs a coverage assessment before a founder relies on the Labour Act rules in this guide, including written particulars, leave, notice and the medical examination.

Exclusion from the Labour Act's worker definition is not exclusion from every other law. The tax, pension and data questions below apply their own statutes, discussed in those questions. The HIV and AIDS (Anti-Discrimination) Act, for example, applies to employers and employees in the public and private sectors. [^nigeria-hiv-act-coverage] As a drafting safeguard, a complete written agreement records an excluded employee's agreed terms.

Calling someone a contractor does not settle the point either. The Labour Act definition reaches a contract personally to execute work, so a long-term individual contractor who works only for the company needs the same coverage analysis as an employee, still subject to the statutory exclusions. [^nigeria-worker-definition] This guide does not set out a Nigerian employee-versus-contractor test.

## What must written employment terms in Nigeria contain? {#written-employment-terms}

**Short answer.** For a worker covered by Nigeria's Labour Act, section 7(1) requires written particulars within three months after employment starts, unless the worker has a written employment contract covering every required particular and has a copy under section 7(6). [^nigeria-written-particulars] [^nigeria-contract-copy] An offer letter can serve that purpose if it meets both conditions.

Section 7(1) lists employer details; the worker's name and address and place and date of engagement; nature of employment; the expiry date of a fixed-term contract; the notice period with regard to section 11; wage rates, calculation, payment method and frequency; hours, holidays and holiday pay, sickness or injury absence and sick-pay terms; and special conditions. [^nigeria-written-particulars] Completing these fields before the offer goes out is a practical safeguard, separate from the statutory three-month deadline. A signature alone does not establish that the contract covers every particular or that the worker has a copy. [^nigeria-contract-copy]

Policies can supply some terms. Section 7(3) permits the statement to refer to another document that the worker has reasonable opportunities to read or can otherwise reasonably access. [^nigeria-accessible-policy] A later change normally needs a written statement within one month under section 7(2), but section 7(4) offers an alternative where the statement says future changes will be entered in the referenced document and each change is duly recorded within one month. [^nigeria-accessible-policy-changes] [^nigeria-accessible-policy-recording] If no copy of a change statement is left with the worker, section 7(2) requires the employer to preserve it and give the worker reasonable opportunities to read it or other reasonable access. [^nigeria-accessible-policy-changes] These provisions govern information about terms; they do not give an employer a general right to change agreed terms on its own.

## Does Nigeria's minimum wage apply to a small startup? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Nigeria's National Minimum Wage Act 2019 does not apply its minimum-wage duty to an establishment employing fewer than 25 persons, or to part-time and commission-paid workers. [^ng-minimum-wage-exemptions] Where no section 4(1) exemption applies, an agreement to pay less than the national minimum wage is void. [^ng-minimum-wage-void-agreement]

Nigerian practitioners report that the 2024 amendment raised the national minimum wage to ₦70,000 a month, shortened the review cycle from five years to three, and kept the section 4 exemptions. [^olisa-agbakoba-minimum-wage-amendment] [^simmonscooper-minimum-wage-exemptions-retained] This guide has not retrieved the enacted 2024 text from an official host, so the rate rests on that commentary rather than on a quoted section.

A startup that will cross 25 employees, or a group with several Nigerian entities, needs a reading of how the Act counts staff in an establishment before it relies on the small-employer exemption. This guide does not resolve how the exemption counts staff across related companies. A full-time salary above ₦70,000 a month avoids the question.

## What paid leave must a Nigerian offer include? {#leave-and-sick-pay}

**Short answer.** For a worker covered by Nigeria's Labour Act, section 18 gives at least six working days' holiday with full pay after twelve months' continuous service, and section 16 gives paid sick leave of up to twelve working days in each calendar year, subject to its conditions. [^ng-annual-holiday-with-pay] [^ng-sick-leave-twelve-days] Sick pay requires temporary illness certified by a registered medical practitioner, a contract that continues with the worker otherwise ready and willing to work, and consent to an employer-nominated examination if requested. [^ng-sick-leave-twelve-days]

For a worker under sixteen, including an apprentice, section 18 sets the floor at twelve working days. Section 18 also bars paying wages in place of the annual holiday while the contract continues and allows deferral by agreement up to 24 months' service. [^ng-annual-holiday-with-pay] Under section 18(4), a worker who leaves after at least six but less than twelve months' continuous service, or at least six months after last qualifying for a holiday, is paid a proportion of one week's full pay for that period. [^ng-holiday-pay-on-leaving] Section 19 counts only wages received in money, excluding overtime and other allowances, in calculating leave pay and sick pay. [^ng-leave-pay-money-wages]

Section 54 gives a woman in a covered undertaking the right to leave work six weeks before expected confinement on a medical certificate, bars work for six weeks after confinement, and requires at least half pay for that absence if she has six months' continuous service. [^ng-maternity-protection-leave] The statute sets a floor for covered workers; a contract or policy can give more.

Sections 16 and 18 apply to workers within the Labour Act's definition, while section 54 is framed around women in the listed undertakings rather than that definition. [^ng-leave-worker-definition] [^ng-maternity-protection-leave] Because sections 16 and 18 do not set leave for an employee outside the worker definition, stating annual and sick leave expressly in the offer letter or handbook is a drafting safeguard for that employee; maternity rights need a separate section 54 assessment.

## How does PAYE tax work for a Nigerian employee under the 2025 tax Acts? {#paye-payroll-tax}

**Short answer.** The Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 requires an employer to deduct an employee's income tax from each salary payment, and the Joint Revenue Board's Personal Income Tax Guidelines 2026 require that tax to be remitted by the 10th day of the following month. [^ng-employer-deducts-employee-tax] [^ng-jrb-paye-remit-by-tenth-day] For a resident individual, the relevant tax authority is ordinarily the State or Federal Capital Territory authority where the employee resides. [^ng-state-authority-resident-individuals] [^ng-nigerian-employment-residence]

Section 51(7) requires the deductions across a year to add up to the employee's tax for that year, so payroll recalculates when pay changes. [^ng-employer-deducts-employee-tax] Separately, section 107(1) of the Nigeria Tax Administration Act makes a person who has not remitted deducted tax by the 21st day of the following month liable for the unremitted amount, a 10% annual administrative penalty and interest at the Central Bank monetary policy rate. [^ng-remit-by-21st-day] A payroll that remits by the 10th meets both dates. Every employer also files an annual return of all employees' emoluments by 31 January. [^ng-paye-annual-employer-return]

The Nigeria Tax Act's Fourth Schedule sets the individual rates: the first ₦800,000 of taxable income at 0%, then bands of 15%, 18%, 21% and 23%, and 25% above ₦50 million. [^ng-individual-income-tax-bands] Section 58 excludes an individual earning the national minimum wage from those rates, and section 162(1)(t) exempts income from an employment paying the national minimum wage or less. [^ng-individual-rates-minimum-wage-exclusion] [^ng-minimum-wage-pay-exempt] Pension, National Housing Fund and health-insurance contributions are among the eligible deductions in computing taxable income. [^ng-eligible-deductions-pension-nhf]

Olaniwun Ajayi's July 2025 guidance, published before the 2026 guidelines, describes a monthly deduction and 21st-day remittance cycle. [^olaniwun-ajayi-paye-monthly-remittance] Udo Udoma & Belo-Osagie give the Lagos example of remitting to the Lagos State Inland Revenue Service, and Aluko & Oyebode report that both Acts took effect on 1 January 2026. [^udo-udoma-paye-residential-state] [^aluko-oyebode-tax-acts-commencement]

The tax-Act cards quote the gazetted copy of each Act published on the National Assembly website.

## Which pension and social-insurance contributions apply to a Nigerian employee? {#pension-and-payroll-contributions}

**Short answer.** A private employer with three or more employees falls within Nigeria's Contributory Pension Scheme, according to the National Pension Commission's reading of section 2 of the Pension Reform Act 2014. [^ng-pension-three-employees] Under the shared arrangement, employees under the scheme contribute at least 8% of monthly emoluments and the employer at least 10%, and the employer remits both within seven working days after paying salaries. [^ng-pension-rates-and-remittance] Section 4(4)(b) of the Act lets the employer instead bear the whole contribution, which must then be at least 20% of the employee's monthly emoluments. [^ng-pension-employer-bears-full-contribution] Monthly emoluments are as the contract defines them but not less than basic salary plus housing and transport allowances. [^ng-pension-monthly-emoluments-base]

Udo Udoma & Belo-Osagie read the Act the same way and add that an employer remits for an employee without an account into a nominal account at an administrator the employer chooses. [^udo-udoma-pension-three-employees] The Commission's FAQ puts the employer-only floor at 18% rather than the 20% in section 4(4)(b), and the Act's figure governs. [^ng-pension-faq-employer-only-rate] [^ng-pension-employer-bears-full-contribution] The Commission's group life guidelines quote section 4(5) as requiring an employer to maintain group life insurance of at least three times each employee's annual total emolument, and describe that cover as mandatory for employees under the scheme. [^ng-group-life-three-times] [^ng-group-life-scheme-employees] The guidelines treat annual total emolument as gross emolument and require the employer to begin written renewal negotiations two months before the cover expires. [^ng-group-life-gross-emolument] [^ng-group-life-renewal] The Commission's FAQ states that foreigners working in Nigeria and employees on temporary, contract or tenured appointments are not required to join the scheme but may join voluntarily. [^ng-pension-foreigners-voluntary] [^ng-pension-temporary-contract-voluntary] Each covered employee opens a retirement savings account with a pension fund administrator of the employee's choice. [^ng-pension-rsa-choice] If an employee does not open an account within six months, the employer asks an administrator to open a temporary account for the remittances. [^ng-pension-temporary-account]

The Employees' Compensation Act 2010 set a minimum monthly employer contribution of 1.0% of total monthly payroll to the Employees' Compensation Fund for the Act's first two years. [^ng-employees-compensation-contribution] After that period, section 33(2) leaves contribution amounts and assessment rates to the Fund's Board, and section 34 has the Board assess employers. [^ng-employees-compensation-assessment] Udo Udoma & Belo-Osagie's start-up guidance also lists Industrial Training Fund contributions for employers with five or more employees or ₦50 million turnover. [^udo-udoma-startup-payroll-levies] This guide has not verified that threshold, which other practitioners state differently, or the National Housing Fund rules.

Pension coverage remains narrow in practice. The Commission reported 11,183,475 retirement savings accounts at the end of the first quarter of 2026, which it put at about 12.1% of an estimated 92 million labour force. [^ng-pencom-rsa-registrations-first-quarter-2026] The National Bureau of Statistics reported that 16.0% of employed people were in wage employment in the first quarter of 2024. [^ng-nbs-wage-employment-share] These figures describe the workforce, not the terms a startup should offer.

## What does Nigeria's data protection law require for employee records? {#employee-data}

**Short answer.** The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 requires an employer collecting personal data directly from a candidate or employee to tell that person, before collection, who the controller is, the lawful basis and purposes, the recipients, the retention period and the employee's rights. [^ng-ndpa-notice-before-collection] Sending that data to a foreign parent company needs a transfer basis under section 41(1) that the employer records under section 41(2). [^ng-ndpa-cross-border-transfer] [^ng-ndpa-record-transfer-basis]

Section 27(1) lists the notice contents, including the right to complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission and any automated decision-making. [^ng-ndpa-notice-before-collection] Because the duty applies before the first direct collection, a privacy notice given before or with the first request for candidate information is a practical safeguard; it names the lawful basis for each purpose.

For a startup whose payroll, HR system or management sits abroad, section 41(1) permits a transfer only where the recipient is subject to adequate protection through law, binding corporate rules, contractual clauses, a code of conduct or certification, or a section 43 condition applies; section 41(2) requires the controller to record the basis and the adequacy assessment. [^ng-ndpa-cross-border-transfer] [^ng-ndpa-record-transfer-basis] An intragroup data agreement between the Nigerian employer and the parent is one way to supply that record.

Aluko & Oyebode note that, without adequate protection, a transfer can proceed only under a section 43 condition, and Olaniwun Ajayi reads the Commission's 2025 General Application and Implementation Directive as requiring Commission approval of a transfer instrument before an organisation relies on it. [^aluko-oyebode-ndpa-section-43-transfers] [^olaniwun-ajayi-gaid-transfer-approval]

Health information from a pre-employment medical examination is sensitive personal data, because the Act's definition includes health status, and section 30(1) allows its processing only on a listed basis such as consent or an employment-law obligation. [^ng-ndpa-health-sensitive-data] [^ng-ndpa-sensitive-data-basis] This guide does not set out the Commission's registration thresholds or the rest of the 2025 Directive.

## Does a Nigerian startup own the code its employees write? {#ip-ownership}

**Short answer.** A Nigerian startup cannot rely on a statutory employer-ownership rule for its employees' code, because the Copyright Act 2022 vests copyright initially in the author unless an agreement provides otherwise. [^ng-copyright-vests-in-author] The Act's employer-ownership rule in section 28(2) covers only government and specified public bodies, and an assignment of copyright has no effect unless it is in writing. [^ng-copyright-government-employer] [^ng-copyright-assignment-in-writing]

The Act's definition of literary work includes computer programmes, so source code written by an employee falls under the section 28 ownership rule. [^ng-copyright-literary-work-software] For a private startup, section 28(1) makes an agreement the mechanism that gives the company ownership, and section 30(3) requires an assignment to be in writing. [^ng-copyright-vests-in-author] [^ng-copyright-assignment-in-writing] A written assignment of copyright in work made in the course of employment is the core drafting safeguard; confidentiality and cooperation terms are practical additions rather than statutory requirements.

Templars and Aluko & Oyebode read the ownership rule the same way: copyright vests initially in the author unless an agreement provides otherwise. [^templars-copyright-vests-in-author] [^aluko-oyebode-employment-contract-copyright] Section 30(10) permits an assignment of future works but not of all future works of an author. [^ng-copyright-future-works-limit] Templars read that limit as requiring some quantification by period, identification or number. [^templars-future-works-quantified] Whether a clause limited to works made in the course of the employment is quantified enough has not been tested in the sources reviewed, so the clause benefits from a defined period and subject matter.

A U.S. parent that needs the code in its own name can take a further written assignment from the Nigerian employer once that employer holds the rights; the parent's title depends on each assignment in the chain being effective. [^ng-copyright-transferable-by-assignment] [^ng-copyright-assignment-in-writing] This guide does not address patents or industrial designs.

## Is a medical examination required when hiring in Nigeria? {#medical-examination}

**Short answer.** For a worker covered by Nigeria's Labour Act, section 8(1) requires examination by a registered medical practitioner at the employer's expense, subject to any applicable State Authority exemption order under section 8(2). [^nigeria-medical-examination] [^nigeria-medical-examination-exemptions] That duty does not permit HIV screening as a condition of employment except in the narrow case section 9 of the HIV and AIDS (Anti-Discrimination) Act 2014 allows. [^nigeria-hiv-testing-limits]

Section 8(2) permits State Authority orders exempting specified agricultural or near-home employment categories; it does not make every such hire automatically exempt. [^nigeria-medical-examination-exemptions] The actual role, the State, any applicable order and the examination arrangements matter before the start date.

Section 9(1) of the HIV Act permits an HIV-test precondition only where two competent medical authorities, working independently, certify to the Court that failure to test creates a clear and present danger of transmission to others. Section 9(4) requires informed and full consent under national confidentiality and counselling guidelines, and section 9(5) preserves medical testing for fitness for work under existing law. [^nigeria-hiv-testing-limits] The HIV Act reaches employers and employees in the public and private sectors, regardless of the Labour Act's worker exclusions. [^nigeria-hiv-act-application]

## What notice and redundancy rules apply when ending a Nigerian employment? {#termination-notice}

**Short answer.** For workers covered by the Labour Act, section 11(2) sets notice to end employment at one day for up to three months' service, one week for more than three months but under two years, two weeks for two years but under five, and one month for five years or more. [^nigeria-notice-periods] A redundancy of covered workers also triggers section 20's duties to inform worker representatives, apply last in, first out within the category of workers affected, subject to merit, and try to negotiate redundancy pay for workers not covered by ministerial redundancy-allowance regulations. [^ng-labour-act-redundancy]

| Contract has continued for | Section 11(2) notice |
| --- | --- |
| Three months or less | One day |
| More than three months but less than two years | One week |
| Two years but less than five years | Two weeks |
| Five years or more | One month |

Notice of one week or more must be in writing under section 11(3). [^nigeria-notice-periods] Section 11(2) specifies these notice periods for covered contracts. Section 11 also excludes the day notice is given, preserves termination without notice for qualifying conduct, allows waiver of notice or payment in lieu, and requires wages payable in money to be paid by the end of the notice period, so the table is not a complete dismissal rule. [^ng-notice-day-conduct-payment-in-lieu]

Section 20 defines redundancy as an involuntary and permanent loss of employment caused by an excess of manpower. [^ng-labour-act-redundancy] Section 20 itself fixes no redundancy payment: it requires best endeavours to negotiate payments for discharged workers not protected by regulations under section 20(2), and leaves any compulsory allowances to those regulations. [^ng-labour-act-redundancy]

AELEX notes that the section 20 procedure protects only employees within the Labour Act's worker definition. [^aelex-redundancy-statutory-workers-only] For other employees, practitioners describe a developing body of case law: Templars treat termination without reasons as still valid where the contract provides for it, while G. Elias describe employers ending a contract without notice as choosing between common-law pay in lieu of notice and international-best-practice compensation based on the contract's duration. [^templars-termination-without-reason] [^g-elias-international-best-practice-termination]

## Can a Nigerian employment contract include a non-compete? {#post-employment-restrictions}

**Short answer.** A Nigerian employment contract may contain a post-employment restraint, and the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 takes an individual's agreed work restrictions outside its prohibitions only where the period is no more than two years. [^ng-fccpa-service-contract-exception] Practitioners read that limit as a ceiling that does not by itself make a restraint enforceable. [^olisa-agbakoba-two-year-ceiling] [^aelex-restraints-prima-facie-unenforceable] For a covered worker, a restraint is also a special condition that the written particulars must include. [^ng-restraint-special-conditions]

Section 68(1)(e) of the Act, in its list of things the Act does not prohibit, covers a service contract in so far as a person who is not a body corporate accepts work restrictions, as an employee or otherwise, during or after termination, for no more than two years. [^ng-fccpa-service-contract-exception] That wording takes a restraint out of the Act's own prohibitions; it does not answer whether a court will enforce a particular duration, territory or customer restriction under restraint-of-trade doctrine.

Olisa Agbakoba Legal reads the two-year limit as a ceiling rather than a safe harbour, and AELEX states the general rule that restraints of trade are prima facie unenforceable unless reasonable and fair. [^olisa-agbakoba-two-year-ceiling] [^aelex-restraints-prima-facie-unenforceable]

Because practitioners treat a restraint as supportable only where it protects a demonstrable legitimate interest, a restraint assessment records the confidential information, assets or relationships needing protection, the employee's access, and the reason for each restriction. [^olisa-agbakoba-two-year-ceiling]
