# Small-Business Asset Purchase Agreements: Closing and Handover[^about]

What must be ready at signing and at closing, what happens when a contract or account cannot be transferred, keeping the seller's employees, and how the buyer confirms it has received every asset.

## What must be ready at signing and at closing? {#closing-exchange}

**Short answer.** What must be ready depends partly on the timetable, because a sign-then-close arrangement lets the parties lock in the price and representations at signing and then have time to secure third-party consents and approvals. [^waylaw-closing-exchange-consents] The agreement can make those items conditions to closing, as one filed asset purchase agreement did for its material consents. [^consents-closing-exchange]

In one filed agreement, the closing took place simultaneously with the execution and delivery of the agreement, so the deliverables that agreement required at closing were due when the parties signed. [^p3-grove-simultaneous] In a deferred closing, the [signing-to-closing question](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-signing-to-closing/us#between-signing-and-closing) covers the extra terms the gap needs.

The table lists deliverables that filed asset purchase agreements assign to each side and when each is due; each row cites one filed example. The [OpenAgreements Closing Checklist](/templates/openagreements-closing-checklist) can track each document, its responsible party and its status through signing and closing.

| Deliverable | Delivered by | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signed agreement and disclosure schedules [^p3-bankrate-disclosure-schedules] | Both parties; each delivers its own schedules | Signing |
| Bill of sale for tangible assets [^p3-luna-bill-of-sale] | Seller | Closing |
| Assignment and assumption agreement for contracts and assumed liabilities [^p3-luna-buyer-deliverables] | Seller signs as assignor; buyer countersigns to assume | Closing |
| Patent, trademark and copyright assignments and a domain name assignment [^p3-grove-ip-domain] | Seller, and any owner who holds a right personally | Closing |
| Lien payoff letters, then filed UCC-3 terminations [^p3-grove-payoff-letters] | Seller, with its lenders | Payoff letters at closing; terminations after closing |
| Third-party consents [^consents-closing-exchange] | Seller obtains, as the agreement allocates | Before closing, as a closing condition |
| Bring-down certificate and secretary's certificate attaching resolutions [^p3-luna-bring-down-secretary] | Each party for itself | Closing |
| Purchase price by wire and escrow amount to the escrow agent [^p3-luna-closing-payments] | Buyer | Closing |

| Closing statement setting out the flow of funds [^p3-chefs-flow-of-funds] | Seller parties sign; buyer pays against it | Closing |

A deliverable can also be a closing condition: in one filed agreement, receiving all of the seller's closing deliveries was the first condition to the buyer's obligation to close, followed by the accuracy of the seller's representations. [^p3-greenfield-deliveries-condition] Under a condition drafted that way, a missing deliverable leaves the buyer's condition unsatisfied, and an outside-date clause can then let the buyer walk away, as in another filed agreement that let the buyer terminate if its closing conditions would not be fulfilled by a stated date. [^p3-greenfield-deliveries-condition][^d2-ce-luna-outside-date]

## What if a lease, contract or account cannot be transferred at closing? {#consent-failure}

**Short answer.** When a lease, contract or account needs a third party's consent to transfer, the agreement can make obtaining the material consents a closing condition, as one filed asset purchase agreement does. [^consents-consent-failure]

In an asset purchase the trigger is consent to assignment: anti-assignment clauses require the counterparty's consent before the buyer can assume the contract. [^settles-anti-assignment] The buyer's side of the work is to find every contract that needs consent, and filed agreements put the list on the seller through a representation that no consent is required except as listed in a schedule. [^p3-greenfield-consents-rep][^p3-luna-consents-rep]

The seller's side is to request and obtain the consents as the agreement allocates that work; one filed agreement required the seller to use best efforts to obtain all material consents, [^p3-luna-seller-best-efforts] and the buyer in that agreement agreed to cooperate in obtaining the scheduled consents without having to change its business or spend material funds. [^p3-luna-buyer-cooperation] Another agreement required both parties to use reasonable efforts to obtain third-party consents to the assignment of assumed contracts before closing. [^p3-salem-both-efforts]

For a business with premises, landlord consent to the lease transfer is described as a usual closing condition in a typical small business sale. [^ratschko-landlord-condition] When a consent is still outstanding at closing, Jennifer M. Settles describes escrowing part of the proceeds until it arrives. [^settles-consent-escrow] Another approach is to close without the contract and pass its benefits through: in one filed agreement, a contract whose consent had not been obtained before closing was deemed not assigned until consent arrived, the seller gave the buyer the contract's benefits in the meantime, and the buyer paid the corresponding liabilities. [^p3-salem-pass-through] Online accounts are covered in [the account-migration question](/practice-guides/asset-purchase/us#migration-timing) and [the app-transfer question](/practice-guides/asset-purchase/us#app-transfer).

## What paperwork and notice rules apply when the buyer keeps the seller's employees? {#employees-and-workforce}

**Short answer.** When the seller's workers continue their employment with the buyer as a related, successor or reorganized employer, USCIS lets the buyer treat them either as new hires with new Forms I-9 or as continuing employees whose previously completed forms the buyer keeps. [^uscis-i9-successor-choice] A buyer that keeps those forms accepts responsibility for any errors or omissions in them. [^uscis-i9-successor-errors]

A buyer that treats the employees as new hires may complete the new forms before the acquisition takes place, as long as it has offered the employee a job and the employee has accepted. [^d2-uscis-i9-new-form-timing] A buyer that keeps the prior forms is expected to review each form with the employee and update or reverify the employee's information as necessary. [^d2-uscis-i9-review-prior]

For larger employers, the federal WARN Act allocates plant-closing and mass-layoff notice duties in a sale: the seller is responsible up to and including the effective date of the sale, the purchaser afterward, and, for WARN notice purposes only, the seller's employees on that date, other than part-time employees, are treated as the purchaser's employees immediately after it. [^warn-2101b-sale] The Act applies only to employers with 100 or more employees, excluding part-time employees, or 100 or more employees working at least 4,000 hours a week in total, exclusive of overtime. [^warn-2101a-employer] The Act's rights are in addition to, and not in lieu of, other contractual or statutory rights of the employees, and its notice period runs concurrently with any notice period another statute requires. [^d2-warn-2105-other-rights] State statutes can reach smaller workplaces, and California's defines a covered establishment as an industrial or commercial facility that employs, or has employed within the preceding 12 months, 75 or more persons. [^d2-cal-lab-1400-5-covered]

## How do I know I have received all the assets? {#digital-handover}

**Short answer.** A contractual handover milestone may not mean every asset has arrived, as one filed purchase agreement shows by expressly contemplating that some assets could still be transferring after its defined migration milestone. [^migration-digital-handover]

Receipt of all the assets is therefore confirmed against the asset schedule, which is also how marketplace escrow works; TrustMRR's FAQ says the seller transfers the agreed assets and escrow releases the funds once both sides confirm the transfer is complete. [^d3-trustmrr-confirm-transfer] A handover record links each domain, repository, account, data set, contract and intellectual-property instrument to its entry in the asset schedule, the same asset-by-asset detail that TrustMRR's escrow requires in the purchase agreement. [^d3-trustmrr-asset-details-dh]

An agreed acceptance window gives the buyer time to run the business before the price is released; one filed online-business purchase gave the buyer fourteen days after migration to inspect the assets, during which the buyer was to operate them as close as possible to the seller's operation, [^p6-jeffs-inspection-window] and released the price only after that period expired. [^d3-jeffs-release-dh] Problems that appear only in operation, such as a login that fails on the buyer's device or a payment integration that stops working, need to be raised within the window; under that agreement, a buyer that gave no written notice within the inspection period was deemed to have waived its contingencies, including changes in the assets' performance. [^d3-jeffs-inspection-waiver]

Where an account has no provider-supported transfer and its password has to pass between the parties, a password manager's sharing feature can carry it; 1Password, for example, creates a unique link and lets the sender choose when the link expires and who can open it. [^p6-1password-share-link] A shared item is a copy, so a password changed after sharing needs a new link, [^p6-1password-share-copy] and a password the buyer changes after receipt is no longer one the seller holds.

Two-factor authentication and recovery settings can stop a handover when the second factor stays with the seller; GitHub states that its support team cannot restore access to an account with two-factor authentication enabled once the two-factor credentials and recovery methods are lost, [^p6-github-2fa-no-restore] and a recovery that relies on a one-time password can take up to three business days. [^p6-github-2fa-three-days] The handover record can therefore list each account's recovery email, phone number, authenticator app, security keys and recovery codes, and confirm that each has been replaced with one the buyer controls before seller access ends, because GitHub warns that an account holder who cannot use any recovery method has permanently lost access. [^d3-github-permanent-loss]

Seller access can be removed when no longer needed, and some transfers leave it in place; after a GitHub repository transfer, the original owner is added as a collaborator on the transferred repository. [^p6-github-original-owner-collaborator]
