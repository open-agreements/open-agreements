# Employee Expense Reimbursement Across the United States: When an Employer Must Repay Business Expenses[^about]

A cross-state framework for general duties covering all necessary or employer-authorized business expenses, the federal minimum-wage floor, and offer letters and policies, with links to state notes and the survey.

This guide covers employee business expense reimbursement across jurisdictions. The [survey](/surveys/expense-reimbursement/us) starts with seven states that have a general reimbursement statute: [California](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/california), [Illinois](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/illinois), [Iowa](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/iowa), [Montana](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/montana), [New Hampshire](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/new-hampshire), [North Dakota](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/north-dakota), and [South Dakota](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/south-dakota). Other jurisdictions will be added in later batches.

## Which states have a general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses? {#which-states}

**Short answer.** California [^cal-lab-2802-indemnify-necessary-expenditures], Montana [^mont-39-2-701-indemnify-necessary-expenses], North Dakota [^nd-34-02-01-indemnify-necessary-expenses], and South Dakota [^sdcl-60-2-1-indemnify-necessary-expenses] require an employer to indemnify what an employee necessarily spends in direct consequence of job duties, while Illinois [^ilcs-115-9-5-liable-if-authorized-or-required], Iowa [^iowa-91a-3-reimburse-authorized-expenses], and New Hampshire [^nh-rsa-275-57-reimburse-requested-expenses] reach only expenses the employer authorized, required, or requested.

The first four statutes contain no pre-approval condition in the cited text. [^cal-lab-2802-indemnify-necessary-expenditures][^mont-39-2-701-indemnify-necessary-expenses][^nd-34-02-01-indemnify-necessary-expenses][^sdcl-60-2-1-indemnify-necessary-expenses] Each of the other three turns on the employer's own decision: Illinois makes the employer liable only for an expense it authorized or required (or where it broke its own written policy), Iowa covers expenses the employer authorized, and New Hampshire covers expenses incurred at the employer's request. [^ilcs-115-9-5-liable-if-authorized-or-required][^iowa-91a-3-reimburse-authorized-expenses][^nh-rsa-275-57-reimburse-requested-expenses]

## What does federal minimum-wage law require for employee business expenses? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, wages count as paid only when paid free and clear, and an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which their cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^cfr-531-35-wages-free-and-clear][^cfr-531-35-required-tools-cut-into-wages]

The federal rule is a floor rather than a general duty to reimburse every business expense. It is breached only when an employer-required cost pushes the week's pay below the minimum or overtime wages due. [^cfr-531-35-required-tools-cut-into-wages]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy shift business expenses to the employee? {#offer-letters}

**Short answer.** California voids agreements waiving its reimbursement protections, while Illinois permits written expense limits subject to statutory restrictions [^cal-lab-2804-waiver-null-and-void][^ilcs-115-9-5-policy-caps-not-de-minimis]. *Gattuso v. Harte-Hanks Shoppers, Inc.* [^gattuso-enhanced-compensation-apportioned] permits identifiable reimbursement through enhanced pay, but an agreed lump sum does not excuse less than full reimbursement [^ca-gattuso-lump]. In Illinois, an employee who fails to follow an established written reimbursement policy is not entitled to reimbursement under the section [^ilcs-115-9-5-noncompliant-employee-not-entitled].

Illinois permits policy specifications for necessary expenses, but its text bars a policy providing no reimbursement or only de minimis reimbursement [^ilcs-115-9-5-policy-caps-not-de-minimis]. The cited reimbursement sections in Iowa, Montana, New Hampshire, North Dakota, and South Dakota do not address waiver expressly [^ia-waiver-text][^mt-waiver-text][^nh-waiver-text][^nd-waiver-text][^sd-waiver-text]. Whatever a policy says, an employer-required tool cost cannot cut into minimum or overtime wages due under the federal rule [^federal-policy-floor].

## Must an employer reimburse remote-work costs such as a personal phone or home internet? {#remote-work}

**Short answer.** California requires a reasonable share of a personal phone bill when an employee must make work calls on that phone [^cochran-reasonable-percentage-phone-bill]. Illinois reimbursement turns on an authorized or required necessary expense or the employer's failure to follow its own policy [^ilcs-115-9-5-remote-liability-if-authorized]. North Dakota excludes the purchase or rental of equipment also used outside work [^nd-34-02-01-dual-use-equipment-excluded].

*Cochran v. Schwan's Home Service, Inc.* [^cochran-required-personal-phone-is-expense] concerns work calls on a personal cell phone; its rule calls for a reasonable percentage of the bill [^cochran-reasonable-percentage-phone-bill]. None of the cited California, Illinois, and North Dakota provisions names remote work [^ca-remote-text][^ilcs-115-9-5-remote-liability-if-authorized][^nd-34-02-01-dual-use-equipment-excluded]. The North Dakota equipment exclusion reaches a personal phone or computer also used outside work, while the quoted exclusion does not address a cost that is not equipment [^nd-34-02-01-dual-use-equipment-excluded].
