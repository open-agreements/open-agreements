# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Pennsylvania[^about]

Pennsylvania has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but the Wage Payment and Collection Law enforces reimbursements an employer has agreed to pay, and a state regulation bars deductions for employer-required expenses that take pay below the minimum wage.

## Must an employer in Pennsylvania reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Pennsylvania has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses, and our review of the Philadelphia and Pittsburgh codes found no local ordinance that does. A 1961 statute names one specific cost: an employer may not require an employee or applicant who works for it for one work week to pay for a medical examination or medical records the employer requires as a condition of employment, unless the law requires the examination. [^pa-medical-exam-cost-employer-duty] A reimbursement the employer has agreed to pay is different: the Wage Payment and Collection Law (WPCL) defines fringe benefits or wage supplements to include reimbursement for expenses, and counts fringe benefits or wage supplements as wages. [^pa-wpcl-fringe-benefits-expenses-duty][^pa-wpcl-wages-include-fringe-duty] The WPCL does not itself create the right to be reimbursed. The Pennsylvania Superior Court has held that the WPCL only lets an employee enforce payment of compensation to which the employee is entitled under an agreement. [^pa-hartman-wpcl-contract-right-duty] Separately, a Department of Labor and Industry regulation provides that no authorized deduction may take pay below the Minimum Wage Act minimum for an expense or charge the employer requires or authorizes in connection with the employee's assigned duties. [^pa-34-code-9-2-expense-floor-duty] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-pennsylvania-duty]

## Which business expenses must a Pennsylvania employer pay for? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Pennsylvania has no general statute listing business expenses an employer must reimburse. A 1961 statute names one specific cost: an employer may not require an employee or applicant who works for it for one work week to pay for a medical examination or medical records the employer requires as a condition of employment, unless the law requires the examination. [^pa-medical-exam-cost-employer-covered] Beyond that, which expenses an employer must pay back depends on what it agreed to pay, because the WPCL enforces agreed reimbursements as fringe benefits or wage supplements. [^pa-wpcl-fringe-benefits-expenses-covered][^pa-hartman-wpcl-contract-right-covered] A federal court applying Pennsylvania law has held that an employee who pleaded the existence, terms and breach of an oral agreement to reimburse business expenses stated a WPCL claim. [^pa-edelman-expense-wpcl-claim-covered] An employer that agrees to pay a reimbursement directly to the employee must pay it within 10 days after the payment is due, or within 60 days after the employee files a proper claim where the agreement sets no payment time. [^pa-wpcl-fringe-payment-timing-covered] Expenses the employer requires or authorizes for the job are also protected at the wage floor: no authorized deduction may take pay below the Minimum Wage Act minimum for such an expense or charge, and that minimum is $7.25 an hour. [^pa-34-code-9-2-expense-floor-covered][^pa-34-code-231-101a-minimum-rate-covered] A payroll deduction for goods, services or similar items the employee buys from the employer is authorized only if the employee agrees in writing or a collective bargaining agreement authorizes it. [^pa-34-code-9-1-employer-purchases-covered] Board, lodging and other facilities the employer customarily furnishes may count as wages only up to their actual cost, exclusive of profit, and only if the deduction was made known to and agreed by the employee at hiring. [^pa-34-code-231-22-board-lodging-covered][^pa-34-code-231-22-agreed-at-hiring-covered] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-pennsylvania-covered]

## Must a Pennsylvania employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Pennsylvania statute specifically requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs. In our review, we found no Pennsylvania regulation specifically requiring reimbursement of those costs. An employer that agrees to reimburse a remote-work cost must pay it, because the WPCL enforces agreed reimbursements as fringe benefits or wage supplements. [^pa-wpcl-fringe-benefits-expenses-remote][^pa-hartman-wpcl-contract-right-remote] A remote-work cost the employer requires or authorizes for the job may not be recovered through a deduction that takes pay below the $7.25 Minimum Wage Act minimum. [^pa-34-code-9-2-expense-floor-remote][^pa-34-code-231-101a-minimum-rate-remote] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-pennsylvania-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Pennsylvania make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** Because the WPCL enforces only reimbursements the employee is entitled to under an agreement, an offer letter or expense policy largely sets which expenses a Pennsylvania employer must repay. [^pa-hartman-wpcl-contract-right-waiver][^pa-wpcl-fringe-benefits-expenses-waiver] Once the employer has agreed to reimburse an expense, the WPCL lets the employee sue for it as wages. [^pa-wpcl-action-any-wages-waiver][^pa-wpcl-wages-include-fringe-waiver] A policy cannot shift costs below the wage floor: no authorized deduction may take pay below the Minimum Wage Act minimum for an expense or charge the employer requires or authorizes for the job. [^pa-34-code-9-2-expense-floor-waiver] An employee paid less than the minimum wage may recover the shortfall, and an agreement to work for less than the minimum wage is no defense. [^pa-mwa-civil-action-no-agreement-waiver] Deductions from wages are limited to those provided by law or authorized by Department of Labor and Industry regulation, and a deduction for items the employee buys from the employer needs the employee's written authorization or a collective bargaining agreement. [^pa-wpcl-deductions-by-law-waiver][^pa-34-code-9-1-employer-purchases-waiver]

## What remedies does a Pennsylvania employee have for an unpaid agreed reimbursement? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** Any employee to whom any type of wages is payable may sue under the WPCL, and wages include fringe benefits or wage supplements such as reimbursement for expenses. [^pa-wpcl-action-any-wages-remedies][^pa-wpcl-wages-include-fringe-remedies][^pa-wpcl-fringe-benefits-expenses-remedies] Where the wages remain unpaid for 30 days beyond the regular payday, or 60 days beyond a proper claim where no regular payday applies, and no good-faith dispute accounts for the nonpayment, the employee may claim liquidated damages of 25 percent of the wages due or $500, whichever is greater. [^pa-wpcl-liquidated-damages-remedies] An employee awarded judgment under the WPCL is also entitled to reasonable attorneys' fees paid by the defendant. [^pa-wpcl-attorney-fees-remedies] A WPCL action must be brought within three years after the day the wages were due and payable. [^pa-wpcl-three-year-limit-remedies] No authorized deduction may take pay below the Minimum Wage Act minimum for an expense or charge the employer requires or authorizes for the job, and an employee paid less than the minimum wage may recover the shortfall with costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^pa-34-code-9-2-expense-floor-remedies][^pa-mwa-civil-action-no-agreement-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Pennsylvania? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-pennsylvania-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Pennsylvania's minimum wage is also $7.25 an hour and rises automatically with any federal increase, and a state regulation bars deductions for employer-required or employer-authorized expenses that take pay below it. [^pa-34-code-231-101a-minimum-rate-federal][^pa-mwa-federal-ratchet-federal][^pa-34-code-9-2-expense-floor-federal]
