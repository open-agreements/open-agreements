# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Colorado[^about]

Colorado has no general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses, but required uniforms, wage-deduction limits, and state and local minimum-wage rules still shift some costs to the employer.

## Must an employer in Colorado reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Colorado has no general statute requiring an employer to reimburse every business expense an employee incurs. The Colorado Wage Act defines wages as amounts paid for labor or service performed and does not list expense reimbursements. [^co-wages-definition-earned-duty] Separate rules still put some costs on the employer: the Colorado Overtime and Minimum Pay Standards Order (COMPS Order) requires the employer to pay for a uniform or special apparel that is a condition of employment, subject to the order's plain-clothing and cleaning exceptions, and the Wage Act bars any deduction from wages except those it lists, which since June 3, 2026 cover things provided under a written agreement only if they primarily benefit the employee and never personal protective equipment. [^co-comps-uniform-cost-duty][^co-comps-uniform-plain-duty][^co-deductions-primary-benefit-duty][^co-sl339-applicability-duty] Apart from deductions required by law under subsection (1)(a), automatic retirement-plan contributions under subsection (1)(a.5) and revocable deductions the employee authorizes under subsection (1)(d), no Wage Act deduction may take pay below the applicable minimum wage. [^co-deductions-floor-exceptions-duty][^co-deductions-allowed-duty][^co-deductions-revocable-duty]

"(1) An employer shall not make a deduction from the wages or compensation of an employee except as follows: (b) Deductions for loans, advances, goods or services, and equipment or property, or any other thing that primarily benefits an employee , which does not include personal protective equipment , provided by an employer to an employee pursuant to a written agreement between such employer and employee, so long as it is enforceable and not in violation of law;"[^co-deductions-primary-benefit-duty]

## Which business expenses must a Colorado employer pay for? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Required uniforms are the cost Colorado names: where a particular uniform or special apparel is a condition of employment, the employer must pay for its purchase, maintenance and cleaning, subject to the COMPS Order's plain-clothing and cleaning exceptions. [^co-comps-uniform-cost-covered] The employer need not clean a plain, washable uniform that needs no special care, need not furnish ordinary, plain, washable clothing prescribed as a uniform unless it requires a special color, make, pattern, logo or material, and may not deduct the cost of ordinary wear and tear of a uniform from wages. [^co-comps-uniform-plain-covered] Personal protective equipment cannot be charged to the employee through a payroll deduction: the Wage Act now excludes it from the deductions an agreement may authorize and defines it as equipment, clothing, respiratory devices, shields and barriers an employer provides against recognized health and safety hazards. [^co-deductions-primary-benefit-covered][^co-ppe-definition-covered] The definition excludes nonspecialty safety-toe footwear and nonspecialty prescription safety eyewear the employer lets the employee wear off the job site, built-in metatarsal protection provided at the employee's request, and logging boots. [^co-ppe-exclusions-covered] The Colorado Court of Appeals has held that deductions for an employer's own costs of doing business are unenforceable under the Wage Act's written-agreement deduction rule. [^co-303-beauty-business-costs-covered] The Colorado Department of Labor and Employment (CDLE), in guidance it describes as not binding law, takes the position that employees cannot be required to pay for items the employer authorizes them to buy for work use, whether or not reimbursement was promised. [^cdle-info16-authorized-purchases-covered][^cdle-info16-not-binding-covered] No general Colorado statute requires reimbursement of tools, equipment or mileage; those costs reach the employer through the deduction limits, the COMPS Order's closed list of minimum-wage credits and the minimum-wage floor. [^co-deductions-primary-benefit-covered][^co-comps-credits-only-covered][^co-deductions-floor-exceptions-covered] The COMPS Order lets an employer exclude business-expense payments from the regular rate used to calculate overtime, and COMPS Order #40 replaced COMPS Order #39 (2024), which still governs events that occurred while it was in effect. [^co-comps-regular-rate-covered][^co-comps-replaces-39-covered]

"6.3.2 The cost of ordinary wear and tear of a uniform or special apparel shall not be deducted from an employee's wages."[^co-comps-uniform-plain-covered]

## Must a Colorado employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Colorado statute requires an employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs as such, and our review of the COMPS Order found no rule that does. [^co-wages-definition-earned-remote][^co-comps-uniform-cost-remote] CDLE's non-binding guidance addresses phones: an employer that requires work use of a cell phone need not reimburse a phone or plan the employee would have anyway, can use for personal reasons and incurs no added cost from using for work, but must pay the added cost of a more expensive or special phone or plan. [^cdle-info16-cell-phone-remote][^cdle-info16-not-binding-remote] Remote-work costs still matter at the wage floor: the COMPS Order allows only the minimum-wage credits it lists, and under federal law an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which their cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^co-comps-credits-only-remote][^fed-tools-of-trade-remote] An employer that recovers equipment it supplies for remote work through a payroll deduction needs a written agreement, and since June 3, 2026 the item must primarily benefit the employee. [^co-deductions-primary-benefit-remote][^co-sl339-applicability-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Colorado make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Colorado offer letter or expense policy cannot override the employer's uniform-payment duty, the Wage Act's limits on deductions or the applicable minimum wage. [^co-comps-uniform-cost-waiver][^co-deductions-primary-benefit-waiver][^co-deductions-floor-exceptions-waiver] A written agreement supports a payroll deduction only for loans, advances, goods or services, equipment or property, or another thing that primarily benefits the employee, never for personal protective equipment, and only if the agreement is enforceable and lawful. [^co-deductions-primary-benefit-waiver] The Colorado Court of Appeals has held that an agreement to deduct the employer's costs of doing business from wages is unenforceable, so a signed deduction authorization does not let an employer charge its business costs to an employee. [^co-303-beauty-business-costs-waiver] The Wage Act's minimum-wage floor does not apply to revocable deductions the employee authorizes under subsection (1)(d), which cover deductions not otherwise listed, such as insurance, savings plans, stock purchases and charities. [^co-deductions-floor-exceptions-waiver][^co-deductions-revocable-waiver] Separately, under federal law an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which their cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-tools-of-trade-waiver] An employee's written or oral agreement purporting to waive or modify rights in violation of the Colorado Wage Act is void, and an agreement to work for less than the minimum wage does not bar an employee from recovering the unpaid balance. [^co-wage-act-nonwaiver-waiver][^co-minimum-wage-recovery-waiver]

## What happens in Colorado if an employer deducts business costs from wages unlawfully? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** A deduction the Wage Act does not permit leaves wages unpaid, and an employee must bring a Wage Act action within two years after the claim accrues, or three years for a willful violation. [^co-deductions-primary-benefit-remedy][^co-wage-act-limitations-remedy] Where business costs pull pay below the minimum wage, the employee may recover the unpaid balance of the minimum wage with reasonable attorney fees and court costs, whatever the employee agreed to. [^co-minimum-wage-recovery-remedy] The 2026 amendments to the deduction rules apply to conduct on or after June 3, 2026. [^co-sl339-applicability-remedy]

## Do Denver and other local minimum wages limit unreimbursed business costs in Colorado? {#local-minimum-wages}

**Short answer.** Yes: apart from its listed exceptions, the Colorado Wage Act bars payroll deductions for business costs or anything else that take pay below the applicable minimum wage, and Colorado lets a local government set a higher minimum wage for work performed within its boundaries. [^co-deductions-floor-exceptions-local][^co-local-wage-authority-local] The 2026 Colorado minimum wage is $15.16 an hour, and the Colorado Department of Labor and Employment reports a 2026 Denver City and County minimum wage of $19.29 an hour, with higher rates also in Edgewater, unincorporated Boulder County and the City of Boulder. [^co-state-minimum-2026-local][^co-cdle-local-table-local] Denver's labor rules apply the Denver minimum wage to work performed within Denver, including remote work for an employer located elsewhere, and bar any deduction other than taxes or repayment of wage advances from bringing pay below the Denver minimum wage. [^den-rule-remote-work-local][^den-rule-deductions-floor-local] A deduction for a work cost that leaves an employee paid the state minimum whole can still underpay an employee working in Denver if it takes pay below the Denver rate. [^co-deductions-floor-exceptions-local][^co-cdle-local-table-local]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Colorado? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-tools-of-trade-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Colorado separately restricts payroll deductions: apart from the exceptions it lists, no Wage Act deduction may take pay below the applicable minimum wage, which in 2026 is $15.16 an hour statewide. [^co-deductions-floor-exceptions-federal][^co-state-minimum-2026-federal]
