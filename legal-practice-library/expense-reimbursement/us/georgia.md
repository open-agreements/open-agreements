# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Georgia[^about]

Georgia has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses and bars local reimbursement mandates, so the federal minimum wage caps how much of the cost of employer-required tools of the trade an employee can be made to bear, while Georgia's pay-frequency statute requires each payment to equal the full net wages due for the period.

## Must an employer in Georgia reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Georgia has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. The Georgia Department of Labor states that resolution of most workplace problems is the responsibility of several federal agencies, and that the U.S. Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division enforces the federal minimum wage, overtime and hours rules. [^gdol-federal-agencies-duty][^gdol-whd-flsa-duty] Georgia's own minimum wage is $5.15 an hour, but the same department states that, with some limited exceptions, the federal minimum wage rate applies. [^gdol-state-minimum-wage-duty] Since 2022, Georgia has barred cities and counties from adopting a wage or employment benefit mandate, defined as a local requirement to pay a wage rate or provide employment benefits that the Georgia Code or federal law does not otherwise require, so no local ordinance can add a reimbursement duty. [^ga-sb331-local-mandate-duty][^ga-sb331-mandate-definition-duty] The Georgia rule closest to the subject is O.C.G.A. § 34-7-2, which requires each regular payment to correspond to the full net amount of wages or earnings due for the pay period. [^ga-34-7-2-full-net-amount-duty] The Georgia Court of Appeals has left open whether an employee may sue under that section. [^milhollin-claim-not-reached-duty] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-georgia-duty]

## Which work costs must a Georgia employer pay for itself? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Georgia has no general statute listing business expenses a private employer must reimburse, and the Georgia Department of Labor points workers to federal agencies for most workplace problems. [^gdol-federal-agencies-covered] Two federal rules put some costs on the employer. Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-georgia-covered] For other costs, the federal wage floor applies: the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-georgia-covered] Georgia's pay-frequency statute adds that each payment must correspond to the full net amount of wages or earnings due for the period. [^ga-34-7-2-full-net-amount-covered] That statute reaches employers of wageworkers in manual, mechanical or clerical labor, but not the farming, sawmill and turpentine industries or officials and department heads employed by the month or year at stipulated salaries. [^ga-34-7-2-coverage-covered] An employee may therefore bear the cost of employer-required tools of the trade only if no applicable regulation assigns that cost to the employer and the cost does not cut into the required minimum or overtime wages. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-georgia-covered][^fed-531-35-tools-georgia-covered] If an employee sues on a written agreement instead, the Georgia Court of Appeals has recognized that an action under a written contract may be brought within six years. [^milhollin-six-year-written-contract-covered]

## Must a Georgia employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Georgia statute requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs. The Georgia Department of Labor states that resolution of most workplace problems is the responsibility of several federal agencies. [^gdol-federal-agencies-remote] A city or county may not fill the gap, because Georgia bars local governments from requiring an employer to provide employment benefits that the Georgia Code or federal law does not otherwise require. [^ga-sb331-local-mandate-remote][^ga-sb331-mandate-definition-remote] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-georgia-remote]

## Can a Georgia offer letter or expense policy make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Georgia offer letter or expense policy cannot shift employer-required tool costs to an employee when doing so cuts into the minimum or overtime wages required by federal law. [^fed-531-35-tools-georgia-waiver] No Georgia statute addresses whether such a document may otherwise limit reimbursement. Nor can it shift the cost of protective equipment that OSHA's general-industry standard requires the employer to provide at no cost, subject to that standard's listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-georgia-waiver] Georgia's pay-frequency statute requires each payment to correspond to the full net amount of wages or earnings due for the period. [^ga-34-7-2-full-net-amount-waiver] The statute does not list which deductions are allowed, and in our review we found no Georgia appellate decision deciding whether an expense charged against pay reduces the full net amount due; the Court of Appeals has also left open whether an employee may sue under that section. [^milhollin-claim-not-reached-waiver] Given that uncertainty, the conservative course is to obtain the employee's written agreement before charging any work cost against pay. If an employee sues on a written agreement, the Georgia Court of Appeals has recognized a six-year period for an action under a written contract. [^milhollin-six-year-written-contract-waiver]

## How long does a Georgia employee have to sue over an unpaid expense reimbursement? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** A Georgia employee who sues on a written agreement generally has six years, because the Georgia Court of Appeals has recognized that an action under a written contract may be brought within six years. [^milhollin-six-year-written-contract-remedies] A claim based only on Georgia's wage-payment statute is different: the same court applied the two-year limitation period for actions to recover wages under laws respecting the payment of wages to a claim under O.C.G.A. § 34-7-2, and held that claim untimely. [^milhollin-two-year-wage-law-remedies][^milhollin-six-year-written-contract-remedies] The court did not decide whether an employee may bring a claim under § 34-7-2 at all. [^milhollin-claim-not-reached-remedies] A claim that the cost of employer-required tools of the trade pushed pay below the federal minimum or overtime wage is a federal wage claim: the employer is liable for the unpaid minimum wages or overtime plus an equal amount as liquidated damages, unless the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds, in which case the court may award less or none; the court must allow a reasonable attorney's fee and costs, and the action must be brought within two years, or three years for a willful violation. [^fed-531-35-tools-georgia-remedies][^usc-29-216-b-liquidated-georgia-remedies][^usc-29-216-b-fees-georgia-remedies][^usc-29-260-good-faith-georgia-remedies][^usc-29-255-a-georgia-remedies] The Georgia Department of Labor states that the U.S. Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division enforces the federal minimum wage and overtime rules. [^gdol-whd-flsa-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Georgia? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-georgia-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Georgia's own minimum wage is $5.15 an hour, but the Georgia Department of Labor states that, with some limited exceptions, the federal minimum wage rate applies, so for a covered employee the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour is the wage the expense may not cut into. [^gdol-state-minimum-wage-federal][^usc-29-206-a-georgia-federal]
