# Employee Expense Reimbursement in North Carolina[^about]

North Carolina has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but reimbursements an employer promises are wages that must be paid on the regular payday, wage deductions need written authorization and notice, and deductions for the employer's benefit may not cut into the minimum wage.

## Must an employer in North Carolina reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** North Carolina has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. The Wage and Hour Act defines a wage as compensation for labor or services rendered, and for its wage-payment sections, G.S. 95-25.6 through 95-25.13, a wage includes sick pay, vacation pay, severance pay, commissions, bonuses and other amounts promised when the employer has a policy or practice of making such payments. [^nc-95-25-2-promised-amounts] The Department of Labor's rule defines those other amounts promised as amounts the employer has promised or has a policy or practice of paying, including travel, moving, educational and telephone expenses. [^nc-0310-promised-expenses] Every employer must pay all wages accruing to an employee on the regular payday, so a promised reimbursement is due on the regular payday like other wages. [^nc-95-25-6-regular-payday] Separate rules still put some costs on the employer. Under G.S. 95-25.8, an employer may withhold wages when State or federal law requires or empowers it, or under the employee's signed written authorization stating the reason and, where the amount is known in advance, the amount. [^nc-95-25-8-withholding-authorization] Where the amount is not known in advance, the employee must also receive advance written notice of the actual amount and of the right to withdraw, with a reasonable opportunity to withdraw. [^nc-95-25-8-unknown-amount] Separate exceptions allow deductions for bona fide overpayments, wage advances requested by the employee and the principal of employer loans, and, once specified criminal proceedings begin, for shortages or damage to the employer's property. [^nc-95-25-8-prepayment-exception][^nc-95-25-8-criminal-process] A withholding for the employer's benefit may reduce wages only to the minimum wage in non-overtime workweeks and for non-overtime hours, and may never reduce overtime wages owed. [^nc-95-25-8-minimum-wage-limit] North Carolina's minimum wage is $6.15 an hour or the federal minimum wage, whichever is higher. [^nc-95-25-3-minimum-wage] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-north-carolina-floor]

## Which business expenses must a North Carolina employer reimburse? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** North Carolina treats expense payments an employer has promised or has a policy or practice of paying as wages, including travel, moving, educational and telephone expenses. [^nc-95-25-2-promised-amounts-covered][^nc-0310-promised-expenses-covered] The employer must pay promised wages even if it failed to make its wage policies available, and wages promised under an unwritten policy or practice count. [^nc-0805-unwritten-practice-covered] The employer must give employees written notice at hiring of the promised wages and make its policies on promised wages available in writing or by posted notice. [^nc-95-25-13-promised-wages-notice-covered] A separate statute makes it unlawful for an employer with 25 or more employees to require a job applicant to pay for a medical examination or for records the employer requires as a condition of the initial hiring. [^nc-14-357-1-hiring-medical-exam-covered] For other costs, the main limits are the deduction statute and the wage floor. The Department of Labor's rule treats tools and equipment required by the employer, required unique or customized uniforms, and transportation that is an incident of and necessary to the employment as items primarily for the employer's benefit that are not counted as wages. [^nc-0301-employer-benefit-items-covered] A withholding for the employer's benefit may not take wages below the minimum wage or reduce overtime wages owed. [^nc-95-25-8-minimum-wage-limit-covered] The state minimum-wage and overtime sections do not apply to a person employed in an enterprise covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, subject to stated exceptions, so for most such employees the federal floor governs. [^nc-95-25-14-enterprise-exemption-covered][^fed-531-35-tools-north-carolina-covered] The Department of Labor's rule also treats a written authorization for a deduction the law does not permit as invalid, and cites the rule requiring employers to provide, at no cost, personal protective equipment the employee does not wear off the jobsite. [^nc-0305-invalid-authorization-covered]

## Must a North Carolina employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No North Carolina statute requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs as such. A promised phone reimbursement is a wage, because the Department of Labor's rule lists telephone expenses among the amounts an employer has promised or has a policy or practice of paying. [^nc-0310-promised-expenses-remote] The rule does not name home internet, but its list is not exhaustive and the statute reaches other amounts promised under a policy or practice. [^nc-0310-promised-expenses-remote][^nc-95-25-2-promised-amounts-remote] Wages must be paid on the regular payday, so the conservative course is to pay a promised internet reimbursement on the regular payday as well. [^nc-95-25-6-regular-payday-remote] The employer must give written notice at least one pay period before changing promised wages, except that wages may be retroactively increased without that notice. [^nc-95-25-13-promised-wages-notice-remote] Federal law bars an employer-required tools-of-the-trade cost from cutting into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-north-carolina-remote] Ordinarily, an employer that wants to deduct the cost of a lost or damaged laptop or phone from wages must have the employee's written authorization, must give written notice of the amount seven days before the payday unless the employee is separating, and may not take wages below the minimum wage. [^nc-95-25-8-property-damage-remote][^nc-95-25-8-minimum-wage-limit-remote] Once criminal process has issued, the employee has been indicted or the employee has been arrested on a charge tied to the damage, the employer may recoup it without written authorization, still subject to the minimum-wage limit, and must repay the amount if the employee is not found guilty. [^nc-95-25-8-criminal-process-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in North Carolina make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** An employer's offer letter, expense policy or practice can create enforceable promised wages in North Carolina, because amounts the employer has promised or has a policy or practice of paying, including travel, moving, educational and telephone expenses, are wages. [^nc-0310-promised-expenses-waiver] North Carolina has no general statute requiring reimbursement of expenses the employer has not promised, but a policy cannot shift costs that other rules place on the employer or recover costs through deductions the Wage and Hour Act does not allow, as described below. The employer must notify employees in writing of promised wages and give written notice at least one pay period before changing them, except that wages may be retroactively increased without that notice. [^nc-95-25-13-promised-wages-notice-waiver] An employer must pay promised wages even if it failed to make its wage policies available, and an unwritten policy or practice can create promised wages. [^nc-0805-unwritten-practice-waiver] A policy that recovers a work cost by deduction from wages must follow G.S. 95-25.8. A deduction whose amount is known in advance needs a written authorization signed on or before the payday that states the reason and the dollar amount or percentage. [^nc-95-25-8-withholding-authorization-waiver] A deduction whose amount is not known in advance needs a signed authorization stating the reason, advance written notice of the actual amount, written notice of the right to withdraw and a reasonable opportunity to withdraw. [^nc-95-25-8-unknown-amount-waiver] The Department of Labor's rule provides that the monetary limits and time requirements of G.S. 95-25.8 may not be waived. [^nc-0305-limits-not-waivable-waiver] It lists uniform rental or cleaning not required by the employer as a deduction for the employee's convenience. [^nc-0305-employee-convenience-waiver] An authorization for a deduction the law does not permit is invalid, and withholding for such a purpose violates the wage-payment sections even under a signed authorization. [^nc-0305-invalid-authorization-waiver] Nothing in the Wage and Hour Act stops an employer from suing in court to collect amounts an employee owes it. [^nc-95-25-8-employer-civil-action-waiver]

## What remedies does a North Carolina employee have for unpaid promised reimbursements, an unlawful deduction or pay pushed below the minimum wage? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** An employer that violates the minimum-wage, overtime or wage-payment sections of the Wage and Hour Act owes the unpaid minimum wages, overtime or amounts due under G.S. 95-25.6 through 95-25.12, plus interest at the legal rate from the date each amount first came due. [^nc-95-25-22-unpaid-amounts] A promised reimbursement is one of those amounts, because the Department of Labor's rule counts promised travel, moving, educational and telephone expenses as wages, and an employee who leaves must be paid all wages due on or before the next regular payday. [^nc-0310-promised-expenses-remedies][^nc-95-25-7-separation-payday-remedies] The court must also award liquidated damages equal to the amount due, unless the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds for believing it was not violating the Act, in which case the court may reduce or deny them. [^nc-95-25-22-liquidated-damages] The court may order the employer to pay costs and reasonable attorneys' fees. [^nc-95-25-22-attorneys-fees] The Commissioner of Labor may bring the action at the request of the employees affected. [^nc-95-25-22-commissioner-action] An action under the section must be brought within two years. [^nc-95-25-22-two-year-limit] A withholding for a purpose the law does not permit is treated by the Department of Labor's rule as a violation of G.S. 95-25.6 or 95-25.7, even under a signed authorization. [^nc-0305-invalid-authorization-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in North Carolina? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-north-carolina-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. North Carolina's minimum wage is $6.15 an hour or the federal minimum wage, whichever is higher. [^nc-95-25-3-minimum-wage-federal] The state minimum-wage and overtime sections do not apply to a person employed in an enterprise covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, subject to stated exceptions. [^nc-95-25-14-enterprise-exemption-federal]
