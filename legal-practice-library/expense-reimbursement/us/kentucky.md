# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Kentucky[^about]

Kentucky has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but it bars making employees pay for employer-required medical examinations and bars wage deductions for fines, shared-till cash shortages, breakage and listed losses.

## Must an employer in Kentucky reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Kentucky has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. One statute names a specific cost: an employer may not require an employee or applicant to pay for a medical examination, or for furnishing records, that the employer requires as a condition of employment. [^ky-krs-336-220-medical-exam-cost-duty] Kentucky's main protection works through deductions rather than reimbursement: an employer may not withhold any part of the agreed wage unless law authorizes the withholding or the employee expressly authorizes a listed type of deduction in writing, and may not deduct fines, cash shortages in a till shared by two or more people or breakage from wages at all. [^ky-krs-337-060-agreed-wage-duty][^ky-krs-337-060-withholding-limits-duty][^ky-krs-337-060-prohibited-deductions-duty] An employer also may not deduct losses on dishonored checks the employee had discretion to accept. [^ky-krs-337-060-prohibited-deductions-duty] For losses from faulty workmanship, lost or stolen property, property damage, customer credit default or customer nonpayment, the prohibition applies when the losses are not attributable to the employee's willful or intentional disregard of the employer's interest. [^ky-krs-337-060-prohibited-deductions-duty] Under the Kentucky regulation on board, lodging and other allowances, the cost of tools of the trade and other facilities primarily for the employer's benefit cannot be counted toward wages. [^ky-803-kar-1-081e-employer-benefit-facilities-duty] Kentucky defines wages to include other similar advantages agreed upon by the employer and employee or provided as an established policy; in our review we found no Kentucky appellate decision deciding whether an agreed expense reimbursement falls within that definition. [^ky-krs-337-010-wages-definition-duty] Given the uncertainty our review found, treating a reimbursement the employer has promised, or provides under an established policy, as wages is a conservative compliance approach. Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-kentucky-duty]

## Which business costs may a Kentucky employer not shift onto employees? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Kentucky has no general statute listing business expenses an employer must reimburse. An employer may not make an employee or applicant pay for a medical examination, or for furnishing records, that it requires as a condition of employment. [^ky-krs-336-220-medical-exam-cost-covered] An employer may not deduct from wages any fines, cash shortages in a common till, cash box or register used by two or more people, breakage, losses on dishonored checks the employee had discretion to accept, or losses from faulty workmanship, lost or stolen property, property damage, customer credit default or customer nonpayment that do not result from the employee's willful or intentional disregard of the employer's interest. [^ky-krs-337-060-prohibited-deductions-covered] Other deductions are lawful only where law authorizes them or the employee expressly authorizes them in writing for insurance premiums, hospital and medical dues, or other deductions that do not amount to a rebate or deduction from the standard wage. [^ky-krs-337-060-withholding-limits-covered] An employer may count the reasonable cost of board, lodging or other facilities it customarily provides as wages, but that cost may not include a profit and the employee's acceptance of the facility must be voluntary and uncoerced. [^ky-803-kar-1-081e-board-lodging-permission-covered][^ky-803-kar-1-081e-no-profit-covered][^ky-803-kar-1-081e-board-lodging-covered] Tools of the trade, other materials and services incidental to the employer's business, and uniforms and their laundering where the business requires a uniform are treated as primarily for the employer's benefit and cannot be counted toward wages. [^ky-803-kar-1-081e-employer-benefit-facilities-covered][^ky-803-kar-1-081e-uniforms-covered] Kentucky's minimum wage is $7.25 an hour. [^ky-krs-337-275-minimum-rate-covered] An employer with ten or more employees that makes deductions must give each employee, at each payment, a statement of the amount and general purpose of each deduction. [^ky-krs-337-070-deduction-statement-covered] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-kentucky-covered]

## Must a Kentucky employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Kentucky statute specifically requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs. In our review, we found no Kentucky regulation specifically requiring reimbursement of those costs. An employer may not recover a remote-work cost by withholding part of the agreed wage, unless law authorizes the deduction or the employee authorizes it in writing within the limits of KRS 337.060. [^ky-krs-337-060-agreed-wage-remote][^ky-krs-337-060-withholding-limits-remote] Equipment and services incidental to carrying on the employer's business cannot be counted toward wages under the Kentucky allowances regulation. [^ky-803-kar-1-081e-employer-benefit-facilities-remote] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-kentucky-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Kentucky make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Kentucky offer letter or expense policy cannot override the statutory limits on shifting costs onto employees through wage deductions. [^ky-krs-337-060-prohibited-deductions-waiver] The bar on deducting fines, shared-till cash shortages, breakage and the listed losses, subject to the conditions in KRS 337.060(2), applies notwithstanding the deductions subsection (1) of KRS 337.060 otherwise allows, including written employee authorizations. [^ky-krs-337-060-prohibited-deductions-waiver] A written authorization supports a deduction only for insurance premiums, hospital and medical dues, or other deductions that do not amount to a rebate or deduction from the standard wage. [^ky-krs-337-060-withholding-limits-waiver] An employer may not require an employee or applicant to pay for a medical examination, or for furnishing records, that the employer requires as a condition of employment. [^ky-krs-336-220-medical-exam-cost-waiver] Acceptance of board, lodging or other facilities counted as wages must be voluntary and uncoerced. [^ky-803-kar-1-081e-board-lodging-waiver] In an action for wages owed under KRS 337.020 to 337.285, an agreement to work for less than the applicable wage rate is no defense, for employees within the narrower definition that applies to that remedy. [^ky-krs-337-385-good-faith-no-defense-waiver][^ky-krs-337-010-385-employee-exclusions-waiver]

## What remedies does a Kentucky employee have for an unlawful deduction or unpaid wages? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** An employer that violates the deduction rules in KRS 337.060 is subject to a civil penalty of $100 to $1,000 and is liable to the employee for the amount withheld plus interest at 10 percent a year. [^ky-krs-337-990-deduction-penalty-remedies] An employer that pays an employee less than the wages the employee is entitled to under KRS 337.020 to 337.285, a range that includes KRS 337.060, is liable for the unpaid amount, an additional equal amount as liquidated damages, and costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^ky-krs-337-385-liquidated-damages-remedies] That remedy, and the commissioner's assignment of wage claims, use a narrower definition of employee that excludes, among others, agricultural workers, bona fide executive, administrative, supervisory and professional employees, outside salespeople and outside collectors, and federal employees. [^ky-krs-337-010-385-employee-exclusions-remedies] The court may award no liquidated damages, or a reduced amount, if the employer shows it acted in good faith with reasonable grounds for believing it was not violating the law. [^ky-krs-337-385-good-faith-remedies] At the employee's written request, the commissioner may take an assignment of the wage claim and sue to collect it. [^ky-krs-337-385-commissioner-assignment-remedies] A court or administrative action under KRS chapter 337 that has no other express limitations period must be commenced within three years after the cause of action accrued. [^ky-krs-337-385-three-years-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Kentucky? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-kentucky-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Kentucky's minimum wage is also $7.25 an hour and rises to match any increase in the federal minimum hourly wage. [^ky-krs-337-275-minimum-rate-federal][^ky-krs-337-275-federal-ratchet-federal] Kentucky's allowances regulation likewise excludes tools of the trade and other facilities primarily for the employer's benefit from the wages counted toward that minimum. [^ky-803-kar-1-081e-employer-benefit-facilities-federal]
