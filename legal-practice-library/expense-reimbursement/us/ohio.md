# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Ohio[^about]

Ohio has no general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses, but employer-required medical examinations cannot be charged to applicants, agreed reimbursements count as wages for Ohio's pay-timing law, and work costs cannot cut into the minimum wage.

## Must an employer in Ohio reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Ohio has no general statute requiring an employer to reimburse every business expense an employee incurs. One statute names a cost: a private employer with three or more individuals in employment may not require a prospective employee or applicant to pay for a medical examination the employer requires as a condition of employment. [^oh-4113-21-medical-exam-duty][^oh-4113-21-private-employer-duty] Ohio's pay-timing statute defines a wage as the net amount of money payable to an employee, including any reimbursement for expenses, so reimbursement that is payable is paid by default on the statute's semimonthly schedule, which also allows daily or weekly payment and refers to a longer customary time lapse or a different time lapse set by written contract or by law. [^oh-4113-15-reimbursement-is-wage-duty][^oh-4113-15-payday-duty][^oh-4113-15-time-lapse-duty] For other costs the main limit is the minimum wage: every employer covered by Article II, section 34a of the Ohio Constitution must pay at least the constitutional rate, which is $11.00 an hour for non-tipped employees in 2026. [^oh-4111-02-minimum-wage-duty][^oh-commerce-2026-rate-duty] Employees under 16 and employees of businesses grossing less than $405,000 in 2026 are owed at least the federal minimum wage instead. [^oh-commerce-2026-rate-duty][^oh-commerce-2026-threshold-duty]

## Which business expenses must an Ohio employer pay for? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** An Ohio private employer with three or more employees may not require an applicant or prospective employee to pay for a medical examination required as a condition of employment. [^oh-4113-21-medical-exam-covered][^oh-4113-21-private-employer-covered] A public employer, or a private employer furnishing services to a public employer under a contract subject to the federal Service Contract Act, may not require any employee, prospective employee or applicant to pay for an initial or later medical examination required as a condition of employment or continued employment. [^oh-4113-21-service-contract-covered] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions such as non-specialty safety-toe footwear and non-specialty prescription safety eyewear that the employer permits employees to wear off the job site. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-ohio-covered][^osha-1910-132-footwear-ohio-covered] Ohio has no general statute requiring reimbursement of other expenses, and its pay-timing statute counts reimbursement for expenses within the net amount of money payable to an employee. [^oh-4113-15-reimbursement-is-wage-covered] The minimum-wage chapter defines a wage as compensation payable in legal tender or checks convertible into cash at full face value, subject to the deductions, charges or allowances the director of commerce permits by rule, and it counts the reasonable cost of board, lodging or other facilities the employer customarily furnishes, except to the extent an applicable bona fide collective bargaining agreement excludes those costs. [^oh-4111-01-wage-definition-covered][^oh-4111-01-board-lodging-covered] Federal law bars a required tools-of-the-trade cost from cutting into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-ohio-covered] The Ohio rate is $11.00 an hour for non-tipped employees in 2026, and employees under 16 and employees of businesses grossing less than $405,000 are owed at least the federal minimum wage instead. [^oh-4111-02-minimum-wage-covered][^oh-commerce-2026-rate-covered][^oh-commerce-2026-threshold-covered] Our review found no Ohio statute or appellate decision saying whether required work costs an employee bears reduce wages for the state minimum wage, so the conservative course is to keep pay, net of those costs, at or above the Ohio rate.

## Must an Ohio employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Ohio statute requires an employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs as such. A remote-work reimbursement that is payable counts as a wage under Ohio's pay-timing statute, whose definition of a wage includes any reimbursement for expenses, and is paid by default on the statute's semimonthly schedule, which also allows daily or weekly payment and refers to a longer customary time lapse or a different time lapse set by written contract or by law. [^oh-4113-15-reimbursement-is-wage-remote][^oh-4113-15-payday-remote][^oh-4113-15-time-lapse-remote] Federal law bars a required tools-of-the-trade cost from cutting into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-ohio-remote] Covered, non-tipped remote employees are entitled to Ohio's $11.00 hourly minimum wage in 2026, except that employees under 16 and employees of businesses grossing less than $405,000 are owed at least the federal minimum wage. [^oh-4111-02-minimum-wage-remote][^oh-commerce-2026-rate-remote][^oh-commerce-2026-threshold-remote] Our review found no Ohio statute or appellate decision saying whether required work costs an employee bears reduce wages for the state minimum wage, so the conservative course is to keep pay, net of those costs, at or above the Ohio rate.

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Ohio make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** An Ohio offer letter or expense policy cannot exempt the employer from Ohio's pay-timing statute, which counts reimbursement for expenses as a wage and bars an employer subject to it from exempting itself by a special contract with an employee or by other means. [^oh-4113-15-reimbursement-is-wage-waiver][^oh-4113-16-no-special-contract-waiver] The medical-examination statute bars a covered private employer from requiring an applicant to pay for a required examination and contains no exception for the applicant's agreement. [^oh-4113-21-medical-exam-waiver] An employer may not pay or agree to pay wages at a rate below the rate Ohio's minimum-wage sections require, and an agreement to work for less than the constitutional minimum wage is no defense to a minimum-wage action. [^oh-4111-13-agree-to-pay-less-waiver][^oh-4111-14-no-defense-waiver] Federal law likewise bars a required tools-of-the-trade cost from cutting into the federal minimum or overtime wages due, whatever a policy says. [^fed-531-35-tools-ohio-waiver]

## What remedies does an Ohio employee have for unpaid reimbursement or pay pushed below the minimum wage? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** Reimbursement payable to an Ohio employee counts as wages subject to the state's pay-timing statute. [^oh-4113-15-reimbursement-is-wage-remedies] By default, wages earned in the first half of a month are due by the first day of the next month and wages earned in the second half by the fifteenth, and the statute also refers to a longer customary time lapse or a different time lapse set by written contract or by law. [^oh-4113-15-payday-remedies][^oh-4113-15-time-lapse-remedies] When wages remain unpaid 30 days beyond the regularly scheduled payday and no contest, court order or dispute accounts for the nonpayment, the employer also owes liquidated damages of 6 percent of the unpaid, undisputed amount or $200, whichever is greater. [^oh-4113-15-liquidated-damages-remedies] A violation of the pay-timing statute is also a first-degree misdemeanor. [^oh-4113-99-misdemeanor-remedies] An employer that violates the medical-examination statute forfeits up to $100 for each violation, and the Bureau of Workers' Compensation and the Public Utilities Commission enforce it. [^oh-4113-21-forfeiture-remedies] For a violation of the constitutional minimum wage, an employee or the attorney general may sue within three years of the violation, or of when a continuing violation ceased, or within one year after notice of the state's final disposition of a complaint, whichever is later. [^oh-4111-14-limitations-remedies] An employer found to have violated the constitutional minimum wage must pay, within 30 days of the finding, the back wages, damages of an additional two times the back wages, and the employee's costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^oh-const-34a-remedy-remedies][^oh-4111-14-damages-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Ohio? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-ohio-federal] In a 2024 decision that included an appeal from the Southern District of Ohio, the Sixth Circuit stated that an employer that requires a minimum-wage employee to provide the employee's own tools must reimburse the full cost, and held for delivery drivers that the entitlement extends no further than the employee's actual costs, which the IRS mileage rate does not measure. [^ca6-parker-full-cost-federal][^ca6-parker-actual-costs-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Ohio's constitutional minimum wage is $11.00 an hour for non-tipped employees in 2026, and employees under 16 and employees of businesses with annual gross receipts below $405,000 in 2026 are owed at least the federal minimum wage. [^oh-commerce-2026-rate-federal][^oh-commerce-2026-threshold-federal][^oh-const-34a-small-employer-federal] Article II, section 34a raises the state rate each January 1 by the inflation rate measured before the preceding September 30. [^oh-const-34a-indexing-federal] Effective January 1, 2027, the Ohio Department of Commerce has set the rate at $11.40 an hour for non-tipped employees and the small-employer threshold at $419,000. [^oh-commerce-2027-rate-federal][^oh-commerce-2027-threshold-federal]
