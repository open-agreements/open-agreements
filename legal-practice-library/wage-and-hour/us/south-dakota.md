# Wage and Hour Law in South Dakota[^about]

How South Dakota sets an indexed $11.85 minimum wage for 2026, leaves overtime and breaks to federal law and employer policy, and sets final-pay, monthly pay-frequency, tip-credit, worker-classification and wage-claim rules.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** South Dakota's minimum wage is $11.85 an hour for 2026. [^sd-dlr-minimum-wage-2026] The statute sets a base rate of $8.50 an hour, [^sd-minimum-wage-statute] which is adjusted every January 1 by the rise in the Consumer Price Index, rounded up to the nearest five cents and never decreased. [^sd-minimum-wage-annual-adjustment] The Department of Labor and Regulation publishes the adjusted rate, and it has set $11.85 for 2026 [^sd-dlr-minimum-wage-2026] and $12.30 for 2027. [^sd-dlr-minimum-wage-2027] An employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act must also pay the federal minimum wage of $7.25, which is lower than the state rate. [^sd-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The state minimum wage does not apply to employees paid an opportunity wage, babysitters or outside salespersons. [^sd-minimum-wage-statute] It also does not apply to employees of a seasonal amusement or recreational establishment, organized camp or religious or nonprofit educational conference center that operates no more than seven months a year or meets a receipts test. [^sd-minimum-wage-seasonal-exemption] An employee under 20 years old may be paid the opportunity wage defined in the federal Fair Labor Standards Amendments of 1996. [^sd-opportunity-wage] Under that federal provision, the wage may be as low as $4.25 an hour, but only during the first 90 consecutive calendar days after the employee is first hired, [^sd-opportunity-wage-federal-rate] and an employer may not displace other employees, including by cutting their hours, wages or benefits, to hire workers at that wage. [^sd-opportunity-wage-displacement] The Department of Labor and Regulation may issue a permit fixing the wage of an apprentice, a learner or a person with a developmental disability. [^sd-apprentice-learner-permit]

An agreement to work for less than the minimum wage is no defense: the employee may recover the full minimum wage and costs. [^sd-agreement-for-less-ineffective]

An employer may not require an employee to pay for a medical examination, or for records the employer requires, as a condition of continued employment. [^sd-medical-exam-cost] The cost of necessary job expenses is discussed in the [South Dakota expense reimbursement guide](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/south-dakota).

An employer of more than 25 people must keep records of each employee's wages, wage rates, job classification and other terms and conditions of employment for a reasonable period. [^sd-records-twenty-five-employees]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** South Dakota has no state overtime statute. The Department of Labor and Regulation states that South Dakota has no labor laws on compensatory time or overtime. [^sd-dlr-no-overtime-law] Overtime therefore comes from the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, which, except as that section otherwise provides, requires a covered employer to pay one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. [^sd-federal-overtime] There is no state daily overtime rule.

A state statute provides that a day's labor in a manufacturing or mechanical occupation is eight hours unless the parties expressly agree otherwise, and the rule does not apply to employment by the week, month or year. [^sd-eight-hour-day-manufacturing] That statute defines a default working day; it does not require a premium rate for longer days.

Federal overtime exemptions and hours-worked rules are discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** South Dakota does not require meal or rest breaks for adult employees. The Department of Labor and Regulation states that no South Dakota law requires an employer to provide rest breaks or meal periods. [^sd-dlr-no-break-law] Whether to give ordinary meal and rest breaks is a matter of employer policy. Federal law separately requires an employer, subject to statutory exemptions, to provide reasonable break time, and a private place other than a bathroom, for an employee to express breast milk for a nursing child for one year after the child's birth. [^sd-federal-pump-break] An employer with fewer than 50 employees is not subject to that requirement if it would impose an undue hardship, measured by significant difficulty or expense relative to the size, financial resources, nature or structure of the business. [^sd-federal-pump-small-employer] For an employer covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, short rest periods that are given must be counted as hours worked, [^sd-federal-rest-periods-paid] while a meal period may be unpaid only if the employee is completely relieved from duty to eat. [^sd-federal-meal-relieved]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** Final wages are generally due by the next regular payday, or later once the employee returns the employer's property, both when the employer separates the employee and when an employee without a written contract for a definite period quits. [^sd-final-pay-discharge] [^sd-final-pay-resignation] When an employer separates an employee from the payroll, unpaid wages are due by the next regular payday for those hours, or as soon after that as the employee returns all of the employer's property. [^sd-final-pay-discharge] The same rule applies when an employee without a written contract for a definite period quits. [^sd-final-pay-resignation]
No South Dakota statute imposes a daily waiting-time penalty. A private employer that is oppressive, fraudulent or malicious in refusing to pay wages due is liable for double the unpaid wages. [^sd-double-damages] An employer that intentionally refuses to pay wages when demanded, or falsely denies the amount owed with intent to secure a discount, harass or defraud, commits a Class 2 misdemeanor. [^sd-refusal-to-pay-misdemeanor]

When wages are in dispute, the employer must give the employee written notice of the wages due less any amount the employee concedes owing the employer, and pay that amount without condition by the usual deadline. Accepting that payment does not release the rest of the claim. [^sd-conceded-wages]

When work stops because of a strike, lockout or other labor dispute, earned wages are due on the next regular payday, together with any deposit held for faithful performance. [^sd-labor-dispute-pay]

The payment-timing rules in sections 60-11-8 through 60-11-23 apply only to cash wages. [^sd-cash-wages-only]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** South Dakota requires payment at least once each calendar month or on regular agreed paydays designated in advance by the employer. [^sd-monthly-pay] Every employer must pay all wages due at least once each calendar month, unless another law provides otherwise, or on regular agreed paydays designated in advance by the employer. [^sd-monthly-pay] Wages may be paid by check, cash or direct deposit to the employee's bank account, unless the employer and employee agree on another form. [^sd-monthly-pay]

The pay-frequency rule covers any person, firm, company, receiver or court officer employing a person of South Dakota, including an agent or officer of those employers. [^sd-wage-payment-employer-definition]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** No South Dakota statute requires an employer to provide a pay statement. The review for this guide of chapters 60-1, 60-11 and 60-12 of the South Dakota Codified Laws and article 47:04 of the Administrative Rules of South Dakota found no provision requiring an employer to give employees a pay stub or listing its contents.
The same review found no general statute listing which deductions an employer may take from wages. Two state rules bear on deductions. In a wage dispute, the employer must give the employee written notice of the wages due less any amount the employee concedes owing the employer, and pay that amount without condition by the usual deadline. [^sd-deduction-conceded-wages] And an employer may hold final wages until the employee returns the employer's property. [^sd-deduction-property-return] For an employer covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, an employee's cost of tools of the trade the employer requires violates federal law in any workweek in which it cuts into the minimum or overtime wages. [^sd-federal-tools-cut-into-wages]

Necessary expenses an employee incurs because of the job are discussed in the [South Dakota expense reimbursement guide](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement/us/south-dakota).

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** South Dakota's general employment statute uses a common-law control test. An employee is a person employed to render personal service otherwise than in an independent calling, who remains entirely under the employer's control and direction. [^sd-employee-definition-control]

For reemployment assistance, employment includes service by anyone who is an employee under the usual common-law rules. [^sd-ui-common-law-employment] Service for wages counts as employment unless the Department of Labor and Regulation is satisfied that the worker is free from control or direction over the work and customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, profession or business. [^sd-ui-two-part-test]

A delivery facilitation contractor is an independent contractor of the platform if the two agree in writing that the contractor is an independent contractor, the platform does not unilaterally prescribe hours when the contractor must be available, the platform does not bar outside work or work through another platform except while the contractor is performing services through its network, and the platform may not end the contract for failing or refusing to accept requests. [^sd-delivery-platform-contractor] The definition of delivery facilitation contractor includes a transportation network company driver. [^sd-delivery-contractor-includes-tnc]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** South Dakota allows a tip credit of up to half the minimum wage. An employer claiming a tip credit must pay a tipped employee a cash wage of at least 50 percent of the minimum wage and make up any shortfall if tips plus the cash wage do not reach the full minimum wage in each pay period. [^sd-tip-credit-half] For 2026 that cash wage is $5.925 an hour. [^sd-dlr-tipped-2026] A tipped employee is one who customarily and regularly receives more than $35 a month in tips. [^sd-tipped-employee-definition]

For an employer covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, the federal tip credit applies only if the employer has told the employee about the tip-credit rules and the employee keeps all tips, apart from a valid tip pool among customarily tipped employees. [^sd-federal-tip-credit-notice] An employer may not keep employees' tips, including by letting managers or supervisors keep any portion, whether or not it takes a tip credit. [^sd-federal-tip-retention]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** The Department of Labor and Regulation enforces the wage-payment rules and may help an employee collect. [^sd-dlr-enforcement] An employee may also sue: an employee paid less than the minimum wage may recover the full minimum wage and costs. [^sd-enforcement-minimum-wage-recovery] The department must ensure compliance and investigate violations of sections 60-11-8 through 60-11-23. It may hold hearings and must cooperate with an employee in enforcing a claim it considers valid. [^sd-dlr-enforcement] A claim for unpaid wages must be filed with the division in writing, and a simple letter giving the amount owed, the employer's name and address and the period worked is enough. [^sd-written-wage-claim] If the department finds an enforceable claim, it must take an assignment of the wages in trust at the employee's request and may sue to collect. [^sd-assignment-in-trust]
In an employee's own suit, double damages are available when a private employer was oppressive, fraudulent or malicious in refusing to pay. [^sd-enforcement-double-damages] When a small-claims wage action is removed to magistrate or circuit court, the court may award the employee costs, including reasonable attorney's fees. [^sd-removed-small-claims-fees]

The department may take assignments of wage claims of up to $500 per claim. [^sd-assignment-five-hundred]

An employer may not discharge, discriminate or retaliate against an employee for complaining to the employer or the department about unpaid wages, or for starting or testifying in a wage proceeding. [^sd-wage-retaliation]

An action for wages, or for a penalty or liquidated damages for failing to pay wages, must generally be brought within two years after the cause of action accrues, whether the wages are owed under a statute or a contract. [^sd-two-year-wage-limitation] Federal Fair Labor Standards Act claims are discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).
