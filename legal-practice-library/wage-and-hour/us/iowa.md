# Wage and Hour Law in Iowa[^about]

How Iowa law ties the minimum wage to the federal rate, leaves overtime to federal law, limits required breaks to young workers, and sets final-pay, pay-frequency, pay-statement, deduction, worker-classification, tip-credit and wage-claim rules.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Iowa's minimum wage is $7.25 an hour. An Iowa employer must pay the state hourly wage of $7.25 or the current federal minimum wage under 29 U.S.C. § 206, whichever is greater, so a federal increase would raise the Iowa rate. [^ia-minimum-wage-state-rate] [^ia-minimum-wage-greater-of-state-or-federal] The Iowa statute borrows the federal Fair Labor Standards Act's definitions of employer and employee, as that act stood on January 1, 2007. [^ia-minimum-wage-greater-of-state-or-federal]
Under Iowa law, an employer need not pay the full state rate until an employee has completed 90 calendar days of employment, and must pay at least $6.35 an hour until then. [^ia-minimum-wage-ninety-day-initial-rate] The Iowa allowance does not displace federal law: an employer covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act must still pay its covered employees the federal minimum wage, except as the federal Act itself provides. [^ia-federal-minimum-wage-rate] The 90-day period counts from the employee's first day of work. [^ia-initial-rate-counted-from-first-day]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

An employee who leaves before day 90 and is rehired by the same employer within three years of the initial hiring may be paid the initial rate until the 90-day period is reached. An employee rehired less than three years after leaving after day 90 may not be paid the initial rate. [^ia-initial-rate-rehire]

The federal exemptions in 29 U.S.C. § 213, as amended to January 1, 2007, apply to the Iowa minimum wage, except as the Iowa statute provides. [^ia-minimum-wage-federal-exemptions] The Iowa minimum wage does not apply to an enterprise with less than $300,000 in annual gross volume of sales or business done. [^ia-minimum-wage-small-enterprise-exemption] That threshold does not apply to businesses laundering, cleaning or repairing clothing or fabrics, construction, hospitals, residential care institutions, schools, institutions of higher education and public agencies. [^ia-minimum-wage-enterprises-covered-regardless]

Iowa cities and counties may not set a higher local minimum wage. A city may not adopt, enforce or administer an ordinance setting employment terms that exceed or conflict with federal or state minimum or living wage law. [^ia-city-minimum-wage-preemption] The same bar applies to counties. [^ia-county-minimum-wage-preemption]

A franchisor is not the employer of a franchisee or of the franchisee's employees under the minimum wage chapter, unless the franchisor agreed in writing to be the employer or the state director found that it exercised control beyond what is customary to protect its trademarks and brand. [^ia-franchisor-not-employer]

Every employer covered by the Iowa minimum wage must keep payroll records for each covered employee, including the basis of pay, hours worked each workday and each workweek, and additions and deductions each pay period. [^ia-minimum-wage-payroll-records] Payroll records must be kept for at least three years from the last date of entry. [^ia-minimum-wage-records-three-years] The employer must also post the state minimum wage notice where employees can readily see it. [^ia-minimum-wage-posting]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Iowa has no state overtime statute. The Iowa minimum wage section requires the greater of the state or federal hourly minimum wage and contains no overtime requirement. [^ia-minimum-wage-section-no-overtime] Overtime for Iowa employees comes from the federal Fair Labor Standards Act. Under the federal Act's general overtime rule, an employer must pay a covered, nonexempt employee at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. [^ia-federal-overtime-forty-hours] Iowa has no statute requiring daily overtime.

Iowa's pay-statement rule refers to the federal overtime exemptions. An employer need not show hours worked on the statement of an employee who is exempt from overtime under 29 C.F.R. pt. 541, unless the employer has a policy or practice of paying exempt employees overtime, a bonus or hour-based pay, in which case the statement must show the hours worked or the payments made, as applicable. [^ia-pay-statement-exempt-hours]

Federal overtime law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** No Iowa statute generally requires meal or rest breaks for adult employees. The only required break in Iowa's wage-and-hour and child labor statutes is for workers under 16. Subject to the child labor chapter's exceptions, a person under 16 who works five hours or more in a day must be given an intermission of at least 30 minutes. [^ia-under-sixteen-thirty-minute-intermission] [^ia-child-labor-exceptions]

For employment subject to section 92.7, Iowa's child labor statute limits a person under 16 to eight hours a day, exclusive of the intermission, and 40 hours a week. While school is in session, work outside school hours may not exceed six hours a day or 28 hours a week. [^ia-under-sixteen-hour-limits] Work by a person under 16 is barred before 7:00 a.m. and after 9:00 p.m., except that from June 1 through Labor Day the hours may extend to 11:00 p.m. [^ia-under-sixteen-time-of-day] The chapter does not prohibit, among other things, a child's work in a business operated by the child's parents, or work by a child under 16 in motion pictures, theatrical productions or musical performances with prior written permission from a parent, guardian or custodian. [^ia-child-labor-exceptions] These are Iowa's limits. For 14- and 15-year-olds in nonagricultural occupations covered by federal child labor rules, the federal limits are tighter: no more than 3 hours on a school day, 18 hours in a school week, 8 hours on a non-school day and 40 hours in a non-school week, and only between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m., or 9 p.m. from June 1 through Labor Day. [^ia-federal-fourteen-fifteen-hours]

Federal law adds a break that Iowa does not: subject to federal exemptions, an employer must give an employee reasonable break time, and a private place other than a bathroom, to express breast milk for one year after the child's birth. [^ia-federal-pumping-break] Among those exemptions, an employer with fewer than 50 employees is exempt if compliance would impose an undue hardship by causing significant difficulty or expense. [^ia-federal-pumping-break-exemptions] Whether a short break must be paid is a federal hours-worked question discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** Except for certain commission balances, when employment is suspended or terminated an Iowa employer must pay all wages earned up to that time, less lawful deductions under section 91A.5, by the next regular payday for the pay period in which the wages were earned. [^ia-final-pay-next-regular-payday] [^ia-final-pay-commission-thirty-days] The rule applies the same way to a discharge and a resignation. [^ia-final-pay-next-regular-payday] Wages that are the difference between a commission credit already paid and the commission actually earned must be paid within 30 days after the suspension or termination. [^ia-final-pay-commission-thirty-days]
Vacation pay is wages when it is due under an agreement or employer policy, and where the policy accrues vacation pro rata, the final increment is proportional to the part of the year actually worked. [^ia-wages-include-vacation-under-policy] [^ia-final-pay-pro-rata-vacation]
An employer that intentionally fails to pay final wages owes the unpaid wages, liquidated damages, court costs and usual and necessary attorney fees. [^ia-final-pay-intentional-damages] The remedies are described in the enforcement answer, [How are wage claims enforced?](#enforcement).

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Unless varied by a written agreement kept as a record, an Iowa employer must pay all wages due, less lawful deductions, at least monthly, semimonthly or biweekly, on regular paydays that are at consistent intervals and designated in advance. [^ia-pay-frequency-regular-paydays] [^ia-pay-frequency-written-variance] A regular payday may not be more than 12 days after the end of the period in which the wages were earned, excluding Sundays and legal holidays. [^ia-payday-twelve-day-lag] An employer and employee may vary these rules by a written agreement kept as a record. [^ia-pay-frequency-written-variance]

Where wages are earned on commission, the employer may, by agreement with the employee, pay a credit against the commissions and settle the difference at regular intervals no more than 12 months apart. [^ia-commission-credit-twelve-months]

Wages must be paid in United States currency or by a written instrument negotiable on demand at full face value, unless the employee agreed in writing to receive some or all wages in kind or another form. [^ia-wages-paid-in-currency] An employee hired on or after July 1, 2005 may be required to accept direct deposit at a financial institution of the employee's choice, unless account costs would reduce wages below the minimum wage, the employee would incur fees from the deposit, or a collective bargaining agreement prohibits the requirement. [^ia-mandatory-direct-deposit] An employer that pays late is liable for any overdraft charge the late payment causes on the employee's account. [^ia-late-pay-overdraft-charge]

The Wage Payment Collection Law's duties to give written notice of wages and paydays at hiring, give advance notice of reductions, and keep three calendar years of payroll records under section 91A.6(1) apply only after the state director notifies an employer that has paid a wage claim with liquidated damages or been assessed a civil penalty. That trigger does not affect the separate payroll-record duty under the minimum wage rules, described in the minimum wage answer. [^ia-notice-duties-after-director-notification] [^ia-director-notification-trigger] [^ia-pay-frequency-minimum-wage-records]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** On each regular payday, an Iowa employer must give each employee a statement showing the hours worked, the wages earned and the deductions made, subject to the exception for hours stated in the next sentence. [^ia-pay-statement-contents] Hours may be left off the statement of an employee exempt from overtime under 29 C.F.R. pt. 541, unless the employer has a policy or practice of paying exempt employees overtime, a bonus or hour-based pay, in which case the statement must show the hours worked or the payments made, as applicable. [^ia-pay-statement-exempt-hours-statement] The statement may be mailed, sent by secure electronic means, handed over at the workplace during working hours, or made available electronically with free and unrestricted printer access; an employee who cannot receive electronic statements must notify the employer in writing one pay period ahead. [^ia-pay-statement-delivery-methods] Within ten working days of an employee's request, the employer must also furnish an itemized statement of earnings and deductions for each pay period with deductions, explaining how they were computed. [^ia-itemized-statement-on-request]
An employer may withhold or divert wages only when state or federal law or a court order requires or permits it, or when the employee has given written authorization for a lawful purpose that benefits the employee. [^ia-deductions-law-or-written-authorization]

Some deductions are barred even with the employee's consent. An employer may not deduct a shortage in a cash register or till shared by two or more people, except under one written agreement per establishment with a full-time manager covering shortages within the 45 days before the most recent regular payday. [^ia-deduction-bar-shared-cash-shortage] An employer may not deduct losses from dishonored checks the employee had discretion to accept and did not abuse, or losses from breakage, damage, customer credit defaults or nonpayment unless the employee willfully or intentionally disregarded the employer's interests. [^ia-deduction-bar-checks-and-breakage]

An employer also may not deduct for lost or stolen property, unless it was equipment specifically assigned to the employee who acknowledged receipt in writing. Customer tips may not be deducted from wages. Unless a collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise, the cost of personal protective equipment needed against workplace hazards may not be deducted either, other than clothing or footwear usable off duty. [^ia-deduction-bar-property-tips-ppe] A migrant labor employer under section 91E.1 may not deduct more than $20 of the cost of relocating an employee to the place of employment. [^ia-deduction-bar-relocation]

The Iowa minimum wage rules count as wages the reasonable cost, as the director determines it, of board, lodging or other facilities furnished to the employee. [^ia-wage-includes-facilities] They do not count facilities primarily for the employer's benefit, such as tools of the trade or uniforms the business requires. [^ia-facilities-for-employer-benefit] Wages count toward the minimum wage only if paid free and clear, and a kickback of any part of the wage to the employer defeats them. [^ia-wages-free-and-clear]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** The Iowa minimum wage adopts the federal Fair Labor Standards Act's definitions of employer and employee, as amended to January 1, 2007, so whether a worker is an employee for the Iowa minimum wage turns on the federal test. [^ia-classification-flsa-definitions] Federal classification is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).
The Wage Payment Collection Law has its own definition. An employee is a natural person employed in Iowa for wages by an employer, including a commission salesperson paid on commissions who takes orders or performs services for a principal, but not a person who buys for their own account for resale. [^ia-wage-payment-employee-definition] The Iowa Supreme Court has held that chapter 91A protects only employees, not independent contractors. [^ia-miller-91a-employees-only] In deciding which a worker is, the court approved a jury instruction making the right to control the worker's physical conduct the most important consideration, alongside other factors such as the right to terminate, required adherence to employer rules, selling products in the employer's name and the share of the worker's time devoted to the employer's business. [^ia-miller-right-to-control-instruction] [^ia-miller-instruction-approved]

Both chapters exclude one statutory category of independent contractor: the truck owner-operator described in the workers' compensation definitions. [^ia-wage-payment-owner-operator-exclusion] [^ia-minimum-wage-owner-operator-exclusion] An owner-operator is an independent contractor while operating its own licensed truck or tractor if the stated conditions are substantially present. The conditions are that it maintains the vehicle, bears the principal operating costs, supplies and employs the operating personnel, is paid on work-related factors rather than time, decides the details and means of performance, and has a contract specifying an independent contractor relationship. [^ia-owner-operator-conditions]

For state unemployment insurance, a different test applies. Under Iowa Workforce Development's rule, an employment relationship exists when the business has the right to control and direct the worker both as to the result and as to the details and means of reaching it, whether or not it actually exercises that control. [^ia-unemployment-right-to-control] The rule is written for chapter 96 and does not by its terms govern wage claims.

The Wage Payment Collection Law also excludes from employer status a client, patient or customer who obtains professional services from a licensed person on a fee basis or as an independent contractor. [^ia-wage-payment-professional-services-client] The chapter's employee definition also excludes certain agricultural workers: the employer's spouse and resident relatives, owner-operators or tenant-operators exchanging labor, and neighbors exchanging labor or services. [^ia-wage-payment-agriculture-exclusions]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Iowa allows a tip credit of up to 40% of the applicable minimum wage for an employee of a restaurant, hotel, motel, inn or cabin who customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips. [^ia-tip-credit-forty-percent] At the $7.25 rate, the credit is at most $2.90, so the employer must pay at least $4.35 an hour in cash wages. [^ia-tip-credit-state-rate] An employee whose tips fall short of the amount the employer determined may file a written appeal with the director of the Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing. [^ia-tip-credit-written-appeal] The appeal does not replace the employer's payment duty: for an employee covered by the federal Act, an employer whose employee receives less in tips than the credit claimed must pay the balance so that wages plus tips reach at least the minimum wage. [^ia-federal-tip-shortfall]

Only tips the employee actually receives as their own money, free of employer control, count toward the tip credit. [^ia-tips-employee-property] No tip credit may be taken for hours in a second occupation in which the employee does not meet the tip qualification. [^ia-tip-credit-dual-jobs]

For each tipped employee, the employer must keep records of the tips the employee reports and the amount by which it treated wages as increased by tips, which may not exceed 40% of the minimum wage. It must also record hours and straight-time pay separately for tipped and non-tipped work. [^ia-tipped-employee-records] An employer may not deduct customer tips from an employee's wages. [^ia-deduction-bar-tips]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** The Iowa minimum wage is enforced under the Wage Payment Collection Law, chapter 91A. [^ia-minimum-wage-enforced-under-91a] An employee may sue for damages under section 91A.8 without first going to the state, as long as the employee has not assigned the claim to the director. [^ia-employee-may-sue-unassigned]
When an employer is shown to have intentionally failed to pay wages, it owes the unpaid wages plus liquidated damages, court costs and usual and necessary attorney fees. [^ia-wage-claim-intentional-damages] In other cases the employer owes only the unpaid wages, court costs and usual and necessary attorney fees. [^ia-wage-claim-other-instances] Liquidated damages are 5% of the unpaid wages multiplied by the number of days, excluding Sundays, legal holidays and the first seven days after the missed payday. They may not exceed the unpaid wages and stop accumulating when the employer is subject to a bankruptcy petition. [^ia-liquidated-damages-formula-and-cap]

An action founded on a claim for wages, or for a liability or penalty for failing to pay wages, must be brought within two years. [^ia-wage-claim-two-year-limitation]

The director of the Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing administers and enforces chapter 91A and may hold hearings and investigate violations. [^ia-director-administers-91a] On an employee's written complaint, the director may determine whether unpaid wages constitute an enforceable claim. If the director finds an enforceable claim, the director must, with the employee's consent, take an assignment in trust of the wage and liquidated damages claim. [^ia-director-assignment-one-year] The director may not accept a complaint more than one year after the wages became due and payable. [^ia-director-assignment-one-year] The director must then sue to recover an assigned claim unless it is settled, and may request reasonable and necessary attorney fees. [^ia-director-civil-action]

An employer that violates chapter 91A or its rules is subject to a civil money penalty of up to $500 per pay period for each violation. [^ia-civil-penalty-five-hundred] The director sets the amount considering the size of the business, the gravity of the violation, the employer's good faith and its history of violations. [^ia-civil-penalty-factors]

An employer may not discharge or discriminate against an employee for filing a complaint, assigning a claim, bringing an action or cooperating in an action. The employee may complain to the director within 30 days, and if an investigation finds a violation, the director must sue in district court, which may order relief that includes rehiring or reinstatement with back pay. [^ia-wage-claim-retaliation] Under the director's rules, a complaint filed more than 30 days after the violation is ordinarily presumed untimely. [^ia-retaliation-complaint-thirty-days]
