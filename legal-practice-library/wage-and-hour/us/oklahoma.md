# Wage and Hour Law in Oklahoma[^about]

How Oklahoma ties its minimum wage to the $7.25 federal rate, leaves overtime and adult breaks to federal law and employer policy, allows the federal $2.13 tipped cash wage, and sets semimonthly paydays, next-payday final pay, written deduction agreements, a right-of-control contractor test and wage-claim remedies.

## What is Oklahoma's minimum wage, and how does it compare with the federal rate? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Oklahoma's general minimum wage is the current federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour for all hours worked, subject to the credits and exceptions in the Oklahoma Minimum Wage Act. [^ok-minimum-wage-equals-federal-rate] [^ok-federal-minimum-wage-seven-twenty-five] The Oklahoma Department of Labor states that the state minimum wage equals the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour. [^ok-dol-faq-minimum-wage-seven-twenty-five]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The state Act reaches an employer with more than ten full-time employees or the equivalent at one location, or a smaller employer that does more than $100,000 of gross business a year, but it does not apply to an employer that is subject to the federal Fair Labor Standards Act and pays the federal minimum wage. [^ok-minimum-wage-employer-coverage-definition] The Act's definition of employee leaves out, among others, farm workers, domestic workers in private homes, bona fide executive, administrative, professional and outside sales employees, part-time employees not on permanent status who work fewer than 25 hours a week, and many students and workers under 18 who have not graduated from high school. [^ok-minimum-wage-farm-domestic-exclusions] [^ok-minimum-wage-employee-exclusions] A separate section requires every employer to pay employees aged 18 or older at least $2.00 an hour. [^ok-two-dollar-floor-adult-employees]

Cities and other political subdivisions may not set their own minimum wage or require employers to give a minimum number of vacation or sick leave days, paid or unpaid, because the Legislature has preempted that field. [^ok-preemption-local-wage-sick-leave] No Oklahoma statute requires private employers to provide paid sick leave.

## Does Oklahoma law require overtime pay? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Oklahoma has no statute requiring private employers to pay overtime, daily or weekly. The Oklahoma Department of Labor says the state has no overtime laws and points employers to the federal rule. [^ok-dol-faq-no-state-overtime] The federal Fair Labor Standards Act requires a covered employer to pay at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek unless a federal exemption applies. [^ok-federal-overtime-forty-hours]

Under the Department of Labor's wage rules, a claim for federal overtime premiums is a matter of federal law, but overtime premiums that an employment agreement promises are treated as regular wages that the Department can collect under the state wage payment statute. [^ok-dol-rule-overtime-federal-or-agreed]

## Must Oklahoma employers give meal or rest breaks? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Apart from paid lactation breaks for state agency employees, Oklahoma law does not require meal or rest breaks for employees aged 16 or older. [^ok-state-agency-paid-lactation-break] The Oklahoma Department of Labor says mandatory break laws apply only to children under 16, and company policy sets break and lunch periods for everyone else. [^ok-dol-faq-breaks-only-under-sixteen] A child under 16 must be allowed a cumulative one-hour rest period for each eight consecutive hours worked and may not work more than five consecutive hours without a cumulative half-hour rest period. [^ok-minor-rest-periods-under-sixteen] For employers covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, short rest periods are customarily paid and must be counted as hours worked, [^ok-federal-short-rest-breaks-paid] while a meal period may be unpaid only if the employee is completely relieved from duty. [^ok-federal-meal-period-relieved-duty]

Oklahoma law lets a private employer provide reasonable unpaid break time for an employee to breast-feed or express breast milk but does not itself require it, [^ok-lactation-break-time-optional] while every state agency must allow lactating employees reasonable paid break time. [^ok-state-agency-paid-lactation-break] Federal law separately requires employers to give an employee reasonable break time to express breast milk each time she needs to for one year after the child's birth, and a private place other than a bathroom. [^ok-federal-pumping-break-one-year] An employer with fewer than 50 employees is exempt from the federal requirement only if compliance would impose an undue hardship. [^ok-federal-pumping-small-employer-hardship]

## When is a final paycheck due in Oklahoma? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** Unless an applicable collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise, when employment ends for any reason the employer must pay the employee's wages in full, less offsets and any amount subject to a bona fide disagreement, by the next regular payday for the pay period in which the work was performed. [^ok-final-pay-next-regular-payday] If the employee asks, the employer may instead pay by certified mail postmarked within the same deadline. [^ok-final-pay-next-regular-payday] The Oklahoma Department of Labor confirms that the employer may wait until the next regular payday whether the employee quit or was fired. [^ok-dol-faq-final-pay-next-payday]

An employer that willfully withholds final wages over which there is no bona fide disagreement owes the employee liquidated damages of 2 percent of the unpaid wages for each day the failure continues, or an amount equal to the unpaid wages, whichever is smaller. [^ok-final-pay-liquidated-damages-two-percent] A bona fide disagreement is an honest and sincere belief, supported by relevant evidence, based on a dispute of a determinative fact or of how the law applies. [^ok-bona-fide-disagreement-definition] To rely on one, the employer must pay the amount it concedes is due, without condition, by the payday deadline, and give a written explanation of why the disputed wages are not owed within 15 days after receiving a Department of Labor wage claim form or the employee's written demand by certified mail. [^ok-bona-fide-disagreement-pay-conceded-amount]

Wages for this purpose include salaries, commissions, holiday and vacation pay, overtime pay, severance pay, bonuses and similar advantages that the employer and employee agreed on or that the employer provides under an established policy, once they are earned and due. [^ok-wages-definition-vacation-severance-bonuses]

## How often must Oklahoma employers pay wages, and what may they deduct? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Apart from the monthly-pay exceptions described next, every Oklahoma employer must pay all wages due at least twice each calendar month on regular paydays designated in advance. [^ok-semimonthly-paydays-itemized-deductions] Exempt employees, public employees, school district employees and employees of certain nonprivate foundations may instead be paid at least once a month. [^ok-semimonthly-paydays-itemized-deductions] No more than 11 days may pass between the end of the pay period and the payday, and the employer has three days after the payday to comply. [^ok-payday-eleven-day-lag]
An employer may not deduct any amount from an employee's wages unless a statute or court order requires it or the deduction is made under a voluntary payroll deduction agreement that the Department of Labor's rule allows. [^ok-dol-rule-deductions-need-agreement] The agreement must be in writing and signed by the employee before any deduction under it is taken. [^ok-dol-rule-deduction-agreement-signed-in-advance]

The rule lists permitted agreed deductions, including repayment of a loan or advance made during and within the scope of employment, recovery of a payroll overpayment, the value of merchandise or uniforms the employee bought, benefit and insurance premiums, deferred compensation or other investment plan contributions, and breakage, loss or cash shortage caused by an employee who was the sole party responsible for the cash or items when the loss occurred. [^ok-dol-rule-deductions-need-agreement] [^ok-dol-rule-deductions-breakage-cash-shortage] Wages must be paid in lawful money, which can include electronic payment, and an employee does not waive these rights by any contract to the contrary. [^ok-semimonthly-paydays-itemized-deductions]

## What must an Oklahoma pay statement show? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** With each payment of wages, an Oklahoma employer must give the employee a brief itemized statement of any and all deductions from the pay. [^ok-pay-statement-itemized-deductions] No Oklahoma statute lists other required contents such as hours worked or rates of pay.

If an employer pays with a check that the bank refuses for insufficient funds or a stop-payment order, the employer must reimburse the employee's resulting fees or costs within 14 days of the employer's notice of the refusal. [^ok-bounced-paycheck-fee-reimbursement] An employer may pay by deposit to a financial institution the employee chooses or, if the employee does not consent or designate one, to a payroll card account. [^ok-direct-deposit-or-payroll-card]

## How does Oklahoma decide who is an employee rather than an independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** For wage payment claims, the Oklahoma Department of Labor's rules exclude independent contractors from the definition of employee and define an independent contractor by the common-law right of control. [^ok-dol-rule-employee-excludes-contractors] An independent contractor works in an independent occupation by the contractor's own methods and is subject to the hiring party's control only as to the end product or final result, not the means of doing the work. [^ok-dol-rule-independent-contractor-control] No one factor is controlling, and the relationship turns on the facts of each case. [^ok-dol-rule-no-single-factor-controls]

The factors the rule lists include the degree of control over the details of the work, whether the worker runs a distinct business, whether the work is usually supervised in the locality, the skill required, who supplies tools and the place of work, the length of the engagement, payment by time or by the job, whether the work is part of the hiring party's regular business, what the parties believe they are creating, and whether either can end the relationship without liability. [^ok-dol-rule-independent-contractor-factors]

## Can Oklahoma employers count tips toward the minimum wage? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Oklahoma law gives credit toward the minimum wage for tips, gratuities, meals and lodging an employee receives, provided the employer pays at least the cash wage required under the federal tip-credit rule. [^ok-tip-credit-federal-cash-wage] Under the federal rule, that cash wage is at least $2.13 an hour, with tips making up the difference to the federal minimum wage. [^ok-federal-tipped-cash-wage-two-thirteen] For an employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, the federal tip credit applies only if the employee has been informed of the federal tip-credit provisions and keeps all tips received, apart from a pool among employees who customarily and regularly receive tips. [^ok-federal-tip-credit-notice-retention]

No Oklahoma statute addresses tip pooling.

## How are unpaid wages recovered in Oklahoma, and what penalties apply? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employee may sue in any court of competent jurisdiction to recover unpaid wages and liquidated damages, alone or on behalf of other employees similarly situated, and the court may award costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^ok-wage-suit-costs-attorney-fees] The Commissioner of Labor may also collect a wage claim through an administrative proceeding, issuing an order of determination that tells the employer to pay within 20 days after receipt unless it seeks reconsideration or appeals to district court, [^ok-order-determination-twenty-day-notice] and a final order left unpaid for 20 days may be recorded with the county clerk and collected like a money judgment. [^ok-commissioner-administrative-wage-claim] [^ok-final-order-recorded-collected-judgment]

An employer found by a court to have paid less than the Oklahoma minimum wage owes the employee double the full amount of the wages, less what it actually paid, plus court costs and reasonable attorney fees of at least $100, and an agreement to work for less is no defense. [^ok-minimum-wage-double-damages-fees] Paying less than the minimum wage is also a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of up to $500, up to six months in county jail, or both. [^ok-minimum-wage-misdemeanor-penalty] The Commissioner may assess a $500 administrative fine against an employer that violates the wage payment statutes on two or more occasions within any six-month period. [^ok-repeat-violation-administrative-fine]
