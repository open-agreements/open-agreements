# Wage and Hour Law in the Northern Mariana Islands[^about]

How the federal $7.25 minimum wage applies in the Northern Mariana Islands under the 2007 federal law, and how the Commonwealth's Minimum Wage and Hour Act adds weekly overtime, an itemized pay statement, liquidated damages for willful violations and a six-month deadline for wage suits, alongside biweekly-pay and deduction rules for foreign national workers.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** The federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour applies in the Northern Mariana Islands to employees covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act. [^cnmi-federal-minimum-wage-applies] [^cnmi-federal-minimum-wage-rate] In 2007 Congress made the federal minimum wage section apply to the Commonwealth, [^cnmi-federal-minimum-wage-applies] and set a schedule of step increases that continued until the Commonwealth rate equaled the federal rate. [^cnmi-federal-transition] The federal rate itself is $7.25 an hour. [^cnmi-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The Commonwealth's own Minimum Wage and Hour Act has not been updated to the federal rate. Its minimum wage section still lists a rate of $3.05 an hour beginning July 1, 1996 for employers other than garment and construction businesses, [^cnmi-local-minimum-wage] and the Law Revision Commission's note to that section records that, as of mid-1997, Congress had not made federal minimum wage laws applicable in the Commonwealth. [^cnmi-covenant-exemption-note] For an employee entitled to the federal minimum wage, the Commonwealth's lower statutory rate does not reduce the federal wage floor. [^cnmi-local-minimum-wage] [^cnmi-federal-minimum-wage-applies] [^cnmi-federal-minimum-wage-rate]

The Commonwealth Act also exempts several categories from its own minimum wage and overtime sections, including family members employed by a son, daughter, spouse or parent, bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees, outside salespersons and outside collectors, and fishing workers. [^cnmi-cmc-9223-exemptions] For each exempt category, the Act directs the director to establish a special minimum wage, [^cnmi-special-minimum-wage-directive] set at 40 to 60 percent of the statutory rate, with a $300-a-month floor for fishing, small-farm and covered domestic workers. [^cnmi-special-minimum-wage]

Foreign national workers employed under the Commonwealth Employment Act may not be paid less than the minimum wage provided by law, and an approved employment contract must pass through any later minimum wage increase. [^cnmi-foreign-worker-minimum-wage]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** The Commonwealth's Minimum Wage and Hour Act generally requires overtime at time and a half for hours over 40 in a week, with a daily exception for a few exempt categories. [^cnmi-overtime-40-hours] Certain exempt fishing, small-farm and domestic workers may not be required to work more than 12 hours a day or 72 hours a week without overtime pay. [^cnmi-ot-special-hours] The Act defines the regular rate by reference to the federal Fair Labor Standards Act. [^cnmi-overtime-40-hours] For employees covered by the federal Act, federal law separately requires the same time-and-a-half rate after 40 hours in a workweek. [^cnmi-federal-overtime]

The Commonwealth Act defines a week or workweek as any period of seven consecutive days. [^cnmi-cmc-9212-workweek] Its overtime section does not apply to the categories listed in 4 CMC § 9223, including bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees. [^cnmi-ot-exemptions] Three of those exempt categories get a different hours rule: fishing and other aquatic-harvest workers, [^cnmi-ot-exemptions] agricultural workers in any workweek in which the employer has fewer than 10 employees, and domestic workers in the employer's home who babysit on a casual basis or who live in the home where the employer cares for a child under 18 or an elderly or handicapped person. [^cnmi-ot-ag-domestic] For them, the Act bars requiring more than 12 hours a day or 72 hours a week without overtime pay. [^cnmi-ot-special-hours]

For a foreign national worker employed under an approved employment contract, the employer may offer overtime work but may not require it, and any time the worker must be present at a place the employer designates counts as working hours for wages and overtime. [^cnmi-foreign-worker-hours]

Federal overtime exemptions and hours-worked rules are discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## Are meal or rest breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** The Commonwealth's Minimum Wage and Hour Act, 4 CMC § 9211 and following sections, contains no meal-period or rest-break requirement for adult employees. Under the federal rules, short rest periods of about 5 to 20 minutes that an employer chooses to give must be counted and paid as hours worked, [^cnmi-federal-rest-periods] while a bona fide meal period during which the employee is completely relieved from duty is not worktime. [^cnmi-federal-meal-periods]

Federal law separately requires reasonable break time and a private place, other than a bathroom, for an employee to express breast milk for one year after a child's birth. [^cnmi-federal-nursing-breaks]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** The Commonwealth's Minimum Wage and Hour Act sets no deadline for paying final wages when an employee leaves and no penalty for paying them late. Under federal law, minimum wages owed for a workweek are due on the employee's regular payday, as the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit held in *Biggs v. Wilson*. [^cnmi-biggs-payday] Overtime earned in a workweek must likewise be paid on the regular payday for the period in which that workweek ends. [^cnmi-federal-overtime-payday] When the correct amount cannot be determined until after the regular pay period, payment may not be delayed beyond the next payday after the computation can be made. [^cnmi-federal-overtime-delay]

Unpaid minimum wages or overtime owed at separation are recoverable under the Commonwealth Act, with an additional equal amount as liquidated damages only for a willful violation, [^cnmi-fp-employer-liability] and under the federal Act, ordinarily with an additional equal amount as liquidated damages without proof of willfulness, [^cnmi-federal-liability] unless the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds for believing it was complying, in which case the court may reduce or deny them. [^cnmi-fp-federal-good-faith] A suit for those amounts under the Commonwealth Act must be filed within six months after the claim accrues, or within one year for a willful violation. [^cnmi-fp-six-month-limitation]

For a foreign national worker, a labor complaint is generally due within six months after the last event it concerns, subject to a discovery rule, and may not be filed more than 30 days after the approved employment contract ends. [^cnmi-fp-labor-complaint-deadline]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** The Commonwealth's Minimum Wage and Hour Act sets no pay-frequency rule. For a foreign national worker employed under an approved employment contract, the Commonwealth Employment Act requires payment every two weeks, in the amount the contract specifies, unless another law provides otherwise. [^cnmi-foreign-worker-biweekly] The Department of Labor's regulations add that such a worker must be paid in cash, by check or by direct deposit in a United States bank, payable in United States currency. [^cnmi-foreign-worker-payment-method]

The Commonwealth Act's definition of a wage is legal tender or a check convertible to cash at face value, plus the reasonable cost, as the Department determines, of board, lodging or other facilities the employer customarily furnishes. [^cnmi-wage-definition]

For foreign national workers, each deduction for an employer expense must be specified in the approved employment contract and itemized on the wage documentation, and no deductions beyond those the regulations allow may be made. [^cnmi-foreign-worker-deductions] The regulations cap total deductions at 30 percent of the worker's biweekly wages or the minimum permitted under the federal Act, whichever is less, except that a non-business employer may deduct up to $100 a month for housing and up to $100 a month for food, local transportation and other benefits, and any employer may deduct amounts a court or administrative order requires or allows. [^cnmi-foreign-worker-deduction-cap]

For employees covered by the federal Act, wages are not treated as paid unless they are paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear. [^cnmi-federal-free-and-clear]

## What must a pay statement show? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** Every employer must give each employee, every pay period, a written statement showing total hours worked, overtime hours, straight-time pay, overtime pay, other pay, total gross pay, the amount and purpose of each deduction, total net pay, the payment date and the pay period covered. [^cnmi-pay-statement]

Employers must also keep, at or near the workplace, a record of each employee's name, address and occupation, the amount paid each pay period and the hours worked each day and each workweek, and must give the Department access to copy those records. [^cnmi-cmc-9232-records]

## Who counts as an employee under the CNMI Minimum Wage and Hour Act? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** The Commonwealth's Minimum Wage and Hour Act names no employee-versus-contractor test of its own. It defines to employ as to permit to work, and an employee as any individual employed by an employer. [^cnmi-employ-definition] Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, to employ includes to suffer or permit to work, [^cnmi-federal-employ] and federal regulations decide employee status by looking at the economic realities of the relationship, asking whether the worker is economically dependent on the potential employer for work or in business for themself. [^cnmi-federal-economic-realities] That federal test governs federal coverage; no Commonwealth authority reviewed for this guide says whether Commonwealth courts apply it under the Commonwealth Act.

The Commonwealth Act's definition of employer reaches individuals, businesses and the Commonwealth government and its agencies, but not the United States government. [^cnmi-employer-definition]

## Can tips count toward the minimum wage? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Under the federal minimum wage, a covered employer may pay a tipped employee a cash wage of at least $2.13 an hour and count tips toward the rest, subject to notice and tip-retention conditions. [^cnmi-federal-tip-credit-formula] The employer must first inform the employee of the tip-credit rules and let the employee keep all tips, apart from a pool among employees who customarily receive tips. [^cnmi-federal-tip-credit-conditions]

The Commonwealth's Minimum Wage and Hour Act contains no tip-credit provision of its own; its definition of a wage counts cash, checks and the reasonable cost of board, lodging or other facilities the employer customarily furnishes, and does not mention tips. [^cnmi-tip-wage-definition]

A tipped employee is one who customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips. [^cnmi-federal-tipped-employee] If tips fall short, the employer must make up the difference so the employee receives at least the minimum wage. [^cnmi-federal-tip-shortfall] An employer may not keep employees' tips for any purpose, including letting managers or supervisors share them, whether or not it takes a tip credit. [^cnmi-federal-tips-kept]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employer that violates the Commonwealth's minimum wage or overtime section owes the affected employees their unpaid minimum wages or overtime, plus an additional equal amount as liquidated damages only for a willful violation. [^cnmi-employer-liability] One or more employees may sue for themselves and others similarly situated, and a prevailing employee recovers a reasonable attorney's fee and costs. [^cnmi-collective-action] [^cnmi-attorney-fees] A suit under the Commonwealth Act must be filed within six months after the claim accrues, or within one year for a willful violation. [^cnmi-six-month-limitation]

Those Commonwealth periods are much shorter than the federal ones. A claim under the federal Act may be brought within two years, or three years for a willful violation, [^cnmi-federal-limitations] and the federal Act ordinarily awards an equal amount as liquidated damages without requiring willfulness, [^cnmi-enf-federal-liability] although a court may reduce or deny them if the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds for believing it was complying. [^cnmi-enf-federal-good-faith] An employee whose Commonwealth wage claim is time-barred may still bring a corresponding federal claim if federal law covers the work and the federal limitation period remains open. [^cnmi-six-month-limitation] [^cnmi-federal-limitations]

At an employee's request, the Department may take an assignment of an underpaid employee's claim in trust and sue to collect it, with the employer paying costs and attorney's fees if the Department prevails. [^cnmi-department-assignment] The Department may also bring an action against an employer for an alleged violation of the Commonwealth's labor or wage laws. [^cnmi-department-actions]

For foreign national workers under approved employment contracts, a Department hearing officer may award unpaid wages or overtime and amounts unlawfully deducted, [^cnmi-hearing-officer-awards] and must assess liquidated damages of twice the unpaid wages or overtime when the worker prevails, unless the officer finds extenuating circumstances. [^cnmi-hearing-officer-double-damages] A labor complaint must be filed within six months after the last event it concerns or, if the conduct could not be discovered then, within six months after a reasonably diligent complainant could have discovered it, and in any case no later than 30 days after the contract ends. [^cnmi-labor-complaint-deadline] A foreign national worker may also sue the employer, or the issuer of the employer's bond, directly in the Commonwealth courts for wages, overtime or other contract benefits. [^cnmi-direct-action]
