# Wage and Hour Law in Vermont[^about]

How Vermont sets an indexed $14.42 minimum wage for 2026 that rises to $14.91 on January 1, 2027, lets hotels, motels, tourist places and restaurants pay tipped service employees half the minimum wage, requires weekly overtime with broad industry exemptions, gives employees reasonable opportunities to eat, pays discharged employees within 72 hours, requires weekly pay unless the employer gives notice of a biweekly or semimonthly schedule, and lets employees recover twice their unpaid wages.

## What is the minimum wage in Vermont? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Vermont's minimum wage is $14.42 an hour in 2026, and the Vermont Department of Labor has announced that it rises to $14.91 an hour on January 1, 2027. [^vt-minimum-wage-2026-2027] The statute adjusts the rate every January 1 by the smaller of five percent or the increase in the Consumer Price Index for the 12 months before the previous September 1, and the rate may never decrease. [^vt-minimum-wage-indexing] Both rates are above the $7.25 federal minimum wage. [^vt-federal-minimum-wage-rate] If the federal minimum wage were ever higher than the Vermont rate for a year, the federal rate would become the Vermont minimum wage for that year. [^vt-minimum-wage-federal-higher]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The minimum wage subchapter applies only to an employer of two or more employees, and its definition of employee leaves out several groups. [^vt-minimum-wage-employee-exclusions] The exclusions include agricultural workers, domestic workers in a private home, federal employees, bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees, newspaper and advertising home deliverers, taxicab drivers, outside salespersons, students working during the school year or vacation periods, and elected and appointed municipal officers. [^vt-minimum-wage-employee-exclusions] Employees of a publicly supported nonprofit organization are also excluded, except laundry employees, nurses' aides and practical nurses. [^vt-minimum-wage-employee-exclusions] An excluded worker may still be covered by the federal minimum wage. [^vt-federal-minimum-wage-rate]

## When is overtime owed in Vermont? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Vermont's general overtime rule is weekly, not daily. [^vt-overtime-forty-hours-exemptions] An employer must pay at least one and one-half times the regular wage rate for work over 40 hours in a workweek. [^vt-overtime-forty-hours-exemptions] The state rule does not apply to employees of a retail or service establishment, a hotel, motel or restaurant, or a seasonal amusement or recreational establishment. [^vt-overtime-forty-hours-exemptions] Federal law separately requires time and one-half for hours over 40 in a workweek for employees it covers, so an employee outside the state rule may still be owed overtime under federal law. [^vt-federal-overtime-forty-hours]

A retail or service establishment is one at which 75 percent of annual sales of goods or services is not for resale and is recognized as retail in the particular industry. [^vt-overtime-forty-hours-exemptions] The state rule also does not cover employees of a transportation business to whom federal overtime law does not apply, employees of political subdivisions, state employees covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, and permanent employees of the General Assembly. [^vt-overtime-other-exemptions]

A hospital, public health center, nursing home, maternity home, therapeutic community residence or residential care home may instead pay overtime on a daily and biweekly basis. [^vt-overtime-health-care-election] To use this alternative, the employer must pay biweekly, file an election with the Commissioner of Labor, and pay time and one-half for work over eight hours in a workday or over 80 hours in the biweekly period. [^vt-overtime-health-care-election]

Bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees are excluded from the minimum wage subchapter, including its overtime rule. [^vt-overtime-white-collar-exclusion] Federal law generally requires an exempt executive, administrative or professional employee to be paid a salary of at least $684 a week. [^vt-federal-white-collar-salary-684]

## Must Vermont employers give meal or rest breaks? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Vermont requires an employer to give employees reasonable opportunities during work periods to eat and to use toilet facilities, but the statute sets no fixed length or schedule for a break and does not say whether the time must be paid. [^vt-meal-opportunity] For employees covered by federal law, rest periods of about 5 to 20 minutes must be counted as hours worked. [^vt-federal-short-rest-paid] A meal period may be unpaid under federal law only if the employee is completely relieved from duty for the purpose of eating a regular meal. [^vt-federal-bona-fide-meal]

For three years after the birth of a child, an employer must give a nursing mother reasonable time throughout the day to express breast milk, and under Vermont law the employer decides whether that time is paid unless a collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise. [^vt-nursing-mothers-time-space] For employees covered by the federal break-time rule, a pumping break counts as hours worked if the employee is not completely relieved from duty during the entire break. [^vt-federal-pumping-break-hours-worked] The employer must also make a reasonable accommodation to provide an appropriate private space that is not a bathroom stall. [^vt-nursing-mothers-time-space] Under Vermont law, an employer may be exempted from these requirements if providing the time or space would substantially disrupt its operations. [^vt-nursing-mothers-time-space]

## When is a final paycheck due in Vermont? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** A Vermont employee who is discharged must be paid within 72 hours of the discharge. [^vt-final-pay-quit-discharge] An employee who quits must be paid on the last regular payday, or on the following Friday if there is no regular payday. [^vt-final-pay-quit-discharge] An employee who is away from the regular place of employment on the scheduled payday is entitled to payment on demand. [^vt-final-pay-quit-discharge]

An employer that fails to pay wages as the weekly payment statute requires forfeits to the employee twice the value of the unpaid wages, plus costs and reasonable attorney's fees, but only if the wages are still unpaid or improperly paid when the suit is brought. [^vt-forfeiture-twice-wages] The same violation can bring a fine of up to $5,000, and corporate officers who control payroll and willfully and without good cause take part in knowing violations are personally liable for the wages due. [^vt-nonpayment-fine-officer-liability]

## How often must Vermont employers pay wages, and what may they deduct? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Vermont generally requires weekly wage payments, but permits biweekly or semimonthly payments after notice to each employee. [^vt-weekly-pay] Each payment must cover wages earned up to a day not more than six days before the payday, and a collective bargaining agreement may allow a lag of up to 13 days for employees it covers. [^vt-weekly-pay] An employer may not require an employee to agree, as a condition of employment, to be paid on a different schedule. [^vt-wage-assignment-to-employer]
An employer may pay by direct deposit or payroll card only with the employee's written authorization. [^vt-direct-deposit-written-authorization] [^vt-direct-deposit-payroll-card] A payroll card is allowed only if the employee consents voluntarily in writing after receiving written disclosure of all payment options and fees, and consent may not be a condition of hire or continued employment. [^vt-direct-deposit-payroll-card]

The statutes allow deductions from the minimum and overtime rates for board, lodging, apparel, rent, utilities, and other items or services usual in a particular employment relationship. [^vt-board-lodging-deductions] No deduction may be made for the care, cleaning or maintenance of required apparel, and a deduction for required apparel needs the employee's express written authorization and may not bring total pay below the minimum wage or include administrative fees. [^vt-apparel-deduction-limits] An employer may not require an employee or applicant to pay for a medical examination required as a condition of employment. [^vt-medical-exam-cost] An assignment of future wages to the employer, or to anyone acting for the employer, is not valid. [^vt-wage-assignment-to-employer]

The Department of Labor states that the Vermont Minimum Wage Rules set out the allowed and prohibited deductions. [^vt-dol-deduction-rules] According to the department, an employer may deduct for goods or services it provided only if the deduction does not reduce wages below the minimum wage and the employee gave written authorization or the employer documented the employee's intent to repay. [^vt-dol-goods-services-deductions] The department also states that an employer may not deduct for claimed damages, cash register shortages, or a medical examination required as a condition of employment. [^vt-dol-prohibited-deductions]

## What must a Vermont pay statement show? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** No Vermont statute lists the contents of a pay statement, but the Department of Labor states that employers must give employees a wage statement with each payment of wages. [^vt-dol-wage-statement] According to the department, the statement must show at least the total hours worked, the hourly rate, gross pay and each deduction, fully itemized. [^vt-dol-wage-statement]

Every employer covered by the minimum wage subchapter must keep a true and accurate record of the hours worked by each employee and the wages paid, and must give the Commissioner of Labor a sworn statement of those records on demand. [^vt-records-hours-wages] An employer that fails to keep the required records, or to furnish them to the Commissioner on demand, may be assessed a civil penalty of up to $100. [^vt-penalty-underpayment-records]

## How does Vermont decide who is an employee rather than an independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** Vermont's wage payment subchapter uses an ABC test. [^vt-wage-payment-abc-test] An individual who has entered into the employment of an employer is an employee unless the employer shows that the individual is free from control or direction over the work, both under the contract and in fact; that the work is outside the usual course of the business or outside all of its places of business; and that the individual is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, profession or business. [^vt-wage-payment-abc-test] The employer must show all three. [^vt-wage-payment-abc-test]

The minimum wage subchapter, which includes the overtime rule, defines an employee more broadly as any individual employed or permitted to work by an employer, subject to the listed exclusions. [^vt-classification-minimum-wage-employee]

## Can Vermont employers count tips toward the minimum wage? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** A Vermont employer in the hotel, motel, tourist place or restaurant industry may pay a service or tipped employee a basic wage rate of not less than one-half the minimum wage. [^vt-tipped-basic-wage] A service or tipped employee is an employee of a hotel, motel, tourist place or restaurant who customarily and regularly receives more than $120 a month in tips for direct and personal customer service. [^vt-tipped-basic-wage] The Department of Labor states that the basic tipped wage rate is $7.21 an hour in 2026 and rises to $7.46 an hour on January 1, 2027. [^vt-tipped-wage-2026-2027]

The Department of Labor states that if an employee's tips during a workweek do not bring pay up to the full minimum wage for each hour, the employer must pay the difference. [^vt-tipped-wage-make-up] Employers outside the hotel, motel, tourist place and restaurant industry may not use the lower basic wage rate, and an employee who does not regularly receive more than $120 a month in tips must be paid the full minimum wage. [^vt-tipped-basic-wage]

## How are unpaid wages recovered in Vermont, and what penalties apply? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employee may sue an employer that fails to pay wages as the wage payment statutes require and recover twice the value of the unpaid wages, plus costs and reasonable attorney's fees, if the wages remain unpaid or improperly paid when the suit is brought. [^vt-enforcement-forfeiture-twice-wages] An employee paid less than the minimum wage may recover twice the minimum wage owed, less any amount the employer actually paid, together with costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^vt-minimum-wage-civil-action] An agreement to work for less than the minimum wage is not a defense. [^vt-minimum-wage-civil-action] A suit based on a violation of the weekly payment statute, which governs paydays and final pay, must be brought within two years after the claim accrues. [^vt-wage-payment-two-year-limit] The minimum wage statute sets no filing deadline of its own, so a minimum wage suit falls under Vermont's general six-year limit for civil actions. [^vt-civil-action-six-years]

An employee may instead file an unpaid-wage complaint with the Department of Labor within two years after the wages were due. [^vt-dol-complaint-two-years] If the Commissioner of Labor finds that the employer willfully withheld wages, the order for collection must provide that the employer pay an additional amount of up to twice the unpaid wages, half of which goes to the employee. [^vt-dol-willful-additional-amount] The Commissioner may also assess a civil penalty of up to $100 for each day an employee is paid less than the required rate. [^vt-enforcement-daily-penalty]

An employer may not discharge or otherwise retaliate against an employee for complaining of a wage payment violation, for cooperating with a Department of Labor investigation, or because the employer believes the employee may do either. [^vt-wage-payment-retaliation] An employee who suffers retaliation may sue for compensatory and punitive damages, reinstatement, restitution of wages, and attorney's fees. [^vt-wage-payment-retaliation] The minimum wage subchapter contains a matching protection. [^vt-minimum-wage-retaliation]
