# School Policy Compliance in New York: Employee Handbook Notices and Workplace Rules[^about]

What a New York independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's employee handbook and workplace policies must contain: required notices, the sexual harassment prevention policy, electronic monitoring, personal accounts, off-duty cannabis use, pay transparency, whistleblower notice and NLRB work-rule standards.

## What must a New York independent school's employee handbook contain, and which notices must accompany it? {#handbook-required-contents}

**Short answer.** New York Labor Law § 203-e(6) requires an employer that provides an employee handbook to include in it a notice of employee rights and remedies under the reproductive health decision-making statute. [^lab-203-e-handbook-notice] An employer that provides a handbook must also include in it its airborne infectious disease exposure prevention plan under Labor Law § 218-b(6). [^lab-218-b-handbook-plan]

| Requirement | What the statute requires | When or where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sexual harassment prevention policy and training, Labor Law § 201-g | A written policy that meets the State model, annual interactive training, and a notice of the policy and training materials | At hiring and at every annual training [^lab-201-g-notice-at-hire-and-training] |
| Electronic monitoring, Civil Rights Law § 52-c | Prior written notice of monitoring of telephone, email and internet use, acknowledged by the employee, and a posted notice | Upon hiring, and posted conspicuously [^cvr-52-c-monitoring-notice-handbook] |
| Airborne infectious disease exposure prevention plan, Labor Law § 218-b | The plan, posted at each worksite and, where the employer provides a handbook, included in the handbook | Posted in a visible and prominent location at each worksite [^lab-218-b-handbook-plan] |
| Lactation, Labor Law § 206-c | The written policy on expressing breast milk developed by the Commissioner of Labor | Upon hire, annually, and on return to work after a birth [^lab-206-c-written-policy-timing] |
| Whistleblower protection, Labor Law § 740 | A posted notice of employee protections, rights and obligations | Posted where employees and applicants frequent [^lab-740-posting-duty] |
| Time off to vote, Election Law § 3-110 | A posted notice of the voting-leave provisions | Posted at least ten working days before every election [^eln-3-110-voting-notice] |
| Disability and paid family leave, Workers' Compensation Law § 229 | A posted notice that the employer has provided disability and family leave benefits | Posted in a conspicuous place [^wkc-229-notice-and-statement] |
| Title VII, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-10 | A posted notice prepared or approved by the EEOC | Posted where notices are customarily posted [^usc-2000e-10-posting-handbook] |

The § 203-e(6) duty applies only to an employer that chooses to provide a handbook, and the statute makes the handbook the place the notice must appear. [^lab-203-e-handbook-notice] The other requirements in the table are written notices or postings that a handbook can carry or sit beside. Whether a religious employer must carry the § 203-e notice is addressed in the [question on the reproductive health notice](/practice-guides/school-policy-compliance/us/new-york-religious-employers#reproductive-health-notice-religious-school).

## What sexual harassment prevention policy and training must a New York school provide? {#sexual-harassment-policy-training}

**Short answer.** Every New York employer, including a private school, must adopt the State's model sexual harassment prevention policy or one that equals or exceeds it, provide the policy to employees in writing, and train all employees annually. [^lab-201-g-policy-adoption] [^lab-201-g-annual-training]

The model policy, and so any policy that matches it, must include a standard complaint form, a procedure for timely and confidential investigation, notice of available forums, a statement that harassment is misconduct subject to sanctions, and a statement that retaliation is unlawful. [^lab-201-g-model-policy-contents] The training must be interactive and cover examples of unlawful conduct, the federal and state law and remedies, and the forums for complaints. [^lab-201-g-training-contents] The written notice of the policy and training goes to each employee at hiring and at every annual training, in English and in the employee's identified primary language, [^lab-201-g-written-notice-timing] and an English notice suffices when the State offers no template in that language. [^lab-201-g-notice-languages] The State publishes a model policy and a model complaint form, and describes the complaint form as a required part of every employer's policy. [^ny-gov-model-complaint-form]

## What notice must a New York school give before monitoring employees' email, phone and internet use? {#electronic-monitoring-notice}

**Short answer.** A New York employer that electronically monitors employee telephone, email or internet use must give each monitored employee prior written notice upon hiring, obtain a written or electronic acknowledgment, and post the notice conspicuously. [^cvr-52-c-prior-notice]

The notice must advise employees that their telephone, email and internet use may be subject to monitoring at any and all times and by any lawful means. [^cvr-52-c-notice-wording] The Attorney General enforces the statute, with civil penalties of up to five hundred dollars for a first offense, one thousand dollars for a second and three thousand dollars for each later offense. [^cvr-52-c-penalties]

## May a New York school require employees or applicants to give access to their personal social media accounts? {#personal-social-media-accounts}

**Short answer.** A New York employer may not request, require or coerce an employee or applicant to disclose login credentials for a personal account, to open a personal account in the employer's presence, or to reproduce its contents, subject to listed exceptions. [^lab-201-i-prohibition] The exceptions include access to accounts used for business purposes and to employer-paid devices where the employee had prior notice. [^lab-201-i-business-account-exceptions]

An employer may not discipline an employee or refuse to hire an applicant for refusing a prohibited request. [^lab-201-i-no-retaliation] The statute does not restrict viewing information available in the public domain or information that an employee or a third party voluntarily shares, including for investigating reported misconduct. [^lab-201-i-public-and-shared-information]

## Can a New York school discipline an employee for off-duty cannabis use or a positive cannabis test? {#off-duty-cannabis-use}

**Short answer.** New York Labor Law § 201-d bars an employer from discriminating against an employee for legal off-duty use of cannabis away from the employer's premises and equipment, but permits action where the employee manifests specific articulable symptoms of impairment while working or where federal or state law requires it. [^lab-201-d-cannabis-off-duty] [^lab-201-d-4-a-impairment]

The Department of Labor's guidance states that a test for cannabis cannot serve as the basis for concluding that an employee was impaired, because such tests do not currently demonstrate impairment. [^dol-cannabis-test-not-impairment] The presence of THC alone therefore does not establish impairment under the statute and guidance. [^lab-201-d-4-a-impairment] [^dol-cannabis-test-not-impairment] An employer remains free to act on impairment shown by articulable symptoms, and the guidance says it may discipline employees who use cannabis during work hours or on employer property, although observable signs of use alone do not show impairment. [^dol-cannabis-on-duty]

## Must a New York school's job postings include a pay range? {#pay-range-job-postings}

**Short answer.** An employer with four or more employees must disclose the compensation or a range of compensation, and any existing job description, in every advertisement for a job, promotion or transfer opportunity performed at least in part in New York. [^lab-194-b-disclosure] [^lab-194-b-employer-defined]

The range is the minimum and maximum annual salary or hourly range that the employer in good faith believes accurate when it posts the advertisement, and an advertisement is any written description of an opportunity made available to a pool of potential applicants, including internally. [^lab-194-b-range-defined] [^lab-194-b-advertise-defined] A violation is subject to a civil penalty under Labor Law § 218, [^lab-194-b-civil-penalty] and retaliation against an applicant or employee for exercising these rights is prohibited. [^lab-194-b-no-retaliation]

## What whistleblower notice must a New York school post, and which employers does Labor Law § 740 cover? {#whistleblower-protection-notice}

**Short answer.** Labor Law § 740 covers any employer with one or more employees, including an institution, and requires it to post a notice of employees' whistleblower protections, rights and obligations in places that employees and applicants frequent. [^lab-740-employer-defined] [^lab-740-posting-notice]

The statute's definition of employee also reaches former employees and independent contractors who are natural persons working in furtherance of the employer's business. [^lab-740-employee-defined]

## Do NLRB standards for handbook work rules apply to a nonprofit independent school? {#nlrb-handbook-rules}

**Short answer.** Where the National Labor Relations Board has jurisdiction, its 2023 *Stericycle* standard presumes unlawful a work rule that reasonably tends to chill employees' exercise of labor-law rights, but the Supreme Court has declined to read the Act to cover teachers in church-operated schools. [^nlrb-stericycle-standard] [^catholic-bishop-church-schools]

Under *Stericycle*, an employer can rebut the presumption by proving that the rule advances a legitimate and substantial business interest that a more narrowly tailored rule could not advance. [^nlrb-stericycle-standard] In *NLRB v. Catholic Bishop of Chicago*, the Court declined to construe the Act to reach teachers in church-operated schools absent a clear expression of congressional intent, because doing so would raise questions under the Religion Clauses. [^catholic-bishop-church-schools]

As of the November 19, 2026 review, the most recent Board record cited here is the Board's August 2, 2023 announcement of the *Stericycle* standard. [^nlrb-stericycle-standard] Where the Board has jurisdiction over a secular nonprofit school, the school's handbook rules on confidentiality, social media and workplace conduct are measured against that standard; whether the Board has jurisdiction over a particular school depends on its own jurisdictional standards, which this guide does not cover. *Catholic Bishop* addressed teachers in church-operated schools, so it does not by itself remove a religious school's non-teaching staff from the Act. [^nlrb-stericycle-standard] [^catholic-bishop-church-schools]
