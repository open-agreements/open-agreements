# School Policy Compliance in Virginia: Child Abuse Reporting and Prevention[^about]

What a Virginia private K–12 school's child-protection policies must reflect: the mandated reporting duty and its 24-hour penalty, reporting through the head of school, investigations of allegations against staff, fingerprint and child abuse registry checks for accredited schools, the job-assistance prohibition, sealed and expunged records, the 2026 private school anti-bullying requirement and the clergy exception.

## Who at a Virginia private school must report suspected child abuse, to whom, and how quickly? {#mandated-reporting}

**Short answer.** A Virginia private school teacher or other employee who has reason to suspect in a professional or official capacity that a child is abused or neglected must report immediately to the local social services department or the state hotline, or immediately notify the school's person in charge. [^va-car-1509-duty] [^va-car-1509-person-in-charge]

The duty attaches to any teacher or other person employed in a private school, not only to licensed or classroom staff. [^va-car-1509-duty] The same list separately names any athletic coach, director or other person 18 or older employed by or volunteering with the athletics program of a private elementary or secondary school, and any person 18 or older who has received training approved by the Department of Social Services for recognizing and reporting child abuse and neglect. [^va-car-1509-coaches-trained] A further subdivision names any person 18 or older associated with or employed by any public or private organization responsible for the care, custody or control of children, which can reach adult volunteers who are not school employees. [^va-car-1509-associated-adults] Reason to suspect expressly includes any suspected violation of the Title 18.2 sexual offenses against children that subsection C cites. [^va-car-1509-sexual-offenses] The duty also does not turn on who the suspected abuser is: § 63.2-1508 provides that nothing in its definition of a valid report relieves a mandated reporter of the duty to report, regardless of the identity of the person suspected. [^va-car-1508-identity]

The reporter must disclose all information that is the basis for the suspicion and, on request, make available the records that document it. [^va-car-1509-disclose] The Department of Social Services lists its 24-hour Child Protective Services Hotline as (800) 552-7096, and states that a person making a report is not required to give a name. [^va-car-dss-hotline] [^va-car-dss-anonymous] Under the version of § 63.2-1509 that takes effect July 1, 2027, the date set by the enactment clauses of 2026 Va. Acts chapters 865 and 900, a mandated reporter reports to the Department's toll-free hotline rather than to a local department. [^va-car-1509-2027-hotline] [^va-car-ch865-effective] [^va-car-ch900-effective]

A mandated reporter who fails to report as soon as possible, and no later than 24 hours after having reason to suspect, is fined up to $500 for a first failure and at least $1,000 for each later failure, and a knowing and intentional failure in a case evidencing acts or attempted acts of rape, sodomy, aggravated sexual battery or object sexual penetration is a Class 1 misdemeanor. [^va-car-1509-penalty] A separate subsection makes a failure to report within 24 hours a Class 1 misdemeanor, and a second conviction a Class 6 felony, where the offense is alleged to have occurred at a private or state-operated hospital, institution or facility to which children have been committed or placed for care and treatment. [^va-car-1509-facility-penalty] In *Creekmore v. Commonwealth*, the Court of Appeals of Virginia affirmed a mandated reporter's conviction under § 18.2-371, which punishes an adult who willfully contributes to an omission that renders a child abused or neglected, holding that her neglect of the duty to report, together with her advice and conduct during therapy, was sufficient. [^va-car-creekmore-holding] [^va-car-18-2-371]

A person who makes a report or provides records under subsection A is immune from civil and criminal liability and administrative sanction unless the person acted in bad faith or with malicious purpose. [^va-car-1509-immunity] No report is required from a person with actual knowledge that the same matter has already been reported to the local department or the hotline. [^va-car-1509-already-reported] Section 63.2-1509 has no anti-retaliation clause of its own; Virginia's general whistleblower statute, § 40.1-27.3, separately bars an employer from retaliating against an employee who in good faith reports a violation of any federal or state law or regulation to a supervisor, governmental body or law-enforcement official. [^va-car-1509-duty] [^va-car-40-1-27-3-retaliation]

## Can a Virginia private school employee tell the head of school about suspected abuse instead of reporting it to social services? {#report-through-head-of-school}

**Short answer.** A Virginia private school teacher or staff member who learns of suspected abuse in the course of professional services may, in place of a report, immediately notify the person in charge of the school or a designee, who must then make the report forthwith. [^va-car-1509-pic-route]

The person in charge who receives the notice must tell the staff member when the report is made to the local department or the hotline and the name of the individual who received it, and must forward any resulting communication, including information about actions taken. [^va-car-1509-pic-feedback] The administrator of every private or parochial school must post in each of its schools a notice that a teacher or other employee with reason to suspect abuse or neglect must report to local or state social services agencies or to the person in charge of the school or a designee, that reporters are immune absent bad faith or malicious purpose, and that gives the Department of Social Services' toll-free hotline. [^va-car-22-1-291-3-notice] The person-in-charge route and its feedback duty carry over into the version of § 63.2-1509 that takes effect July 1, 2027, with the report going to the Department's hotline. [^va-car-1509-2027-pic] [^va-car-ch865-effective-pic]

The statute does not say what a staff member who has notified the person in charge must do if no confirmation arrives within the 24-hour reporting window. [^va-car-1509-pic-feedback] [^va-car-1509-penalty-pic] Subsection G excuses a report only where the person has actual knowledge that the matter has already been reported to the local department or the hotline, so a staff member without that confirmation cannot rely on it, and a direct call to the hotline is the course that leaves no gap. [^va-car-1509-already-reported-pic]

Section 63.2-1509 names the local department and the Department's hotline as the places a report goes; it does not list the police. [^va-car-1509-duty-pic] In *Cupp v. Delta Air Lines, Inc.*, the Supreme Court of Virginia answered a certified question by holding that a person outside the mandated-reporter list who makes a good-faith complaint only to law enforcement has not made a complaint under § 63.2-1510 and so is not immune under § 63.2-1512. [^va-car-cupp-holding] [^va-car-63-2-1510] [^va-car-63-2-1512-current] The court added that its answer does not by itself make such a person liable; liability is decided by the ordinary process of adjudicating torts. [^va-car-cupp-not-liable] Section 63.2-1510 is repealed effective July 1, 2027 by the second enactment clauses of chapters 865 and 900, and the version of § 63.2-1512 effective that day immunizes a report under § 63.2-1509 but no longer names complaints under § 63.2-1510. [^va-car-63-2-1510] [^va-car-ch865-repeal] [^va-car-63-2-1512-2027] [^va-car-ch865-effective-pic]

## What happens when a Virginia private school employee is accused of abusing a student? {#allegations-against-staff}

**Short answer.** A suspicion that a Virginia private school employee abused a student is reported to social services like any other, and a valid report involving a caretaker at a private school must be investigated rather than handled as a family assessment. [^va-car-1509-duty-staff] [^va-car-1506-investigate]

A local department designated as a differential response agency may investigate any report, but § 63.2-1506(C) lists valid reports that must be investigated, including cases involving a caretaker at a private or public school. [^va-car-1506-investigate] A report is valid only if, among other elements, the alleged abuser is the child's parent or other caretaker, subject to narrower categories the section treats separately that do not concern school staff. [^va-car-1508-caretaker] The child protective services regulations define a caretaker to include anyone with an obligation, on any legally recognizable basis, to look after a child left in that person's care, and persons responsible by virtue of their positions of conferred authority. [^va-car-22vac40-705-10-caretaker] The investigating local department must decide within 45 days whether the report is founded or unfounded, extendable with written justification to 60 days, or to 90 days when the investigation is conducted with law enforcement and both agree. [^va-car-1505-45-days] These are the provisions in force before July 1, 2027; 2026 Va. Acts chapters 865 and 900 restructure child protective services intake from that date, beginning with reports going to the Department's hotline rather than to a local department, so a school's procedures should be rechecked against the versions then in effect. [^va-car-1509-2027-hotline-staff] [^va-car-ch865-effective-staff] The acts also direct the Department to phase in centralized intake between July 1, 2028 and July 1, 2030, and they let each local department keep taking complaints and determining their validity until July 1, 2028 or, if later, until the Commissioner phases it in. [^va-car-ch865-phase-in] [^va-car-ch865-local-intake] [^va-car-ch900-local-intake]

Several rules for school-employee investigations reach only public schools. Section 63.2-1511 governs complaints against a teacher, principal or other person employed by a local school board or in a school operated by the Commonwealth, and its gross negligence or willful misconduct standard for good-faith acts of supervision, care or discipline applies only to those employees. [^va-car-1511-public-scope] [^va-car-1511-gross-negligence] The investigation procedures in § 63.2-1516.1 are likewise limited to employees of a local school board or a state-operated school. [^va-car-1516-1-public-scope] A local department's duty under § 63.2-1505 to notify the relevant school board without delay of a founded complaint applies to an employee of a school division. [^va-car-1505-school-board-notice] A complaint against a private school employee therefore proceeds under the general investigation rules described above. An accredited private school must search the registry of founded complaints of child abuse and neglect before hiring for a position with direct contact with students, as described in the [question on the child abuse registry check](#registry-search). [^va-car-296-4-search-staff]

## Must a Virginia private school fingerprint new employees, and what may it ask applicants about their criminal records? {#criminal-history-checks}

**Short answer.** A Virginia private school accredited under § 22.1-19 must require every applicant who accepts employment to submit fingerprints for a state and FBI criminal history check, but the statute does not require an unaccredited private or religious school to comply. [^va-car-296-3-fingerprints] [^va-car-296-3-unaccredited] [^va-car-22-1-19-accreditation]

The requirement covers full-time, part-time, permanent and temporary employment, and the accreditation that triggers it includes accreditation by the Virginia Council for Private Education, which § 22.1-19 directs the Board of Education to authorize. [^va-car-296-3-fingerprints] [^va-car-22-1-19-accreditation] The Central Criminal Records Exchange reports only whether the applicant meets or does not meet the criteria for employment, based on whether the applicant has ever been convicted of a barrier crime, and it may report to a private organization coordinating records for the school under a written agreement with the Department of State Police. [^va-car-296-3-fingerprints] It does not disclose charges or convictions to the school, and an applicant denied employment who disputes the record may obtain the procedure for getting a copy from the FBI. [^va-car-296-3-no-disclosure] At a temporary teacher's written request, a school that employed the teacher may share the meets-or-does-not-meet result with another accredited private school that has hired the teacher, with immunity for transfers made in good faith and not the result of gross negligence or willful misconduct. [^va-car-296-3-no-disclosure] The school may disclose the results to the Council for accreditation purposes, and a school operating a regulated child day program accepts a qualifying child day background check in place of this one for individuals who must undergo that check. [^va-car-296-3-council-daycare]

The parallel public school rules do not reach private schools: § 22.1-296.1, which requires conviction certifications on applications and bars employing anyone convicted of a child sexual abuse offense, is addressed to school boards. [^va-car-296-1-school-boards]

A private school for students with disabilities licensed by the Board of Education under Chapter 16 of Title 22.1 has a separate licensing duty that does not depend on accreditation. [^va-car-22-1-319-school-definition] The Board's licensing regulations require fingerprint-based criminal history checks for every applicant who accepts employment and for regular volunteers and contractors, including student interns, who will be alone with a student. [^va-car-8vac20-671-210-fingerprints]

An employer or educational institution may not require an applicant to disclose an arrest, charge, conviction or civil offense that has been expunged, and a willful violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor. [^va-car-392-4-expunged] Separately, private employers not subject to federal laws or regulations in the hiring process, and educational institutions, may not require an applicant to disclose an arrest, charge or conviction that has been sealed, with exceptions that include a position for which the Code requires the employer to make that inquiry. [^va-car-392-15-sealed] [^va-car-392-15-exceptions] An application that asks about prior arrests, charges or convictions must tell the applicant that sealed information need not be disclosed, except for applications within the exceptions, and each willful violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor. [^va-car-392-15-notice-penalty] The text does not answer whether an accredited school's duty to require fingerprinting under § 22.1-296.3 is a case in which the Code requires the employer to make the inquiry: that section requires a fingerprint check and has the Exchange report only whether the applicant meets the barrier-crime criteria, without directing the school to ask applicants about their records, and § 22.1-296.3 itself imposes no duty on an unaccredited school. [^va-car-296-3-fingerprints] [^va-car-392-15-exceptions] Including the sealed-records notice on any application that asks about criminal history satisfies the notice rule whichever way that question is resolved. [^va-car-392-15-notice-penalty]

## Must a Virginia private school check the child abuse and neglect registry before hiring staff who will work with students? {#registry-search}

**Short answer.** A Virginia private school accredited under § 22.1-19 must require each applicant offered or accepting a job with direct contact with students to give written consent and the necessary personal information for a search of the Department of Social Services' registry of founded child abuse and neglect complaints. [^va-car-296-4-search] [^va-car-22-1-19-registry]

The search requirement applies to full-time, part-time, permanent and temporary positions requiring direct contact with students, and searches must be requested in conformance with the Board of Social Services' regulations. [^va-car-296-4-search] If the applicant has lived in another state within the last five years, the school must also require consent to obtain that state's information on founded complaints and must take reasonable steps to determine whether the applicant was the subject of one, and the applicant may be required to pay for the search. [^va-car-296-4-out-of-state] The Department must respond within 10 business days where there is no registry match and within 30 business days where there is a match. [^va-car-63-2-1515-response]

If the response shows that the applicant is the subject of a founded case of child abuse and neglect, the applicant must be denied employment or the employment rescinded. [^va-car-296-4-denial] An applicant denied employment because of the registry record receives a copy of the information, and the school must keep the Department's information confidential. [^va-car-296-4-confidential] Section 22.1-296.4 names school boards and the governing boards or administrators of private schools accredited under § 22.1-19; it does not name unaccredited private schools. [^va-car-296-4-search] [^va-car-22-1-19-registry] A private school for students with disabilities licensed by the Board of Education must separately obtain written consent for a registry search from each applicant who accepts employment requiring direct contact with students and from regular volunteers and contractors, including student interns, who will be alone with a student, under the Board's licensing regulations. [^va-car-8vac20-671-210-registry] [^va-car-22-1-319-school-definition-registry]

## May a Virginia private school help a departing employee suspected of sexual misconduct with a student find a new job? {#job-assistance-sexual-misconduct}

**Short answer.** A Virginia private school accredited under § 22.1-19 must adopt and implement policies barring its personnel from helping a colleague obtain a new job when they know or have probable cause to believe the colleague engaged in unlawful sexual misconduct regarding a minor or student. [^va-car-296-3-job-assistance] [^va-car-22-1-19-job-assistance]

The required policy covers any governing board member, administrator, employee, contractor or agent of the school, and the person being helped may be any of those as well. [^va-car-296-3-job-assistance] The prohibition turns on assistance in obtaining a new job, not on the form an agreement with the departing person takes. [^va-car-296-3-job-assistance] Section 22.1-296.3 does not require an unaccredited private or religious school to comply with it. [^va-car-296-3-unaccredited-jobs]

The public school counterparts do not reach private schools. Section 22.1-79.8 directs the Department of Education and local school boards to adopt policies implementing 20 U.S.C. § 7926 for local school boards and their employees, contractors and agents. [^va-car-22-1-79-8] Section 7926 itself is addressed to a State, a state educational agency, or a local educational agency that receives federal funds under that chapter, carves out the routine transmission of administrative and personnel files, and lifts the prohibition in listed circumstances after the information has been properly reported to law enforcement. [^us-va-car-7926-scope] [^us-va-car-7926-exception] Section 22.1-296.5's duty to answer an employment verification request within 10 working days applies between school boards. [^va-car-22-1-296-5]

The reporting duty runs to each staff member individually, so what the school decides about a departing employee does not change any staff member's obligation to report a reason to suspect abuse, as described in the [question on who must report](#mandated-reporting). [^va-car-1509-duty-jobs]

## Must a Virginia private school's code of student conduct prohibit bullying? {#anti-bullying-policy}

**Short answer.** The chief administrator of each Virginia private elementary or secondary school must include in its code of student conduct a prohibition against bullying, together with procedures for addressing bullying and cyberbullying. [^va-car-279-6-1-procedures] [^va-car-279-6-1-minimums]

At a minimum, the policies must require notice to the parent of any student involved in a confirmed incident of bullying within 24 hours of confirming it, prohibit and set procedures for cyberbullying between enrolled students, protect a student who is a victim of or witness to bullying or cyberbullying from being deterred from reporting or seeking support by fear of retaliation, and prohibit hazing. [^va-car-279-6-1-minimums] The policies must also be consistent with state, federal and case law, address training or informing school personnel on managing student conduct, and include policies to reduce bias and harassment in enforcing the code, and they may follow the Board of Education's model policies. [^va-car-279-6-1-procedures] The section is addressed to each private elementary or secondary school in the Commonwealth, accredited or not, and it does not prevent a school from adopting more comprehensive policies. [^va-car-279-6-1-procedures] [^va-car-279-6-1-minimums]

Section 22.1-279.6:1 was enacted by 2026 Va. Acts chapters 236 and 237, identical acts whose only enactment clause adds the section, and a law enacted at a regular session takes effect on the first day of July after the session adjourns unless the bill sets another date, so the section took effect July 1, 2026. [^va-car-ch236-bullying] [^va-car-ch237-bullying] [^va-car-const-art-4-13] Section 22.1-276.01 defines bullying for the purposes of Article 3 of Chapter 14, the article that contains § 22.1-279.6:1, as aggressive and unwanted behavior intended to harm, intimidate or humiliate, involving a real or perceived power imbalance, and repeated over time or causing severe emotional trauma, and it excludes ordinary teasing, horseplay, argument and peer conflict. [^va-car-276-01-bullying] [^va-car-279-6-1-article] The public school rule in § 22.1-279.6(D) is addressed to each school board and runs its 24-hour parent notice from the principal's learning of an allegation, rather than from confirmation of an incident. [^va-car-279-6-d-public]

## Does the clergy exception excuse a chaplain or minister at a Virginia religious school from reporting suspected child abuse? {#clergy-exception}

**Short answer.** Virginia's clergy exception is written into the subdivision that makes ministers, priests, rabbis, imams and accredited religious practitioners mandated reporters, and the separate subdivision covering any teacher or other person employed in a private school contains no such exception. [^va-car-1509-clergy] [^va-car-1509-duty-clergy]

Under subdivision A 19, a member of the clergy need not report when the information supporting the suspicion is required by the religious organization's doctrine to be kept confidential, or would be subject to § 8.01-400 or § 19.2-271.3 if offered as evidence in court. [^va-car-1509-clergy] Those two statutes protect information communicated in a confidential manner to a regular minister, priest, rabbi or accredited practitioner, properly entrusted in a professional capacity, by a person seeking spiritual counsel and advice, in civil discovery and in criminal testimony respectively. [^va-car-8-01-400] [^va-car-19-2-271-3] The exception attaches to that information, so a teacher, counselor or coach who learns the same facts independently remains under subdivision A 5 or A 16. [^va-car-1509-duty-clergy] [^va-car-1509-coaches-clergy]

Section 63.2-1509 does not say which subdivision governs a chaplain or minister who is also employed by the school, such as one who teaches religion or counsels students as a member of staff. [^va-car-1509-clergy] [^va-car-1509-duty-clergy] In our review of Virginia opinions citing § 63.2-1509 we found no decision resolving that overlap. Subdivision A 5's text covers any person employed in a private school without a confidentiality qualification, so treating information a chaplain learns in an employment role as reportable under subdivision A 5 is the reading that does not depend on the clergy exception. [^va-car-1509-duty-clergy]
