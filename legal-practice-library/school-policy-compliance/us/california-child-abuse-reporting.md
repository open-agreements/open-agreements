# School Policy Compliance in California: Child Abuse Reporting and Prevention[^about]

What a California private K–12 school's child-protection policies must reflect: who is a mandated reporter and where reports go, why internal procedures cannot replace the individual report, annual mandated reporter training and the signed acknowledgment, the professional-boundaries policy, prior-employer misconduct inquiries and the statewide data system, agreements that suppress misconduct reports, and Department of Justice fingerprinting.

## Who must report suspected child abuse at a California private school, and to whom? {#mandated-reporting}

**Short answer.** Every employee, governing board member and qualifying adult volunteer of a California private school is a mandated reporter who must report child abuse or neglect they know of or reasonably suspect in their work to a police or sheriff's department or the county welfare department. [^ca-pc-11165-7-school-reporters] [^ca-pc-11166-a-duty] [^ca-pc-11165-9-agencies]

Penal Code § 11165.7(a)(1) names the employees, volunteers and governing board or body members of a private school, and it counts as a volunteer any person over 18 who interacts with pupils outside the immediate supervision and control of the pupil's parent or guardian or a school employee. [^ca-pc-11165-7-school-reporters] Paragraph (a)(2) adds the employees, volunteers and board members of a public or private school contractor to a private school whose duties require contact with or supervision of pupils at that school. [^ca-pc-11165-7-contractors] Except as those and other listed paragraphs of § 11165.7(a) provide, volunteers who work with children are not mandated reporters, although the statute encourages them to obtain training and to report. [^ca-pc-11165-7-volunteers] A clergy member, meaning a priest, minister, rabbi, religious practitioner or similar functionary of a church, temple or recognized denomination or organization, is also a mandated reporter. [^ca-pc-11165-7-clergy]

The trigger is knowledge or reasonable suspicion, which exists when it is objectively reasonable, on facts that could cause a reasonable person in a like position to suspect abuse or neglect, and it requires neither certainty nor a specific medical indication. [^ca-pc-11166-a1-reasonable-suspicion] The reporter must make an initial telephone report immediately or as soon as is practicably possible and send a written follow-up report within 36 hours of receiving the information. [^ca-pc-11166-a-duty] The report goes to a police or sheriff's department, not including a school district police or security department, to a county probation department if the county has designated it, or to the county welfare department, and the agency must accept the report even if it lacks jurisdiction. [^ca-pc-11165-9-agencies]

For a religiously affiliated school, the clergy limit is narrow: a clergy member who learns of abuse during a penitential communication, such as a sacramental confession, is not subject to the reporting duty for that communication. [^ca-pc-11166-d-clergy] That limit does not affect a clergy member's duty when acting in some other capacity that would make the clergy member a mandated reporter, which includes serving as a private school employee under § 11165.7(a)(1). [^ca-pc-11166-d-clergy] [^ca-pc-11165-7-school-reporters]

A mandated reporter who fails to report known or reasonably suspected abuse or neglect commits a misdemeanor punishable by up to six months in county jail, a $1,000 fine or both, and if a reporter intentionally conceals a failure to report an incident the reporter knew to be abuse or severe neglect, the failure is a continuing offense until an agency discovers it. [^ca-pc-11166-c-penalty] A mandated reporter is not civilly or criminally liable for any report the Act requires or authorizes. [^ca-pc-11172-immunity]

## Can a California private school require staff to report suspected child abuse to the principal or head of school instead of calling the authorities? {#internal-reporting-procedures}

**Short answer.** A California private school may adopt internal procedures that keep supervisors and administrators informed of abuse reports, but those procedures may not impede a staff member's individual duty to report or direct staff to let a supervisor file or process the report. [^ca-pc-11166-i-individual]

The reporting duties under § 11166 are individual, no supervisor or administrator may impede or inhibit them, and no person making a report may be sanctioned for making it. [^ca-pc-11166-i-individual] Telling an employer, supervisor, school principal, school counselor, coworker or anyone else is not a substitute for the report to an agency listed in § 11165.9, and internal procedures may not require the reporter to disclose the reporter's identity to the employer. [^ca-pc-11166-i-identity-substitute]

When two or more mandated reporters jointly know of a suspected case and agree among themselves, one member selected by mutual agreement may make the telephone report and a single signed report, but any member who knows the designated member failed to report must then report. [^ca-pc-11166-h-team] Section 11166(h) rests on agreement among reporters who jointly know of the abuse, while § 11166(i)(1) bars an internal policy from directing an employee to allow a supervisor to file or process a mandated report under any circumstances. [^ca-pc-11166-h-team] [^ca-pc-11166-i-individual] The statute allows internal procedures that facilitate reporting and apprise supervisors and administrators of reports, so long as they do not require the reporter to disclose the reporter's identity to the employer; a procedure that asks staff to tell the head of school after making the report therefore has to leave room for a reporter who prefers to stay anonymous. [^ca-pc-11166-i-identity-substitute] [^ca-pc-11166-i-individual]

## What child abuse reporting training and acknowledgment must California private school employees and volunteers complete? {#mandated-reporter-training}

**Short answer.** Since July 1, 2026, a California private school must give its employees, volunteers and others working on its behalf who are mandated reporters annual training on mandated reporting and on preventing abuse of children in school settings. [^ca-edc-44691-c-training] [^ca-edc-44691-h-operative]

Penal Code § 11165.7(d) sets the same duty for private schools commencing July 1, 2026, by reference to Education Code § 44691, and the training must cover child abuse and neglect identification and reporting. [^ca-pc-11165-7-d-training] The mandated-reporting training must tell staff that failing to report known or reasonably suspected abuse or neglect is a misdemeanor punishable by up to six months in county jail, a $1,000 fine or both, and the prevention training covers abuse on school grounds, by school personnel and volunteers, or in school-sponsored programs. [^ca-edc-44691-c-training] Staff hired during the school year receive both trainings, and volunteers receive them when they begin volunteering. [^ca-edc-44691-c-training]

A school may use the State Department of Social Services' online training module or an equivalent module developed to meet § 44691, approved by the school's public entity risk pool joint powers authority or liability insurance provider, and reported to the State Department of Education. [^ca-edc-44691-d-alternative] From January 1, 2027, SB 1083 (Stats. 2026, ch. 676) amends § 44691(d) so that a private school's module no longer needs a risk pool's or liability insurer's approval: "For private schools, the alternative training module shall be approved by the private school’s governing authority."[^ca-sb-1083-44691-governing-authority] [^ca-sb-1083-44691-governing-authority] The school must have a process for each trainee to show proof of completion within the first six weeks of each school year, of employment or of volunteer service, which may be a sign-in sheet or a certificate of completion submitted to the governing board or body. [^ca-edc-44691-c-proof] A person who completed the Department of Social Services training and received a certificate within the previous 12 months satisfies the requirement. [^ca-edc-44691-e-f-certificate] The absence of training does not excuse a mandated reporter from the reporting duty unless a statute specifically provides otherwise. [^ca-pc-11165-7-f-no-excuse]

Separately, before starting employment and as a condition of it, each mandated reporter must sign a statement on a form the employer provides, confirming knowledge of § 11166 and a commitment to comply, informing the employee of the reporting obligations and of the confidentiality rights under § 11167(d), and the employer must give the employee copies of §§ 11165.7, 11166 and 11167. [^ca-pc-11166-5-statement] The employer retains the signed statements and bears their cost. [^ca-pc-11166-5-retention] Section 11165.7(c)(1) requires employers to provide that statement whether or not they also provide training. [^ca-pc-11165-7-c-statement]

## Must a California private school adopt a written policy on professional boundaries and electronic communication between staff and students? {#professional-boundaries-policy}

**Short answer.** California's Education Code required the governing body of each private school, by July 1, 2026, to adopt written policies that explicitly address professional boundaries and set limits on staff, volunteer and contractor communication with pupils that excludes the pupil's parent or guardian. [^ca-edc-32100-b-policies]

The boundaries policy must address boundaries between pupils and school employees, adult volunteers and school contractors, among pupils, and among the adults who work, volunteer or contract at the school. [^ca-edc-32100-b-policies] The communication limits cover contact during or outside the school day through social media platforms, text messaging and other forms of communication that do not include the pupil's parent or guardian, and the policy may vary the limits by the pupil's age or grade. [^ca-edc-32100-b-policies] The governing body must also adopt written policies, plans or specifications on school facilities and furnishings so that classroom and nonclassroom spaces are easily supervised. [^ca-edc-32100-b-policies] The statute states the Legislature's intent that private schools, like public schools, be free of violent crimes and sexual offenses by pupils, school employees, volunteers and contractors. [^ca-edc-32100-a-intent] Section 32100(c), which encourages work with a risk pool or insurer on prevention best practices, names only school districts, county offices of education and charter schools. [^ca-edc-32100-c-public]

The Superintendent of Public Instruction was required, by July 1, 2026, to develop and disseminate to private schools resources on appropriate adult-to-pupil and pupil-to-pupil boundaries, indicators of inappropriate behavior and options to report abuse. [^ca-edc-51950-c-resources] A private school may, but need not, provide annual abuse-prevention instruction to all enrolled pupils using those materials, and a parent or guardian may excuse a child from it. [^ca-edc-51950-d-instruction] The annual staff training on preventing abuse by school personnel is addressed in the [question on mandated reporter training](#mandated-reporter-training).

## What must a California private school ask an applicant's former school employers about egregious misconduct before hiring? {#employment-history-review}

**Short answer.** A California private school considering an applicant for any position must obtain the applicant's list of prior school employers and ask each one whether the applicant was the subject of credible complaints of, substantiated investigations into, or discipline for egregious misconduct. [^ca-edc-44051-a] [^ca-edc-44939-5-c]

The applicant must list every school district, county office of education, charter school, state special school or diagnostic center and private school that previously employed the applicant. [^ca-edc-44051-a] [^ca-edc-44939-5-c] Section 44051 applies to any position at a private school, certificated or not, and asks about credible complaints, substantiated investigations or discipline for egregious misconduct that were used to support a substantiated investigation; § 44939.5 adds a parallel inquiry for certificated positions that covers matters required to be reported to the Commission on Teacher Credentialing. [^ca-edc-44939-5-c] [^ca-edc-44051-a] Egregious misconduct means immoral conduct that is the basis for an offense listed in Education Code §§ 44010 or 44011 or in Penal Code §§ 11165.2 to 11165.6, the child abuse and neglect definitions. [^ca-edc-44932-egregious] A school that has reported an employee's egregious misconduct to the Commission must, on inquiry and notwithstanding any other law, disclose that fact to a school considering the employee and give it a copy of the relevant information in its possession. [^ca-edc-44051-a3]

Education Code § 44052 directs the Commission, on or before July 1, 2027 and contingent on an appropriation, to build a statewide data system covering every person in any position at a private school. [^ca-edc-44052-a-system] Once it exists, the private school organization responsible for hiring must review the system before hiring anyone for any position to see whether an investigation produced a substantiated report. [^ca-edc-44052-b-review] The school must also report each hire within 30 calendar days and each departure within 10 calendar days. [^ca-edc-44052-c-e-hires] Within 10 calendar days of starting and of completing an egregious misconduct investigation, the school must notify the system, which records only substantiated reports and not unfounded or inconclusive ones, and the school must report an employee's departure in the middle of an investigation. [^ca-edc-44052-f-investigations]

Section 44052(h) defines egregious misconduct and local educational agency, which it limits to public entities, but does not define private school. [^ca-edc-44052-h-definitions]

SB 1083 (Stats. 2026, ch. 676), approved September 27, 2026, recasts §§ 44051 and 44052 and adds new duties; it has no urgency clause, so its amendments take effect on January 1, 2027, subject to the later deadlines below. [^ca-sb-1083-44051-inquiry] For §§ 44051 to 44053 it defines a private school as a privately owned or operated school that enrolls six or more pupils, has filed the affidavit required by Education Code § 33190 and is not a charter school. [^ca-sb-1083-private-school-defined] As amended, § 44051 requires a private school considering an applicant for any position to ask each disclosed prior school employer whether the applicant was the subject of "any credible complaints of, investigations into, or discipline for, egregious misconduct"[^ca-sb-1083-44051-inquiry], without the current limit to matters used to support a substantiated investigation. [^ca-sb-1083-44051-inquiry] A responding school must disclose whether it submitted a report of the applicant's egregious misconduct to the statewide data system, provide the relevant information or evidence that supported a substantiated report and other employment history, and preserve the evidence of a substantiated report. [^ca-sb-1083-44051-disclosure] Each private school must request access to the data system by May 1, 2027, or, if established later, within 30 days of being established and before any hiring, and must give the Commission a current email address for the system's notices. [^ca-sb-1083-44052-access]

New § 44053 requires a private school that receives "any credible complaint or other reason to believe that an employee has engaged in egregious misconduct"[^ca-sb-1083-44053-investigations] to investigate; by July 1, 2027, to adopt a written employee investigation policy setting out the investigation steps, reasonable timelines for starting and concluding investigations, and the rights and responsibilities of employer and employee; and to complete each investigation even if the employee leaves, submitting any substantiated report to the data system. [^ca-sb-1083-44053-investigations] New § 45125.15 requires a private school, on or before July 1, 2027, when contracting for the services to pupils described in § 45125.1(j), to include and enforce the contractor fingerprinting requirements of § 33192, to obtain from the contractor the names of its employees previously employed by a California school and check them against the data system, and to require the contractor to disclose any credible complaint of, or discipline for, reportable conduct by the employees who will provide the services. [^ca-sb-1083-45125-15-contracts]

## Can a California private school agree to keep an employee's abuse or misconduct allegations confidential when the employee resigns or is let go? {#agreements-suppressing-abuse-information}

**Short answer.** A California private school may not agree to prevent a mandatory report of egregious misconduct to the Commission on Teacher Credentialing or another state or federal agency, or to expunge credible complaints, substantiated investigations or discipline for egregious misconduct from an employee's personnel file. [^ca-edc-44939-5-a] [^ca-edc-44939-5-b]

The expungement bar has one exception: a school may remove, or agree to remove, documents containing allegations that were the subject of a hearing before an arbitrator, school board, personnel commission, Commission on Professional Competence or administrative law judge in which the employee prevailed, the allegations were found false, not credible or unsubstantiated, or the discipline was found unwarranted. [^ca-edc-44939-5-b] Egregious misconduct covers sex offenses, controlled substance offenses and child abuse and neglect, as described in the [question on prior-employer inquiries](#employment-history-review). [^ca-edc-44932-egregious-agreements]

A school that has reported the employee's egregious misconduct to the Commission must, notwithstanding any other law, disclose that fact and provide the relevant information when another school considering the employee asks, so a confidentiality or neutral-reference promise does not displace that duty. [^ca-edc-44051-a3-agreements] Each staff member's duty to report suspected abuse is also individual, and no supervisor or administrator may impede it, whatever the school has agreed with a departing employee. [^ca-pc-11166-i-agreements]

In *Randi W. v. Muroc Joint Unified School District*, the California Supreme Court held that school district officials' letters of recommendation giving unreserved praise to a former administrator, despite their alleged knowledge of complaints of his sexual misconduct with students, were misleading statements that could support liability for fraud or negligent misrepresentation. [^ca-randi-w-misleading] The court held that the writer of a letter of recommendation owes third persons a duty not to misrepresent a former employee's qualifications and character when the misrepresentation would present a substantial, foreseeable risk of physical injury to them. [^ca-randi-w-duty]

For childhood sexual assault occurring on or after January 1, 2024, there is no time limit to sue a person or entity that owed the plaintiff a duty of care and whose wrongful or negligent act was a legal cause of the assault, while earlier assaults remain under the limitations law as it read on December 31, 2023. [^ca-ccp-340-1-a] [^ca-ccp-340-1-p] In an action under that section, a plaintiff who proves the assault resulted from a cover up may recover up to treble damages against a defendant found to have covered it up, unless another law prohibits it, and a cover up is a concerted effort to hide evidence relating to childhood sexual assault. [^ca-ccp-340-1-b] From January 1, 2027, SB 1083 (Stats. 2026, ch. 676) adds to § 44051 that information and evidence relating to a substantiated report must be preserved by the school where the report was filed and provided on inquiry "regardless of any proposed or actual agreement, settlement, or stipulation not to provide that evidence"[^ca-sb-1083-44051-preserve-agreements]. [^ca-sb-1083-44051-preserve-agreements]

## Must a California private school fingerprint employees through the Department of Justice, and when may it ask about criminal history? {#criminal-history-checks}

**Short answer.** A California private school must require each applicant for a paid position involving contact with minor pupils to submit fingerprints to the Department of Justice, and it may not employ the applicant until the Department completes its state criminal history check. [^ca-edc-44237-a] [^ca-edc-44237-b] [^ca-edc-44237-d-e]

Section 44237 applies to every person or entity offering or conducting private school instruction at the elementary or high school level, and it defines employment as engaging a person who will have contact with pupils to work on a regular paid full-time or part-time basis, or a paid seasonal basis. [^ca-edc-44237-b] It does not apply to a secondary school pupil working at the pupil's own school or to a parent or guardian working exclusively with the parent's own children. [^ca-edc-44237-b] Because the definition is limited to paid positions, the section's text does not reach unpaid volunteers. [^ca-edc-44237-b]

Under § 44237 as published on January 1, 2026, the Department of Justice reviews the FBI summary and tells the school only whether the applicant has convictions, or arrests pending final adjudication, for any sex offense, controlled substance offense, crime of violence, or serious or violent felony. [^ca-edc-44237-c3] The school may not employ a person convicted of a violent or serious felony, or a person a public school district could not employ because of a conviction, and such a person may not own or operate a private school. [^ca-edc-44237-d-e] A certificate of rehabilitation and pardon lifts the bar, and for a serious felony that is not also violent, so does proof to the sentencing court by clear and convincing evidence of at least one year's rehabilitation for school employment. [^ca-edc-44237-h-j] The school must request subsequent arrest service from the Department, and it must keep the Department's information confidential, stored in a locked file accessible only to the custodian of records and destroyed after the hiring decision. [^ca-edc-44237-f] [^ca-edc-44237-n]

AB 2796 (Stats. 2026, ch. 313), an urgency statute that took effect when it was approved on September 18, 2026, amended § 44237 to require each such applicant to undergo a fingerprint-based state and national criminal history background check, with the school submitting fingerprint images to the Department of Justice under Penal Code § 11105(u), and replaced subdivision (c) with a single sentence: "The Department of Justice shall provide a state and federal response pursuant to subdivision (p) of Section 11105 of the Penal Code."[^ca-ab-2796-44237] [^ca-ab-2796-44237] [^ca-ab-2796-urgency] The same act provides that a statutory requirement to conduct a federal criminal history check does not apply while the FBI has not authorized the entity to conduct one, that the entity may decide on the state check in the meantime, and that once authorized it must have applicants and employees who did not undergo the federal check resubmit their fingerprints. [^ca-ab-2796-11105-003] SB 685 (Stats. 2026, ch. 975), approved later, on September 30, 2026, and taking effect January 1, 2027, also amends § 44237, but its text keeps the earlier two-sets-of-fingerprints wording of subdivision (a) and the earlier subdivision (c), including the limited notice described above. [^ca-sb-685-44237] AB 2796's provision combining the two amendments operates only if AB 2796 was enacted after SB 685, [^ca-ab-2796-sec-30] so a school should confirm with the Department of Justice which form of the procedure applies from January 1, 2027, while it keeps fingerprinting each covered applicant and does not employ the applicant until the Department completes its check of the state criminal history file, which subdivision (d) requires in each version. [^ca-edc-44237-d-e] [^ca-ab-2796-44237] [^ca-sb-685-44237]

California's Fair Chance Act makes it unlawful for an employer with five or more employees to ask about conviction history on an application, or to inquire into or consider it, before making a conditional offer of employment. [^ca-gov-12952-a] The Act does not apply to a position where an employer is required by state, federal or local law to conduct criminal background checks or to restrict employment based on criminal history, which describes a private school position covered by § 44237. [^ca-gov-12952-d] [^ca-edc-44237-a] For a position outside § 44237, such as one without pupil contact, an employer the Act covers that intends to deny the position because of conviction history must make an individualized assessment of the offense, the time elapsed and the job, give written notice and allow the applicant at least five business days to respond. [^ca-gov-12952-c] The Fair Chance Act, Government Code § 12952, sits in Article 1, Unlawful Practices, Generally, of Chapter 6 of Part 2.8 of the Government Code, the part that holds the Fair Employment and Housing Act and runs from § 12900 to § 12999, [^ca-gov-12952-part-2-8] and FEHA's definitions section provides that, as used in that part, employer does not include a religious association or corporation not organized for private profit. [^ca-gov-12926-d-religious]
