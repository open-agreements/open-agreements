# School Policy Compliance in Texas: Child Abuse Reporting and Prevention[^about]

What a Texas private K–12 school's child-protection policies must reflect: the duty to report child abuse within 24 hours or immediately, Department of Family and Protective Services investigations of school staff, the chief administrative officer's 48-hour misconduct report, the do-not-hire registry, separation agreements and references, criminal history checks, the educator-student relationship offense and the bullying statute's reach.

## Who must report suspected child abuse at a Texas private school, to whom, and how fast? {#mandated-reporting}

**Short answer.** Every person in Texas with reasonable cause to believe a child's health or welfare has been adversely affected by abuse or neglect must report immediately, and a professional must report within 24 hours of first having reasonable cause. [^tx-fam-261-101-a-duty] [^tx-fam-261-101-b-professional]

The general duty in § 261.101(a) applies to any person, so it reaches every private school employee and volunteer whatever the person's title or certification. [^tx-fam-261-101-a-duty] A professional's report is due not later than the 24th hour after the hour the professional first has reasonable cause to believe the child has been or may be abused or neglected, and a professional may not delegate to or rely on another person to make the report. [^tx-fam-261-101-b-professional] Senate Bill 571 (2025) shortened that deadline from the 48th hour to the 24th hour, according to the Senate Research Center's analysis of the enrolled bill. [^tx-sb571-analysis-24-hours]

Section 261.101(b) defines a professional as a person licensed or certified by the state, or an employee of a facility licensed, certified or operated by the state, who has direct contact with children in the normal course of official duties or duties for which a license or certification is required, and it says the term includes teachers and nurses. [^tx-fam-261-101-b-professional] The definition does not say whether a private school teacher who holds no state certificate, at a school the state does not license, is a professional, so the 24-hour clock and the bar on delegation clearly reach certified teachers, licensed nurses and other licensed staff but are uncertain for uncertified staff. [^tx-fam-261-101-b-professional] The Texas Education Agency describes the duty as applying to each school employee individually and states that it cannot be satisfied by internal reporting alone. [^tx-tea-duty-to-protect] Because the general duty already requires an immediate report from everyone, a school policy consistent with both subsections requires each staff member to report personally and immediately, treating the 24-hour limit as an outer bound for professionals rather than a grace period. [^tx-fam-261-101-a-duty] [^tx-fam-261-101-b-professional]

A report may go to a law enforcement agency or the Department of Family and Protective Services, but, other than a report to a state agency that operates, licenses, certifies or registers the facility, it must go to the Department when the suspected abuse involves a person responsible for the child's care, custody or welfare. [^tx-fam-261-103-recipient] That term includes school personnel and volunteers at the child's school, so a report about a staff member or volunteer goes to the Department. [^tx-fam-261-001-school-personnel] For chapter 261, a law enforcement agency means the Department of Public Safety, a city police department, a county sheriff's office or a county constable's office, a list that does not include the peace officers a private school with at least 1,500 students may commission under Education Code § 37.0818. [^tx-fam-261-001-law-enforcement] [^tx-educ-37-0818-private-peace-officers] The Department takes reports through the Texas Abuse Hotline at 1-800-252-5400 or online, does not accept email reports, and does not accept anonymous reports of child abuse and neglect. [^tx-dfps-report-abuse]

A person required to report under § 261.101(a) who knowingly fails to do so commits a Class A misdemeanor, and a professional's knowing failure is a Class A misdemeanor or, if the professional intended to conceal the abuse or neglect, a state jail felony. [^tx-fam-261-109-penalty] A person who reports in good faith is immune from civil and criminal liability, but the immunity does not cover a person who reports the person's own abuse or neglect of a child or who acts in bad faith or with malicious purpose. [^tx-fam-261-106-immunity] An employer may not suspend, terminate or take other adverse employment action against a professional who in good faith reports abuse to a supervisor, a facility administrator, a state regulatory agency or a law enforcement agency, or who cooperates with a government investigation. [^tx-fam-261-110-retaliation] That protection does not extend to a person who reports, or cooperates in an investigation of, the person's own abuse or neglect of a child. [^tx-fam-261-110-own-conduct] A professional who prevails may recover actual damages, exemplary damages against a private employer, attorney's fees and reinstatement, and adverse action before the 61st day after a good-faith report is rebuttably presumed to be retaliation. [^tx-fam-261-110-remedies] [^tx-fam-261-110-presumption]

The Education Code's child abuse reporting policy and its sexual abuse and maltreatment policy are addressed to school districts and open-enrollment charter schools, not private schools. [^tx-educ-38-004-public-policy] [^tx-educ-38-0041-public-policy]

## What happens when a Texas private school employee is accused of abusing a student? {#allegations-against-staff}

**Short answer.** The Department of Family and Protective Services investigates a report of suspected abuse of a child in a Texas private school, and the school's chief administrative officer must separately report evidence of certain employee misconduct to the State Board for Educator Certification within 48 hours. [^tx-fam-261-406-investigation] [^tx-educ-22a-301-private-report]

When the Department determines that the abuse or neglect involves an employee of a private elementary or secondary school, it notifies the private school's chief executive officer about the investigation in writing, which may be done by email to a publicly available official address. [^tx-fam-261-105-notice] The Department sends the completed investigation report to a private school's chief executive officer, the report is edited to protect the identity of the person who reported unless the Texas Education Agency or the State Board for Educator Certification requests that identity, and a law enforcement agency may conduct its own investigation. [^tx-fam-261-406-investigation]

The chief administrative officer's separate report to the State Board for Educator Certification is required, in addition to the Family Code report, whenever the officer becomes aware of evidence that a person employed by or seeking employment in the private school engaged in the misconduct described in Education Code § 22A.051(a)(2)(A) through (D). [^tx-educ-22a-301-private-report] That misconduct is abusing or otherwise committing an unlawful act with a student or minor, including physical mistreatment or a threat of violence that the Penal Code does not justify, being involved in or soliciting a romantic relationship or sexual contact with a student or minor, inappropriate communications with a student or minor, and failing to maintain appropriate boundaries with a student or minor, the last two as defined by board rule. [^tx-educ-22a-051-misconduct-list] If there is evidence that the employee may have engaged in that misconduct and the employee resigns before the investigation is complete, the officer must submit the evidence collected; the report is due within 48 hours of the officer becoming aware of the evidence, in writing, on the board's form and through the Texas Education Agency's portal, and it must include the name of the student or minor victim, which is not public information. [^tx-educ-22a-301-private-report] Anyone else who knows or has reason to believe that a private school employee or applicant engaged in that misconduct may also report, and a person who reports in good faith, or who communicates in good faith with a private school administrator about an alleged incident, is immune from civil and criminal liability. [^tx-educ-22a-301-private-report] Senate Bill 571 replaced the earlier deadline, the seventh day after the officer knew of a criminal record or of a termination following misconduct, with the 48-hour deadline running from awareness of evidence. [^tx-sb571-analysis-private-48-hours]

A person employed by or seeking employment in a private school who holds no educator certificate or permit and is the subject of such a report is entitled to notice and a hearing before the Commissioner of Education, and if the person does not request a hearing and the Commissioner determines the misconduct occurred, the person is added to the registry of persons not eligible for public school employment. [^tx-educ-22a-101-private-hearing] [^tx-educ-22a-101-registry-placement] The Texas Education Agency describes this as new authority to investigate certain allegations involving non-certified individuals. [^tx-tea-duty-noncertified] For a school in the education savings account program, the Comptroller must also report evidence of the same misconduct by a person employed by or contracting with the school to serve a participating child, [^tx-educ-22a-302-esa-reporting] and must require participating providers, as a condition of participation, to supply the information it needs to do so. [^tx-educ-22a-302-esa-provider]

The principal-to-superintendent and superintendent-to-board reporting duties and the parent-notice policy in chapter 22A apply to an educational entity, which the chapter defines as a school district, district of innovation, open-enrollment charter school, other charter entity, regional education service center or shared services arrangement, not a private school. [^tx-educ-22a-001-educational-entity] [^tx-educ-22a-053-public-parent-notice] What a school may say publicly about an employee accused of a sexual offense against a student is addressed in the [question on educator-student relationships](#educator-student-relationships).

## What abuse and misconduct history can a Texas private school check before hiring staff who work with students? {#registry-and-history-checks}

**Short answer.** Texas gives private schools access to the Texas Education Agency's registry of persons not eligible to work for public school entities, but the statute's duty to refuse to hire a listed person binds only those public entities. [^tx-educ-22a-151-private-access] [^tx-educ-22a-151-registry] [^tx-educ-22a-001-educational-entity-registry]

Section 22A.151 requires an educational entity to discharge or refuse to hire a person listed on the registry and requires the agency to give private schools equivalent access to it. [^tx-educ-22a-151-registry] [^tx-educ-22a-151-private-access] An educational entity is a school district, district of innovation, open-enrollment charter school, other charter entity, regional education service center or shared services arrangement, so the hiring bar does not by its terms reach a private school. [^tx-educ-22a-001-educational-entity-registry] The registry lists, among others, persons whose educator certificate was denied or revoked, persons the Commissioner determined engaged in abuse, romantic or sexual involvement, inappropriate communications or boundary violations with a student or minor, and persons temporarily placed on it. [^tx-educ-22a-151-listed-persons] Those Commissioner determinations include uncertified private school employees reported by their school's chief administrative officer, as described in the [question on allegations against staff](#allegations-against-staff). [^tx-educ-22a-101-private-hearing-registry]

Chapter 22A's pre-employment affidavit, in which an applicant discloses past abuse or misconduct investigations, registry listing and any resignation in lieu of termination from a public or private school, is required of a person applying to work for an educational entity. [^tx-educ-22a-055-affidavit] Because a private school is not an educational entity, the statute does not require the affidavit of a private school's applicants. [^tx-educ-22a-001-educational-entity-registry] [^tx-educ-22a-055-affidavit]

A private school that applies for approval as an education service provider in the education savings account program must give the Comptroller documentation that each employee who will interact with a participating child is not identified as having engaged in the listed misconduct through the interagency reportable conduct search engine. [^tx-educ-29-358-esa-search] The statute still describes that misconduct by reference to former § 22.093(c)(1)(A) or (B), a section Senate Bill 571 (2025) transferred into chapter 22A without a conforming change to the cross-reference. [^tx-educ-29-358-esa-search] [^tx-educ-22a-052-transfer] At the request of a private school at which a teacher or administrator has applied for employment, the school district or open-enrollment charter school employing that person must give the private school a document evaluating the person's performance. [^tx-educ-21-355-evaluations] What a former employer may say in a reference is addressed in the [question on separation agreements and references](#agreements-suppressing-abuse-information).

## Can a Texas private school agree to keep an abuse or misconduct allegation confidential when an employee resigns or is let go? {#agreements-suppressing-abuse-information}

**Short answer.** When there is evidence that a Texas private school employee may have abused or engaged in sexual or boundary misconduct with a student or minor, the chief administrative officer must submit the evidence to the State Board for Educator Certification even if the employee resigns first. [^tx-educ-22a-301-resignation]

Section 22A.301 places that duty on the officer, and section 261.101 places the duty to report abuse on each person with reasonable cause to believe it occurred, with a professional barred from relying on anyone else to make the report; neither section makes an exception for a resignation, severance or confidentiality agreement. [^tx-educ-22a-301-resignation] [^tx-fam-261-101-agreements] Chapter 22A of the Education Code, chapter 261 of the Family Code and chapter 103 of the Labor Code, as reviewed for this page, contain no provision expressly voiding a confidentiality term in a private school's separation agreement, but the reporting duties apply by their own terms whatever such a term says. [^tx-educ-22a-301-resignation] [^tx-fam-261-101-agreements]

A certified educator who helps another person obtain employment at a school district, private school or open-enrollment charter school, other than by routinely transmitting administrative and personnel files, knowing that the person previously engaged in sexual misconduct with a minor or student in violation of the law, risks suspension or revocation of the educator's certificate. [^tx-educ-21-0581-assisting]

A private school's chief administrative officer, and anyone else, who in good faith communicates with an administrator of a private school about an alleged incident of misconduct by a person employed by or seeking employment in a private school is immune from civil and criminal liability. [^tx-educ-22a-301-immunity] More generally, an employer may disclose information about a current or former employee's job performance to a prospective employer on request, and it is immune from civil liability for that disclosure unless clear and convincing evidence shows that it knew the information was false or acted with malice or reckless disregard for the truth. [^tx-lab-103-003-disclosure] [^tx-lab-103-004-immunity] The same chapter does not require an employer to give a reference. [^tx-lab-103-005-no-reference]

## Must a Texas private school run criminal background checks on staff and volunteers? {#criminal-history-checks}

**Short answer.** Texas Education Code § 22.083(b) permits, rather than requires, a private school to obtain criminal history record information on a person it intends to employ in any capacity. [^tx-educ-22-083-private-may]

The same subsection lets the school obtain that information on a contractor's employee or applicant who has or will have continuing duties related to the contracted services and direct contact with students, and § 22.0835(b) lets it obtain criminal history on volunteers and volunteer applicants. [^tx-educ-22-083-private-may] [^tx-educ-22-0835-volunteers] For chapter 22's criminal history subchapter, a private school is a school offering instruction in any grade from prekindergarten through grade 12 that is not operated by a governmental entity. [^tx-educ-22-081-private-school] A qualified private school, one that is accredited by an organization recognized by the Texas Education Agency or the Texas Private School Accreditation Commission and that the Department of Public Safety has found eligible under the National Child Protection Act of 1993, may obtain state criminal history from the Department and FBI records under Government Code § 411.0972. [^tx-gov-411-0972-qualified] Under Education Code § 22.08361, a qualified private school may require an employee or applicant to submit to a fingerprint-based national criminal history review before employment, including by submitting fingerprints and photographs. [^tx-educ-22-08361-national] Criminal history a qualified private school obtains may be released only to its subject, and the school must destroy it after use. [^tx-gov-411-0972-qualified]

Within the same subchapter, a check is required when a private school contracts for student transportation: except as subsections (c) and (d) provide for commercial transportation companies, the school must obtain criminal history on the contractor's bus drivers, and a driver convicted of a felony or a misdemeanor involving moral turpitude may not drive students without the permission of the private school's chief executive officer. [^tx-educ-22-084-bus] The director of a private school must promptly notify the State Board for Educator Certification in writing upon obtaining information, by a means other than the Department of Public Safety's clearinghouse, that an applicant for or holder of a Texas educator certificate has a reported criminal history. [^tx-educ-22-087-notify]

The national fingerprint review that § 22.0833 requires of noncertified employees applies to persons offered employment by a school district, open-enrollment charter school or shared services arrangement, not by a private school. [^tx-educ-22-0833-public] Under Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 142.002, an employer cannot be sued solely for negligent hiring or supervision based on an employee's conviction, unless it knew or should have known of the conviction and the offense falls within one of the statute's exceptions, which include sexually violent offenses and offenses committed in substantially similar duties or conditions. [^tx-cprc-142-002-hiring]

## Does Texas's improper educator-student relationship offense apply to private school employees? {#educator-student-relationships}

**Short answer.** Texas Penal Code § 21.12 makes it a second-degree felony for an employee of a public or private primary or secondary school to engage in sexual contact, sexual intercourse or deviate sexual intercourse with a person enrolled at the school where the employee works. [^tx-penal-21-12-offense]

The offense also reaches an employee in a position requiring educator certification, whether or not the employee holds it, who engages in that conduct with a person the employee knows is enrolled at another public or private school or is a student participant in an educational activity sponsored by a school district or school, if enrolled students are the activity's primary participants. [^tx-penal-21-12-offense] It also covers online solicitation conduct with such a person, regardless of the person's age. [^tx-penal-21-12-offense] It is an affirmative defense that the employee was the student's spouse, or was not more than three years older and in a relationship with the student that began before the employment. [^tx-penal-21-12-defenses] Conduct constituting the offense is sexual conduct within the Family Code's definition of abuse, so it triggers the reporting duties in the [question on who must report](#mandated-reporting). [^tx-fam-261-001-abuse-21-12]

A public or private school, or the person or entity that operates it, may not release externally to the general public the name of an employee accused of this offense until the employee is indicted, except as necessary to report the accusation to the Texas Education Agency, another state agency or local law enforcement, to report it to the school's members or community in accordance with the school's policies or with the religious law it observes, or to investigate it. [^tx-penal-21-12-name-release] The name of the enrolled student involved may not be released to the public and is not public information. [^tx-penal-21-12-name-release]

Inappropriate communications and failures to maintain appropriate boundaries with a student or minor, as defined by board rule, are misconduct a private school's chief administrative officer must report under Education Code § 22A.301. [^tx-educ-22a-051-communications] [^tx-educ-22a-301-communications] The written electronic-communications policy required by Education Code § 38.027 is addressed to a school district. [^tx-educ-38-027-public]

## Must a Texas private school adopt a student anti-bullying policy? {#anti-bullying-policy}

**Short answer.** Texas Education Code § 37.0832 directs the board of trustees of each school district to adopt a bullying policy and does not name private schools. [^tx-educ-37-0832-district-policy]

A district's policy must, among other things, prohibit bullying, prohibit retaliation against a person who reports bullying in good faith, provide for notice to the alleged victim's parent or guardian by the third business day after a report, establish procedures for reporting, including anonymous reporting, and for investigating incidents, and set out counseling options. [^tx-educ-37-0832-district-policy] The statute defines bullying as a single significant act or a pattern of acts by one or more students that exploits an imbalance of power and has one of the listed effects, and it includes cyberbullying. [^tx-educ-37-0832-definition] It reaches cyberbullying off school property when the conduct interferes with a student's educational opportunities or substantially disrupts the orderly operation of a classroom, school or school-sponsored activity. [^tx-educ-37-0832-applicability] The policy must appear annually in the district's student and employee handbooks. [^tx-educ-37-0832-handbooks]

Bullying by an adult employee of a student is a separate matter: the misconduct a private school's chief administrative officer must report includes physical mistreatment of, or a threat of violence to, a student or minor, as described in the [question on allegations against staff](#allegations-against-staff). [^tx-educ-22a-051-bullying-adult]
