# School Policy Compliance in the District of Columbia: School Safety, Health and Security[^about]

The safety and health rules a District of Columbia independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: fire evacuation drills and plans, AEDs and cardiac emergency response plans, medication action plans and emergency epinephrine, medical marijuana at school, concussion rules for school athletics, immunization and health certificates, and firearms on school grounds.

## How often must a District of Columbia private school hold fire evacuation drills, and must it keep an evacuation plan? {#fire-drills-and-evacuation-plans}

**Short answer.** The District's fire code, as the Fire and EMS Department's code-enforcement bulletin sets it out, requires an approved fire safety and evacuation plan for each Group E educational occupancy and monthly emergency evacuation drills in which all occupants take part. [^dc-fems-group-e-plan] [^dc-fems-drill-frequency]

The bulletin states that its content was compiled from the 2015 edition of the International Fire Code. [^dc-fems-bulletin-basis] The Department of Buildings states that the 2017 District of Columbia Construction Codes, in effect since May 29, 2020, consist of the 2015 International Code Council model codes as amended by Title 12 of the District of Columbia Municipal Regulations, and that Title 12 DCMR is the source for the codes' official text. [^dc-dob-2017-construction-codes] The bulletin lists schools under the educational (Group E) heading without distinguishing public from private schools, and the plan and drill duties attach to that occupancy classification. [^dc-fems-group-e-plan]

The first evacuation drill of each school year must be held within 10 days after classes begin, drills must be held at varying times so that they cannot be told apart from actual fires, and outdoor assembly areas must keep each class separate for accountability. [^dc-fems-educational-drills] Records of each required drill must show, among other items, who conducted it, its date and time, the number of occupants evacuated and the problems encountered, and where a fire alarm system is provided the drill must be started by activating that system. [^dc-fems-drill-records] The plan must be reviewed or updated annually or when staff assignments, occupancy or the building's layout change, and employees must be trained on the plan at new-employee orientation and at least annually after that, with training records kept. [^dc-fems-plan-maintenance] [^dc-fems-employee-training]

The bulletin states that the fire code does not require a lockdown plan, and that where a building has one, lockdown drills do not substitute for any required fire and evacuation drill. [^dc-fems-lockdown] A lockdown plan must be approved by the fire code official and must include, among other items, instructions for reporting an emergency that requires a lockdown. [^dc-fems-lockdown-contents] The D.C. Code's duty to create and implement school building security and emergency operations plans, in consultation with the Metropolitan Police Department and the Homeland Security and Emergency Management Agency, is assigned to the District of Columbia Public Schools and does not reach a private school. [^dc-code-5-132-03a-dcps-plans]

## Must a District of Columbia independent or religious school keep an AED on site and adopt a cardiac emergency response plan? {#aed-and-cardiac-emergency-plan}

**Short answer.** D.C. Code § 7-2371.02c requires every school, defined to include independent, private and parochial schools, to keep at least one AED on site and to adopt and implement a cardiac emergency response plan by the beginning of School Year 2025–2026. [^dc-code-7-2371-01-school] [^dc-code-7-2371-02c-aed-and-plan]

The AED must be in a location identified in the school's cardiac emergency response plan, must be appropriate for use on children and adults, and must follow a maintenance schedule, established "in coordination with the Department of General Services"[^dc-code-7-2371-02c-aed-and-plan], that includes periodic testing, periodic inspection and annual maintenance. [^dc-code-7-2371-02c-aed-and-plan] Athletic coaches, coaching assistants, athletic trainers, the athletic director, any team or game physician, the school nurse and every anticipated AED user the school designates must complete an approved CPR and AED program, with new hires in those positions completing it within 60 days after their hire date. [^dc-code-7-2371-02c-training] At least one person trained in a CPR and AED program must be present during the school's hours of operation and during any athletic activity, and every school employee must be told at least annually where each AED is and be given the school's plan. [^dc-code-7-2371-02c-training]

The Fire and EMS Department, with the Office of the State Superintendent of Education, must keep a model plan available to schools that covers cardiac arrest on school grounds, including at athletic events and practices, and addresses a cardiac emergency response team, drills and annual review. [^dc-code-7-2371-02c-model-plan] The Mayor must require each school to keep a written record of the testing, inspection and maintenance of each AED and must establish a procedure for monitoring school adherence. [^dc-code-7-2371-02c-mayor]

As a person or entity that acquires an AED, a school must also see that its Facility AED Coordinator is trained in CPR and AED use and that users maintain their certification, maintain and test the AED according to the manufacturer's guidelines and keep written records, and notify the Fire and EMS Department's Chief and the call center of each AED's location and type, notifying the Chief if an AED is removed. Anyone who uses an AED on a person in cardiac arrest must call 911 as soon as possible. [^dc-code-7-2371-02-acquirer] A person or entity that in good faith and without compensation uses an AED is immune from civil liability for resulting personal injury if the person acts as an ordinary, reasonably prudent person would have acted, and that immunity extends to the person or entity responsible for the site where the AED is located. [^dc-code-7-2371-03-immunity] The immunity does not apply to gross negligence or willful or wanton misconduct, or to a licensed or certified health professional acting within the scope of the license or certification or of the professional's employment. [^dc-code-7-2371-03-limits]

## Who may give students medication at a District of Columbia private school, and when may a student carry an inhaler or epinephrine? {#medication-action-plans}

**Short answer.** D.C. Code § 38-651.02, whose subchapter covers private and parochial schools, lets a student carry and self-administer medication or seizure treatment at school, at school activities and on school transportation once a valid medication action plan or seizure action plan is on file. [^dc-code-38-651-01-school] [^dc-code-38-651-02-self-administration]

The self-administration right covers asthma, anaphylaxis, a seizure disorder or other illness, includes non-medication seizure treatment, and is subject to the subchapter's other conditions. [^dc-code-38-651-02-self-administration] A valid medication action plan contains a licensed health practitioner's signed authorization, including, for self-administered medication, confirmation that the student has been instructed in the proper technique and has demonstrated the ability to self-administer effectively; the responsible person's signed authorization; and a written acknowledgment that the school and its employees and agents are immune from civil liability for good-faith performance. [^dc-code-38-651-03-plan-contents] The plan must be amended immediately after any change in the student's health or treatment and updated at least annually. [^dc-code-38-651-03-updates]

For a student who does not self-administer, a school employee or agent trained and certified through the Mayor's medication administration training program may give medication under a valid plan if the responsible person delivered the medication to the school, the employee works under a licensed health practitioner's general supervision and, except in emergency circumstances, the responsible person gave the first dose of a new medication. [^dc-code-38-651-05-administration] The training is taught by a health-care professional licensed in the District, and the certification is valid for 3 years. [^dc-code-38-651-04-training] A trained employee or agent need not be certified to give seizure treatment under a valid seizure action plan, [^dc-code-38-651-05-administration] and a school must tell, at a minimum, all personnel with direct contact with students who have seizure action plans about the Department of Health's voluntary seizure-awareness training and authorize them to attend it. [^dc-code-38-651-03a-seizure]

The school must keep the plans in the health suite or another designated, easily accessible location, maintain a list of students with plans and their emergency contacts, and keep records of certified staff and of every emergency administration. [^dc-code-38-651-08-records] Emergency response information, including the names of staff trained and certified to give medication in emergencies, must be posted in all schools. [^dc-code-38-651-07-posting] Medication a family leaves at school must be labeled and stored where the student has immediate access in an emergency, and no school is required to store more than a 3-school-day supply for any one student. [^dc-code-38-651-09-storage] A student who self-administers medication for a purpose other than the student's own treatment may be disciplined, but the discipline may not limit the student's access to prescribed medication. [^dc-code-38-651-10-misuse] The school and its employees and agents are immune from civil liability for good-faith performance under the subchapter, except for criminal acts, intentional wrongdoing, gross negligence, or wanton or willful misconduct. [^dc-code-38-651-11-immunity]

## May a District of Columbia private school keep epinephrine auto-injectors, albuterol or other emergency medication for students who have no prescription on file? {#stock-epinephrine-and-emergency-medications}

**Short answer.** The District's stock-epinephrine and undesignated emergency medication programs for schools cover only DCPS and public charter schools, but D.C. Code § 7-2381.02 lets a health care professional prescribe epinephrine auto-injectors to any entity other than a public school that has a trained employee or agent. [^dc-code-38-651-01-public-school] [^dc-code-38-651-04a-public-epinephrine] [^dc-code-38-651-06-public-uema] [^dc-code-7-2381-01-authorized-entity] [^dc-code-7-2381-02-prescription]

The Student Access to Treatment subchapter defines public school as a DCPS school or a public charter school and excludes any parochial or private school. [^dc-code-38-651-01-public-school] The undesignated epinephrine auto-injector plan run by the Office of the State Superintendent of Education authorizes a public school to possess and administer auto-injectors and requires each public school to have at least 2 certified employees or agents available during all hours of the school day. [^dc-code-38-651-04a-public-epinephrine] The undesignated emergency medications plan, which covers albuterol, glucagon and medications the Department of Health designates by rule, likewise places its staffing and storage duties on each public school. [^dc-code-38-651-01-undesignated-medications] [^dc-code-38-651-06-public-uema]

A private school is an authorized entity under the separate anaphylaxis statute, which defines that term as "any entity or organization other than a public school"[^dc-code-7-2381-01-authorized-entity]. [^dc-code-7-2381-01-authorized-entity] An employee or agent of an authorized entity who has completed the statute's training program may give an auto-injector to, or administer it to, any individual the employee or agent believes in good faith is experiencing anaphylaxis, or give it to that individual's parent, guardian or caregiver for immediate administration. [^dc-code-7-2381-03-use] The authorized entity and its employees or agents are immune from civil or criminal liability for actions the statute authorizes, but not for gross negligence, recklessness or intentional misconduct. [^dc-code-7-2381-06-immunity]

Apart from that statute, the Student Access to Treatment subchapter allows any school covered by it, including a private school, to procure medication for asthma or other illness for use in emergency circumstances, properly stored in an easily accessible location. [^dc-code-38-651-09-procure] The Mayor's medication administration training program certifies school employees and agents to give medication in emergency circumstances to any student experiencing an acute episode of asthma, anaphylaxis or other illness. [^dc-code-38-651-04-emergency-training]

## Must a District of Columbia private or religious school let a student use medical marijuana on campus during the school day? {#medical-marijuana-at-school}

**Short answer.** D.C. Code § 38-651.03(a-1) requires each school, defined to include parochial and private schools, to adopt policies permitting a student who is a qualifying medical marijuana patient to administer medical marijuana on campus during the school day as the student's medical authorization provides. [^dc-code-38-651-01-school-cannabis] [^dc-code-38-651-03-a1-cannabis]

The rule reaches a student who is a qualifying patient, under the District's medical marijuana law, when failing to administer medical marijuana during the school day would disrupt the student's ability to participate in school instruction. [^dc-code-38-651-03-a1-cannabis] The marijuana must be in a non-smokable form, and the student's medication action plan must include an authorized practitioner's certification that failing to administer it during the school day would disrupt the student's ability to participate in instruction. [^dc-code-38-651-03-a1-cannabis] The subsection's only stated release is federal: a school may stop complying if, after October 7, 2019, the federal government issues a communication indicating that federal funding will be withheld from the District or a school within the District if the school continues to authorize medical marijuana on campus. [^dc-code-38-651-03-a1-cannabis] The definition that brings private schools within the rule reaches "Any charter school, parochial school, or private school in the District"[^dc-code-38-651-01-school-cannabis]. [^dc-code-38-651-01-school-cannabis]

## What concussion rules apply to a District of Columbia private school's sports teams and physical education classes? {#concussion-rules-for-school-athletics}

**Short answer.** D.C. Code § 7-2871.02 requires an athlete 18 or younger suspected of a concussion in school athletics, including physical education classes, to be removed immediately and kept out until a licensed or certified health-care provider evaluates the athlete and gives written clearance. [^dc-code-7-2871-01-definitions] [^dc-code-7-2871-02-removal]

The chapter defines athletic activity to include a school-sponsored interscholastic program and physical education classes that are part of a school curriculum, and defines school to include any charter, parochial or private school in the District. [^dc-code-7-2871-01-definitions] Before an athlete may participate, the organizing entity must give the Department of Health's concussion materials to the athlete and a parent or guardian, and both must sign and return an acknowledgment of receipt. [^dc-code-7-2871-04-materials] The Mayor establishes the concussion training program by rulemaking and decides by rulemaking which individuals must complete it. [^dc-code-7-2871-03-training]

Cardiac emergencies in school sports are addressed through the school's cardiac emergency response plan: the model plan the Fire and EMS Department maintains covers cardiac arrest during a school's athletic events and practices on school grounds, and each school must have at least one person trained in CPR and AED use present during any athletic activity. [^dc-code-7-2371-02c-athletics]

## What immunization and health certificate rules must a District of Columbia private school enforce? {#immunization-and-health-certificates}

**Short answer.** D.C. Code § 38-502 bars a student from attending a school, defined to include private, parochial and independent schools, without a certification of immunization or a recognized exemption, and § 38-602 requires each private-school student to furnish an annual certificate of health unless a religious exemption applies. [^dc-code-38-501-school] [^dc-code-38-502-certification] [^dc-code-38-602-health-certificate] [^dc-code-38-603-religious-exemption]

A responsible person must furnish the certification of immunization to the private, parochial or independent school at least annually. [^dc-code-38-502-certification] The school must distribute immunization information to a responsible person for each admitted student at least annually, and when a certification is incomplete it must notify a responsible person of the missing immunizations, the deadline for certification or exemption and the other listed items, including a copy of the certificate of health form. [^dc-code-38-504-notices] Unless the exclusion standards provide otherwise, the school may not let the student attend for more than 20 school days after that notice without a completed certification, although a student in the middle of a series that cannot be completed within the 20 school days may keep attending while the treatments continue if, within the 20 school days, the school receives the provider's written notice that immunization is in progress. [^dc-code-38-505-exclusion]

An immunization is not required for a student whose responsible person objects in good faith and in writing to the public health authorities that it would violate the responsible person's religious beliefs, or for whom the public health authorities have a physician's or nurse-practitioner's written certification that it is medically inadvisable. [^dc-code-38-506-exemptions]

The certificate of health must be completed and signed by a physician or advanced practice nurse who examined the student within the 12 months before the first day of the school year or the student's enrollment, whichever is later, and a student under 6 entering pre-kindergarten, kindergarten or first grade in a private school must also furnish a certificate of testing for lead poisoning. [^dc-code-38-602-health-certificate] The statute also directs the Mayor to set requirements for dental examinations and for elementary and secondary students to submit certificates of dental health. [^dc-code-38-602-dental] Those certificates and examinations are not required if a minor student's parent or guardian, or an adult student, submits in good faith a written notarized statement to the principal or other appropriate school official that the examination would violate the established tenets and practices of the family's church or religious denomination. [^dc-code-38-603-religious-exemption]

## What limits does District of Columbia law place on firearms on private and religious school grounds? {#firearms-on-school-grounds}

**Short answer.** D.C. Code § 7-2509.07 bars a person holding a concealed-carry license from carrying a pistol in the building and grounds, including any adjacent parking lot, of a public or private elementary or secondary school. [^dc-code-7-2509-07-schools]

A licensee does not violate the school-grounds rule while driving into and immediately parking at the school to pick up or drop off a student or child, if the pistol is secured as District law requires before the licensee leaves the vehicle. [^dc-code-7-2509-07-pickup] A violation subjects the licensee to revocation of the license in addition to any other penalty. [^dc-code-7-2509-07-revocation] Carrying a concealed pistol in a church, synagogue, mosque or other place of regular religious worship is presumed prohibited unless the property is posted to allow it or the owner or authorized agent allows it personally, but such a place may not authorize carrying where services are conducted in a location the school-grounds list covers. [^dc-code-7-2509-07-worship]

The District also declares all areas within 1,000 feet of an appropriately identified public or private elementary or secondary school, meaning one with a sign identifying it as a gun free zone, to be a gun free zone, where illegally carrying a gun is punishable by up to twice the otherwise authorized fine and imprisonment. [^dc-code-22-4502-01-zone] The enhancement does not apply to a person legally licensed to carry a firearm in the District who lives or works within 1,000 feet of the zone, or to listed law enforcement and other officers. [^dc-code-22-4502-01-exemptions]

Federal law separately makes it unlawful to knowingly possess a firearm that has moved in or affects interstate commerce at a place the individual knows, or has reasonable cause to believe, is a school zone, subject to listed exceptions. [^usc-18-922q-dc-school-zone] A school zone includes the grounds of a parochial or private school and the area within 1,000 feet of those grounds, and the term State includes the District of Columbia. [^usc-18-921-dc-school-zone-definitions] [^usc-18-921-dc-state-definition] The exceptions include possession by an individual licensed by the State where the school zone is located, if that State's law requires law enforcement to verify the individual's qualification before issuing the license, possession for use in a program the school approves, and possession under a contract between the school and the individual or the individual's employer. [^usc-18-922q-dc-school-zone]
