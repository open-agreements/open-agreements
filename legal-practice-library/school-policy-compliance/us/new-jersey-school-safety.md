# School Policy Compliance in New Jersey: School Safety, Health and Security[^about]

The safety and health rules a New Jersey independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: fire and security drills, safety plans and critical incident mapping, AEDs and emergency action plans, epinephrine, inhalers and opioid antidotes, concussion and cardiac rules for school sports, and firearms on school grounds.

## How many fire and school security drills must a New Jersey private school hold each year? {#fire-and-security-drills}

**Short answer.** N.J.S.A. 18A:41-1 requires every principal of a school of two or more rooms, or of a one-room school above the first story, to hold at least one fire drill and one school security drill each month during school hours, including summer months when instructional programs run. [^njsa-18a-41-1-monthly-drills]

The Department of Education applies the drill law to nonpublic schools: its statement-of-assurance form requires a nonpublic school to submit the statement to the county prosecutor's office by June 30 each school year, and the chief school administrator certifies, "pursuant to N.J.S. 18A:41-1"[^nj-doe-drill-assurance-certification], that the requirements for practicing school security drills have been met for the period the school's facilities were subject to the drill law based on occupancy. [^nj-doe-drill-assurance-nonpublic] [^nj-doe-drill-assurance-certification] The Department's drill guide adds that every school, public and nonpublic, completes the Department's Security Drill Record Form. [^nj-doe-drill-guide-record-forms]

All students and staff must fully participate in each drill to the greatest extent practicable, fire escapes where provided must be used in every fire drill, and an actual security emergency during the month that includes activities equivalent to a drill counts as that month's security drill. [^njsa-18a-41-1-participation] A law enforcement officer must be present at a minimum of one school security drill each school year to recommend improvements to the drill procedures. [^njsa-18a-41-1-law-enforcement] The statute's separate sentence on keeping exterior doors and exits locked is addressed to employees of a school district or receiving school. [^njsa-18a-41-1-participation]

A nonpublic school must also keep in each student's record the student's mobility, sensory, medical, communication and other needs in a fire drill, security drill or actual emergency, including whether the student can participate safely and fully without supports. [^pl-2023-c212-student-needs-record] Where supports are needed, a written plan must be kept in the student record, [^pl-2023-c212-written-plan] and a nonpublic school, where applicable, must review those determinations at least once a year. [^pl-2023-c212-annual-review]

## Must a New Jersey nonpublic school adopt a school safety plan, install a panic alarm or give building maps to the police? {#safety-plans-alarms-and-mapping}

**Short answer.** N.J.S.A. 18A:41-7.1 requires a nonpublic school's chief school administrator to give local police critical incident mapping data for its schools and grounds, but the safety-plan rule in N.J.A.C. 6A:16-5.1 and the panic-alarm mandate in Alyssa's Law apply only to school districts and public school buildings. [^njac-6a-16-5-1-district-plans] [^alyssas-law-public-buildings] [^njsa-18a-41-7-1-mapping]

Unless a section indicates otherwise, Chapter 6A:16 of the Administrative Code applies to public school districts, charter schools, renaissance school projects, jointure commissions, educational services commissions and approved private schools for students with disabilities acting under contract for public districts, and its safety-plan section is addressed to each school district. [^njac-6a-16-1-2-scope] Section 6A:16-5.1 requires each school district to develop and implement plans, procedures and mechanisms for safety and security in the district's public elementary and secondary schools. [^njac-6a-16-5-1-district-plans] Alyssa's Law requires a silent panic alarm linked to local law enforcement in each public elementary and secondary school building. [^alyssas-law-public-buildings] A school district may instead use an emergency mechanism the Department of Education approves as an alternative to a panic alarm. [^alyssas-law-alternative] That conclusion is limited to these two provisions: it does not address fire-code, building-code or local emergency-planning obligations, which this guide does not cover.

The mapping duty does reach nonpublic schools; where the school building is in a municipality with no municipal police department, the data goes to an entity the Superintendent of State Police designates. [^njsa-18a-41-7-1-mapping] The data must be revised whenever it changes, be compatible with law enforcement platforms, be printable and be verified by an annual walkthrough, and it includes aerial images, floor plans with room numbers, building access points and the locations of hazardous materials and utility shut-offs. [^njsa-18a-41-7-1-mapping] [^njsa-18a-41-7-1-format-and-contents]

The chief school administrator of a nonpublic school must also ensure that all full-time employees receive annual school safety and security training, including instruction on security drills, conducted in consultation with law enforcement, fire and emergency medical services personnel. [^njsa-18a-41-7-staff-training] Substitutes must receive information and training on the school's safety and security procedures. [^njsa-18a-41-7-substitutes] Supervisors of an outside youth program that uses the building outside school hours must receive the school's non-confidential emergency information, which may not include student records, and the sponsoring organization must train its own employees and volunteers and file a statement of assurance before the school authorizes use of the building, renewed annually. [^njsa-18a-41-7-youth-programs]

## Must a New Jersey independent or religious school keep an AED on site and have staff trained to use it? {#aed-requirements}

**Short answer.** Janet's Law requires a nonpublic school with any of grades kindergarten through 12 to keep an accessible AED at each school and to have a coach, licensed athletic trainer or other designee trained in CPR and AED use present at athletic events and practices. [^njsa-18a-40-41a-aed] [^njsa-18a-40-41a-trained-staff]

The defibrillator must be in an unlocked location on school property with an identifying sign, be accessible during the school day and whenever a school-sponsored athletic event or team practice involving the school's students is taking place, and be within reasonable proximity of the athletic field or gymnasium. [^njsa-18a-40-41a-aed] The person present at the event or practice is the team coach or licensed athletic trainer, or another designated staff member if there is no coach or licensed athletic trainer, and must be trained in CPR and AED use, although the school is in compliance if a State-certified emergency services provider or other certified first responder is on site. [^njsa-18a-40-41a-trained-staff] Each defibrillator must be tested and maintained to the manufacturer's guidelines, and the local emergency medical services provider must be told about the device, its type and its location. [^njsa-18a-40-41a-testing-notice] Janet's Law extends to a nonpublic school and its employees the civil immunity of the State's AED statute, N.J.S.A. 2A:62A-27, for acquiring and using defibrillators. [^njsa-18a-40-41a-immunity] That immunity covers a person or entity that acts in good faith and has complied with the AED statute, [^njsa-2a-62a-27-immunity-conditions] and it does not reach gross negligence or willful or wanton misconduct. [^njsa-2a-62a-27-gross-negligence]

The same law requires a nonpublic school to adopt and implement an emergency action plan for a sudden cardiac event. [^njsa-18a-40-41b-action-plan] The plan must list at least five school employees, team coaches or licensed athletic trainers holding current CPR and AED certifications, updated at least once each semester, and must assign who responds to the person, calls 911, starts CPR, retrieves and uses the defibrillator and guides emergency responders. [^njsa-18a-40-41b-plan-contents]

## What must a New Jersey nonpublic school's policies provide on emergency epinephrine, inhalers and opioid overdose medication? {#medication-and-epinephrine}

**Short answer.** New Jersey law requires the chief school administrator of a nonpublic school to adopt policies for the emergency administration of epinephrine and of an opioid antidote, and to permit a qualifying student to self-administer medication for asthma or another potentially life-threatening illness, a life-threatening allergic reaction or adrenal insufficiency. [^njsa-18a-40-12-5-epinephrine-policy] [^njsa-18a-40-12-24-opioid-policy] [^njsa-18a-40-12-3-self-administration]

The epinephrine policy must keep a student's prescribed epinephrine in a secure but unlocked location accessible to the school nurse and designees, have the nurse or a designee promptly available at school and school-sponsored functions, and require transport to a hospital emergency room after administration even if symptoms appear resolved. [^njsa-18a-40-12-5-policy-requirements] Separately from that prescribed-epinephrine route, it must let the school nurse or a trained designee give epinephrine to a student without a known history of anaphylaxis, or whose parents have not completed the authorization and notice steps, when the nurse or designee in good faith believes the student is having an anaphylactic reaction, and require each public and nonpublic school to keep a stock supply of epinephrine auto-injectors under a standing protocol. [^njsa-18a-40-12-5-stock-supply] The school nurse has primary responsibility for administering epinephrine and designates, in consultation with the chief school administrator, employees who volunteer to administer it when the nurse is not present. [^njsa-18a-40-12-6-nurse-and-designees]

Self-administration requires the parents' written authorization, a physician's written certification that the student has the condition and is capable of, and has been instructed in, the proper method of self-administration, written notice to the parents that the school and its employees incur no liability, the parents' signed acknowledgment and indemnity, and annual renewal. [^njsa-18a-40-12-3-self-administration] A student permitted to self-administer may carry the inhaler, auto-injector or adrenal-insufficiency medication at all times, provided the student does not endanger the student or other persons through misuse. [^njsa-18a-40-12-3-carry]

The opioid antidote policy must require each school that includes any of grades nine through 12, and permit any other school, to obtain a standing order and maintain a supply of opioid antidotes in a secure but unlocked and easily accessible location, and must direct the school nurse or a trained designee to administer an antidote to any person the nurse or designee in good faith reasonably believes is experiencing an opioid overdose. [^njsa-18a-40-12-24-opioid-policy] Where a school maintains a supply, the antidotes must be accessible during regular school hours and school-sponsored functions in the school or on adjacent grounds. [^njsa-18a-40-12-24-access] The policy must also require hospital transport by emergency services personnel after administration, even if symptoms appear resolved. [^njsa-18a-40-12-24-transport]

The diabetes-care provisions are framed around the public school system: the Legislature enacted them to address the care of students with diabetes "within the public school system"[^njsa-18a-40-12-11-public-school-system], and the glucagon designation runs to volunteer employees of the school district. [^njsa-18a-40-12-11-public-school-system] [^njsa-18a-40-12-14-glucagon-district]

## What concussion and sudden cardiac arrest rules apply to a New Jersey nonpublic school's sports programs? {#concussion-and-cardiac-sports-rules}

**Short answer.** A New Jersey nonpublic school's sports programs must remove a student suspected of a concussion or showing sudden cardiac arrest warning signs until a physician clears the student, distribute the State concussion fact sheet and cardiac arrest pamphlet, and require preparticipation physicals for grades six through 12. [^njsa-18a-40-41-4-removal] [^njsa-18a-40-41h-cardiac-removal] [^njsa-18a-40-41-2-fact-sheet] [^njsa-18a-40-41-pamphlet] [^njsa-18a-40-41-7-physical]

A student suspected of a concussion or other head injury during a competition or practice must be removed immediately and may not return until a physician or other licensed provider trained in concussion management has evaluated the student, a physician so trained has given written clearance, and the student is back at regular school activities without symptoms. [^njsa-18a-40-41-4-removal] The concussion fact sheet goes to parents annually with a signed acknowledgment from the student and parent, and the school physician, each person who coaches a nonpublic school interscholastic, intramural or cheerleading program, and each athletic trainer involved in such a program must complete the Department of Education's head injury safety training program. [^njsa-18a-40-41-2-fact-sheet] [^njsa-18a-40-41-2-training] The written concussion policy requirement in N.J.S.A. 18A:40-41.3 is imposed on each school district. [^njsa-18a-40-41-3-district-policy] A separate provision shields a nonpublic school from liability for injury or death due to the action or inaction of persons employed by or under contract with a youth sports team organization that operates on school grounds, if the organization gives the school proof of liability insurance of at least $50,000 per person, per occurrence and a statement of compliance with the school's policies for managing concussions and head injuries. [^njsa-18a-40-41-5-youth-sports]

A student who shows symptoms or early warning signs of sudden cardiac arrest while participating in an athletic activity must be removed immediately by the coach and may not return until a licensed physician evaluates the student and gives written clearance. [^njsa-18a-40-41h-cardiac-removal] The governing body or chief school administrator of a nonpublic school must ensure that a coach who knowingly violates that rule is suspended for the rest of the season on a first violation, for that season and the next on a second, and permanently on a third. [^njsa-18a-40-41h-coach-suspension] Each year, before the student participates in an athletic activity, the student and a parent must sign and return the form acknowledging receipt and review of the sudden cardiac arrest pamphlet. [^njsa-18a-40-41-pamphlet-form] The preparticipation physical must be done within 365 days before the first day of official practice in the season, with a parent-signed health history update when it is more than 90 days old, and a nonpublic school may not let a student in grades six through 12 participate without those documents. [^njsa-18a-40-41-7-timing] [^njsa-18a-40-41-7-physical] The examining practitioner must complete the State's cardiac screening module, and the nonpublic school retains the original signed certification. [^njsa-18a-40-41d-cardiac-module]

## What limits does New Jersey law place on firearms on nonpublic school grounds? {#firearms-on-school-grounds}

**Short answer.** New Jersey makes it a third-degree crime to knowingly possess a firearm in or on any part of the buildings or grounds of any school without the written authorization of the institution's governing officer, even for a person holding a carry permit or firearms purchaser identification card. [^njsa-2c-39-5-e1-firearms]

The offense reaches "any school, college, university, or other educational institution"[^njsa-2c-39-5-e1-firearms], with no limit to public schools, so it covers the buildings and grounds of an independent or religious school; the governing officer's written authorization takes possession outside this particular offense, and this answer does not address other firearms laws that may apply. [^njsa-2c-39-5-e1-firearms] Knowing possession of certain other weapons on school buildings or grounds without that written authorization is a fourth-degree crime, and knowing possession of an imitation firearm on school buildings or grounds without that written authorization, or while on any school bus, is a disorderly persons offense. [^njsa-2c-39-5-e2-e3-other-weapons]

For armed security, New Jersey's special law enforcement officer statute authorizes Class Three officers to exercise full police powers while providing security at a public or nonpublic school on school premises during school hours or while students or teachers occupy it. [^njsa-40a-14-146-11-class-three] This answer does not address the 2022 carry-permit statute's separate list of places where permit holders may not carry, which has been the subject of federal litigation.
