# School Policy Compliance in Virginia: School Safety, Health and Security[^about]

The safety and health rules a Virginia independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: fire drills under the Statewide Fire Prevention Code, the public-school crisis-plan, threat-assessment and AED mandates that do not reach private schools, emergency medication authority for accredited private schools, student-athlete health statutes, firearms on school property and armed school security officers.

## How many fire and lockdown drills must a Virginia private school hold each year? {#fire-and-lockdown-drills}

**Short answer.** A Virginia private school's fire-drill obligation comes from the Statewide Fire Prevention Code, which applies to all matters relating to structures and premises and incorporates the 2021 International Fire Code, while the Title 22.1 drill statutes bind only public schools. [^va-ss-sfpc-applicability] [^va-ss-sfpc-ifc-2021] [^va-ss-22-1-137-fire-drills] [^va-ss-22-1-137-2-lockdown]

Section 22.1-137 directs every public school to hold fire drills under the Statewide Fire Prevention Code. [^va-ss-22-1-137-fire-drills] Every public school must also hold at least one tornado drill each school year, [^va-ss-22-1-137-1-tornado] and a lock-down drill at least once during the first 20 school days and once more after the first 60 days, with at least 24 hours' notice to parents that need not give the exact date and time. [^va-ss-22-1-137-2-lockdown] Each school board must deliver training on emergency safety procedures to each student and employee in each school at least once a year. [^va-ss-22-1-137-3-training] The 2024 requirement that evacuation plans include provisions to help students with mobility impairments evacuate alongside their peers applies to divisionwide and public school plans. [^va-ss-22-1-137-4-mobility]

The Fire Prevention Code's base drill requirements come from the 2021 International Fire Code, which the Virginia regulation adopts by reference as an enforceable part of the Code, and Virginia's amendment to that code's drill section bars emergency evacuation drills in school buildings during periods of mandatory testing required by the Virginia Board of Education. [^va-ss-sfpc-ifc-2021] [^va-ss-sfpc-testing-exception] The State Fire Marshal's office, writing on July 16, 2020 under the 2015 edition, described the Code as requiring educational occupancies to have an approved fire safety and evacuation plan and to hold emergency evacuation drills monthly for all occupants. [^va-ss-sfm-2020-monthly] The same memo states that an evacuation caused by an unplanned fire alarm or other emergency does not replace a required drill, and that lockdown drills do not substitute for the Code's fire and evacuation drills. [^va-ss-sfm-2020-no-substitution] The Department of Criminal Justice Services' current drill guidance, addressed to public schools, reads the Code of Virginia, the Standards of Accreditation and the Fire Prevention Code together as requiring one fire drill within the first 10 school days and one every month after that. [^va-ss-dcjs-drill-guidance] The State Fire Marshal's memo describes the 2015 edition, not the 2021 edition the regulation now incorporates by reference. [^va-ss-sfm-2020-monthly] [^va-ss-sfpc-ifc-2021]

A private school for students with disabilities licensed by the Board of Education has its own drill schedule. Chapter 16 of Title 22.1 defines such a school as a privately owned and operated preschool, school or educational organization offering instruction for tuition or other consideration to students with listed disabilities. [^va-ss-22-1-319-school-definition] The Board's licensing regulations require each such school to hold an emergency evacuation drill in each building occupied by students at least once a week for the first 20 school days and then once a month for the rest of the school year, [^va-ss-8vac20-671-380-evacuation] and at least one tornado drill, one earthquake drill and two lockdown drills each year, one lockdown drill in September and one in January. [^va-ss-8vac20-671-380-drills]

## Must a Virginia private school adopt a crisis and emergency plan, form a threat assessment team or install a panic alarm system? {#crisis-plans-threat-assessment-and-panic-alarms}

**Short answer.** Virginia's requirements for school safety audits, crisis and emergency management plans and threat assessment teams bind local school boards and the schools they supervise, and its 2026 wearable panic alarm law only permits school boards to equip public school employees. [^va-ss-22-1-279-8-crisis-plan] [^va-ss-22-1-79-4-threat-assessment] [^va-ss-22-1-79-4-1-panic-alarm]

Section 22.1-279.8 requires each school board to ensure that every school it supervises develops a written school crisis, emergency management and medical emergency response plan with local law enforcement, fire, emergency medical services and emergency management officials. [^va-ss-22-1-279-8-crisis-plan] The same section requires each local school board to have all schools under its supervisory control conduct an annual school safety audit with the chief law-enforcement officer of the locality, [^va-ss-22-1-279-8-audit] and, as part of each audit, to create or certify a detailed and accurate floor plan for each public school building. [^va-ss-22-1-279-8-floor-plan] Section 22.1-79.4 requires each local school board to adopt threat assessment team policies consistent with the model policies of the Virginia Center for School and Campus Safety. [^va-ss-22-1-79-4-threat-assessment] The wearable panic alarm statute, enacted in 2026, defines the system as a wearable device that signals the local 9-1-1 center and, when appropriate, a schoolwide emergency notification, and it permits any school board to provide one to any school board employee in a public school building. [^va-ss-22-1-79-4-1-panic-alarm] That conclusion is limited to these Title 22.1 provisions; it does not address fire-code lockdown-plan approval, building-code or local requirements that may apply to a private school building.

The Virginia Center for School and Campus Safety, created within the Department of Criminal Justice Services, must develop a model critical incident response training program for public school personnel and others serving schools that is also made available to private schools in the Commonwealth. [^va-ss-9-1-184-private-schools]

## Must a Virginia independent or religious school keep an AED on site or adopt a cardiac emergency response plan? {#aed-and-cardiac-emergency-plans}

**Short answer.** Virginia's AED and cardiac emergency response plan mandates are addressed to public elementary and secondary schools, but a private school that maintains an AED on its property is immune from civil liability for injury from its emergency use, absent gross negligence or willful or wanton misconduct. [^va-ss-22-1-274-4-aed] [^va-ss-22-1-271-9-cerp] [^va-ss-8-01-225-aed-maintainer]

Section 22.1-274.4 requires each local school board to plan for and place an AED in every public elementary and secondary school in its division. [^va-ss-22-1-274-4-aed] Section 22.1-271.9 requires each public elementary or secondary school to adopt a cardiac emergency response plan or athletic emergency action plan covering sudden cardiac arrest on school grounds and, where the school has an athletic program, at its practices and events. [^va-ss-22-1-271-9-cerp] A public school with an athletic program must have one of those plans in place at all times, and the plan must address, among other elements, a cardiac emergency response team and at least annual drills. [^va-ss-22-1-271-9-elements] The Department of Criminal Justice Services' drill guidance reads the statute as requiring at least one cardiac response drill a year. [^va-ss-dcjs-cardiac-drill]

For a private school, the Good Samaritan statute governs liability rather than any placement duty. A person who maintains an AED on property it owns or controls is immune for injury from the AED's emergency use unless the injury results from gross negligence or willful or wanton misconduct of that person or its agent or employee. [^va-ss-8-01-225-aed-maintainer] A person who operates an AED in an emergency, trains operators or orders AEDs is immune when the person performing the defibrillation acts as an ordinary, reasonably prudent person would have acted, with the same exception for gross negligence or willful or wanton misconduct. [^va-ss-8-01-225-aed-operator] The separate ordinary-negligence immunity for school employees who give emergency care, CPR or AED treatment on school property runs to employees of a school board or of an approved local health department. [^va-ss-8-01-225-school-board-employees]

## Who may give students emergency epinephrine, asthma inhalers, glucagon, seizure medication or naloxone at a Virginia private school? {#medication-and-epinephrine}

**Short answer.** Virginia's Drug Control Act lets a trained employee of an accredited private school or a licensed school for students with disabilities, acting under a prescriber's order or standing protocol, possess and administer epinephrine and albuterol. [^va-ss-54-1-3408-d-epinephrine] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-h-glucagon] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-bb-adrenal] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-cc-seizure] Parallel provisions, each requiring a written order or standing protocol, cover glucagon, adrenal-crisis and seizure rescue medications. [^va-ss-54-1-3408-d-epinephrine] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-h-glucagon] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-bb-adrenal] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-cc-seizure]

The epinephrine and albuterol authority requires that the employee be authorized by a prescriber and trained in administering the medication, and albuterol may be given to a student diagnosed with a condition requiring it who is believed to be experiencing or about to experience an asthmatic crisis. [^va-ss-54-1-3408-d-epinephrine] The glucagon, insulin-assistance, adrenal-crisis and seizure authority each requires a written order or standing protocol, the parents' consent, training and a student diagnosed with the relevant condition, and each takes effect only when a licensed nurse, advanced practice registered nurse, physician or physician assistant is not present or, for seizure medication, cannot reach the student in time. [^va-ss-54-1-3408-h-glucagon] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-bb-adrenal] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-cc-seizure] For routine prescription drugs, the Act does not prevent a person from giving drugs to a student of an accredited private school if the person has completed a Board of Nursing-approved training program, has written parental authorization, follows the prescription label and gives only pharmacy-dispensed drugs, kept in their original labeled container, that the student or a parent or guardian would normally administer. [^va-ss-54-1-3408-p-routine]

Each of these authorizations names a private school "accredited pursuant to § 22.1-19 as administered by the Virginia Council for Private Education"[^va-ss-54-1-3408-d-epinephrine], and the epinephrine, glucagon, adrenal-crisis and seizure authorizations also name a school for students with disabilities licensed by the Board of Education; the routine-administration provision names only accredited private schools. [^va-ss-54-1-3408-d-epinephrine] [^va-ss-54-1-3408-p-routine] Va. Code § 22.1-19 makes private school accreditation available only at the private school's request. [^va-ss-22-1-19-accreditation] [^va-ss-22-1-19-vcpe] A trained employee of an accredited private school or a licensed school for students with disabilities who gives epinephrine to a student believed in good faith to be having an anaphylactic reaction is not liable for ordinary negligence, and neither is the school. [^va-ss-8-01-225-epinephrine-immunity] Separately from any school role, any person may administer naloxone to someone believed to be experiencing or about to experience a life-threatening opioid overdose, [^va-ss-54-1-3408-aa-naloxone] and a person who does so in good faith is not liable for civil damages unless the act or omission was the result of gross negligence or willful and wanton misconduct. [^va-ss-8-01-225-naloxone-immunity]

The policy mandates in Title 22.1 are addressed to public school boards: local school boards must adopt policies letting students with asthma or anaphylaxis carry and self-administer inhalers and auto-injectable epinephrine, [^va-ss-22-1-274-2-self-carry] and each local school board must plan for a supply of at least two unexpired doses of an opioid antagonist in each public elementary and secondary school. [^va-ss-22-1-274-4-1-opioid-supply]

## Do Virginia's concussion, sudden cardiac arrest, extreme heat and sports physical rules apply to a private school's athletic program? {#concussion-cardiac-and-heat-sports-rules}

**Short answer.** Virginia's student-athlete statutes on concussions, sudden cardiac arrest, extreme heat and preparticipation physicals impose their requirements on local school divisions, school boards and public middle and high school students, not on private schools, except that a recreational youth sports program using public school property must adopt or follow concussion policies. [^va-ss-22-1-271-5-concussion] [^va-ss-22-1-271-8-cardiac] [^va-ss-22-1-271-10-heat] [^va-ss-22-1-271-7-physical] [^va-ss-22-1-271-5-youth-programs]

Each local school division's concussion policy must require an annual review and signed acknowledgment of concussion information by each student-athlete and parent, and must remove a student-athlete suspected of a concussion or brain injury from play, with no return that day or until a licensed health care provider has evaluated the student and given written clearance. [^va-ss-22-1-271-5-concussion] Each local school division's sudden cardiac arrest policy must require a student-athlete with symptoms that may lead to sudden cardiac arrest to be removed from play immediately, with no return until a licensed health care provider evaluates the student and gives written clearance. [^va-ss-22-1-271-8-cardiac] [^va-ss-22-1-271-8-removal] Each school board must also adopt student-athlete extreme heat safety policies under Board of Education guidelines, a requirement enacted in 2025. [^va-ss-22-1-271-10-heat] A public middle or high school student may not play on or try out for an interscholastic team without a signed Preparticipation Physical Evaluation form attesting to a physical examination no more than 14 calendar months before the form was signed. [^va-ss-22-1-271-7-physical]

Each of these duties is placed on a local school division, a school board or a public middle or high school student, and none of the five provisions quoted here names a private school. [^va-ss-22-1-271-5-concussion] [^va-ss-22-1-271-8-cardiac] [^va-ss-22-1-271-10-heat] [^va-ss-22-1-271-7-physical] The concussion statute has one separate trigger: each non-interscholastic youth sports program using public school property must either establish concussion policies consistent with the local division's policies or the Board's guidelines, or follow the division's policies, and the statute defines such a program as one organized for recreational athletic competition or instruction for youth. [^va-ss-22-1-271-5-youth-programs] A private school's recreational program that uses public school fields or gyms can meet that definition; its interscholastic teams on its own property do not. [^va-ss-22-1-271-5-youth-programs]

## What limits does Virginia law place on firearms and other weapons on private and religious school grounds? {#firearms-on-school-grounds}

**Short answer.** Virginia makes it a Class 6 felony to knowingly possess a firearm on the property of any public, private or religious preschool, elementary, middle or high school, including its buildings and grounds, subject to the exemptions the statute lists. [^va-ss-18-2-308-1-firearms]

The exemptions cover, among others, weapons possessed as part of the school's curriculum or activities or a program the school sponsors or authorizes, law-enforcement officers, an unloaded firearm in a closed container in a motor vehicle, a concealed handgun permit holder with a concealed handgun while in a motor vehicle in the school's parking lot or driveway, a school security officer authorized to carry a firearm under Va. Code § 22.1-280.2:1, and a licensed armed security officer hired by a private or religious school for the protection of students and employees as authorized by the school. [^va-ss-18-2-308-1-exemptions] The same prohibition reaches the portion of any property open to the public and then used exclusively for school-sponsored functions or extracurricular activities while they are taking place, and any school bus the school owns or operates. [^va-ss-18-2-308-1-firearms] Knowingly possessing a stun weapon, a knife other than a folding pocket knife with a metal blade under three inches, or certain other weapons on the same property is a Class 1 misdemeanor. [^va-ss-18-2-308-1-other-weapons] Knowingly possessing a firearm inside a school building with intent to use it, attempting to use it, or displaying it in a threatening manner is a Class 6 felony carrying a five-year mandatory minimum sentence served consecutively with any other sentence. [^va-ss-18-2-308-1-threatening] For a private or religious preschool, the statute applies only during operating hours and does not reach a person who lives on the property while in that residence. [^va-ss-18-2-308-1-preschool]

Federal law adds the Gun-Free School Zones Act, 18 U.S.C. § 922(q), which makes it unlawful to knowingly possess a firearm that has moved in or affects interstate commerce at a place the individual knows, or has reasonable cause to believe, is a school zone. [^va-ss-usc-18-922-q-2-a] A school zone covers the grounds of a public, parochial or private school and the area within 1,000 feet of them. [^va-ss-usc-18-921-a-26] The federal exceptions include possession by an individual for use in a program approved by the school, possession under a contract between the school and the individual or the individual's employer, and possession by a law enforcement officer acting in an official capacity. [^va-ss-usc-18-922-q-2-b]

## May a Virginia private or religious school employ its own school security officer and authorize that officer to carry a firearm? {#armed-school-security-officers}

**Short answer.** A Virginia private or religious school may employ school security officers and may authorize one to carry a firearm on duty, but only if the six conditions in Va. Code § 22.1-280.2:1 are met. [^va-ss-22-1-280-2-1-sso] The officer must have been a law-enforcement officer within the 10 years before hiring and have left in good standing, meet the training and qualifications in subsection C of § 18.2-308.016, have filed proof of critical incident, evacuation and threat assessment training with the Department of Criminal Justice Services, have received training in the locality where employed if the training came from a local law-enforcement agency, be verified by the local chief law-enforcement officer as not prohibited by state or federal law from possessing, purchasing or transporting a firearm, and hold the school's grant of authority to carry. [^va-ss-22-1-280-2-1-sso]

A school security officer is someone a local school board or a private or religious school employs for the singular purpose of maintaining order and discipline, preventing crime, investigating policy violations and detaining students who violate the law or school policy, and who is responsible solely for the safety, security and welfare of students, faculty, staff and visitors in the assigned school. [^va-ss-9-1-101-sso-definition] The Virginia Center for School and Campus Safety provides training for and certification of school security officers. [^va-ss-9-1-184-sso-certification] The 10-year law-enforcement history may come from service as an active Virginia law-enforcement officer or from employment with a federal, state or local law-enforcement agency in substantially similar duties, and the school must solicit the chief law-enforcement officer's input on the officer's qualifications. [^va-ss-22-1-280-2-1-sso]

The school-property weapons statute exempts both a school security officer authorized to carry a firearm under § 22.1-280.2:1 and an armed security officer licensed under Title 9.1 who is hired by a private or religious school for the protection of students and employees as authorized by the school. [^va-ss-18-2-308-1-security-exemptions] The second exemption does not depend on the § 22.1-280.2:1 conditions: it requires a security officer licensed under Article 4 of Chapter 1 of Title 9.1 whom the school hires for the protection of students and employees and authorizes. [^va-ss-18-2-308-1-security-exemptions]
