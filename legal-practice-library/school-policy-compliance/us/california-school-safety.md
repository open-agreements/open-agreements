# School Policy Compliance in California: School Safety, Health and Security[^about]

The safety and health rules a California independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: fire and earthquake drills, the earthquake emergency procedure system, the workplace violence prevention plan, AEDs, emergency epinephrine and opioid antagonists, concussion, cardiac and football rules for school sports, and firearms and other weapons on school grounds.

## How often must a California private school hold fire drills and earthquake drills? {#fire-and-earthquake-drills}

**Short answer.** Education Code § 32001 requires the person in charge of any public, private or parochial school to hold a fire drill every calendar month at the elementary level, at least four times a school year at the intermediate levels and at least twice a school year at the secondary level. [^ca-edc-32001-fire-drills]

The same person must sound the fire alarm signal at least once every calendar month. [^ca-edc-32001-fire-drills] Every public, private or parochial school building with an occupant capacity of 50 or more pupils, or with more than one classroom, must have a dependable and operative fire alarm system, and the person in charge must sound the alarm on discovering a fire unless the school has an automatic fire detection and alarm system. [^ca-edc-32001-fire-alarm]

Earthquake drills come from a separate article of the Education Code addressed to private schools. The governing board of each private school must establish an earthquake emergency procedure system in every private school building under its jurisdiction with an occupant capacity of 50 or more pupils or more than one classroom. [^ca-edc-35296-earthquake-system-drills] That system must include a drop procedure, in which each student and staff member takes cover under a table or desk with the head protected and the back to the windows, practiced at least once each school quarter in elementary schools and at least once a semester in secondary schools. [^ca-edc-35297-drop-procedure]

## Must a California private school adopt a comprehensive school safety plan or an earthquake disaster plan? {#safety-and-disaster-plans}

**Short answer.** The comprehensive school safety plan required by Education Code § 32281 is the responsibility of each school district and county office of education, but § 35296 requires the governing board of each private school to establish an earthquake emergency procedure system in each qualifying building. [^ca-edc-32281-district-plans] [^ca-edc-35296-earthquake-system-plans]

Section 32281 assigns the overall development of comprehensive school safety plans to each school district and county office of education for its schools operating kindergarten or any of grades 1 to 12, and it does not name private schools. [^ca-edc-32281-district-plans] That conclusion is limited to § 32281: it does not address fire-code, building-code or local emergency-planning obligations, which this guide does not cover.

The private school earthquake system must include a school building disaster plan, ready for implementation at any time, for maintaining the safety and care of students and staff; a drop procedure; protective measures to be taken before, during and after an earthquake; and a program to make sure that students and both certificated and classified staff know, and are properly trained in, the system. [^ca-edc-35297-system-contents] The Legislature's stated reason is that all private elementary and high schools need school disaster plans, and specifically an earthquake emergency procedure system, so that pupils and staff act instinctively and correctly when an earthquake strikes. [^ca-edc-35295-findings] The governing board may work with the Office of Emergency Services and the Seismic Safety Commission to develop the system. [^ca-edc-35296-earthquake-system-plans]

A private school is also an employer, and unless an exemption applies, Labor Code § 6401.9 requires an employer's written workplace violence prevention plan to include procedures for workplace violence emergencies, including evacuation or sheltering plans appropriate and feasible for the worksite. [^ca-lab-6401-9-emergency-procedures-plans]

## Must a California private school have a written workplace violence prevention plan for its staff? {#workplace-violence-prevention-plan}

**Short answer.** Since July 1, 2024, Labor Code § 6401.9 has required every employer, including a private school, to establish, implement and maintain an effective written workplace violence prevention plan, subject to the exemptions the statute lists. [^ca-lab-6401-9-plan] [^ca-lab-6401-9-operative]

The section applies to all employers, employees, places of employment and employer-provided housing, except for listed health care, corrections and law enforcement employers, employees teleworking from a location of their choice that the employer does not control, and places of employment where fewer than 10 employees work at any given time that are not accessible to the public and comply with the injury and illness prevention program requirement the statute cross-references. [^ca-lab-6401-9-scope-exemptions] The Division of Occupational Safety and Health may order an exempt employer to comply. [^ca-lab-6401-9-scope-exemptions]

The statute's definition of workplace violence counts violence directed at employees by students or visitors as Type 2 violence, alongside violence by strangers, coworkers and people with a personal relationship to an employee. [^ca-lab-6401-9-violence-types] The plan must be in writing and easily accessible to employees at all times, and it may be a stand-alone section of the written injury and illness prevention program. [^ca-lab-6401-9-plan] It must include effective procedures for alerting employees to a workplace violence emergency, evacuation or sheltering plans, and how to obtain help from security personnel and law enforcement. [^ca-lab-6401-9-emergency-procedures] It must also be reviewed at least annually, when a deficiency becomes apparent and after a workplace violence incident. [^ca-lab-6401-9-annual-review]

The employer must record every workplace violence incident in a violent incident log. [^ca-lab-6401-9-log] Employees must receive training when the plan is first established and annually after that, covering the plan and how to participate in it, how to report incidents without fear of reprisal, job-specific hazards and the incident log, with an opportunity for interactive questions and answers with a person knowledgeable about the plan. [^ca-lab-6401-9-training] Hazard and incident-log records must be kept for at least five years and training records for at least one year. [^ca-lab-6401-9-records] The statute directs the Occupational Safety and Health Standards Board to adopt a standard on the plan no later than December 31, 2026. [^ca-lab-6401-9-standards]

## Must a California independent or religious school keep an AED on site, and what follows once it has one? {#aed-requirements}

**Short answer.** Health and Safety Code § 19300 requires an AED on the premises of an occupied Group E educational building with an occupancy of 200 or more that was built on or after January 1, 2017, or later renovated as the statute describes. [^ca-hsc-19300-covered-buildings] [^ca-hsc-19300-aed-required]

An older building is covered once it receives $100,000 of tenant improvements or of renovations in one calendar year, or any tenant improvement for a place of assembly, on or after January 1, 2020. [^ca-hsc-19300-covered-buildings] The requirement excludes structures owned or operated by a local government entity, and an existing AED in the structure's common area satisfies it. [^ca-hsc-19300-exclusions] Outside § 19300, Health and Safety Code § 1797.196 states that neither it nor the Civil Code's AED immunity section requires a building owner or manager to acquire an AED. [^ca-hsc-1797-196-no-duty] The Education Code's requirement to acquire an AED for each school with an interscholastic athletic program, § 35179.6, is addressed to school districts and charter schools. [^ca-edc-35179-6-districts]

Any person or entity that acquires an AED must comply with placement regulations, notify the local EMS agency of the device's existence, location and type, maintain and test it to the manufacturer's guidelines, test it at least biannually and after each use, inspect it at least every 90 days and keep maintenance and testing records. [^ca-hsc-1797-196-acquirer-duties] When an AED is placed in a public or private K–12 school, the principal must ensure that administrators and staff annually receive information on sudden cardiac arrest, the school's emergency response plan and proper AED use, post instructions in at least 14-point type next to every AED, and notify employees at least annually of where the AEDs are; at a school serving grades 6 to 12, the principal must also notify pupils annually. [^ca-hsc-1797-196-school-duties] An entity that acquires an AED and complies with § 1797.196(b) is not liable for civil damages from the use of the AED in emergency care, and a person who uses an AED in good faith and not for compensation is likewise protected. [^ca-civ-1714-21-immunity] Neither protection applies to gross negligence or willful or wanton misconduct. [^ca-civ-1714-21-gross-negligence]

Health and Safety Code § 124238.5 adds duties for a youth sports organization: from January 1, 2027, its coaches must hold CPR and AED certification renewed at least every two years and it must have a written cardiac emergency response plan, and from January 1, 2028, its athletes must have access to an AED at every official practice or match. [^ca-hsc-124238-5-youth-sports] The term has the meaning given in § 124235, which defines a youth sports organization as an organization, business, nonprofit entity or local governmental agency that sponsors or conducts amateur sports competitions, training, camps or clubs in which persons 17 or younger participate, and that definition contains no exception for schools. [^ca-hsc-124238-definitions] [^ca-hsc-124235-youth-sports-definition] On its face, that definition reaches a private school, as a nonprofit entity or business, that sponsors or conducts amateur sports competitions for students 17 or younger.

AB 387 (Stats. 2026, ch. 502), approved September 27, 2026 with no urgency clause, amends § 124238.5 from January 1, 2027. [^ca-ab-387-response-plan] A youth sports organization's written cardiac emergency response plan must be reviewed annually with coaches and staff and must detail the location of any AEDs and the procedures for sudden cardiac arrest, the responsibilities of the coach, administration and athletes, how they will be notified and trained, and an annual electronic communication to parents or guardians of enrolled participants with the plan or a link to it, the AED locations and the staff to be notified. [^ca-ab-387-response-plan] From January 1, 2028, a public or private local facility with a permanent sports infrastructure, meaning a fixed facility designed and maintained for organized sports that is regularly permitted, rented or leased for youth sports programs, that procures and maintains an AED must ensure that a youth sports organization using the facility for a permitted practice or match has access to it, and where no AED is available at the facility, the youth sports organization must ensure access to an operational one for the entire practice or match. [^ca-ab-387-infrastructure-defined] [^ca-ab-387-facility-access] The amended section also provides: "This section shall not be construed to require a public or private local facility or its employees to procure or install an AED or to operate or administer an AED in the event of a cardiac emergency or suspected cardiac emergency."[^ca-ab-387-no-duty-to-procure] A private school whose fields are regularly permitted or rented to outside youth sports programs therefore takes on the access duty only for an AED it has chosen to procure. [^ca-ab-387-no-duty-to-procure]

## Must a California private school stock emergency epinephrine or naloxone and train staff to give it? {#epinephrine-and-opioid-antagonists}

**Short answer.** Education Code §§ 49414 and 49414.3 leave each California private school to decide whether to make emergency epinephrine and naloxone or another opioid antagonist, with trained personnel, available at the school. [^ca-edc-49414-private-choice] [^ca-edc-49414-3-private-choice]

In making either decision, the school must evaluate the emergency medical response time to the school and decide whether calling emergency medical services is an acceptable alternative, and a private school that exercises the authority receives no state funds specifically for it. [^ca-edc-49414-private-choice] [^ca-edc-49414-3-private-choice] The stocking mandate in § 49414(a) is imposed on local educational agencies, which the section defines as schools operated by a school district, county office of education or charter school. [^ca-edc-49414-lea-mandate] [^ca-edc-49414-lea-definition]

Each public and private school may designate volunteers to receive initial and annual refresher training in the storage and emergency use of epinephrine delivery systems, given by the school nurse or another qualified person designated by an authorizing physician. [^ca-edc-49414-volunteers] The opioid antagonist section gives each public and private elementary and secondary school the same option for naloxone and bars granting or withholding a benefit based on an offer to volunteer and retaliation against anyone who withdraws the offer. [^ca-edc-49414-3-volunteers] A school nurse or, if none is available, a volunteer may administer naloxone or another opioid antagonist, volunteers only by nasal spray or auto-injector, to a person showing potentially life-threatening symptoms of an opioid overdose at school or a school activity when a physician is not immediately available, and a used or expiring supply must be restocked within two weeks after use and before its expiration date. [^ca-edc-49414-3-administration] A person trained under that section who administers it in good faith and not for compensation is not subject to professional review, civil liability or criminal prosecution, except for gross negligence or willful and wanton misconduct. [^ca-edc-49414-3-immunity]

The Education Code's rules on a pupil carrying and self-administering prescription epinephrine, in § 49423, and on pupils 12 and older carrying over-the-counter naloxone, in § 49414.35, are each defined by reference to a school district, county office of education or charter school. [^ca-edc-49423-lea-definition] [^ca-edc-49414-35-lea-definition]

## What concussion, sudden cardiac arrest and football practice rules apply to a California private school's sports programs? {#concussion-cardiac-and-football-rules}

**Short answer.** A California private school offering athletics must remove an athlete suspected of a concussion or head injury for at least the rest of the day and until a licensed provider clears the athlete in writing, and must sideline a student who faints during or after athletic activity until medical clearance. [^ca-edc-49475-removal] [^ca-edc-33479-1-school] [^ca-edc-33479-5-removal]

Each rule names private schools: the concussion section applies if a private school elects to offer an athletic program, and the sudden cardiac arrest article defines a school to include a private school that elects to conduct athletic activities. [^ca-edc-49475-removal] [^ca-edc-33479-1-school] If the provider finds a concussion or head injury, the athlete must also complete a graduated return-to-play protocol of at least seven days under a licensed health care provider's supervision, and each year the athlete and a parent or guardian must sign and return a concussion and head injury information sheet before practice or competition begins. [^ca-edc-49475-removal] The concussion rules do not apply to athletic activity during the regular school day or in a required physical education course. [^ca-edc-49475-pe-exclusion]

The cardiac article reaches interscholastic athletics, other school-sponsored contests including cheerleading and club sports, and practices and scrimmages for them. [^ca-edc-33479-1-school] A student who shows other symptoms of sudden cardiac arrest may be removed by an athletic trainer or authorized person who reasonably believes the symptoms are cardiac related, and without one present a coach who observes symptoms must notify the parent or guardian. [^ca-edc-33479-5-removal] A removed student may return only after written clearance from a physician and surgeon, or a nurse practitioner or physician assistant practicing under standardized procedures. [^ca-edc-33479-5-removal] Each year, before a student participates, the school must collect and retain the California Interscholastic Federation's sudden cardiac arrest information sheet or, for activities the Federation does not govern, a signed acknowledgment from the student and parent. [^ca-edc-33479-3-information-sheet] A coach must complete the sudden cardiac arrest training course and retake it every two years, and may not coach until the course is complete. [^ca-edc-33479-6-coach-training]

A private school with an athletic program must also limit high school and middle school football teams to two full-contact practices a week during the preseason and regular season, cap the full-contact portion of a practice at 90 minutes a day and hold no full-contact practice in the off-season. [^ca-edc-35179-5-football] The written emergency action plan for sudden cardiac arrest, concussion and heat illness required by Education Code § 35179.4 is addressed to school districts and charter schools. [^ca-edc-35179-4-districts]

## What limits does California law place on firearms and other weapons on private school grounds? {#firearms-and-weapons-on-school-grounds}

**Short answer.** California's Gun-Free School Zone Act makes it a crime to possess a firearm in a place the person knows, or reasonably should know, is within a public or private K–12 school's grounds or 1,000 feet of them, with listed exceptions including peace officers and authorized armed security guards. [^ca-pen-626-9-offense] [^ca-pen-626-9-school-zone] [^ca-pen-626-9-officers] [^ca-pen-626-9-security-guards]

The officer exception covers duly appointed California peace officers, on-duty peace officers of another state or the federal government, people they summon to assist, on-duty military members and armored vehicle guards, and the security guard exception covers a guard authorized to carry a loaded firearm under the Penal Code's article on that authority. [^ca-pen-626-9-officers] [^ca-pen-626-9-security-guards] A concealed-carry license holder may carry within the 1,000-foot zone but not within any building, real property or parking area under the control of a public or private K–12 school, or on an immediately adjacent street or sidewalk, except as § 26230(b), (c) or (e) allows. [^ca-pen-626-9-license-holders] A violation of the school-zone prohibition by possessing a firearm on the grounds of a public or private K–12 school is punishable by imprisonment for two, three or five years. [^ca-pen-626-9-penalty] The written-permission exceptions in § 626.9 are limited to the grounds of public or private universities and colleges, so the section gives a K–12 school no power to authorize a teacher or other employee to carry a firearm on campus. [^ca-pen-626-9-university-permission]

Penal Code § 626.10 separately makes it a public offense to bring or possess a dirk, dagger, ice pick, knife with a blade longer than 2½ inches, folding knife with a locking blade, unguarded razor, taser, stun gun, BB or pellet gun or spot marker gun on the grounds of a public or private K–12 school, with exceptions for listed officers. [^ca-pen-626-10-offense] The prohibition does not apply to listed blades and box cutters brought at the direction of a certificated or classified employee for a school-sponsored activity or class, or possessed for a lawful purpose within the scope of the person's employment. [^ca-pen-626-10-employee-exceptions] The school principal or a designee may give written permission for BB or pellet guns, spot marker guns, razor blades or box cutters. [^ca-pen-626-10-principal-permission] Any certificated or classified employee or school peace officer of a public or private K–12 school may seize a listed weapon from a person on school grounds if the employee knows, or has reasonable cause to know, that the person is prohibited from bringing or possessing it there. [^ca-pen-626-10-seizure] This answer does not address the separate statutory list of places where a concealed-carry license holder may not carry.
