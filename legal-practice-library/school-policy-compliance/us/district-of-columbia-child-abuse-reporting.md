# School Policy Compliance in the District of Columbia: Child Abuse Reporting and Prevention[^about]

What a District of Columbia independent or religious K–12 school's child-protection policies must reflect: mandatory reporting to the Child and Family Services Agency or the police, the adult duty to report child sexual abuse, the staff sexual abuse policy and training, hiring and reference rules, criminal background checks, truancy referrals and the student sexual harassment policy.

## Who must report suspected child abuse at a District of Columbia private school, and to whom? {#mandated-reporting}

**Short answer.** A teacher or school official at a District of Columbia private school must immediately report to the Child and Family Services Agency or the police on knowing or having reasonable cause to believe that a child known professionally has been abused, is in immediate danger of abuse or is neglected. [^dc-car-4-1321-02-reporters] [^dc-car-4-1321-02-duty] [^dc-car-4-1321-03-oral]

The list of mandatory reporters in D.C. Code § 4-1321.02(a) names "A school official"[^dc-car-4-1321-02-reporters], "A teacher"[^dc-car-4-1321-02-reporters] and "An athletic coach"[^dc-car-4-1321-02-reporters] without limiting any of those categories to public schools, so it reaches the staff of independent and religiously affiliated schools in the same terms. [^dc-car-4-1321-02-reporters] The duty covers a child the reporter knows in the professional capacity for which the reporter is designated, and it is triggered when the child has been, or is in immediate danger of being, abused; is a neglected child; has been, or is in immediate danger of being, the victim of sexual abuse or attempted sexual abuse prohibited by Chapter 30 of Title 22, or was assisted, induced, facilitated or permitted to engage in prostitution; or has a non-accidental injury caused by a bullet, knife or other sharp object. [^dc-car-4-1321-02-duty] Abused, for this purpose, takes its meaning from D.C. Code § 16-2301(23), which includes infliction of physical or mental injury and sexual abuse or exploitation, and from the sexual abuse and trafficking categories listed in § 4-1301.02(1)(A). [^dc-car-4-1301-02-abused] [^dc-car-16-2301-abused] The parent-discipline carve-out in § 16-2301(23)(B) is limited to physical discipline a parent, guardian or custodian administers to the parent's own child, so it does not reach discipline by school staff. [^dc-car-16-2301-abused]

The reporter makes an immediate oral report to the Child and Family Services Agency or the Metropolitan Police Department, and a written report if either agency asks for one or the abuse involves drug-related activity. [^dc-car-4-1321-03-oral] The Agency takes reports 24 hours a day at (202) 671-SAFE, or (202) 671-7233, and offers mandated reporters an online portal for non-emergency reports. [^dc-car-cfsa-hotline] A reporter who learns of abuse as an employee, agent or contractor of a school must also immediately notify the person in charge of the institution or a designated agent, who then makes a report; that internal notice does not relieve the original reporter of the duty to report. [^dc-car-4-1321-02-institution] Internal approval therefore cannot stand in place of the report itself. [^dc-car-4-1321-02-institution]

The statute's exclusions are narrow: a reporter employed or supervised by a lawyer need not report what is learned solely in the course of a representation or a prospective client's request for representation, and the duty does not apply to domestic violence, human trafficking or sexual assault counselors "while acting in their capacity as a counselor"[^dc-car-4-1321-02-exclusions]. [^dc-car-4-1321-02-exclusions] A mandatory reporter who willfully fails to report may be fined, imprisoned up to 180 days, or both. [^dc-car-4-1321-07-penalty] A person or institution that participates in good faith in making a report is immune from civil and criminal liability, and good faith is presumed unless rebutted. [^dc-car-4-1321-04-immunity]

## Must every adult at a District of Columbia private school, not only teachers, report suspected child sexual abuse to the police? {#adult-sexual-abuse-reporting}

**Short answer.** Every person 18 or older in the District of Columbia, including every adult on a private school's staff, must immediately report to the police on knowing or having reasonable cause to believe that a child under 16 is a victim of sexual abuse, subject to listed exceptions. [^dc-car-22-3020-52-duty] [^dc-car-22-3020-51-definitions]
The exceptions cover a victim whose own abuser is the alleged perpetrator, a lawyer or lawyer's employee whose knowledge arises solely in a representation, a minister or Christian Science practitioner who learns of the abuse through a qualifying confession, and domestic violence, human trafficking and sexual assault counselors as to confidential communications. [^dc-car-22-3020-52-exceptions]

Sexual abuse, for this duty, means a violation of the District's sexual abuse offenses or of the other offenses the definition lists, and a call to 911 or a report to the Child and Family Services Agency counts as a report to the police. [^dc-car-22-3020-51-definitions] [^dc-car-22-3020-52-duty] The duty runs to every adult, not to a list of professions, so it reaches an administrative assistant, a groundskeeper or an adult volunteer who is not a mandatory reporter under § 4-1321.02. [^dc-car-22-3020-51-definitions] [^dc-car-22-3020-52-duty] Section 22-3020.52(d) states that it should not be construed as altering the special duty of the mandatory reporters listed in § 4-1321.02(a), and § 4-1321.02(e) provides that a person who violates § 4-1321.02 is not prosecuted under this adult-reporting subchapter. [^dc-car-22-3020-52-exceptions] [^dc-car-4-1321-02-not-prosecuted]

For a religiously affiliated school, the confession exception is limited to a priest, clergyman, rabbi or other duly appointed, licensed, ordained or consecrated minister, or an accredited Christian Science practitioner, whose knowledge comes from a confession or penitential communication made directly to the minister in confidence, expressly for a spiritual or religious purpose, in the minister's professional capacity and in the course of discipline enjoined by the religious body; "A confession or communication made under any other circumstances does not fall under this exemption."[^dc-car-22-3020-52-exceptions] [^dc-car-22-3020-52-exceptions] The § 4-1321.02 duty described in the [question on who must report](#mandated-reporting) attaches to a child a mandatory reporter knows "in their professional capacity for which they have been designated as a mandatory reporter"[^dc-car-4-1321-02-duty-clergy], so a member of the clergy who also teaches carries the teacher's duty for what the member learns as a teacher. [^dc-car-4-1321-02-duty-clergy]

A person required to report under this subchapter who willfully fails to do so is subject to a civil fine of $300, adjudicated by the Office of Administrative Hearings. [^dc-car-22-3020-54-penalty] A good-faith reporter is immune from civil and criminal liability for making the report and for participating in any judicial proceeding involving it. A person discharged or discriminated against in employment because of a good-faith report may sue, and if the court finds that the person was required to report, reported in good faith and suffered that treatment as a result, it may order appropriate relief, including reinstatement with back pay. [^dc-car-22-3020-55-immunity]

## What must a District of Columbia private school's policy on student sexual abuse by staff contain, and what abuse training must go with it? {#staff-sexual-abuse-policy}

**Short answer.** A District of Columbia independent, private or parochial school must adopt, implement and publish a policy to prevent and address student sexual abuse by staff, and must train staff on child abuse and sexual misconduct at hiring and at least every two years. [^dc-car-38-951-01-school] [^dc-car-38-951-02-policy] [^dc-car-38-951-02-publish] [^dc-car-38-951-02-training]

Chapter 9A of Title 38 defines a school as "a public, public charter, independent, private, or parochial school organized or authorized to operate under the laws of the District"[^dc-car-38-951-01-school] offering any grade from pre-kindergarten through 12th grade, and it defines staff to include volunteers and employees of a contractor who act as the school's agents. [^dc-car-38-951-01-school] The policy must include a protocol for the school's response to an allegation of student sexual abuse by staff, including compliance with the reporting duties in §§ 4-1321.02 and 22-3020.52 [^dc-car-4-1321-02-duty-policy] [^dc-car-22-3020-52-duty-policy]; a protocol for contacting the Child and Family Services Agency and informing school leadership; a protocol for informing the school community that protects the investigation and confidentiality; avenues for parents to report; mechanisms implementing 20 U.S.C. § 7926, with penalties for staff who violate it [^dc-car-usc-7926-a-policy]; guidance limiting staff–student communication outside school, including electronic and telephone contact; and a list of resources for affected students and families. [^dc-car-38-951-02-policy] The school must give the policy to staff, parents and, in a developmentally appropriate manner, students, and must make it publicly available, including on the school website. [^dc-car-38-951-02-publish]

Student sexual abuse means sexual abuse, as defined for the universal adult reporting duty, committed against a student, and sexual misconduct means any communication or act directed toward a student that is designed to establish a sexual relationship, such as dating, sexual dialogue or sexually suggestive comments. [^dc-car-38-951-01-terms] Staff training covers recognizing and reporting sexual misconduct, student sexual abuse and child abuse; receiving disclosures in a supportive, trauma-informed manner; prevention and warning signs; and communicating with students and parents. [^dc-car-38-951-02-training] The school must also give parents training and information on those subjects every year. [^dc-car-38-951-02-training] The Office of the State Superintendent of Education must maintain a model policy that may be used to satisfy the policy requirement. [^dc-car-38-951-02-model] The Office states that it revised its model policies in June 2025 to align with the 2020 Title IX rule and to reinforce the adult duty to report child sexual abuse, the reporting duty when a student asks to remain anonymous, and the prohibition on assisting the employment of perpetrators. [^dc-car-osse-ssoaa-2025] A separate policy and training requirement for student-on-student sexual harassment, sexual assault and dating violence is addressed in the [question on student policies](#student-harassment-and-bullying-policies).

## Must a District of Columbia private school check a job applicant's child abuse and sexual misconduct history with past employers before hiring? {#employment-history-review}

**Short answer.** The District of Columbia's pre-hiring due diligence statute, D.C. Code § 38-951.03, binds the District of Columbia Public Schools, public charter schools and their contracted service providers, not independent, private or parochial schools. [^dc-car-38-951-03-lea-duty] [^dc-car-38-951-01-lea]

Section 38-951.03(a) applies to a local education agency, which § 38-951.01(2) defines as the District of Columbia Public Schools system or a public charter school or group of charter schools under one charter, and to that agency's contracted service providers. [^dc-car-38-951-03-lea-duty] [^dc-car-38-951-01-lea] For those employers, a position involving direct interaction with students requires the applicant's current employer and previous employers for the preceding 20 years whose employment involved direct interaction with children, at least one character reference, a release and a written affirmation about child abuse or sexual misconduct investigations (other than those finding the allegations false or unsubstantiated), separations and license actions, followed by a criminal background check, contact with each former employer in the preceding seven years or the previous three employers, whichever period is longer, contact with the character reference, and searches of the National Sex Offender Public Registry and the national teacher-credential clearinghouse. [^dc-car-38-951-03-lea-duty] The Office of the State Superintendent of Education describes those duties as applying to local education agencies, except those that exclusively serve students over 18 years of age, and their contracted service providers. [^dc-car-osse-ssoaa-lea]

A private entity that is a contracted service provider of a local education agency is covered in that capacity. [^dc-car-38-951-03-lea-duty]

A private school is nonetheless on the receiving end of the public system's records: each local education agency or contracted service provider must keep a record of any allegation against staff of sexual misconduct, child abuse or failure to report child abuse, with the outcome of any investigation, and must provide it to "another local education agency or school that is considering hiring the individual when contacted"[^dc-car-38-951-03-records]. [^dc-car-38-951-03-records] Because Chapter 9A defines school to include independent, private and parochial schools, a private school considering a former public or charter school employee may request that record. [^dc-car-38-951-03-records] [^dc-car-38-951-01-school-hiring] The private school's own staff sexual abuse policy must include mechanisms implementing 20 U.S.C. § 7926, which governs help given to a departing employee and is addressed in the [question on references and separation agreements](#agreements-assisting-new-employment). [^dc-car-38-951-02-7926-hiring] [^dc-car-usc-7926-a-hiring] Criminal background checks are addressed in the [question on criminal background checks](#criminal-background-checks).

## Can a District of Columbia private school give a neutral reference or a separation agreement to an employee suspected of sexual misconduct with a student? {#agreements-assisting-new-employment}

**Short answer.** A District of Columbia private school's abuse policy must implement 20 U.S.C. § 7926, which bars helping a school employee obtain a new job when the helper knows or has probable cause to believe the employee engaged in unlawful sexual misconduct with a minor or student, with exceptions. [^dc-car-38-951-02-7926] [^dc-car-usc-7926-a]
The bar does not reach the routine transmission of administrative and personnel files, and it lifts only once the information has been properly reported to a law enforcement agency with jurisdiction and to any other authority the law requires, and the matter has been officially closed or found to lack probable cause, the person has been charged and acquitted or otherwise exonerated, or no charge or indictment has followed within four years of the report to law enforcement. [^dc-car-usc-7926-a] [^dc-car-usc-7926-b]

Section 7926(a) is addressed to States, state educational agencies and funded local educational agencies, which must have laws, regulations or policies prohibiting any school employee, contractor or agent from assisting a school employee, contractor or agent in obtaining a new job on those facts. [^dc-car-usc-7926-a] D.C. Code § 38-951.02(a)(5) carries that prohibition into the policy every District school must adopt, including an independent, private or parochial school, and requires the policy to state penalties for staff who violate it. [^dc-car-38-951-02-7926] [^dc-car-38-951-01-school-7926] The Office of the State Superintendent of Education reads the District statute and federal law together as prohibiting schools from assisting employees known or suspected to have committed sexual misconduct with a minor from gaining future employment. [^dc-car-osse-ssoaa-assist] Section 7926(d) leaves room for a State to adopt greater protections. [^dc-car-usc-7926-b]

Neither statute defines the word assisting, and in our review a CourtListener search found no opinion citing D.C. Code §§ 38-951.01 through 38-951.03 or quoting § 7926(a)'s operative words, so we found no decision applying either provision to a reference or separation agreement given by a District private school. [^dc-car-usc-7926-a] [^dc-car-38-951-02-7926] A favorable reference, a promise of a neutral reference that conceals a pending allegation, or a resignation package designed to let the employee move to another school fits the ordinary meaning of assisting a person in obtaining a new job, so the conservative course is to limit what the school provides to the routine transmission of administrative and personnel files unless one of the § 7926(b) exceptions applies. [^dc-car-usc-7926-a] [^dc-car-usc-7926-b] Whatever the school agrees with a departing employee, the duties to report under D.C. Code § 4-1321.02 and § 22-3020.52 rest on the individual reporter and do not depend on any agreement. [^dc-car-4-1321-02-duty-agreements] [^dc-car-22-3020-52-duty-agreements]

## Must a District of Columbia private school run criminal background checks on staff, and when may it ask applicants about criminal records? {#criminal-background-checks}

**Short answer.** D.C. Code § 4-1501.03 requires criminal background checks only at a covered child or youth services provider, which reaches a private school only if the District licenses it or contracts with it to provide direct services to children or youth. [^dc-car-4-1501-03-required] [^dc-car-4-1501-02-covered]

The definition covers District agencies and "any private entity that is licensed by or contracts with the District"[^dc-car-4-1501-02-covered] to provide direct services to children or youth, or services for their benefit, that affect their health, safety and welfare. It excludes foster parents and grantees, except that it includes an employee or volunteer of a host employer or grantee in a District youth employment program who has direct contact with or unsupervised access to participating youth. [^dc-car-4-1501-02-covered] A private elementary or secondary school engaged in legally required educational functions is excluded from the definition of a child development facility under the child care licensing statute, so the school's K–12 program does not become a licensee by that route. [^dc-car-7-2031-exclusion] Where the chapter applies, applicants for paid employment, employees, and unsupervised volunteers must apply for checks through the FBI and Metropolitan Police Department record systems. [^dc-car-4-1501-03-required] [^dc-car-4-1501-02-covered] Positions without direct contact with children and youth, volunteers with only supervised contact, and holders of an active federal security clearance are exempt; an applicant may be offered employment contingent on a satisfactory check and may work in a supervised setting meanwhile; a volunteer with direct contact may not begin volunteering unsupervised until satisfactory results arrive; and employees and unsupervised volunteers working in an unsupervised setting are subject to periodic checks. [^dc-car-4-1501-03-required] A local education agency must also run a check under this chapter as part of its pre-hiring due diligence, as described in the [question on the employment history review](#employment-history-review). [^dc-car-38-951-03-check]

The District's Fair Criminal Record Screening law applies to an employer with more than 10 employees in the District and bars questions about arrests and about accusations that are not pending or did not lead to conviction, and it bars conviction questions until after a conditional offer. [^dc-car-32-1341-employer] [^dc-car-32-1342-inquiries] Those prohibitions "shall not apply"[^dc-car-32-1342-inquiries] where federal or District law requires consideration of criminal history, to a position in a government program designed to employ people with criminal histories, or "To any facility or employer that provides programs, services, or direct care to minors or vulnerable adults."[^dc-car-32-1342-inquiries] [^dc-car-32-1342-inquiries] A K–12 school provides programs and services to minors, so on the statute's text the timing limits do not reach its hiring. [^dc-car-32-1342-inquiries]

## When must a District of Columbia private school refer a student's unexcused absences to the Child and Family Services Agency? {#truancy-referrals}

**Short answer.** A District of Columbia private or parochial school must refer a student aged 5 through 13 to the Child and Family Services Agency no later than two business days after the student accrues 10 unexcused full school day absences within a school year. [^dc-car-38-208-referrals] [^dc-car-38-201-institution]

The attendance statute defines an educational institution to include an independent school, a private school, a parochial school and a private instructor, so the referral duty reaches nonpublic schools in the same terms as public ones. [^dc-car-38-201-institution] The referral is made "pursuant to § 4-1321.02(b)(1)(B)"[^dc-car-38-208-referrals], the mandatory reporter provision that requires a reporter who knows a child aged 5 through 13 in a professional capacity to report 10 or more days of unexcused absences within a school year to the Agency. [^dc-car-38-208-referrals] [^dc-car-4-1321-02-truancy] For a student aged 14 through 17, the school instead refers the student to the Court Social Services Division of the Superior Court and the Office of the Attorney General Juvenile Section within two business days after the 15th unexcused full school day absence. [^dc-car-38-208-referrals]

The school has discretion over either referral when the 10th or 15th unexcused absence falls within the final 10 school days of the school year. [^dc-car-38-208-referrals] Within two business days of the 10th unexcused absence, the school must also notify the Office of the State Superintendent of Education, which sends the parent its truancy prevention resource guide. [^dc-car-38-208-osse-notice] The same section sets up Department of Human Services truancy pilots at schools the Mayor selects. [^dc-car-38-208-pilot] In School Year 2026–27, a selected school must refer a student aged 14 through 17 to the Department of Human Services within two school days after 15 unexcused full-day absences, and a student aged 10 through 13 within two school days after 10, [^dc-car-38-208-pilot-current] and it is exempt from the subsection (c) referral requirements only for students 14 through 17, so the Child and Family Services Agency referral still applies to its students aged 5 through 13. [^dc-car-38-208-pilot-older] [^dc-car-38-208-referrals]

## Must a District of Columbia private school adopt a student anti-bullying policy or a policy on student-on-student sexual harassment? {#student-harassment-and-bullying-policies}

**Short answer.** A District of Columbia private school must adopt and publish a policy to prevent and address student-on-student sexual harassment, sexual assault and dating violence, but the District's bullying prevention statute requires a bullying policy from it only if it is a District grantee. [^dc-car-38-952-01-school] [^dc-car-38-952-02-policy] [^dc-car-2-1535-01-scope] [^dc-car-2-1535-03-policy]

Chapter 9B of Title 38 defines a school to include an independent, private or parochial school offering any grade from pre-kindergarten through 12. [^dc-car-38-952-01-school] Its policy must include a statement prohibiting student-on-student sexual harassment, sexual assault and dating violence that acknowledges the school's duty to take immediate and appropriate action to investigate when it knows or reasonably should know of such acts; response procedures to stop each act, prevent its recurrence and address its effects whether or not there is a criminal investigation, refer and inform complainants, resolve discipline and protect complainants' confidentiality; the school's plan to meet its obligations under the federal and District laws the statute lists, including mechanisms to protect complainants' safety and a prompt, fair and impartial complaint procedure run by officials who receive annual training, giving both parties the same opportunity to have an advisor or advocate present, setting a standard for resolving complaints and requiring contemporaneous written notice of the result, any appeal procedure, any change and when the result becomes final; a protocol for counseling and intervention that includes reporting to the Child and Family Services Agency when a student's behavior suggests the student may be a victim of abuse, guidance on conduct at school events, online and in transit, and a resource list. [^dc-car-38-952-02-policy] Sexual harassment, for this purpose, is unwelcome sexual conduct that can reasonably be predicted to place the victim in reasonable fear of physical harm, substantially harm the victim's physical or mental health, or substantially interfere with the victim's academic performance, attendance or participation. [^dc-car-38-952-01-harassment] The school must train staff on these subjects at hiring and at least every two years, give parents information on warning signs and on discussing these topics, and provide the policy to staff, parents and students and post it publicly, including on its website. [^dc-car-38-952-02-training]

The District's bullying prevention statute, D.C. Code § 2-1535.01 et seq., requires a bullying prevention policy from each District agency, educational institution and grantee. [^dc-car-2-1535-03-policy] It defines an educational institution as "any local education agency that receives funds from the District of Columbia"[^dc-car-2-1535-01-scope], and a grantee as an entity that, on behalf of the District government or through District funding, provides services, activities or privileges to youth. [^dc-car-2-1535-01-scope] A private school that is not a District grantee therefore falls outside that statute, while one that provides youth services through District funding is covered as a grantee. [^dc-car-2-1535-01-scope] A covered policy must include the statutory definition of bullying, a prohibition, the expected code of conduct, consequences, reporting and investigation procedures, an appeal process and a statement prohibiting retaliation. [^dc-car-2-1535-03-policy]
