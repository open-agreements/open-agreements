# Negotiating and Drafting Small-Business Asset Purchase Agreements in the United States[^about]

How the buyer and seller of a profitable small or online business negotiate, draft and carry out an asset purchase agreement, from the choice of structure and the assets sold to price, remedies, closing and the transfer of each asset.

## What should an APA cover when buying a small business? {#small-business-apa-overview}

**Short answer.** When a small business is sold through an asset purchase agreement (APA), federal tax guidance generally treats each asset as sold separately and requires both the buyer and the seller to allocate the price among the business assets transferred. [^p1-irs-sale-each-asset][^p1-irs-residual-method-both-parties]

This guide addresses purchases of profitable businesses with $1 million or less of earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA), such as online and software businesses, local service businesses and restaurants, and not pre-profit or venture-backed acquisitions; the EBITDA figure is a scope choice, not a legal threshold. The guide covers negotiating, drafting and carrying out the agreement and transferring the assets, not the investigation of the business before signing, for which the [OpenAgreements Due Diligence Request List](/templates/openagreements-due-diligence-request-list) lists documents and information a buyer can request.

This guide is not a 50-state survey; it uses Delaware statutes to illustrate corporate and Uniform Commercial Code rules because Delaware's Division of Corporations reports that over two-thirds of the Fortune 500 call Delaware their corporate home, [^p1-delaware-corporate-home] and it uses California and New York for decisions and statutes that change an asset buyer's exposure, such as California's product-line successor-liability decision [^p1-ray-product-line-example] and New York's advance notice for bulk sales of business assets. [^p1-ny-bulk-sale-notice-example]

The asset-by-asset structure matches federal tax guidance, under which the IRS generally treats each asset of a sold business as sold separately for determining the treatment of gain or loss. [^p1-irs-sale-each-asset]

For an operating business, the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) points a buyer to the contracts, leases, cash flow and inventory of the business. [^sba-operating-business-diligence] An online business also depends on assets such as its websites, domain names and social-media accounts, which one filed agreement for an online publishing business transferred as listed on a schedule, together with the website content and software code; [^d1-salem-websites-overview] [Buying an App Business](/practice-guides/buying-an-app-business/us) treats [domain names](/practice-guides/buying-an-app-business/us#domain-closing), [provider accounts](/practice-guides/buying-an-app-business/us#migration-timing), [code and hosting projects](/practice-guides/buying-an-app-business/us#website-migration) and [app-store listings](/practice-guides/buying-an-app-business/us#app-transfer) separately.

For licenses and permits, the SBA describes two routes: obtaining them from the current owner or applying for new ones. [^p1-sba-licenses-from-owner]

Transferring contracts in an asset purchase typically triggers any anti-assignment clause and may require the counterparty's consent, [^potomac-asset-purchase-anti-assignment] so the parties decide whether closing waits for a missing [consent](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-closing-and-handover/us#consent-failure), a lawful interim arrangement applies, or the affected asset is excluded with a price change.

The price allocation has tax consequences for each side, because the IRS requires an allocation of the purchase price to determine the buyer's basis in each acquired asset and the seller's gain or loss on each asset; the [allocation question](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#purchase-price-allocation) addresses the details. [^p1-i8594-allocation-basis-gain]

The underlying contracts, the issuing authorities and the applicable state or industry rules decide whether a particular asset, license or permit transfers; the SBA separately directs a buyer to identify the federal, state and local permits and licenses the business needs. [^p1-sba-permit-levels]
