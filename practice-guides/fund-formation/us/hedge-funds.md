# Hedge Fund Formation: Adviser Registration, Offering Exemptions, and Fund Documents[^about]

A plain-language guide for hedge fund managers, and for the smaller investors they admit, to forming a U.S. hedge fund, covering adviser registration, managers outside the United States, who can be charged a performance fee, Rule 506(b) and 506(c) offerings, the Section 3(c)(1) and 3(c)(7) exclusions, anti-fraud rules, and Form D, with separate guides to the limited partnership agreement and the private placement memorandum.

Hedge fund formation combines entity law, securities law, adviser regulation, tax, and negotiated economics. A hedge fund is an open-end private fund: investors contribute capital when they subscribe and can withdraw it periodically. This guide covers U.S. hedge funds; private equity and other closed-end drawdown funds work differently and are not covered here. It is written first for the manager of an emerging or small hedge fund, who can use it to identify the questions the fund's documents must answer and then work with fund counsel on the answers for the particular fund. It is written second for a smaller investor who may not have counsel of their own: the same questions show what a hedge fund's documents let the manager charge, change, or withhold, and where an investor's rights depend on a notice period, a vote, or the manager's discretion.

**Documents.** The limited partnership agreement and the private placement memorandum have their own guides: the [hedge fund limited partnership agreement guide](/practice-guides/fund-formation/us/hedge-fund-limited-partnership-agreement) and the [hedge fund private placement memorandum guide](/practice-guides/fund-formation/us/hedge-fund-private-placement-memorandum). The questions on this page cover the rules that apply across the fund's documents: adviser registration, a manager outside the United States, who can be charged a performance fee, Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c) offerings, Section 3(c)(1) and 3(c)(7) funds, the anti-fraud rules, Form D, and what the subscription agreement and investor questionnaire should say.

## Does a hedge fund manager need to register as an investment adviser? {#hedge-fund-manager-investment-adviser-registration}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund manager generally must register as an investment adviser with the SEC or a state securities regulator unless an exemption applies, such as the exemption for a U.S. adviser that acts solely for qualifying private funds and manages less than $150 million in private-fund assets. [^adviser-registration-baseline] [^private-fund-adviser-exemption]

The SEC's staff states the baseline this way: "Private fund advisers are generally investment advisers that are required to register with the SEC or applicable state securities regulators as a registered investment adviser, unless they are exempt from applicable registration requirements (for example, as an exempt reporting adviser)."[^adviser-registration-baseline] [^adviser-registration-baseline]

An exempt adviser still has obligations. The SEC's glossary explains: "While exempt reporting advisers are not registered with the SEC, they are still subject to specific reporting requirements and certain other Investment Advisers Act and federal rules, and they also may be subject to state filing or registration requirements."[^sec-glossary-exempt-reporting-adviser] [^sec-glossary-exempt-reporting-adviser] Whether a manager is registered or required to be registered also decides whether the custody rule and the performance-fee restriction discussed elsewhere in this guide apply to it.

## What should the fund documents say when the investment manager is outside the United States? {#hedge-fund-investment-manager-outside-the-united-states}

**Short answer.** The fund documents should state the manager's status under the Investment Advisers Act, because the performance-fee restriction applies only to an adviser registered or required to be registered with the SEC. [^non-us-performance-fee-scope]

A manager whose principal office and place of business is outside the United States may be registered with the SEC, exempt as a non-U.S. private fund adviser, or exempt as a foreign private adviser. [^non-us-private-fund-adviser] [^non-us-foreign-private-adviser-exemption] A foreign private adviser has no place of business in the United States and fewer than 15 clients and investors in the United States in its private funds, among other conditions. [^non-us-foreign-private-adviser-definition]

For a manager that is neither registered nor required to register with the SEC, the federal performance-fee restriction does not apply, so under federal law a requirement that every investor be a qualified client is a condition of the fund's own choosing. [^non-us-performance-fee-scope] The condition still has a purpose, because Rule 205-3 provides that "section 205(a)(1) of the Act will apply with regard to a natural person or company who was not a party to the contract and becomes a party (including an equity owner of a private investment company advised by the adviser) when the adviser is required to register."[^non-us-qualified-client-transition] [^non-us-qualified-client-transition] The [Hedge Fund Limited Partnership Agreement](/templates/openagreements-hedge-fund-limited-partnership-agreement) makes qualified-client status a condition of admission whether or not the rule applies, and lets the general partner waive it by side letter only while the manager is neither registered nor required to register, with the SEC or under a state law that applies the same restriction. The non-U.S. manager subscription documents keep the test and that waiver on the same terms, so an investor admitted after the manager must register is screened as Rule 205-3 requires.

The manager's location does not take it outside the offering's bad-actor rule: Rule 506(d) lists among the covered persons "any investment manager of an issuer that is a pooled investment fund"[^non-us-bad-actor-covered-persons], together with that manager's general partners, managing members, directors, executive officers, and other officers participating in the offering. [^non-us-bad-actor-covered-persons]

The SEC's Form ADV Part 2A brochure requirements apply to an adviser registered with, or applying for registration with, the SEC, and not to an exempt reporting adviser. [^non-us-form-adv-brochure] For a manager exempt from SEC registration, the non-U.S. manager subscription agreement below has the investor acknowledge receiving, in the memorandum or a written supplement to it, a description of the manager's regulatory status, any regulator that supervises it at home, and where it keeps its books and records.

A non-U.S. manager that relies on the foreign private adviser exemption has to count its investors in the United States. The SEC explained that "A non-U.S. adviser would need to count the same U.S. investors (except for holders of short-term paper with respect to a fund relying on section 3(c)(1)) in order to rely on the foreign private adviser exemption."[^non-us-counting-investors] [^non-us-counting-investors] Rule 202(a)(30)-1 treats an investor as in the United States when it is a U.S. person under Regulation S, with an exception for certain discretionary accounts. [^non-us-in-the-united-states]

The [Hedge Fund Subscription Agreement (Manager Outside the U.S.)](/templates/openagreements-hedge-fund-subscription-agreement-non-us-manager) and the [Hedge Fund Investor Questionnaire (Manager Outside the U.S.)](/templates/openagreements-hedge-fund-investor-questionnaire-non-us-manager) make these changes to the standard subscription documents. They also have the subscriber acknowledge the privacy notice the manager provides under its home-country law, a notice the manager supplies separately, and extend the anti-money-laundering and sanctions representations to the manager's home jurisdiction.

## Who can be charged a hedge fund performance fee? {#who-can-be-charged-a-hedge-fund-performance-fee}

**Short answer.** An adviser registered or required to be registered with the SEC may charge a performance fee to an investor in a hedge fund relying on section 3(c)(1) only if that investor is a qualified client under Rule 205-3. [^qualified-client-statutory-baseline] [^qualified-client-rule]

The restriction does not apply to an advisory contract with a fund relying on section 3(c)(7). [^advisers-act-205-b-4] For contracts governed by the SEC's June 29, 2026 order, a client qualifies with at least $1.4 million under the adviser's management immediately after entering the contract, or with a net worth of more than $2.7 million immediately before. [^qualified-client-current-order] In a private investment company, each equity owner charged the fee is treated as the client, and an owner not charged it is not. [^qualified-client-pooled-fund-lookthrough]

So the rule limits who may be charged. It does not itself require a fund to turn away an investor who will not be charged. Some fund documents, including the [Hedge Fund Limited Partnership Agreement](/templates/openagreements-hedge-fund-limited-partnership-agreement), still require every investor to be a qualified client, as a contractual choice rather than a legal requirement. That template lets the general partner waive the contractual screen for a particular investor by side letter, but only where the manager is neither registered nor required to be registered with the SEC, so that section 205(a)(1) does not reach it, [^qualified-client-statutory-baseline] and, as further conditions the template itself sets, where no state applies the same restriction to the manager under its own law and where the general partner has confirmed that the manager relies on no state registration exemption conditioned on every investor being a qualified client. If a later change would make charging the performance fee to a waived investor unlawful, the template stops charging it to that investor. The template's offering-eligibility requirement, that an investor be accredited or otherwise eligible under the exemption the offering relies on, may not be waived.

## How do Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c) differ? {#rule-506b-vs-rule-506c}

**Short answer.** Rule 506(b) bars general solicitation of a hedge fund's investors, while Rule 506(c) permits it if every purchaser is accredited and the fund takes reasonable steps to verify that status. [^offering-general-solicitation-limit] [^offering-rule-506c] [^offering-rule-506c-verification]

The SEC staff’s glossary calls section 4(a)(2) the private-placement exemption and describes Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c) as pathways. [^sec-4a2] Rule 506(b) permits up to 35 non-accredited purchasers in a 90-day period when they have the required knowledge or purchaser representative. [^offering-rule-506b-purchasers] Selling to a non-accredited purchaser also triggers specified information requirements. [^offering-nonaccredited-information]

Rule 506(c) allows broader solicitation only with accredited purchasers and reasonable verification. [^offering-rule-506c] [^offering-rule-506c-verification] Both Rule 506 paths retain resale restrictions and bad-actor disqualification. [^offering-resale-limit] [^offering-bad-actor-disqualification]

## What is the difference between a 3(c)(1) fund and a 3(c)(7) fund? {#section-3c1-vs-section-3c7-funds}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund relying on section 3(c)(1) of the Investment Company Act may have no more than 100 beneficial owners, while one relying on section 3(c)(7) may be owned only by qualified purchasers. [^private-fund-section-3c1] [^private-fund-section-3c7]

Neither exclusion is available to a fund making a public offering. [^private-fund-section-3c1] [^private-fund-section-3c7] The SEC's glossary defines private funds by these two exclusions: "A private fund is a pooled investment vehicle that is excluded from the definition of investment company by Section 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7) of the Investment Company Act—commonly referred to as a 3(c)(1) Fund or a 3(c)(7) Fund."[^sec-glossary-private-fund] [^sec-glossary-private-fund]

The SEC staff explains that an investment company includes an issuer primarily engaged in investing, reinvesting, or trading securities. [^fast-company] "These companies are commonly known as ‘private investment companies.’ Some private investment companies are commonly known as ‘hedge funds.’"[^fast-private] [^fast-private] The 3(c)(1) count has a look-through rule for certain company investors. [^private-fund-section-3c1-lookthrough]

Accredited investor is a Regulation D sale-eligibility status. [^investor-gate-accredited-investor] Qualified purchaser is the 3(c)(7) ownership status; for a natural person it generally requires at least $5 million in investments. [^investor-gate-qualified-purchaser] Qualified client is a separate performance-compensation status. [^exclusions-qualified-client-rule]

## Do anti-fraud rules apply to a hedge fund offering? {#anti-fraud-rules-for-hedge-fund-offerings}

**Short answer.** Federal anti-fraud rules apply to a hedge fund offering even though it is exempt from registration, so material misstatements and misleading omissions in its offering documents can create liability. [^sec-antifraud] [^antifraud-securities-act-section-17a] [^antifraud-rule-10b5] [^pooled-vehicle-antifraud-rule]

The SEC’s staff says, "Antifraud provisions of the federal securities laws broadly apply to all funds and advisers, whether or not they are otherwise subject to or required to register under other provisions of the federal securities laws."[^sec-antifraud] [^sec-antifraud] Securities Act section 17(a) governs fraud in offers and sales. [^antifraud-securities-act-section-17a] Exchange Act Rule 10b-5 governs deceptive statements and conduct in purchases or sales. [^antifraud-rule-10b5] Rule 206(4)-8 separately governs an adviser’s statements to pooled-vehicle investors and prospective investors. [^pooled-vehicle-antifraud-rule]

## What is Form D, and when is it filed? {#form-d-filing}

**Short answer.** Form D is an SEC notice for a Regulation D offering, ordinarily filed within 15 days after the first investor becomes irrevocably contractually committed to invest. [^sec-form] [^form-d-filing-rule]

The SEC states that a deadline falling on a Saturday, Sunday, or holiday moves to the next business day. [^sec-form] Rule 503(a) supplies the notice requirement. [^form-d-filing-rule] The SEC staff says a Regulation D offering does not itself exempt a placement agent from broker-dealer registration. [^placement-agent-reg-d-not-exempt] Solicitation, negotiation, and transaction-based compensation are among staff registration indicators. [^placement-agent-registration-indicators]

## What should a hedge fund subscription agreement say about subscribing and admission? {#hedge-fund-subscription-agreement-admission}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund subscription agreement is the contract through which an investor agrees to invest, and under Delaware law an investor admitted after the fund's formation becomes a limited partner at the time and on the conditions the partnership agreement provides or, if it is silent, on the consent of all partners. [^sa-sec-subscription-agreement] [^sa-delaware-admission]

Because Delaware admits a limited partner as the partnership agreement provides, a subscription agreement that states admission in the partnership agreement's own terms, binds the investor to the partnership agreement on admission, and lets the partnership agreement govern a conflict avoids creating a second admission test that could disagree with the first. [^sa-delaware-admission] As a drafting matter, the same reasoning applies to the other terms the partnership agreement and memorandum already set, such as the fund's name, its general partner and manager, the minimum investment, the subscription dates, withdrawal rights, fees, transfers, and dispute resolution.

One private placement memorandum filed with the SEC, for Constitution Capital Evergreen Partnership Fund, requires a completed subscription document by the acceptance date that fund sets. [^sa-filed-subscription-deadline] The points that follow are drafting analysis rather than rules of law: when a fund admits investors on recurring subscription dates, a subscription agreement that is silent on when completed documents and cleared funds must arrive, or that conflicts with the memorandum, leaves the administrator to decide which date an investor's money is invested on. A subscription agreement filed with the SEC for CIFC Direct Lending Evergreen Fund reserves the right to reject a subscription in whole or in part. [^sa-filed-cifc-reject] A subscription that binds the fund only on acceptance, but that the investor cannot revoke once delivered, lets the fund rely on it while it completes its identity and eligibility checks.

## What should hedge fund subscription documents say about investor eligibility? {#hedge-fund-subscription-documents-investor-eligibility}

**Short answer.** Hedge fund subscription documents set out the qualifications required to invest, and where the offering or the partnership agreement requires accredited-investor status, that status is tested at the time of each sale to the investor. [^se-filed-qualifications] [^se-accredited-time-of-sale]

Rule 501(a) defines an accredited investor as a person within one of its categories, or whom the issuer reasonably believes is within one, at the time of the sale of the securities to that person. [^se-accredited-time-of-sale] Rule 506(b) can also admit a limited number of non-accredited purchasers who meet its knowledge requirements, [^se-rule-506b-purchasers] so a fund's own documents decide whether accredited status is required. Because status is tested at each sale, a questionnaire completed on first subscription does not by itself cover a later additional contribution. Each category carries its own conditions: the natural-person net-worth test leaves the primary residence out of assets, [^se-accredited-net-worth] the income test has separate individual and joint levels, [^se-accredited-income] and an entity of a type the rule does not otherwise list qualifies by owning investments of more than $5,000,000 only if it was not formed to acquire the securities offered. [^se-accredited-entity-investments] The current categories include holders of professional certifications the SEC has designated, knowledgeable employees of a fund relying on section 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7), and family offices, [^se-accredited-professional-certification] [^se-accredited-knowledgeable-employee-family-office] so a questionnaire copied from an older form can leave out categories an investor actually meets.

The eligibility conditions come from two places: the exemptions the fund relies on, and the admission conditions of its partnership agreement, which can be stricter than the law. A partnership agreement that requires every investor to be a qualified client, as a contractual choice, is one example; the question on who can be charged a performance fee explains the legal rule. Questions that ask for less than the partnership agreement requires leave a condition unchecked, and questions that ask for more can turn away an investor the partnership agreement would admit.

For a fund relying on section 3(c)(1), the statutory look-through counts the holders of a company investor that owns 10 percent or more of the fund's outstanding voting securities and is, or but for section 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7) would be, an investment company. [^se-section-3c1-look-through] A questionnaire therefore asks an entity investor whether it holds 10 percent or more of the fund's voting securities and whether it is, or but for section 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7) would be, an investment company, with its number of beneficial owners where the look-through applies. As further diligence, it can also ask why the investor was formed, how much of its assets the investment represents, and whether its owners choose individually whether to invest. A subscription questionnaire filed with the SEC asks those questions next to whether any other circumstance requires treating the subscriber as more than one person under section 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7). [^se-filed-investment-entity-questions]

A representation or question that asks only for a legal conclusion, such as that the investor is not a restricted person or not a benefit plan investor, asks the investor to apply a definition it may not know and gives the fund little to rely on when the answer is wrong. Questions about facts the investor knows or can find out, such as an amount, an owner, a license held, a form filed, or an event and its date, leave the general partner to apply the definition, which makes the answers more reliable and an error easier to trace. An investor's facts can also change after admission, so an obligation to report an answer that stops being accurate, and a confirmation or new questionnaire with each additional contribution, keep the facts the fund relies on current.

## What should hedge fund subscription documents say about bad actors, new issues, taxes, and money laundering? {#hedge-fund-subscription-documents-compliance-information}

**Short answer.** Hedge fund subscription documents collect the investor facts behind the fund's compliance screens, including the factual inquiry Rule 506(d) calls for into a 20 percent holder's disqualifying events and the eligibility representation FINRA Rule 5130 requires before a broker-dealer sells the fund a new issue. [^cc-rule-506d-covered-persons] [^cc-rule-506d-factual-inquiry] [^cc-finra-5130-representation]

Rule 506 is unavailable for a sale if a covered person, which includes a beneficial owner of 20 percent or more of the issuer's outstanding voting equity securities, calculated on the basis of voting power, has a disqualifying event, [^cc-rule-506d-covered-persons] subject to exceptions that include events before September 23, 2013, a Commission waiver for good cause, written advice from the court or regulator that entered the order, and the issuer's reasonable care. [^cc-rule-506d-exceptions] An issuer cannot establish the rule's reasonable-care exception without factual inquiry into whether disqualifications exist. [^cc-rule-506d-factual-inquiry] A person can reach the 20 percent level directly or through an investor, so the inquiry reaches each person that would cross it through an investor, with its ownership chain and the dates of any events.

FINRA Rule 5130 generally bars a broker-dealer from selling a new issue to an account in which a restricted person has a beneficial interest, [^cc-finra-5130-restriction] and requires it to obtain, within the twelve months before the sale, a representation that the account is eligible. [^cc-finra-5130-representation] A fund that buys new issues therefore needs each investor's restricted-person status, and a fund procedure that refreshes the answers often enough lets it give a broker-dealer a representation that falls within that twelve-month window. Rule 5130 defines material support by income and deems immediate family members living in the same household to support each other, so questions about family members collect household facts as well as income. [^cc-finra-5130-material-support] FINRA Rule 5131 separately restricts a broker-dealer's allocations of new issues to accounts in which an executive officer or director of a public company or a covered non-public company, or a person materially supported by one, has a beneficial interest, where the company is, among other circumstances the rule lists, an investment banking client of that broker-dealer, so the questionnaire asks which companies are involved and leaves the broker-dealer to apply the rule, [^cc-finra-5131-spinning] and deems persons living in the same household to support each other, [^cc-finra-5131-material-support] so an investor's Rule 5130 answers do not answer the Rule 5131 question.

The tax, anti-money-laundering, and public-records questions serve the fund's own procedures. Tax forms and self-certifications, kept current, let the fund withhold and report correctly, while withholding itself and the charging of taxes to an investor are partnership-agreement terms. Identity, beneficial-ownership, sanctions, and source-of-funds questions follow the onboarding procedures the fund and its administrator actually use; the question on PPM subscriptions explains the status of FinCEN's investment-adviser rule. Public-records status matters most for public investors, such as a public pension plan or a public university's endowment: knowing which freedom-of-information or public-records law applies lets the general partner decide what confidential information to give that investor and ask for notice of any request for fund information.

## What should a hedge fund subscription agreement say about the investor's power of attorney and indemnity? {#hedge-fund-subscription-agreement-power-of-attorney-and-indemnity}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund subscription agreement can limit the investor's power of attorney to executing the partnership agreement and its indemnity to breaches of the investor's representations, capped at its commitment, as the ILPA Model Subscription Agreement drafts them. [^pa-ilpa-poa] [^pa-ilpa-indemnity]

The SEC staff describes a subscription agreement as the contract through which investors contract to invest in a private fund. [^pa-sec-subscription-agreement] What follows is drafting analysis, not a statement of what any law requires. A power of attorney limited to executing a counterpart signature page to the partnership agreement does what a subscription needs. A grant that also reaches amendments and other filings overlaps any power of attorney the partnership agreement itself contains, and can widen it; ILPA's drafting note says that where the partnership agreement has a customary power of attorney there is no reason for the subscription power to extend further. [^pa-ilpa-poa-note] The [Hedge Fund Subscription Agreement](/templates/openagreements-hedge-fund-subscription-agreement) takes the narrow approach and leaves the power of attorney in the [Hedge Fund Limited Partnership Agreement](/templates/openagreements-hedge-fund-limited-partnership-agreement) to operate by its own terms.

An indemnity that covers only an investor representation that was false when given, up to the investor's subscription amount, gives the fund recourse for the harm a false eligibility answer causes without exposing the investor to open-ended liability for the fund's own compliance; ILPA's drafting note prefers no indemnity and accepts one limited to representations the investor can reasonably give. [^pa-ilpa-indemnity-note] An uncapped indemnity for any breach of any covenant goes further and shifts more of that risk to the investor.

A subscription form can also include representations by the fund or its general partner to the investor, about matters such as the fund's formation, its authority, and the offering's compliance, along with most-favored-nation rights and other concessions. A manager-side form can leave those to side letters. Where it does, the memorandum and the partnership agreement are the principal statements from the fund available to the investor before any side letter is agreed, which is why what they say matters to an investor reading the subscription agreement.

## What should hedge fund subscription documents say about an investment manager outside the United States? {#hedge-fund-subscription-documents-non-us-manager}

**Short answer.** Hedge fund subscription documents for a manager whose principal office and place of business is outside the United States reflect its Investment Advisers Act status, which may be SEC registration, the non-U.S. private fund adviser exemption, or the foreign private adviser exemption. [^nu-non-us-private-fund-adviser-exemption] [^nu-foreign-private-adviser-exemption]

The non-U.S. private fund adviser exemption turns on where the adviser's principal office and place of business is, not where it is organized, and requires that the adviser have no client that is a United States person other than qualifying private funds and that it manage from any U.S. place of business only private fund assets of less than $150 million. [^nu-non-us-private-fund-adviser-exemption] A foreign private adviser has no place of business in the United States, fewer than 15 clients and investors in the United States in the private funds it advises, and less than $25 million attributable to them, among other conditions. [^nu-foreign-private-adviser-definition] [^nu-foreign-private-adviser-exemption] That count runs across all of the private funds the manager advises, not only this one. [^nu-foreign-private-adviser-definition] As a drafting matter, the questionnaire for a foreign private adviser can therefore ask each investor whether it is in the United States.

Section 205(a)(1) reaches only an adviser registered or required to be registered with the SEC. [^nu-advisers-act-205-a-1] For a manager that is neither, a qualified-client screen in the subscription documents, if the partnership agreement requires one, is a contractual admission condition rather than a legal one, and its assets-under-management question refers to assets under that manager's management, as Rule 205-3 measures the test. [^nu-qualified-client-aum-test] Keeping the screen still matters, because if the manager later becomes required to register, section 205(a)(1) applies to any person who becomes a party after that, including a new equity owner of a private investment company it advises. [^nu-rule-205-3-c-2] Rule 506(d) lists any investment manager of a pooled investment fund, and that manager's directors, executive officers, and other officers participating in the offering, among its covered persons, wherever the manager is organized, so the bad-actor inquiry names the manager and reaches an investor, or an investor's principal, who holds such a position with it. [^nu-rule-506d-covered-persons]

The rest of this answer is drafting analysis, not a statement of any jurisdiction's law. The tax, anti-money-laundering, benefit plan, bad-actor, and new-issue questions depend on the fund and its investors rather than on where the manager is based. The documents' description of the manager can name its home regulator, its status under the Advisers Act and its home law, and where its books and records are kept, and counsel in the manager's home jurisdiction is the source for any notices, such as data-privacy notices, or anti-money-laundering steps that its law may add alongside the fund's U.S. procedures.
