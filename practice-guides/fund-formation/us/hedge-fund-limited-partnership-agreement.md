# Hedge Fund Formation: The Limited Partnership Agreement[^about]

A plain-language guide for hedge fund managers, and for the smaller investors they admit, to a U.S. hedge fund's limited partnership agreement, covering its purpose and parties, performance fees, hurdles and high-water marks, lock-ups and withdrawals, capital accounts, manager duties and protections, expenses, side letters, amendment, and dissolution.

Hedge fund formation combines entity law, securities law, adviser regulation, tax, and negotiated economics. A hedge fund is an open-end private fund: investors contribute capital when they subscribe and can withdraw it periodically. This guide covers U.S. hedge funds; private equity and other closed-end drawdown funds work differently and are not covered here. It is written first for the manager of an emerging or small hedge fund, who can use it to identify the questions the fund's documents must answer and then work with fund counsel on the answers for the particular fund. It is written second for a smaller investor who may not have counsel of their own: the same questions show what a hedge fund's documents let the manager charge, change, or withhold, and where an investor's rights depend on a notice period, a vote, or the manager's discretion.

This guide covers the limited partnership agreement: its purpose and parties, performance fees, hurdles and high-water marks, lock-ups and withdrawals, capital accounts, the manager's duties and protections, expenses, side letters, amendment, and dissolution. It is part of the [hedge fund formation practice guide](/practice-guides/fund-formation/us/hedge-funds), which covers adviser registration, a manager outside the United States, who can be charged a performance fee, Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c) offerings, Section 3(c)(1) and 3(c)(7) funds, the anti-fraud rules, and Form D; the [private placement memorandum](/practice-guides/fund-formation/us/hedge-fund-private-placement-memorandum) has its own guide.

## What is the purpose of a hedge fund limited partnership agreement? {#hedge-fund-limited-partnership-agreement-purpose}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund limited partnership agreement (LPA) is the contract that sets the fund's key legal terms and mechanics, including how profits are split between the general partner and the investors, what management fees are charged, and when investors may withdraw. [^sec-lpa]

A hedge fund is a type of private fund. [^sec-hedge] [^inv-hedge] A private fund pools money from several investors without registering as an investment company. [^sec-private-definition] The SEC's staff describes the role of a limited partnership agreement this way: "if you structure your private fund as a limited partnership, a limited partnership agreement, or LPA, will document the fund’s key legal terms and mechanics."[^sec-lpa] [^sec-lpa] The [Hedge Fund Limited Partnership Agreement](/templates/openagreements-hedge-fund-limited-partnership-agreement) is one such agreement for a hedge fund.

Delaware law gives maximum effect to freedom of contract, so the terms of a Delaware limited partnership agreement, rather than an assumed industry norm, largely govern the fund. [^delaware-lpa-contract-policy] A limited partnership agreement can expand, restrict, or eliminate the partners' duties, but it cannot eliminate the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. [^delaware-lpa-duty-terms]

## Who are the parties to a hedge fund limited partnership agreement? {#hedge-fund-limited-partnership-agreement-parties}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund limited partnership agreement binds the fund's general partner, which manages the fund, and its limited partners, the investors who commit capital to it. [^sec-gp] [^sec-lp]

The SEC staff’s glossary describes a general partner as the person or entity that raises money and manages a limited partnership fund. [^sec-gp] It says, "A limited partner is an investor who commits capital to a private fund."[^sec-lp] [^sec-lp] Delaware law permits classes of limited partners with different rights. [^delaware-lpa-classes]

The SEC’s staff says a fund may have a separate adviser. [^sec-adviser] An investment management agreement often governs the adviser’s responsibilities. [^sec-ima]

## What should a hedge fund agreement say about performance fees? {#hedge-fund-performance-fees}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund agreement states the performance fee, which a limited partnership agreement filed with the SEC for Third Point Enhanced sets at 20 percent of each investor's net increase after the management fee and any loss-recovery balance, reallocated to the general partner. [^third-point-filed-performance-allocation]

The SEC's glossary gives the plain meaning: "Performance fees are compensation provided to an adviser based on the performance of a client’s portfolio."[^sec-glossary-performance-fees] [^sec-glossary-performance-fees] A limited partnership can take the fee as a performance allocation, meaning a share of each investor's gains moves from that investor's capital account to the general partner. [^third-point-filed-performance-allocation] The gains measured can include both realized gains, on investments the fund has sold, and unrealized gains, on investments it still holds, as a limited partnership agreement filed with the SEC for The Lion Fund II provides. [^lion-filed-realized-unrealized]

Investor.gov, the SEC's investor-education site, describes typical hedge-fund terms: "In addition, they also typically pay a performance fee of 15-20% of the hedge fund’s profit subject to certain measures, such as a high-water mark and a hurdle rate, which can limit when a manager is entitled to performance fees."[^inv-fee] [^inv-fee]

## What should a hedge fund agreement say about hurdles and high-water marks? {#hedge-fund-hurdles-and-high-water-marks}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund agreement states whether the performance fee applies only to gains above a hurdle rate and whether a high-water mark or loss carryforward requires earlier losses to be recovered before a new fee is charged. [^lion-hurdle-and-loss-carryforward] [^third-point-loss-recovery]

A hurdle is a minimum return the investor keeps before the manager shares in gains. A high-water mark, or loss carryforward, means the manager takes no new performance fee until the investor's earlier losses are made up. Investor.gov explains why both matter to an investor: they "can limit when a manager is entitled to performance fees"[^inv-fee-hurdles]. [^inv-fee-hurdles]

Limited partnership agreements filed with the SEC use both tools in different forms. The Lion Fund II agreement charges 25 percent of the increase that exceeds a hurdle rate, subject to a loss carryforward. [^lion-hurdle-and-loss-carryforward] The Third Point Enhanced agreement subtracts the investor's loss recovery account balance before calculating its 20 percent allocation. [^third-point-loss-recovery]

## What should a hedge fund agreement say about lock-ups and withdrawals? {#hedge-fund-lock-ups-and-withdrawals}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund agreement states when an investor may first withdraw, how much notice a withdrawal needs, and when the general partner may cap or pause withdrawals. [^stilwell-filed-lock-up] [^solasglas-filed-notice] [^apollo-filed-withdrawal-gate] [^apollo-filed-suspension]

A lock-up delays the first date on which an investor may withdraw. [^stilwell-filed-lock-up] A notice period sets how far ahead a withdrawal request must arrive. [^solasglas-filed-notice] A gate caps the total withdrawals the fund pays for a period. [^apollo-filed-withdrawal-gate] A suspension pauses withdrawals in stated circumstances, such as a market closure. [^apollo-filed-suspension] Investor.gov describes the usual picture for hedge funds: "Hedge funds typically limit opportunities to redeem, or cash in, your shares to four times a year or fewer."[^inv-redeem] [^inv-redeem]

Limited partnership agreements filed with the SEC show a wide descriptive range. One bars withdrawals until the second anniversary of each investment, and another lets the general partner cap aggregate withdrawals in a calendar month at 10 percent of net asset value. [^stilwell-filed-lock-up] [^apollo-filed-withdrawal-gate] Delaware law separately prohibits a distribution that would make the limited partnership insolvent, meaning that its liabilities, other than what it owes partners for their partnership interests, would exceed the fair value of its assets. [^delaware-distribution-solvency-limit] That limit is section 17-607(a) of the Delaware Revised Uniform Limited Partnership Act, and it applies whatever the agreement's withdrawal terms say. [^delaware-distribution-solvency-limit]

## What should a hedge fund agreement say about capital accounts, side pockets, and new issues? {#hedge-fund-capital-accounts-side-pockets-and-new-issues}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund agreement keeps a capital account for each partner that records the partner's share of the fund's net profit or loss, and it says how side-pocket investments and initial public offering shares, called new issues, are kept apart from the main portfolio. [^capital-account-filed-net-increase] [^capital-account-filed-designated-security] [^solasglas-filed-new-issues]

A capital account is a running balance for one partner. An amount credited to the account is added to that balance, and an amount debited is subtracted. The SEC's glossary explains how gains are shared: "Investors generally share in the profits and losses in proportion to their interest in the fund."[^sec-pooled] [^sec-pooled] The profit and loss can include unrealized gains and losses on investments the fund still holds, as a limited partnership agreement filed with the SEC for The Lion Fund II provides. [^lion-filed-realized-unrealized-2] A performance allocation then moves a share of a partner's gains from that partner's capital account to the general partner. [^capital-account-filed-net-increase]

A side pocket holds an investment apart from the rest of the portfolio. One limited partnership agreement filed with the SEC creates a separate sub-account for each partner that participates in an investment designated for that treatment. [^capital-account-filed-designated-security] New issues are shares sold in an initial public offering. FINRA Rule 5130 generally bars a broker-dealer from selling new issues to an account in which a restricted person has a beneficial interest. [^finra-new-issue-restriction] Limited partnership agreements filed with the SEC respond by allocating new-issue profits and losses only as FINRA's rules permit. [^lion-filed-new-issues] [^solasglas-filed-new-issues]

## What should a hedge fund agreement say about duties, exculpation, and indemnification? {#hedge-fund-manager-duties-exculpation-and-indemnification}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund agreement states the manager's duties, when exculpation excuses the manager from paying damages, and which claims the fund pays through indemnification, within Delaware's limits on modifying duties and liability. [^delaware-duty-modification] [^delaware-liability-limitation]

Investor.gov states the baseline: "Hedge funds . . . are subject to the same prohibitions against fraud as are other market participants, and their managers owe a fiduciary duty to the funds that they manage."[^inv-duty] [^inv-duty] Delaware's partnership statute separately addresses the duties owed under Delaware law: a partnership agreement can expand, restrict, or eliminate them, but it cannot eliminate the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. [^delaware-duty-modification] Delaware also preserves liability for bad-faith violations of that covenant. [^delaware-liability-limitation]

Exculpation addresses damages owed by a covered person; indemnification addresses covered claims or costs paid by the fund. A Carlyle LPA filed with the SEC defines disabling conduct to include fraud, bad faith, willful misconduct, gross negligence, and willful material breach. [^carlyle-filed-disabling-conduct] That list illustrates one sponsor’s choice.

## What should a hedge fund agreement say about expenses, valuation, and reporting? {#hedge-fund-expenses-valuation-and-reporting}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund agreement identifies which expenses the fund pays and any cap on them, who values the portfolio, and which financial reports investors receive. [^third-point-filed-expense-cap] [^solasglas-filed-general-partner-valuation] [^apollo-filed-quarterly-statements]

Limited partnership agreements filed with the SEC show an expense cap using manager reimbursement above 1.75% of average net assets and another limiting expenses to 1.25% of net assets subject to exclusions. [^third-point-filed-expense-cap] [^endowment-filed-expense-cap] The SEC’s examination staff observed advisers charging funds for expenses their agreements did not permit. [^risk-expense] The staff also observed valuations contrary to disclosed methods and sometimes resulting in fee overcharges. [^risk-value]

ILPA, an association of institutional investors, describes organizational expenses as legal, administrative, and compliance costs of private equity fund formation historically borne by limited partners. [^ilpa-definition] As an investor-side recommendation for private equity, ILPA proposes a cap at the lower of five basis points of target assets under management or $10 million. [^ilpa-cap] That proposal is not a legal requirement or a claim about market practice.

## What should a side letter to a hedge fund limited partnership agreement say? {#hedge-fund-side-letters}

**Short answer.** A side letter to a hedge fund limited partnership agreement is a separate agreement giving one investor rights that other investors do not receive, and limited partnership agreements filed with the SEC expressly permit them. [^third-point-filed-side-letters] [^apollo-filed-other-agreements]

A well-known, uncontroversial model is the National Venture Capital Association's [Management Rights Letter](/templates/nvca-management-rights-letter), which a venture fund asks each portfolio company to sign to build its case for an exemption from the ERISA plan-asset rules. [^nvca-purpose] The example shows why a side letter can be a benign tool. The right it grants serves one investor's own regulatory position rather than improving that investor's economics. Under the Labor Department's plan-asset regulation, a venture capital operating company must hold investments that carry management rights, which the regulation defines this way: "The term ‘management rights’ means contractual rights directly between the investor and an operating company to substantially participate in, or substantially influence the conduct of, the management of the operating company."[^erisa-rights] [^erisa-rights]

Limited partnership agreements filed with the SEC expressly let the general partner enter into separate agreements with particular limited partners, including agreements that alter or supplement the main agreement's terms for that partner. [^third-point-filed-side-letters] [^apollo-filed-other-agreements] Disclosure is the check on side letters. The SEC's examination staff reported advisers that gave select investors side letters with preferential liquidity terms without adequately disclosing them, so other investors were unaware of the potential harm. [^risk-side]

## What's the process for amending a hedge fund limited partnership agreement? {#amending-a-hedge-fund-limited-partnership-agreement}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund limited partnership agreement sets the investor vote needed to amend it and may let the general partner make narrower amendments alone. [^stilwell-filed-amendment-power] [^seidman-filed-amendment-threshold]

The SEC staff’s glossary says an LPA governs the limited partner’s relationship with the fund and general partner. [^sec-lp-2] Limited partnership agreements filed with the SEC use different voting and manager-only amendment mechanisms. [^stilwell-filed-amendment-power] [^seidman-filed-amendment-threshold] A Stilwell agreement allows general-partner amendments subject to an adverse-effect condition. [^stilwell-filed-amendment-power] A Seidman agreement uses a capital-based investor threshold for other amendments. [^seidman-filed-amendment-threshold] A Lion Fund II agreement requires the general partner and limited partners owning at least 51 percent of interests, and allows consent by silence after notice. [^lion-filed-amendment-threshold] The governing LPA determines the vote, class rights, and notice process.

## What should a hedge fund agreement say about dissolution? {#hedge-fund-dissolution}

**Short answer.** A hedge fund agreement says what happens when the fund dissolves, and a limited partnership agreement filed with the SEC for Stilwell Value Partners has the general partner liquidate the fund or appoint a liquidator. [^stilwell-filed-liquidation-on-dissolution]

The SEC staff’s glossary says an LPA governs a limited partner’s relationship with the fund and general partner. [^sec-lp-3] In a Stilwell agreement filed with the SEC, the general partner liquidates the partnership or appoints a liquidator after dissolution. [^stilwell-filed-liquidation-on-dissolution] For an adviser using the custody rule’s pooled-vehicle audit route, the rule calls for an audit upon liquidation and prompt distribution of audited statements afterward. [^custody-rule-annual-audit]
