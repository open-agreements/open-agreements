# Stay-or-Pay and Employee Repayment in Arizona[^about]

Arizona has no generally applicable statute aimed at stay-or-pay or training-repayment terms outside a narrow peace-officer rule, so a repayment clause is tested as a contract, while the wage-withholding statute, treble damages and the state and local minimum-wage floors limit taking the money out of pay.

## Are repayment terms enforceable in Arizona? {#repayment-enforceability}

**Short answer.** No generally applicable Arizona statute addresses stay-or-pay, training-repayment or other employee repayment terms. One narrow statute covers peace officers: a hiring law enforcement agency may not require a certified peace officer to repay certification or related costs in an effort to be reimbursed under the agency-to-agency reimbursement statute [^az-sop-peace-officer-no-repayment]. Where a repayment term fixes the amount owed on a breach, the Arizona Supreme Court enforces that pre-set amount only when it compensates the non-breaching party rather than penalizing the breaching party [^az-sop-dobson-bay-compensate-not-penalize].

"A liquidated damages contract provision is enforceable if the pre-determined amount for damages seeks to compensate the non-breaching party rather than penalize the breaching party."[^az-sop-dobson-bay-compensate-not-penalize]

In *Dobson Bay Club II DD, LLC v. La Sonrisa de Siena, LLC*, the court adopted Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 356(1) as the test for a stipulated damages provision [^az-sop-dobson-bay-penalty-test]. Under that test, an amount is unreasonable, and unenforceable as a penalty, if the difficulty of proving the loss is slight and either no loss occurs or the stipulated sum is grossly disproportionate to the loss [^az-sop-dobson-bay-grossly-disproportionate]. A penalty finding does not void the whole contract: the non-breaching party can still recover its actual damages [^az-sop-dobson-bay-actual-damages].

Whether Arizona's restrictive-covenant rules reach a repayment term is an unresolved analogy, not a holding: *Valley Medical Specialists v. Farber* states the reasonableness test for a restrictive covenant [^az-sop-farber-reasonableness], and no Arizona appellate decision located for this guide applies it to a training-repayment or bonus-repayment clause. The conservative course is to treat a repayment amount that is triggered by competitive work, rather than measured by the employer's cost, as exposed to that test. Under it, the Arizona Supreme Court will not enforce a restraint that is greater than necessary to protect the employer's legitimate interest, or whose protected interest is outweighed by the hardship to the employee and the likely injury to the public [^az-sop-farber-reasonableness].

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A fixed repayment figure set well above the employer's actual training or bonus cost invites a penalty challenge, and if the clause fails the employer is left to prove its actual loss [^az-sop-dobson-bay-grossly-disproportionate][^az-sop-dobson-bay-actual-damages].

## Can an Arizona employer claw back pay the employee already earned? {#clawback-earned-comp}

**Short answer.** No Arizona appellate decision located for this guide decides whether a bonus or other payment already made can be recovered under a stay-or-pay term, so whether a particular clawback is enforceable is unsettled. Taking the money out of wages is limited separately: an Arizona employer may not withhold or divert any portion of an employee's wages unless state or federal law requires or empowers it, the employee gave prior written authorization, or there is a reasonable good faith dispute over the amount of wages due [^az-sop-withholding-grounds].

Whether a sum is protected by the wage statutes depends on Arizona's definition of wages: nondiscretionary compensation due an employee in return for labor or services, for which the employee has a reasonable expectation to be paid [^az-sop-wages-definition]. A sign-on payment or bonus that falls within that definition is a wage once it is due, and keeping it back is a withholding governed by A.R.S. 23-352 [^az-sop-wages-definition][^az-sop-withholding-grounds].

## Can an Arizona employer take a repayment out of the final paycheck? {#final-pay-deduction}

**Short answer.** An Arizona employer may deduct a repayment from final wages only on a statutory withholding ground [^az-sop-withholding-grounds-final-pay] and without leaving the employee's workweek wages below the minimum wage [^az-sop-r20-5-1206-shortfall]. A written revocation does not stop withholding that resolves a debt or obligation to the employer [^az-sop-written-authorization-revocation]. Any deduction that takes the employee's pay for a workweek below the minimum wage is a separate problem, because no verbal or written agreement or employment contract may waive the rights the minimum-wage article gives [^az-sop-minimum-wage-no-waiver].

"An employer shall not withhold wages under a written authorization from the employee past the date specified by the employee in a written revocation of the authorization, unless the withholding is to resolve a debt or obligation to the employer or a court orders otherwise."[^az-sop-written-authorization-revocation]

The third ground in A.R.S. 23-352 covers a reasonable good faith dispute over the wages due, including the amount of any claim of debt, reimbursement, recoupment or set-off the employer asserts against the employee [^az-sop-good-faith-dispute]. The dispute must concern the amount of wages due and be reasonable and in good faith, including where the employer asserts a repayment debt or set-off [^az-sop-good-faith-dispute]. Whether a particular repayment claim meets that standard turns on the facts, so the conservative course is to rely on a written authorization.

Timing still runs while the deduction question is open. A discharged employee must be paid wages due within seven working days or by the end of the next regular pay period, whichever is sooner; an employee who quits must be paid no later than the regular payday for the pay period in which the termination occurred [^az-sop-final-pay-timing]. If an employer fails to pay wages due in violation of that chapter, the employee may recover in a civil action an amount that is treble the unpaid wages [^az-sop-treble-damages].

The minimum wage under A.R.S. 23-363 is adjusted each January 1 for the increase in the cost of living [^az-sop-minimum-wage-adjustment], and the Industrial Commission's rule requires an employer whose combined wages fall short of the minimum wage for a workweek to pay the difference [^az-sop-r20-5-1206-shortfall]. Two cities add their own rules. Tucson's ordinance bars any deduction, except as required by law or court order, that results in the employee receiving less than the minimum wage [^az-sop-tucson-deduction-floor]. Flagstaff's ordinance also provides that no verbal or written agreement or employment contract may waive the rights it gives [^az-sop-flagstaff-no-waiver].

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> A deduction authorization that sets no minimum-wage limit can produce an unlawful deduction in any workweek where the repayment takes the employee below the state or local minimum wage [^az-sop-minimum-wage-no-waiver][^az-sop-tucson-deduction-floor].

## Are training or tuition repayments treated differently in Arizona? {#training-repayment}

**Short answer.** Arizona has no generally applicable statute that gives training, tuition or relocation repayment a separate rule. The one narrow exception bars a hiring law enforcement agency from requiring a certified peace officer to repay certification or related costs in an effort to be reimbursed under the agency-to-agency reimbursement statute [^az-sop-peace-officer-no-repayment-training]. Where a repayment term stipulates damages for breach, its enforceability is measured by Arizona's stipulated-damages test [^az-sop-dobson-bay-penalty-test-training], and a payroll deduction to collect it needs the same statutory ground as any other withholding [^az-sop-withholding-grounds-training]. Where the federal minimum-wage and overtime rules apply, a required repayment that works as a kickback of wages is also measured against them [^az-sop-flsa-kickback].

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act rule, wages count as paid only when they are paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear [^az-sop-flsa-free-and-clear], and the wage requirements are not met where the employee kicks back the whole or part of the wage to the employer or to another person for the employer's benefit [^az-sop-flsa-kickback]. The rule illustrates the point with employer-required tools: there is a violation in any workweek when their cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages required [^az-sop-flsa-tools-workweek]. Arizona's own floor applies separately, because an employer whose combined wages fall below the state minimum wage for a workweek must pay the difference [^az-sop-r20-5-1206-shortfall-training].

## How are Arizona repayment and wage-withholding rules enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** A training repayment obligation integrated into the employment contract is enforced as a claim on that contract, subject to Arizona's one-year limitations period [^az-sop-worldwide-jet-one-year]. On the employee's side, wages withheld without a statutory ground can support treble damages [^az-sop-treble-damages-enforcement], but Arizona courts treat that award as discretionary rather than mandatory [^az-sop-crum-treble-discretionary].

In *Worldwide Jet Charter, Inc. v. Toulatos*, the offer letter incorporated a promissory note and a training reimbursement agreement, and the Court of Appeals held that those documents were part of the employment contract [^az-sop-worldwide-jet-one-year]. The court rejected the employer's argument that it was only collecting a debt, because the debt arose from the alleged breach of the employment contract [^az-sop-worldwide-jet-debt-from-breach]. The statute sets one year for an action for breach of an oral or written employment contract [^az-sop-12-541-employment-contract].

The Attorney General has also brought a wage-law case against a public employer's training repayment policy [^az-sop-ag-tolleson-wage-authority]. The office has announced a suit against the City of Tolleson alleging that its firefighter training repayment agreement is not a genuine effort to recover training costs but an unenforceable penalty and a de facto non-compete agreement [^az-sop-ag-tolleson-allegation]. That announcement reports allegations and a motion for a preliminary injunction, not a court ruling [^az-sop-ag-tolleson-injunction-motion], so it shows enforcement interest rather than settled law.

## What repayment structure is safest under Arizona law? {#safest-structure}

**Short answer.** A repayment amount tied to the employer's actual, documented cost is better placed to read as compensation rather than a penalty [^az-sop-dobson-bay-compensate-not-penalize-safest]. Prior written authorization from the employee is one statutory ground for a payroll deduction [^az-sop-withholding-grounds-safest]. A deduction cannot leave a workweek's combined wages below the minimum wage, because the employer must pay the shortfall [^az-sop-r20-5-1206-shortfall-safest] and no agreement may waive minimum-wage rights [^az-sop-minimum-wage-no-waiver-safest]. Where those conditions cannot be met, paying final wages in full and pursuing the repayment as a contract claim avoids the treble-damages exposure for wages withheld without a statutory ground [^az-sop-treble-damages-safest].
