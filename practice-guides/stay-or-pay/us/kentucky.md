# Stay-or-Pay and Employee Repayment in Kentucky[^about]

Kentucky has no statute that limits stay-or-pay or training-repayment terms in private employment, so a repayment promise is enforced as a contract and a fixed amount owed for leaving is tested under the liquidated-damages penalty rule, while KRS 337.060 separately limits taking the money out of wages.

## Are repayment terms enforceable in Kentucky? {#repayment-enforceability}

**Short answer.** Kentucky has no statute that limits stay-or-pay or training-repayment terms in private employment, and the Kentucky Court of Appeals has held a city entitled to reimbursement under a police officer's written promise to repay training costs if he breached his employment agreement [^ky-sop-waters-contract-enf][^ky-sop-waters-holding-enf]. A term fixing damages for breach is subject to the liquidated-damages penalty rule [^ky-sop-mattingly-reasonable-enf], and the Kentucky Supreme Court in *Mattingly Bridge Co. v. Holloway & Son Construction Co.* adopted the rule that a term fixing unreasonably large liquidated damages is unenforceable on grounds of public policy as a penalty [^ky-sop-mattingly-penalty-enf].

"A term fixing unreasonably large liquidated damages is unenforceable on grounds of public policy as a penalty."[^ky-sop-mattingly-penalty-enf]

*Mattingly Bridge* adopted Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 356(1), under which damages for breach may be liquidated in the agreement only at an amount that is reasonable in light of the anticipated or actual loss caused by the breach and the difficulties of proof of loss [^ky-sop-mattingly-reasonable-enf]. The Court of Appeals applied that standard to an employee in *Daniel Boone Clinic, P.S.C. v. Dahhan*, enforcing a $75,000 liquidated damages provision in a physician's employment contract payable for violating its restrictive covenant [^ky-sop-dahhan-terms-enf][^ky-sop-dahhan-enforced-enf].

The Kentucky Supreme Court has refused to enforce an end-of-employment forfeiture that bore no relation to loss. In *Man O' War Restaurants, Inc. v. Martin*, a shareholder who was also an employee had to return his stock for the sum he originally paid on termination of his employment [^ky-sop-man-o-war-issue-enf], and the court adopted the view that requiring a return of the stock at the price paid, without interest or appreciation, was a form of unreasonably excessive liquidated damages [^ky-sop-man-o-war-excessive-enf].

A repayment term bundled with a non-compete or confidentiality promise raises a consideration question. In *Charles T. Creech, Inc. v. Brown*, the Kentucky Supreme Court held that an agreement signed by an existing at-will employee lacked consideration because it did not require the employer to forbear the exercise of some legal right or otherwise result in some detriment to the employer [^ky-sop-creech-consideration-enf]. As a drafting matter, a repayment promise is better supported when it is exchanged for a new obligation the employer was not already bound to perform [^ky-sop-creech-consideration-enf].

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A repayment amount set without regard to the employer's anticipated or actual loss risks being struck as a penalty [^ky-sop-mattingly-reasonable-enf][^ky-sop-man-o-war-excessive-enf].

## Can a Kentucky employer claw back pay the employee already earned? {#clawback-earned-comp}

**Short answer.** No Kentucky appellate decision located for this guide decides whether a bonus or other payment the employee has already earned can be recovered under a stay-or-pay term. Kentucky's wage statutes define wages to include earned bonuses [^ky-sop-337-010-wages-claw], and KRS 337.060 bars an employer from withholding any part of the wage agreed upon [^ky-sop-337-060-withhold-claw], unless law authorizes the withholding or the employee expressly authorizes a qualifying deduction in writing [^ky-sop-337-060-exception-claw].

"‘Wages’ includes any compensation due to an employee by reason of his or her employment, including salaries, commissions, vested vacation pay, overtime pay, severance or dismissal pay, earned bonuses, and any other similar advantages agreed upon by the employer and the employee or provided to employees as an established policy."[^ky-sop-337-010-wages-claw]

To the extent a bonus-repayment provision fixes damages for breach, the liquidated-damages rule requires an amount that is reasonable in light of the anticipated or actual loss [^ky-sop-mattingly-reasonable-claw]. Whether earned compensation can be recovered at all remains unsettled, so the conservative course is to condition a bonus on continued service before it is earned rather than to rely on recovering a bonus already earned. Recovering the money from wages is a separate question, because an earned bonus is a wage [^ky-sop-337-010-wages-claw] and KRS 337.060 governs any withholding from wages [^ky-sop-337-060-withhold-claw].

## Can repayment come out of a Kentucky final paycheck? {#final-pay-deduction}

**Short answer.** A Kentucky employer may take a repayment out of wages, including final wages, only when law authorizes the deduction or the employee has expressly authorized it in writing for a deduction of the kind KRS 337.060(1) permits [^ky-sop-337-060-exception-final]. An employee who leaves or is discharged must be paid in full all wages earned no later than the next normal pay period or fourteen days after leaving, whichever last occurs, and no employer may by any means secure exemption from that rule [^ky-sop-337-055-final-pay-final].

"This section shall not make it unlawful for an employer to withhold or divert any portion of an employee's wage when the employer is authorized to do so by local, state, or federal law or when a deduction is expressly authorized in writing by the employee to cover insurance premiums, hospital and medical dues, or other deductions not amounting to a rebate or deduction from the standard wage arrived at by collective bargaining or pursuant to wage agreement or statute, nor shall it preclude deductions for union dues where such deductions are authorized by joint wage agreements or collective bargaining contracts negotiated between employers and employees or their representative and meet the requirements of KRS 336.135."[^ky-sop-337-060-exception-final]

The written-authorization exception reaches insurance premiums, hospital and medical dues, and other deductions not amounting to a rebate or deduction from the standard wage [^ky-sop-337-060-exception-final]. In an unpublished 2014 decision in *Hunt v. North American Stainless*, the Court of Appeals stated that while KRS 337.060 allows an employer to deduct certain expenses, the educational expenses the employer claimed were not within the scope of the Act [^ky-sop-hunt-2014-scope-final]. No Kentucky appellate decision located for this guide decides whether a deduction to repay a bonus falls within the exception. As a drafting matter, collecting a bonus repayment separately avoids relying on an unresolved application of the written-authorization exception [^ky-sop-337-060-exception-final]. KRS 337.060(2) also bars deducting fines and breakage outright, and bars deducting losses from defective or faulty workmanship, lost or stolen property or damage to property unless the loss is attributable to the employee's willful or intentional disregard of the employer's interest [^ky-sop-337-060-banned-final].

*Hunt v. North American Stainless* shows the risk. At the exit interview the employer told the employee that he would have to sign a promissory note for $9,720 of college program costs or it would deduct that amount from his final paychecks [^ky-sop-hunt-exit-final]. The jury found no express or implied contract requiring repayment [^ky-sop-hunt-no-contract-final], and found that the employer withheld final wages for more than fourteen days in violation of KRS 337.055 and lacked a good faith basis for deducting the educational expenses under KRS 337.060 [^ky-sop-hunt-verdict-final].

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> Netting a training or bonus repayment against the last paycheck risks a civil penalty of $100 to $1,000 plus liability to the employee for the amount withheld with ten percent annual interest [^ky-sop-337-990-penalty-final].

## Are training or tuition repayments treated differently in Kentucky? {#training-repayment}

**Short answer.** Kentucky has no statute that treats private-sector training, tuition or relocation repayment differently from other repayment terms, and a term fixing damages for breach remains subject to the liquidated-damages penalty rule [^ky-sop-mattingly-reasonable-train]. In *Waters v. City of Pioneer Village*, the Court of Appeals enforced a police officer's promise to repay academy costs and held that the city's recovery rested on the employment contract rather than on the statute governing reimbursement between law enforcement agencies [^ky-sop-waters-contract-train][^ky-sop-waters-holding-train].

"Because we are holding that.the City is entitled to be reimbursed under the employment contract rather than under the statute, any amounts recovered are pursuant to the contract, and the language in the statute regarding the subtraction of the costs of training provided by the Academy is not applicable."[^ky-sop-waters-holding-train]

The statute in *Waters*, KRS 70.290, is the one Kentucky statute located for this guide that addresses training-cost recovery on departure, and it is limited to law enforcement. City and county law enforcement agencies may require a newly appointed deputy sheriff or peace officer in the Kentucky Law Enforcement Foundation Fund Program to sign an employment contract of no longer than five years from graduation from the Department of Criminal Justice Training [^ky-sop-70-290-contract-train]. If the officer takes a peace officer job with another law enforcement agency, the new agency, not the officer, must reimburse the hiring agency's actual costs of the initial hiring, including training, equipment, salary and fringe benefits [^ky-sop-70-290-reimburse-train]. For contracts entered into on or after June 29, 2017, that reimbursement is not prorated [^ky-sop-70-290-proration-train].

*Hunt* is a reminder that a tuition program without a signed repayment promise may leave the employer with no contract claim: the jury found no express or implied contract requiring the employee to repay the educational expenses [^ky-sop-hunt-no-contract-train].

## What happens to a Kentucky employer that over-collects a repayment from pay? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employer that pays an employee covered by KRS 337.385 less than the wages owed under KRS 337.020 to 337.285, a range that includes the final-pay and deduction rules, is liable to the employee for the unpaid wages, an additional equal amount as liquidated damages, costs and reasonable attorney's fees [^ky-sop-337-385-liability-enf2]. For KRS 337.385, unless the context requires otherwise, the covered employees exclude categories that include individuals employed in agriculture and individuals employed in a bona fide executive, administrative, supervisory or professional capacity or as an outside salesman or outside collector, as administrative regulations define those terms [^ky-sop-337-010-exclusions-enf2]. A court may reduce or deny liquidated damages if the employer shows that it acted in good faith with reasonable grounds for believing it was not violating those sections [^ky-sop-337-385-good-faith-enf2].

"Except as provided in subsection (3) of this section, any employer who pays any employee less than wages and overtime compensation to which such employee is entitled under or by virtue of KRS 337.020 to 337.285 shall be liable to such employee affected for the full amount of such wages and overtime compensation, less any amount actually paid to such employee by the employer, for an additional equal amount as liquidated damages, and for costs and such reasonable attorney's fees as may be allowed by the court."[^ky-sop-337-385-liability-enf2]

Civil penalties apply as well. A violation of KRS 337.055 carries a penalty of $100 to $1,000 for each offense, with each failure to pay final wages as required counted as a separate offense [^ky-sop-337-990-055-enf2], and a violation of KRS 337.060 carries a penalty of $100 to $1,000 plus liability to the employee for the amount withheld with ten percent annual interest [^ky-sop-337-990-060-enf2]. An action under the chapter that has no other express limitations period must be commenced within three years after the cause of action accrued [^ky-sop-337-385-limit-enf2].

## What repayment structure is safest under Kentucky law? {#safest-structure}

**Short answer.** The safest Kentucky repayment term sets an amount the employer can show is reasonable in light of its anticipated or actual loss [^ky-sop-mattingly-reasonable-safe]. As a drafting matter, the repayment promise should be exchanged for a new obligation the employer was not already bound to perform, such as paying for training it had no prior duty to provide, because in *Creech* an agreement failed for lack of consideration where it did not require the employer to forbear a legal right or otherwise suffer a detriment [^ky-sop-creech-consideration-safe]. As a drafting matter, a schedule that reduces the balance as the employee serves the commitment keeps the amount closer to the employer's unrecovered cost and further from the unreasonably excessive forfeiture struck down in *Man O' War* [^ky-sop-man-o-war-excessive-safe].
Collecting the balance by separate demand or action is safer than withholding pay. KRS 337.060 bars withholding any part of agreed wages [^ky-sop-337-060-withhold-safe] except as law authorizes or the employee expressly authorizes a qualifying deduction in writing [^ky-sop-337-060-exception-safe], and final wages must be paid in full by the later of the next normal pay period or fourteen days after the employee leaves [^ky-sop-337-055-final-pay-safe].
