# Stay-or-Pay and Employee Repayment in Utah[^about]

Utah has no statute that limits stay-or-pay terms, and the Utah Supreme Court presumes a stipulated damages amount enforceable unless it is unconscionable; the Utah Payment of Wages chapter separately bars an employer from requiring an employee to return part of the wages to be paid and allows a deduction from wages, including final wages, only on a listed ground such as the employee's express written authorization.

## Are repayment terms enforceable in Utah? {#repayment-enforceability}

**Short answer.** No Utah appellate decision located for this guide rules on a training, signing-bonus or relocation repayment agreement. No Utah statute located for this guide limits stay-or-pay terms between an employer and an employee. The general Utah framework for liquidated damages is the closest authority for a repayment amount that works as stipulated damages for leaving early, though no Utah decision located for this guide applies it to an employee repayment term [^ut-sop-comcast-presumption-enforceable-enf]. In *Commercial Real Estate Investment v. Comcast of Utah II* the Utah Supreme Court held that courts begin with the longstanding presumption that liquidated damages clauses are enforceable [^ut-sop-comcast-presumption-enforceable-enf], and it explained that even its earlier decisions applying the penalty approach treated penalties as unenforceable because they are unconscionable [^ut-sop-comcast-penalties-unconscionable-enf]. The court also rejected judging a clause with hindsight rather than as of the time the contract was formed [^ut-sop-comcast-no-hindsight-enf].

"Instead, courts should begin with the longstanding presumption that liquidated damages clauses are enforceable."[^ut-sop-comcast-presumption-enforceable-enf]

*Comcast* was a commercial lease case, not an employment case: the court upheld the stipulated amount as compensation for a tenant's breach of its duty to continuously operate a building [^ut-sop-comcast-amount-not-unreasonable-enf]. No Utah decision located for this guide applies *Comcast* to an employee's promise to repay training, bonus or relocation money, or decides whether a term framed as repayment of an actual advance is tested the same way. The court still treats an unconscionable penalty as unenforceable [^ut-sop-comcast-penalties-unconscionable-enf]. As a drafting recommendation rather than a rule from a Utah decision, tying the amount to the employer's documented cost and reducing it as the employee serves the commitment leaves less room for that challenge.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A repayment clause whose amount or trigger is so one-sided that it oppresses or unfairly surprises the employee, judged as of signing, risks being refused enforcement as an unconscionable penalty [^ut-sop-comcast-substantive-test-enf][^ut-sop-comcast-penalties-unconscionable-enf][^ut-sop-comcast-no-hindsight-enf].

## Can a Utah employer claw back pay the employee already earned? {#clawback-earned-comp}

**Short answer.** The Utah Payment of Wages chapter limits recovering earned pay through the paycheck. Wages are the amounts due the employee for labor or services, however the amount is calculated [^ut-sop-wages-definition-clawback], and an employer may not require an employee to rebate, refund, offset or return part of the wage, salary or compensation to be paid except as Utah Code 34-28-3(6) allows [^ut-sop-no-rebate-clawback]. That subsection permits a deduction the employee expressly authorizes in writing, among other grounds [^ut-sop-deduction-limits-clawback]. No provision of the chapter can be set aside by mutual agreement unless the Division approves the agreement [^ut-sop-no-contravening-agreement-clawback]. No Utah appellate decision located for this guide decides whether a signing, retention or relocation bonus already paid can be recovered under a stay-or-pay term.

"An employer may not require an employee to rebate, refund, offset, or return a part of the wage, salary, or compensation to be paid to the employee except as provided in Subsection (6) ."[^ut-sop-no-rebate-clawback]

Utah Code 34-28-3(7) addresses compensation to be paid to the employee [^ut-sop-no-rebate-clawback]; it does not mention money the employer has already paid out. No Utah decision located for this guide decides whether an employee's written promise to repay a bonus or relocation payment made in advance is enforceable as a contract debt, or how the liquidated damages framework [^ut-sop-comcast-presumption-enforceable-clawback] applies to it. Utah Code 34-28-1 also excludes from the chapter any employment where an agreement provides for different terms of payment [^ut-sop-chapter-coverage-clawback]; no Utah decision located for this guide reads that exclusion to allow a repayment term to override the deduction limits, and Utah Code 34-28-7 requires Division approval for an agreement that sets the chapter aside [^ut-sop-no-contravening-agreement-clawback]. The conservative course is to put the repayment obligation in a written agreement signed before payment, with the amount and the service period, and not to make it depend on forfeiting wages the employee has already earned.

## Can a Utah employer take a repayment out of the final paycheck? {#final-pay-deduction}

**Short answer.** In employment covered by the Utah Payment of Wages chapter, an employer may deduct a repayment from wages, including final wages, only on a ground the law allows [^ut-sop-deduction-limits-final-pay]. The chapter does not cover the state and its political subdivisions, agricultural employment except for the final-pay section, household domestic service, or employment under an agreement providing for different terms of payment [^ut-sop-chapter-coverage-final-pay]. Utah Code 34-28-3(6) permits a deduction the employee expressly authorizes in writing, one a court order or law requires, or an offset a hearing officer or administrative law judge would find warranted [^ut-sop-deduction-limits-final-pay]. The Labor Commission's rule also treats as lawful a deduction repaying an advance or loan made during employment whose receipt the employee acknowledged in writing [^ut-sop-advance-loan-deduction-final-pay]. Otherwise the employer may not require the employee to return part of the wages to be paid [^ut-sop-no-rebate-final-pay], and it may not deduct any sums once it has failed to pay wages on time [^ut-sop-late-pay-no-deduction-final-pay]. The employer must give the employee a statement on each regular payday showing the total amount of each deduction [^ut-sop-deduction-statement-final-pay]. Final wages are due within 24 hours when the employer separates the employee [^ut-sop-final-pay-employer-separation-final-pay], and on the next regular payday when an employee without a written contract for a definite period resigns [^ut-sop-final-pay-resignation-final-pay]. Those deadlines do not apply to the commission-based earnings of a sales agent who has custody of the principal's accounts, money or goods, if the net amount due is determined only after an audit or verification [^ut-sop-final-pay-sales-agent-final-pay].

"An employer may not withhold or divert part of an employee's wages unless: (a) the employer is required to withhold or divert the wages by: (i) court order; or (ii) state or federal law; (b) the employee expressly authorizes the deduction in writing; (c) the employer presents evidence that in the opinion of a hearing officer or an administrative law judge would warrant an offset; or (d) subject to Subsection (8) , the employer withholds or diverts the wages: (i) as a contribution of the employee under a contract or plan that is: (A) described in Section 401(k), 403(b), 408, 408A, or 457, Internal Revenue Code; and (B) established by the employer;"[^ut-sop-deduction-limits-final-pay]

"H. Sums deducted from wages, in the proper amounts, for enforcement of a valid attachment or garnishment shall be honored by the Division; I. Sums deducted from wages as repayment to the employer by the employee of advances or loans made to the employee by the employer, provided that the: 1. Advance or loan to the employee occurred while the employee was in the employ of the employer; and 2. Employee's receipt of the advance or loan is evidenced by the employee's written acknowledgment;"[^ut-sop-advance-loan-deduction-final-pay]

The advance-or-loan rule speaks of money the employer advanced or lent to the employee. On its terms it does not describe a fixed sum owed for leaving early that the employer never paid to the employee, and no Utah decision located for this guide decides whether training paid to a third party, or a signing or relocation bonus, is an advance or loan under it [^ut-sop-advance-loan-deduction-final-pay]. No Utah decision located for this guide decides whether a written authorization signed at hire, before the amount owed is known, satisfies Utah Code 34-28-3(6)(b) for a later deduction of training or bonus costs. Utah's wage-payment chapter located for this guide has no express rule that a deduction may not take pay below the minimum wage. For an employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, wages count as paid only when paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear [^ut-sop-flsa-free-and-clear-final-pay], and the federal wage requirements are not met where the employee kicks back part of the wage to the employer [^ut-sop-flsa-kickback-final-pay]. No decision located for this guide decides whether deducting repayment of a bona fide advance of training, bonus or relocation money is a kickback for the employer's benefit under that rule.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A final-pay deduction clause that lets the employer hold final wages past the statutory deadline while it works out a repayment balance risks forfeiting any deduction, and after an employer-initiated separation, continuing wages of up to 60 days after the employee's written demand [^ut-sop-late-pay-no-deduction-final-pay][^ut-sop-final-pay-continuing-wages-final-pay].

## Are training or relocation repayments treated differently in Utah? {#training-repayment}

**Short answer.** No Utah statute located for this guide treats an employee's training, tuition or relocation repayment differently from bonus repayment. Utah's Post-Employment Restrictions Act generally limits a non-compete agreement entered into on or after May 10, 2016 to one year after employment ends [^ut-sop-noncompete-one-year-training], bars healthcare non-compete agreements entered into on or after May 6, 2026 [^ut-sop-noncompete-healthcare-training], and exempts reasonable severance agreements made at or after termination and agreements tied to the sale of a business where the individual receives value from the sale [^ut-sop-noncompete-exceptions-training], but its definition of a non-compete agreement lists exclusions for nonsolicitation, nondisclosure and confidentiality agreements and does not mention training or bonus repayment agreements [^ut-sop-noncompete-definition-training]. The general liquidated damages framework is the closest authority for a repayment term that works as stipulated damages for leaving early, though no Utah decision located for this guide applies it to an employee [^ut-sop-comcast-presumption-enforceable-training], and any collection from wages must fit Utah Code 34-28-3(6) [^ut-sop-deduction-limits-training] or the Labor Commission's advance-or-loan rule [^ut-sop-advance-loan-deduction-training].

"(a) Except as provided in Subsection (2) and in addition to any requirements imposed under common law, for a non-compete agreement entered into on or after May 10, 2016, an employer and an employee may not enter into a non-compete agreement for a period of more than one year from the day on which the employee is no longer employed by the employer."[^ut-sop-noncompete-one-year-training]

No Utah appellate decision located for this guide addresses a private employer's training, tuition or relocation repayment agreement, or decides whether a repayment term that restrains leaving is a non-compete agreement under the Act. Utah courts refuse to enforce a penalty that is unconscionable [^ut-sop-comcast-penalties-unconscionable-training]; as a drafting recommendation, keeping the amount at or below the employer's documented training or relocation cost avoids building in a sum beyond reimbursement.

## How are Utah repayment and wage-deduction rules enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employee covered by the Payment of Wages chapter [^ut-sop-chapter-coverage-enforcement] who says a repayment was wrongly taken from wages may file a wage claim with the Division, which accepts claims from $50 through $10,000 [^ut-sop-wage-claim-limits-enforcement], within one year after the wages were earned [^ut-sop-wage-claim-deadline-enforcement]. For a claim of $10,000 or less the employee must exhaust that process before suing, with exceptions [^ut-sop-wage-exhaustion-enforcement]. The employee may go straight to court if the claim exceeds $10,000, if it is joined with other claims against the same employer totaling more than $10,000, or if several employees' combined claims in one action exceed $10,000 [^ut-sop-wage-court-direct-enforcement]. The Division may assess a penalty of 5% of unpaid wages daily for up to 20 days [^ut-sop-division-late-penalty-enforcement], and a court may award actual damages and 2.5% of unpaid wages daily for up to 20 days after a final order, plus the final-pay penalty if applicable [^ut-sop-court-late-penalty-enforcement].

Where the employer separated the employee, if final wages are not paid within 24 hours of a written demand, the employee's wages continue at the separation rate from the date of demand until paid, for no more than 60 days [^ut-sop-final-pay-continuing-wages-enforcement], recoverable in a civil action commenced within 60 days after separation [^ut-sop-final-pay-action-deadline-enforcement]. That continuing-wage remedy sits in the subsection on employer-initiated separations; when an employee without a written contract for a definite period resigns, earned and unpaid wages are due on the next regular payday [^ut-sop-final-pay-resignation-enforcement]. No Utah decision located for this guide addresses how an employer collects an employee repayment balance. In the commercial lease dispute in *Comcast* the district court awarded about $1.7 million in liquidated damages plus interest on a contract claim [^ut-sop-comcast-award-enforcement]. The Utah Supreme Court upheld the stipulated amount [^ut-sop-comcast-amount-not-unreasonable-enforcement]. An employer that concedes a wage claim may pay it within ten working days of the Division's letter without the Division penalty [^ut-sop-wage-claim-cure-enforcement], unless it is a repeat offender with more than two valid wage claims within a running year that do not arise from the same facts [^ut-sop-wage-claim-repeat-offender-enforcement].

## What repayment structure is safest under Utah law? {#safest-structure}

**Short answer.** Because Utah courts refuse to enforce an unconscionable penalty [^ut-sop-comcast-penalties-unconscionable-safest], the recommended conservative structure ties the repayment amount to the employer's documented cost and reduces it as the employee serves the commitment. Utah courts otherwise presume a stipulated damages amount enforceable [^ut-sop-comcast-presumption-enforceable-safest], judged as of the time the contract was formed [^ut-sop-comcast-no-hindsight-safest]. As a drafting recommendation rather than a rule from a Utah decision, the repayment agreement should be written, signed before the employer pays for the training, bonus or relocation, and state the amount and the service period. Any deduction should rest on the employee's express written authorization [^ut-sop-deduction-limits-safest], together with, for an advance or loan, the employee's written acknowledgment of receipt [^ut-sop-advance-loan-deduction-safest], be taken only from wages paid on time [^ut-sop-late-pay-no-deduction-safest], appear on the payday statement [^ut-sop-deduction-statement-safest], and, for an employee covered by the federal Act, not operate as a kickback of the wages the Act requires [^ut-sop-flsa-free-and-clear-safest][^ut-sop-flsa-kickback-safest]. Where the balance is disputed, paying final wages in full on time [^ut-sop-final-pay-employer-separation-safest][^ut-sop-final-pay-resignation-safest] and pursuing the balance separately avoids, after an employer-initiated separation, continuing-wage liability [^ut-sop-final-pay-continuing-wages-safest].
