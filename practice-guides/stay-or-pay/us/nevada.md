# Stay-or-Pay and Employee Repayment in Nevada[^about]

Nevada has no statute specifically regulating stay-or-pay or training-repayment agreements in private employment, so a provision fixing the amount owed for leaving is tested under the liquidated-damages penalty rule, while NRS 608.100, NRS 608.110 and NAC 608.160 separately limit taking the money back out of pay.

## Are repayment terms enforceable in Nevada? {#repayment-enforceability}

**Short answer.** Nevada has no statute specifically regulating stay-or-pay or training-repayment agreements in private employment, and a provision fixing the amount owed for breach is subject to Nevada's liquidated-damages penalty test. The Nevada Supreme Court treats liquidated damage provisions as prima facie valid, so the party challenging one must establish that it amounts to a penalty [^nv-sop-haromy-valid-enf], by persuading the court that the liquidated damages are disproportionate to the actual damages the injured party sustained [^nv-sop-haromy-penalty-enf]. Separately, NRS 608.100(2) makes it unlawful to require an employee to return any part of the wage, salary or compensation earned by and paid to the employee [^nv-sop-608-100-rebate-enf].

"In order to prove a liquidated damage clause constitutes a penalty, the challenging party must persuade the court that the liquidated damages are disproportionate to the actual damages sustained by the injured party."[^nv-sop-haromy-penalty-enf]

In *Mason v. Fakhimi*, the Nevada Supreme Court described a penalty as a sum inserted in a contract as a punishment for default rather than as the measure of compensation for breach, and distinguished it from liquidated damages because a penalty is for the purpose of securing performance [^nv-sop-mason-penalty-def-enf]. If a provision is a penalty, the breaching party may recover the difference between the liquidated damages and the actual damages sustained [^nv-sop-mason-difference-enf]. A repayment amount set to keep the employee in the job, rather than to estimate what the employer loses when the employee leaves, sits closer to that definition of a penalty [^nv-sop-mason-penalty-def-enf].

Nevada's non-compete statute does not reach a repayment term as such. NRS 613.195 defines a noncompetition covenant as an agreement that, upon termination, prohibits the employee from pursuing a similar vocation in competition with, or becoming employed by a competitor of, the employer [^nv-sop-613-195-definition-enf]. No Nevada appellate decision located for this guide decides whether a repayment obligation triggered by joining a competitor is a noncompetition covenant under that definition.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A repayment amount set without regard to the employer's actual loss from the departure risks being struck as a penalty, leaving the employer to recover only its actual damages [^nv-sop-haromy-penalty-enf][^nv-sop-mason-difference-enf].

## Can a Nevada employer claw back pay the employee already earned? {#clawback-earned-comp}

**Short answer.** No Nevada appellate decision located for this guide decides whether a bonus already earned and paid can be recovered under a stay-or-pay term. NRS 608.100(2) makes it unlawful for any employer to require an employee to rebate, refund or return any part of the wage, salary or compensation earned by and paid to the employee [^nv-sop-608-100-rebate-claw]. Chapter 608's definition of wages excludes any bonus or arrangement to share profits [^nv-sop-608-012-wages-claw], but NRS 608.100(2) also reaches compensation, a word the definition does not limit [^nv-sop-608-100-rebate-claw].

"2. It is unlawful for any employer to require an employee to rebate, refund or return any part of the wage, salary or compensation earned by and paid to the employee."[^nv-sop-608-100-rebate-claw]

Because the rebate bar turns on compensation earned by and paid to the employee [^nv-sop-608-100-rebate-claw], the conservative course is to make a bonus conditional on continued service before it is earned and paid, rather than to rely on recovering a bonus the employee has already earned. Where a repayment term fixes damages for leaving, the liquidated-damages penalty rule also applies [^nv-sop-haromy-penalty-claw].

Pay for hours worked is a wage under NRS 608.012 [^nv-sop-608-012-wages-claw], and NRS 608.016 bars an employer from requiring an employee to work without wages during a trial or break-in period [^nv-sop-608-016-break-in-claw]. A repayment term cannot be used to recover pay for that time.

## Can repayment come out of a Nevada final paycheck? {#final-pay-deduction}

**Short answer.** A Nevada employer may deduct a repayment from wages, including final wages, only under a written authorization that meets NRS 608.110 and NAC 608.160. Apart from amounts required by law and employee benefit contributions, NAC 608.160 bars a deduction unless the employer reasonably believes the employee is responsible for the amount, the deduction is for a specific purpose, pay period and amount, and the employee voluntarily authorizes it in writing [^nv-sop-nac-608-160-conditions-final]. An employer may not use a blanket authorization made in advance by the employee [^nv-sop-nac-608-160-blanket-final].

"3. An employer may not use a blanket authorization that was made in advance by the employee to withhold any amount from the wages due the employee."[^nv-sop-nac-608-160-blanket-final]

NRS 608.110(1) permits other deductions authorized by written order of an employee [^nv-sop-608-110-written-order-final]. In *Coast Hotels & Casinos, Inc. v. Nevada State Labor Commission*, the Nevada Supreme Court held that the phrase permits an employer to withhold amounts from an employee's wages if the employee authorizes the withholding in writing [^nv-sop-coast-plain-language-final]. The court also held that language in a pre-employment form agreeing to the employer's reimbursement policy cannot authorize deductions for shortages that may or may not occur in the future [^nv-sop-coast-future-final], and that NRS 608.110 contemplates a voluntary written authorization [^nv-sop-coast-voluntary-final]. By the same reasoning, a repayment clause signed at hiring is not by itself an authorization to deduct a balance that arises only on a later departure [^nv-sop-coast-future-final].

Timing is strict. When an employer discharges an employee, the wages and compensation earned and unpaid become due and payable immediately [^nv-sop-608-020-discharge-final]. When an employee resigns or quits, the wages and compensation earned and unpaid must be paid by the earlier of the regular payday or seven days after the resignation [^nv-sop-608-030-quit-final]. Payment must be made in lawful money or a negotiable check unless the employee has agreed in writing to some other disposition of the wages or a court or government agency has directed one [^nv-sop-608-120-payment-final], and every deduction must appear on an itemized list given at the time of payment [^nv-sop-608-110-itemized-final].

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> Holding back final wages to net a training or bonus repayment without a qualifying authorization risks waiting-time penalties: if the employer fails to pay within 3 days after a discharged employee's wages become due, or on the due date for an employee who resigns or quits, the employee's wages continue at the same rate from separation until paid or for 30 days, whichever is less [^nv-sop-608-040-penalty-final].

## Are training or tuition repayments treated differently in Nevada? {#training-repayment}

**Short answer.** No Nevada statute or appellate decision located for this guide treats private-sector training, tuition or relocation repayment differently from other repayment terms. A provision fixing a training-repayment amount owed for an early departure is subject to the same liquidated-damages penalty test [^nv-sop-haromy-penalty-train]. Nevada's wage regulations do separately treat training required by the employer as time worked, which the employer must pay at not less than minimum wage [^nv-sop-nac-608-130-pay-train][^nv-sop-nac-608-130-required-train].

"(a) Is considered to be time worked by the employee if the training is required by the employer."[^nv-sop-nac-608-130-required-train]

Training required by an agency or entity other than the employer is not time worked, even if it keeps the employee eligible for a particular job [^nv-sop-nac-608-130-outside-train]. Because required training time is paid time [^nv-sop-nac-608-130-pay-train], and NRS 608.100(2) bars requiring an employee to return compensation earned and paid [^nv-sop-608-100-rebate-train], a training-repayment term is safer when it covers the employer's out-of-pocket course, tuition or travel costs rather than the wages paid for hours spent in training.

NRS 613.195 bars a noncompetition covenant from applying to an employee paid solely on an hourly wage basis, exclusive of tips or gratuities [^nv-sop-613-195-hourly-train]. That bar is part of the non-compete statute, which reaches only agreements that prohibit competing with or working for a competitor of the employer [^nv-sop-613-195-definition-train]. No Nevada statute located for this guide bars a training-repayment term for an hourly employee, but as a drafting matter, a repayment obligation that is triggered only by joining a competitor invites the argument that it operates as a noncompetition covenant [^nv-sop-613-195-definition-train].

## What happens to a Nevada employer that over-collects a repayment from pay? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employee whose final wages are not paid as NRS 608.020 to 608.050 require may bring a civil action against the employer within 2 years after the failure [^nv-sop-608-135-action-enf2], and the Nevada Supreme Court has held that chapter 608 provides a private right of action for unpaid wages [^nv-sop-neville-private-enf2]. A violation of the chapter's wage-payment provisions, including the deduction and rebate rules, is a misdemeanor, and the Labor Commissioner may impose an administrative penalty of up to $5,000 for each violation [^nv-sop-608-195-penalty-enf2].

"Accordingly, we conclude that NRS Chapter 608 provides a private right of action for unpaid wages."[^nv-sop-neville-private-enf2]

Late final pay adds waiting-time penalties. If an employer fails to pay within 3 days after the wages of a discharged employee, or of an employee placed on nonworking status, become due, or fails to pay an employee who resigns or quits on the day the wages are due, the employee's wages continue at the same rate from the day of separation until paid or for 30 days, whichever is less [^nv-sop-608-040-penalty-enf2]. Under NRS 608.050, an employee who is not paid wages due on discharge or layoff may collect wages at the contract rate for each day of default, for up to 30 days [^nv-sop-608-050-penalty-enf2]. An employee who establishes that the wages sued for are justly due, after making a written demand at least 5 days before suit for a sum not exceeding the amount found due, is entitled to a reasonable attorney fee [^nv-sop-608-140-fees-enf2]. If a court finds that a noncompetition covenant applies to an employee paid solely by the hour, it must award the employee reasonable attorney's fees and costs [^nv-sop-613-195-fees-enf2].

## What repayment structure is safest under Nevada law? {#safest-structure}

**Short answer.** The safest Nevada repayment term sets an amount the employer can show is not disproportionate to its actual damages from the departure [^nv-sop-haromy-penalty-safe], such as documented out-of-pocket training or hiring costs, and reduces the balance as the employee serves the commitment. As a drafting matter, a bonus should be conditioned on continued service before it is earned and paid, because NRS 608.100(2) bars requiring an employee to return compensation earned by and paid to the employee [^nv-sop-608-100-rebate-safe].
Collecting the balance by separate demand or action is safer than withholding pay. A deduction requires the employee's voluntary written authorization for a specific purpose, pay period and amount [^nv-sop-nac-608-160-conditions-safe], and a blanket authorization given in advance, such as one in the original repayment agreement, cannot be used [^nv-sop-nac-608-160-blanket-safe]. Final wages are due immediately on discharge [^nv-sop-608-020-discharge-safe] and by the earlier of the regular payday or seven days after a resignation [^nv-sop-608-030-quit-safe].
