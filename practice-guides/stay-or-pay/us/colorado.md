# Stay-or-Pay and Employee Repayment in Colorado[^about]

Colorado permits recovery of qualifying training costs on a two-year prorated schedule; other repayment terms raise noncompete questions, and the Wage Act separately limits payroll deductions.

## Are repayment terms enforceable in Colorado? {#repayment-enforceability}

**Short answer.** Colorado voids a covenant not to compete that restricts an individual's right to receive compensation for labor, but the statute expressly permits an employer to recover the reasonable, prorated cost of qualifying education and training [^co-covenant-void-rule][^co-training-recovery-exception]. Subsection (3) of C.R.S. § 8-2-113 also excludes from the prohibition a provision requiring repayment of a scholarship given to an apprentice who fails to comply with the scholarship agreement's conditions [^co-scholarship-apprenticeship-rule].
The statute does not define a covenant not to compete. It says the general assembly intends to preserve the state and federal case law in effect before August 10, 2022 that defines what counts as a prohibited covenant not to compete [^co-case-law-intent-preserved]. A repayment term outside the four subsection (3) categories, such as a sign-on or retention bonus repayable on resignation, therefore turns on whether a court treats it as a restriction on the worker's right to be paid for labor elsewhere; the conservative course is to assume it may be, because the statute lists the training provision among covenants that would otherwise be prohibited [^co-case-law-intent-preserved][^co-training-recovery-exception].
Fitting a subsection (3) category is not the end of the analysis. Any covenant not to compete otherwise permissible under subsection (2) or (3) is void unless the employer gives the worker notice of it and its terms before the worker accepts the offer, or at least fourteen days before the covenant or the change that supports it takes effect for a current worker; the notice must be a separate, signed document in clear and conspicuous terms [^co-covenant-notice-requirement].
The 2022 rewrite of the statute that created the current training-cost rule applies to covenants not to compete entered into or renewed on or after its effective date, August 10, 2022 [^co-hb1317-applicability][^co-case-law-intent-preserved]. A repayment agreement entered into before August 10, 2022 and not renewed on or after that date remains subject to the earlier statutory framework [^co-hb1317-applicability].

"(2) (a) Except as provided in subsections (2)(b), (2)(d), and (3) of this section, a covenant not to compete that restricts the right of an individual to receive compensation for performance of labor is void."[^co-covenant-void-rule]

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> A training-repayment clause that meets the cost and proration limits is still void if the employer skipped the separate, signed notice, so a signed offer letter alone does not preserve it [^co-covenant-notice-requirement].

## Can a Colorado employer claw back pay already earned? {#clawback-earned-comp}

**Short answer.** A Colorado employer cannot forfeit compensation once it is earned, vested, and determinable, because the Wage Act treats that compensation as wages and voids any agreement waiving Wage Act rights [^co-wages-earned-vested][^co-wage-act-nonwaiver]. The Wage Act counts as wages all amounts for labor or service performed and bonuses or commissions earned under the parties' agreement, and says no amount is wages until it is earned, vested, and determinable [^co-wages-earned-vested].
The Colorado Supreme Court applied that structure to a forfeiture clause in *Nieto v. Clark's Market, Inc.*, holding that vacation pay an employer chooses to provide is no less protected than other wages and cannot be forfeited once earned [^co-nieto-forfeiture-void]. Any agreement, written or oral, by which an employee purports to waive or modify Wage Act rights is void [^co-wage-act-nonwaiver].
The line that matters is when the money is earned. Because an amount is not wages until it is earned, vested, and determinable [^co-wages-earned-vested], a bonus written so that it is earned only after a stated period of service is treated differently from one paid outright and then subject to repayment. In our review, we found no Colorado appellate decision deciding whether a contract claim to recover a bonus already paid conflicts with these rules, so the conservative course is to treat a paid, earned bonus as wages that cannot be taken back through payroll and to make any repayment term a training-cost provision that fits C.R.S. § 8-2-113(3)(a) [^co-wages-earned-vested][^co-training-recovery-clawback].

"We conclude that, although the CWCA does not entitle an employee to vacation pay, when an employer chooses to provide it, such pay is no less protected than other wages or compensation and, thus, cannot be forfeited once earned."[^co-nieto-forfeiture-void]

## Can repayment come out of a Colorado final paycheck? {#final-pay-deduction}

**Short answer.** A Colorado employer may set off a repayment against final wages only as a deduction the Wage Act permits, which for a loan or advance means a written agreement that is enforceable and lawful, and since June 3, 2026 that deduction may not take pay below the applicable minimum wage [^co-final-pay-setoff][^co-deduction-loans-advances][^co-deduction-floor-2026]. The final-pay statute preserves the employer's right to set off deductions under C.R.S. § 8-4-105 owed by the employee [^co-final-pay-setoff], and section 8-4-105 bars every deduction it does not list [^co-deduction-loans-advances].
Senate Bill 26-160, approved June 3, 2026 and applying to conduct on or after its effective date, rewrote the two parts of section 8-4-105 that a repayment uses [^co-sl339-applicability]. Deductions are now allowed for loans, advances, goods or services, equipment or property, or any other thing that primarily benefits an employee, other than personal protective equipment, provided under a written agreement so long as it is enforceable and not in violation of law [^co-deduction-loans-advances]. The minimum-wage limit now reads that, except for deductions under subsections (1)(a), (1)(a.5), and (1)(d), nothing in the section authorizes a deduction below the applicable minimum wage [^co-deduction-floor-2026]. Before that change, the limit applied to every deduction under the section [^co-deduction-floor-2025]; either way, a repayment taken under the loan-and-advance paragraph must leave pay at or above minimum wage.
The written-agreement paragraph ties the deduction to enforceability: a deduction is permitted only so long as the agreement is enforceable and not in violation of law [^co-deduction-loans-advances]. A training-repayment term that is void under C.R.S. § 8-2-113 therefore cannot be collected through payroll, and the noncompete statute requires that any permitted training recovery would not violate article 4 of title 8, the Wage Act [^co-training-recovery-final-pay]. Before the 2026 amendment, the Court of Appeals held in *303 Beauty Bar LLC v. Division of Labor Standards & Statistics* that an agreement for deductions covering the employer's costs of doing business is unenforceable under section 8-4-105(1)(b) [^co-303-beauty-business-costs].

"(2) EXCEPT FOR A DEDUCTION MADE IN ACCORDANCE WITH SUBSECTION (1)(a), (1)(a.5), OR (1)(d) OF THIS SECTION , nothing in this section authorizes a deduction below the applicable minimum wage."[^co-deduction-floor-2026]

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> A final-paycheck deduction for training costs that exceeds the prorated amount the noncompete statute allows, or that takes the last check below minimum wage, is an unauthorized deduction rather than a permitted setoff [^co-training-recovery-final-pay][^co-deduction-floor-2026].

## Does Colorado treat training or tuition repayments differently? {#training-repayment}

**Short answer.** Colorado treats training repayment differently from other repayment terms: the noncompete statute permits recovery of the reasonable cost of education and training that is distinct from normal on-the-job training and transferable under the Attorney General's rule, reduced proportionately over the two years after the training [^co-training-recovery-training]. Recovery is capped at the reasonable costs of the training, decreases month by month over the two years after the training ends, and must not violate the federal Fair Labor Standards Act or the Colorado Wage Act; a public employer may spread the decrease over a longer period [^co-training-recovery-training].
Since April 30, 2026, the Attorney General's rule defines transferable training. A training or credential is transferable when the employee engages in it voluntarily and it directly results in a degree or certification from a listed Colorado institution, a license defined in Colorado, another state's or a tribe's law or a federal agency's grant, or a training or credential recognized by an industry or trade association, a union, or another organization representing employers or workers in the occupation [^co-ag-transferability-rule]. The statute gives the Attorney General authority to adopt rules to implement and enforce the training provision [^co-ag-rulemaking-authority]. Mandatory training, or training that satisfies none of the rule's listed outcomes, falls outside the training-repayment exception [^co-ag-transferability-rule].
Subsection (3) of C.R.S. § 8-2-113 names education and training costs and apprenticeship scholarships, and no other repayment category [^co-training-recovery-training][^co-scholarship-apprenticeship-training]. A relocation-cost repayment therefore has no express carve-out, and the conservative course is to treat it like any other repayment term that may restrict a worker's right to be paid for labor elsewhere [^co-covenant-void-training].

"A training or credential is transferable for the purposes of C.R.S. Section 8-2-113(3)(a) when it is engaged in voluntarily by the employee and directly results in either"[^co-ag-transferability-rule]

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> A training-repayment schedule that keeps the full cost owed until a cliff date, rather than reducing it with each month after the training, exceeds the recovery the statute allows [^co-training-recovery-training].

## What happens to a Colorado employer that enforces a void repayment term? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** A Colorado employer that presents, enters into, or tries to enforce a void covenant is liable for actual damages and a $5,000 penalty per worker harmed, and the Attorney General may recover three times any training-cost recovery or attempted recovery that violates the training provision [^co-void-covenant-prohibited][^co-covenant-penalties]. The Attorney General and any harmed worker or prospective worker may sue for injunctive relief and penalties, and a worker may also recover actual damages, reasonable costs and attorney fees [^co-covenant-penalties].
A court may reduce or eliminate the penalty if the employer shows it acted in good faith with reasonable grounds to believe it was not violating the statute [^co-covenant-good-faith]. A worker, or a later employer that has hired or is considering hiring the worker, may also seek a declaratory judgment that the covenant is unenforceable [^co-covenant-declaratory].
For a worker who primarily resided or worked in Colorado at termination, the covenant may not require the worker to litigate its enforceability outside Colorado, and Colorado law governs enforceability for a worker who primarily resided and worked in Colorado at termination, notwithstanding a contrary contract term [^co-covenant-choice-of-law].

"(8) (a) An employer shall not enter into, present to a worker or prospective worker as a term of employment, or attempt to enforce any covenant that is void under this section."[^co-void-covenant-prohibited]

## What repayment structure is safest under Colorado law? {#safest-structure}

**Short answer.** The safest Colorado repayment term recovers only the reasonable cost of training that is distinct from on-the-job training and transferable under the Attorney General's rule, and reduces the amount owed month by month over the two years after the training [^co-training-recovery-structure][^co-ag-transferability-structure]. Under the rule, that means training the employee takes voluntarily that directly results in a qualifying degree, certification, license, or recognized credential [^co-ag-transferability-structure].
The term should then be delivered with the statute's notice: before a prospective worker accepts the offer, or for a current worker at least fourteen days before the earlier of the covenant's effective date and the effective date of any added compensation or changed terms that supply consideration for it, in a separate document the worker signs, written in clear and conspicuous terms in the language the employer and worker use to discuss the worker's performance [^co-covenant-notice-structure]. The statute treats the notice as sufficient when it comes with a copy of the agreement, names the agreement and states that it contains a covenant not to compete that could restrict the worker's options for later employment, and points the worker to the specific sections that contain the covenant [^co-covenant-notice-contents].
If the employer plans to collect through payroll, the deduction needs a written agreement that is enforceable and lawful and, after June 3, 2026, must leave pay at or above minimum wage [^co-deduction-loans-structure][^co-deduction-floor-structure].
