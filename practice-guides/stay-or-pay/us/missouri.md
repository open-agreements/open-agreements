# Stay-or-Pay and Employee Repayment in Missouri[^about]

Missouri has no statute that limits stay-or-pay terms, and the Missouri Court of Appeals in Smith v. Kriska enforced a prorated police training-repayment agreement that was a condition of employment; on discharge, earned wages are due that day without abatement or deduction, but older Missouri authority allows an employer to set off a valid employee debt, and the minimum wage and a court's penalty review still limit what the employer can take or recover.

## Are repayment terms enforceable in Missouri? {#repayment-enforceability}

**Short answer.** Missouri courts have enforced a training-repayment agreement that was a condition of employment [^mo-sop-kriska-training-agreement-enforced][^mo-sop-kriska-not-adhesion][^mo-sop-kriska-not-unconscionable]. In *Smith v. Kriska* the Missouri Court of Appeals affirmed a judgment for the St. Louis police board against a former officer and rejected the argument that the agreement was an adhesion contract because signing it was a prerequisite to employment [^mo-sop-kriska-not-adhesion]. The agreement applied if the officer resigned within forty-eight months after training and took a job with another law enforcement agency within a year [^mo-sop-kriska-competing-employment-trigger], and required repayment of training costs and salary, reduced by one forty-eighth for each month served [^mo-sop-kriska-prorated-reimbursement-term]. No Missouri statute located for this guide limits stay-or-pay terms.

"Defendant was provided training at no cost to himself, while he received a salary, in exchange for a commitment to work for the Department for four years after the conclusion of training, or, if he chose to leave the Department earlier, to reimburse the training costs on a pro-rata basis. These terms did not demonstrate a strong, gross, and manifest inequality."[^mo-sop-kriska-not-unconscionable]

*Kriska* involved a public employer, the St. Louis Board of Police Commissioners [^mo-sop-kriska-board-agreement], and a repayment obligation that depended on the officer joining another law enforcement agency [^mo-sop-kriska-competing-employment-trigger]. A private employer's claim can fail on the employer's own performance. In *Pepsi Midamerica v. Harris* the Court of Appeals stated that an employer suing on a training-repayment agreement must show its own substantial compliance with the contract [^mo-sop-pepsi-substantial-compliance-required], and held that the employer could not recover because it materially breached its promise to provide $2,500 worth of truck driver training [^mo-sop-pepsi-training-not-provided].

A repayment term that fixes an amount owed for leaving early can also be tested as a penalty. Missouri courts enforce liquidated damages clauses but not penalty clauses [^mo-sop-grand-bissell-penalty-rule], and they apply the Restatement test, under which the amount must be a reasonable forecast of just compensation for the harm caused by the breach and the harm must be incapable or very difficult of accurate estimation [^mo-sop-grand-bissell-restatement-test]. Missouri also requires the party enforcing the clause to show at least some actual harm caused by the breach [^mo-sop-grand-bissell-actual-harm]. No Missouri appellate decision located for this guide applies that test to an employee repayment term, so the conservative course is to tie the amount to the employer's documented cost and reduce it as the employee serves the commitment, as the agreement in *Kriska* did.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A flat repayment amount that does not decline with service and is not tied to what the employer spent risks being treated as an unenforceable penalty rather than a reasonable forecast of the employer's harm [^mo-sop-grand-bissell-restatement-test].

## Can a Missouri employer claw back pay the employee already earned? {#clawback-earned-comp}

**Short answer.** No Missouri appellate decision located for this guide decides whether a signing, retention or relocation bonus already paid can be recovered under a stay-or-pay term. The closest Missouri authority treats an amount the employer advanced on the employee's promise to repay as a debt: in *McLaurin v. Frisella Moving & Storage Co.* the Court of Appeals stated that when an employee who owed the employer for an advanced employment-agency fee was discharged, the employer had the right to apply the wages due against that debt [^mo-sop-mclaurin-setoff-at-discharge-clawback]. Wages already earned are a different matter, because an employer that pays less than the Minimum Wage Law requires owes the unpaid wages plus twice that amount as liquidated damages [^mo-sop-unpaid-wage-liquidated-damages-clawback], and an agreement to work for less is no defense [^mo-sop-underpayment-agreement-no-defense-clawback].

In *McLaurin* the employer's evidence was that it paid the fee on the employee's promise to repay, which the employee denied [^mo-sop-mclaurin-disputed-promise-clawback], and the court's statement about the set-off right rested on that evidence [^mo-sop-mclaurin-setoff-at-discharge-clawback]. The jury found for the employee and awarded unpaid wages and penalties [^mo-sop-mclaurin-jury-verdict-clawback]. The conservative course is to state the repayment obligation for any bonus or advance in a written agreement signed before payment, with the amount and the service period, so that the employer can show a valid debt if it later seeks repayment.

## Can a Missouri employer take a repayment out of the final paycheck? {#final-pay-deduction}

**Short answer.** According to the Court of Appeals, a discharging Missouri employer may apply final wages against a valid debt the employee owes [^mo-sop-mclaurin-setoff-at-discharge], subject to wage-law limits. Those limits include Mo. Rev. Stat. § 290.110, which makes a discharged employee's earned wages due that day at the contract rate, without abatement or deduction [^mo-sop-final-pay-discharge-deadline], and the federal rule that wages count as paid only when paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear [^mo-sop-flsa-free-and-clear]. The court added that denying the set-off of a valid debt might render the final-pay statute unconstitutional [^mo-sop-mclaurin-valid-debt-setoff]. If the employee asks the foreman or timekeeper in writing to send the money to a station or office where a regular agent is kept and it does not arrive within seven days, wages continue as a penalty from the date of discharge until paid, for no more than sixty days [^mo-sop-final-pay-discharge-deadline]. The section does not apply to an employee paid primarily by commission whose duties include collecting accounts or caring for stock or merchandise, where an audit is needed to determine the net amount due [^mo-sop-final-pay-commission-exception].

"Employer wage withholding mandated by an agreement giving an employer the right to withhold money from an employee’s final paycheck is not an assignment of wages within the meaning of section 432.030."[^mo-sop-kriska-withholding-not-assignment]

Missouri has no general statute located for this guide that requires written consent for a wage deduction. The agreement in *Kriska* expressly let the board deduct the amount to be reimbursed from money owed to the officer at resignation [^mo-sop-kriska-deduction-clause], and the court held that such withholding is not an assignment of wages [^mo-sop-kriska-withholding-not-assignment]. That holding matters because Mo. Rev. Stat. § 432.030 requires a wage assignment to be written, dated and stated in amount, and voids any assignment of wages not yet earned when it is made [^mo-sop-wage-assignment-unearned-void]. Section 290.110 states a deadline only for discharge and refusal to further employ [^mo-sop-final-pay-discharge-deadline], and no Missouri decision located for this guide addresses whether its penalty applies to a deduction from the final pay of an employee who quits.

The minimum wage limits repayment deductions taken for the employer's benefit. The Missouri minimum wage is $15.00 an hour from January 1, 2026 [^mo-sop-minimum-wage-fifteen-dollar-rate], and Missouri's overtime section is interpreted in accordance with the Fair Labor Standards Act and its regulations unless the Minimum Wage Law provides otherwise [^mo-sop-overtime-flsa-interpretation]. Under the federal rule, wages count as paid only when paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear [^mo-sop-flsa-free-and-clear], and the federal wage requirements are not met where the employee kicks back part of the wage to the employer or for the employer's benefit [^mo-sop-flsa-kickback]. No Missouri authority located for this guide decides how the free-and-clear rule applies to a training or bonus repayment, so the conservative course is to keep a repayment deduction from reducing a workweek's pay below the minimum wage or cutting into overtime pay.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A final-pay deduction clause that permits withholding a disputed repayment balance risks a continuing-wage penalty of up to sixty days when the employer cannot establish the debt [^mo-sop-final-pay-discharge-deadline][^mo-sop-mclaurin-valid-debt-setoff].

## Are training or relocation repayments treated differently in Missouri? {#training-repayment}

**Short answer.** No Missouri statute located for this guide treats training, tuition or relocation repayment differently from bonus repayment. The leading Missouri decision is a training case: in *Smith v. Kriska* the Court of Appeals enforced a police training agreement [^mo-sop-kriska-training-agreement-enforced-training] that applied if the officer resigned within forty-eight months after training and joined another law enforcement agency within a year [^mo-sop-kriska-competing-employment-trigger-training], and required repayment of training costs and salary, prorated by months served [^mo-sop-kriska-prorated-reimbursement-term-training]. The court held that training at no cost to the officer, with salary, in exchange for a four-year commitment or pro rata reimbursement did not show the gross inequality needed for unconscionability [^mo-sop-kriska-not-unconscionable-training]. In *Pepsi Midamerica v. Harris*, by contrast, a private employer could not recover under a driver training-repayment agreement because it materially breached its promise to provide the training [^mo-sop-pepsi-training-not-provided-training].

"Specifically, he shall reimburse the Board in an amount equal to the sum of said training costs and salary payments divided by forty-eight (48), then multiplied by the number of months less than forty-eight (48) which the employee has served the Board as a Police Officer."[^mo-sop-kriska-prorated-reimbursement-term-training]

No Missouri decision located for this guide addresses relocation repayment. Where a relocation or tuition repayment provision fixes damages for breach in advance, the Missouri test requires that the amount be a reasonable forecast of just compensation for the harm caused by the breach and that the harm be incapable or very difficult of accurate estimation [^mo-sop-grand-bissell-restatement-test-training]. No Missouri decision located for this guide applies that test to an employee repayment provision.

## How are Missouri repayment and wage-deduction rules enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** A Missouri employer enforces a repayment agreement by suing on the contract [^mo-sop-kriska-training-agreement-enforced-enforcement]. In *Smith v. Kriska* the trial court found that the officer had breached his employment contract and awarded the board $4,253.40 plus interest and costs, which the Court of Appeals affirmed [^mo-sop-kriska-training-agreement-enforced-enforcement]. If a discharged employee's written request to the foreman or timekeeper to send the money to a station or office with a regular agent goes unmet for seven days, the employer faces continuing wages from the discharge date until payment, capped at sixty days [^mo-sop-final-pay-discharge-deadline-enforcement], unless the employee falls within the exception for certain commissioned employees whose pay requires an audit [^mo-sop-final-pay-commission-exception-enforcement]. An employer that pays less than the Minimum Wage Law requires owes the unpaid wages plus twice that amount as liquidated damages, costs and reasonable attorney fees [^mo-sop-unpaid-wage-liquidated-damages-enforcement], and an action to collect the deficiency must be started within three years [^mo-sop-wage-claim-three-year-limit-enforcement].

Where the repayment agreement fixes the amount owed, the employer must show at least some actual harm caused by the breach before a liquidated damages clause can be triggered [^mo-sop-grand-bissell-actual-harm-enforcement]. An employee's agreement to work for less than the minimum wage is no defense to a Minimum Wage Law action [^mo-sop-underpayment-agreement-no-defense-enforcement], so an employee's consent to a repayment term does not by itself answer a claim that pay fell below that rate.

## What repayment structure is safest under Missouri law? {#safest-structure}

**Short answer.** The safest Missouri structure borrows the terms the Court of Appeals enforced in *Smith v. Kriska* [^mo-sop-kriska-training-agreement-enforced-safest]: a signed agreement [^mo-sop-kriska-training-began-safest] for a specific total amount [^mo-sop-kriska-specific-amount-safest] and a repayment that falls by one forty-eighth for each month served [^mo-sop-kriska-prorated-reimbursement-term-safest]. In *Kriska* the officer began training the day before he signed [^mo-sop-kriska-training-began-safest], so having the employee sign before training begins goes beyond the facts of that case; signing before the employer pays anything is a drafting recommendation, not an assured result. The employer must also deliver the promised training, because an employer that materially breached that promise could not recover [^mo-sop-pepsi-training-not-provided-safest]. The amount should track the employer's documented cost, because a stipulated amount must be a reasonable forecast of just compensation for the harm caused by the breach [^mo-sop-grand-bissell-restatement-test-safest]. A deduction clause in the agreement is not a void assignment of wages [^mo-sop-kriska-withholding-not-assignment-safest]. For employees covered by § 290.110, a discharged employee's wages are due that day without abatement or deduction, and continuing wages may follow when payment does not reach the requested station or office within seven days after a written request to the foreman or timekeeper [^mo-sop-final-pay-discharge-deadline-safest]; the section does not cover certain commissioned employees whose net pay requires an audit [^mo-sop-final-pay-commission-exception-safest]. Where the balance is disputed, paying the final wages in full and pursuing the balance separately avoids that penalty. Because wages count as paid only when paid free and clear [^mo-sop-flsa-free-and-clear-safest], the conservative course is to keep any repayment deduction from reducing pay below the minimum wage.
