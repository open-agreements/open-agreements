# Buying an App Business in the United States[^about]

How the buyer of an app or small online software business protects itself, often through a marketplace and an escrow service: escrow and inspection, the marketplace's standard agreement, checking revenue against app-store reports, confirming the app runs without the seller, keys and secrets, and moving the app, domain, accounts and hosting at closing.

## How does an online escrow service protect the buyer and seller of an app, and what happens if the inspection period lapses? {#escrow-inspection-and-release}

**Short answer.** An escrow service such as Escrow.com holds the buyer's money through an agreed inspection period and treats a buyer that neither accepts nor rejects as having accepted, so the money goes to the seller when the period ends. [^escrow-deemed-acceptance] [^escrow-lapse-releases-funds]

Escrow.com's inspection periods run from 1 to 30 calendar days, are agreed at the start, and should allow enough time for required authentication or appraisal. [^escrow-inspection-length-start] The period starts when the buyer marks receipt or Escrow.com verifies or receives confirmation of delivery, which is why the [handover checklist](#escrow-inspection-handover-checklist) matters before anything is marked received. [^escrow-inspection-start] A reminder before release is not guaranteed. [^escrow-notice-not-guaranteed]

Escrow.com's instructions require the buyer to return rejected property before receiving a refund, and failure to return it within the specified period causes payment to the seller. [^escrow-return-before-refund]

Unless the parties agree otherwise in the transaction, the buyer pays Escrow.com's fees, which are nonrefundable once paid. [^escrow-default-fee-payer] The buyer's agreed share is automatically added to the purchase price. [^escrow-agreed-fee-added] On cancellation or return, however, the buyer bears the entire escrow fee, which is deducted from the refund regardless of an earlier split. [^escrow-buyer-pays-fee-on-cancel] [^escrow-refund-fee-regardless] Consequently, a contractual split alone does not change that deduction; reimbursement by the seller would be needed to restore the seller's agreed share to the buyer. [^escrow-refund-fee-regardless]

Escrow does not check title: Escrow.com assumes no responsibility for the condition of ownership or the sufficiency of the documents that convey it. [^escrow-no-ownership-responsibility]

## Is a marketplace's standard purchase agreement enough, or should it be negotiated? {#marketplace-standard-form}

**Short answer.** A marketplace's standard purchase agreement may come without any assurance that it fits a particular deal, so whether it is enough depends on whether its terms cover that deal's price structure, assets and risks. [^p6-trustmrr-templates-convenience]

TrustMRR's terms, for example, describe its document templates as a convenience only and disclaim any representation about their legal sufficiency or appropriateness for a specific situation. [^p6-trustmrr-templates-convenience]

The standard agreement is also where the buyer's protection sits, because the platform stays outside it; TrustMRR's terms say it is not the buyer or seller and not a party to any acquisition agreement between users. [^d3-trustmrr-not-a-party] TrustMRR's terms also state that the acquisition agreement and asset transfer remain between the buyer and seller, [^p6-trustmrr-agreement-between-parties] and that transferring the startup's assets is solely the responsibility of the buyer and seller. [^p6-trustmrr-transfer-responsibility] Protection against inaccurate revenue figures, missing assets or failed transfers therefore comes from the purchase agreement's own terms, because the platform does not guarantee either party, the assets or the outcome; [^d3-trustmrr-no-guarantee] the questions on [revenue figures](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#screenshots), [recovery for inaccurate representations](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-after-closing/us#remedy-allocation) and [handover](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-closing-and-handover/us#digital-handover) describe those terms.

A price paid partly after closing is one sign that the default needs more; TrustMRR's FAQ says the platform supports only a single lump-sum payment through escrow and that other payment arrangements are handled outside it. [^p6-trustmrr-lump-sum] A deal that needs a holdback, deferred payment, earnout or seller note therefore needs its own terms, discussed under [deferred payment](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#escrow-holdback-and-deferred-payment) and [earnouts](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#price-mechanisms), and a route the platform's terms permit, which for TrustMRR means written approval before completing a transaction outside its standard escrow. [^d3-trustmrr-approval-route-sf]

A general asset description is another sign; TrustMRR's FAQ says escrow needs verifiable asset details and that missing or vague asset details can lead to rejection of the agreement. [^p6-trustmrr-asset-details] An itemized [asset schedule](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-what-transfers/us#schedules) serves the escrow review, because TrustMRR creates the escrow transaction from the APA's asset details, and also gives the buyer a fixed list against which delivery is later checked. [^d3-trustmrr-escrow-from-apa]

Assets that need third-party consent or a migration in several steps, customer obligations that straddle closing and a seller restraint on competing are other signs, covered under [consents](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-closing-and-handover/us#consent-failure), [account migration](#migration-timing), [customer obligations](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-after-closing/us#customer-obligation-cutoff) and [seller non-competes](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-after-closing/us#seller-covenants). Whether a particular platform form already covers them is a question for the text of the revision the parties will sign, because it is the executed document that may create binding obligations according to its terms. [^d3-trustmrr-executed-document]

## How can a buyer check an app's subscription revenue against the app-store reports? {#subscription-revenue-app-store-reports}

**Short answer.** A buyer can compare the seller's revenue figures with Apple's monthly financial reports and Google Play's monthly earnings reports, which report proceeds, sales or transactions for the reporting period. [^apple-financial-reports-monthly] [^play-earnings-monthly]

Apple generates financial reports on its fiscal calendar for periods containing purchases or refunds. [^apple-financial-reports-monthly] Google Play's earnings reports contain the prior month's transactions and are typically available by the fifth of the following month. [^play-earnings-monthly]

Apple limits financial-report downloads to the Account Holder, Admin and Finance roles and retains reports for ten years. [^apple-financial-reports-roles] [^apple-financial-reports-retention] After transfer, the buyer receives Apple sales and payment information only for later transactions, while Google Play's existing earnings reports do not transfer. [^apple-sales-data-split] [^play-reports-do-not-transfer] Consequently, the historical reports need to come from the seller rather than from the buyer's post-transfer reporting access. [^apple-sales-data-split] [^play-reports-do-not-transfer]

Apple's Partner Share means proceeds per unit after applicable taxes and Apple's commission. [^apple-partner-share-net] Its Title field uses the app name for downloads but the Product ID for in-app purchases, so filtering only by the app name can miss those purchase rows. [^apple-title-product-id] Google Play does not recommend estimated sales reports for accounting because they omit withholding taxes and chargebacks. [^play-estimated-not-accounting]

One filed marketplace-brokered agreement contained a seller warranty that the revenue and other details given to the broker were truthful and accurate, so price or trial history given to the broker as part of those details could fall within that warranty (see [how the agreement defines revenue figures](/practice-guides/asset-purchase-price-and-payment/us#screenshots) and the seller's [dashboard figures](/practice-guides/selling-an-app-business/us#dashboard-figures-accuracy)). [^jeffs-seller-truthful-details]

## How is a buyer protected if the seller does not disclose a drop in traffic or revenue? {#disclosure-of-traffic-revenue-drops}

**Short answer.** A buyer's contractual protection against an undisclosed drop in traffic or revenue rests mainly on the purchase agreement's representations about the figures the seller supplied, because marketplaces such as Acquire.com and Flippa leave checking those figures to the buyer. [^jeffs-seller-truthful-details-revenue-drops] [^acquire-no-diligence] [^flippa-buyer-investigates]

Acquire.com states that it performs no due diligence and is not responsible for a seller's truthfulness about a listing's existence, quality, accuracy or completeness. [^acquire-no-diligence] Flippa makes the buyer responsible for investigating the asset and listing information, including financial statements and liabilities. [^flippa-buyer-investigates]

One filed marketplace-brokered agreement contained a seller warranty that the revenue, profit, expense and pageview details given to the broker were truthful and accurate. [^jeffs-seller-truthful-details-revenue-drops] The same agreement waived performance-related contingencies after inspection and acknowledged that earnings and traffic could decline. [^jeffs-waiver-after-inspection] [^jeffs-earnings-may-decline] That waiver reaches any discrepancies, fluctuations or changes in performance, not only disclosed declines, but it opens with "Except as otherwise provided by this Agreement"[^jeffs-waiver-after-inspection], which is the wording that leaves room for a claim under the seller's separate accuracy warranty. [^jeffs-waiver-after-inspection] [^jeffs-seller-truthful-details-revenue-drops]

In *ABRY Partners V, L.P. v. F & W Acquisition LLC*, the Delaware Court of Chancery held that a damages cap could not limit remedies for the seller's intentional misrepresentation of a fact embodied in the contract. [^abry-lies-not-capped] California Civil Code section 1668 makes contractual exemptions from responsibility for a party's own fraud contrary to public policy. [^cal-civ-1668-own-fraud]

This guide does not survey state-law duties to disclose where the contract is silent, and the seller's side of the same issue is in [pricing changes before listing](/practice-guides/selling-an-app-business/us#pricing-changes-before-listing).

## How can a buyer confirm the app runs without the seller's accounts and services? {#app-runs-without-seller}

**Short answer.** A buyer can confirm that the app runs without the seller by building and running the delivered code in accounts the buyer controls, since Apple leaves the hand-over of code and build assets to the seller and Google's site-move guidance tests a copy on the new host. [^apple-transferor-delivers-code] [^google-copy-and-test]

Apple makes the transferor responsible for delivering the code and build assets directly to the recipient and explaining capabilities or App Store configuration needed for future updates. [^apple-transferor-delivers-code] Google's site-move guidance calls for uploading a copy to the new hosting provider and thoroughly testing every aspect of how users interact with it. [^google-copy-and-test]

The transfer also involves account-specific setup: Apple requires new provisioning profiles in the recipient's developer account, associated with the transferred app's identifier and distribution certificate. [^apple-provisioning-profiles] Google Play calls for updates to account settings and apps for integrated services such as Firebase and Google Analytics. [^play-integrated-services] Vercel requires project integrations to be added again after transfer. [^vercel-integrations-again]

A repository transfer alone does not demonstrate that every dependency has moved: GitHub keeps existing webhooks, services, secrets and deploy keys associated with the repository. [^github-transfer-secrets-stay] That persistence means a successful repository transfer does not itself establish who controls the services behind its existing credentials, which the [inventory of keys and services](#secrets-and-services-inventory) records. [^github-transfer-secrets-stay]

## Should a buyer review the live app or the code repository? {#review-live-app-or-repository}

**Short answer.** A buyer's review can cover both the live app and the delivered repository, because the app users run transfers with its store listing while the seller hands over the code and build assets separately. [^apple-transfer-keeps-reviews] [^apple-transferor-delivers-code-or-repository]

Apple states that the transferred app retains its reviews, ratings and bundle identifier, and users continue receiving updates. [^apple-transfer-keeps-reviews] The seller separately supplies the code and build assets and explains capabilities or configuration needed for future updates. [^apple-transferor-delivers-code-or-repository]

When accepting an Apple transfer, the recipient reviews the previous owner's privacy disclosures; missing or deleted disclosures must be completed before a new version is submitted. [^apple-accept-privacy-details] Google Play states that advertising software development kit integrations in the app's package files need updating after transfer so advertising traffic is credited to the correct account. [^play-ad-sdk-update]

Updates can also change existing behavior: Apple states that keychain sharing continues only until the app is updated and requires rebuilding the keychain for updates. [^apple-keychain-until-update]

One filed marketplace-brokered agreement required the seller to maintain the asset as it was leading up to sale and avoid actions outside normal business practices throughout migration. [^jeffs-ordinary-course] A term of that kind reaches a code change made outside normal business practices during the migration. [^jeffs-ordinary-course] A change made before signing turns instead on what the seller disclosed and represented, the seller's side of which is in [the cleaned-up code question](/practice-guides/selling-an-app-business/us#cleaned-up-code-disclosure).

## What should the inventory of keys, secrets and services list, and when are keys replaced? {#secrets-and-services-inventory}

**Short answer.** The inventory can list each key, secret and connected service with where it lives and who controls it, and credentials the seller can still reach can be replaced once the transfer completes, as Apple requires for an app's shared secret. [^github-transfer-secrets-stay-services-inventory] [^apple-shared-secret]

GitHub keeps webhooks, services, secrets and deploy keys associated with a transferred repository. [^github-transfer-secrets-stay-services-inventory] Apple also transfers configured app webhooks unless the transferor deletes them first. [^apple-webhooks-transfer]

Apple Pay transactions continue while the original certificates remain valid, but the recipient needs a new merchant identifier for the first update. [^apple-pay-merchant-id] Apple's push-notification certificates remain valid until expiry, after which the recipient team must generate a new certificate. [^apple-push-certificates]

For auto-renewable subscriptions, Apple requires the recipient to obtain the existing app-specific shared secret before acceptance and generate a new one after transfer so outsiders no longer have access. [^apple-shared-secret] Google Play suggests that both owners consider the security of the upload and app-signing keys when transferring an app using Play App Signing. [^play-signing-keys]

Removing a person from a GitHub organization does not prevent repository access through a deploy key whose private key that person still holds. [^github-deploy-key-warning] Amazon Web Services does not automatically delete an access role created for the old organization's management account when a member account leaves. [^aws-role-not-deleted] Consequently, transfer and membership removal alone do not establish exclusive buyer control over retained credentials and access roles. [^github-deploy-key-warning] [^aws-role-not-deleted]

## How should the escrow inspection period line up with the handover checklist? {#escrow-inspection-handover-checklist}

**Short answer.** The agreed inspection period needs enough time for the planned handover checks because the parties fix it when the Escrow.com transaction starts and Escrow.com permits extensions only at its discretion. [^escrow-inspection-length-start-handover-checklist] [^escrow-inspection-not-modified]

Escrow.com's periods run from 1 to 30 calendar days and should allow adequate time for required authentication or appraisal. [^escrow-inspection-length-start-handover-checklist] The clock starts when the buyer marks receipt or Escrow.com verifies or receives confirmation of delivery, so an early receipt acknowledgment can start inspection before other checks are finished. [^escrow-inspection-start-handover-checklist]

Platform processing can consume part of that time: Apple states that an accepted transfer can take up to two business days to complete. [^apple-accept-processing] Google Play states that its support team reviews and replies to transfer requests within two business days, following the target developer's review and approval. [^play-request-review]

One filed marketplace-brokered agreement instead measured its fourteen-day inspection period from completed migration. [^jeffs-inspection-from-migration] That agreement allowed the buyer to request termination only for a substantial deviation, defined as inspection-period revenue below half the prorated average monthly revenue. [^jeffs-substantial-deviation]

Escrow.com releases funds to the seller if the buyer takes no action before inspection ends. [^escrow-lapse-releases-funds-handover-checklist] A jointly requested extension remains discretionary, and changes to transaction instructions require every party, including Escrow.com, which reserves the right to reject them. [^escrow-inspection-not-modified] [^escrow-supplemental-instructions]

## What must be ready before an Apple app can transfer? {#app-transfer}

**Short answer.** An Apple app transfer in App Store Connect requires the recipient Account Holder's Apple Account and the recipient account's Team ID, so the buyer's developer account has to exist before the transfer can be requested. [^apple-transfer-app-transfer]

Account enrollment and any organization verification belong early in the closing plan rather than at the moment of transfer, because an organization enrolling in Apple's developer program needs an Apple Account with two-factor authentication turned on, [^p6-apple-enroll-2fa] and, unless it is a government entity, a D-U-N-S Number that Apple uses to verify the organization's identity, legal entity status and address. [^p6-apple-enroll-duns]

Apple's transfer criteria then apply to both accounts and to the app; neither account can be in a pending or changing state, and both parties must have accepted the latest paid and free agreements; [^p6-apple-criteria-accounts] the app must also have at least one version released to the App Store. [^p6-apple-criteria-released] An app that is currently available for pre-order cannot be transferred, [^d3-apple-no-preorder] so a pre-order or a pending membership change is a concrete closing dependency, not a reason to exchange account passwords.

The recipient's acceptance and then the completed transfer are the evidence to record: after the transfer is initiated the app keeps a Pending App Transfer status until the recipient accepts it or the request expires after 60 days, [^d3-apple-pending-60-days] and an accepted transfer can take up to two business days to complete. [^apple-accept-processing-app-transfer] The app leaves the transferor's account on transfer, so Apple advises the transferor to back up all information about the app first. [^d3-apple-backup]

The seller's side of each transfer, including the App Store steps, is in [Selling an App Business](/practice-guides/selling-an-app-business/us#seller-transfer-steps).

## How is the domain name transferred at closing? {#domain-closing}

**Short answer.** A domain transfer at closing can be constrained by ICANN's transfer rules, including the general rule that a domain cannot move to a new registrar within 60 days after a change to the registrant's contact information, although some registrars may offer an opt-out, which ICANN says registrars need not provide. [^registrar-confirmation-domain-closing]

The asset schedule lists each domain by exact name, registered holder, registrar and expiry date, because each registration is a contract between the registrant and a particular registrar [^p6-icann-registrant-contract] and, for a generic top-level domain such as .com, the registrar sends its expiration notices to the registered name holder. [^p6-icann-errp-notices]

The handover plan can say whether the closing needs a change of registrant, a transfer between registrars or both, because ICANN's transfer rules address a transfer from one registrar to another and a transfer from one registrant to another as separate moves. [^d3-icann-two-kinds] Only the registered name holder, the administrative contact or a person expressly authorized by either of them can start a transfer, [^d3-icann-who-initiates] and a transfer between registrars requires an authorization code, also called an AuthInfo or transfer code. [^d3-icann-authinfo] For a move between registrars, the process starts with the registrar that will receive the name, which must confirm the registrant's intent on ICANN's standardized form. [^d3-icann-gaining-registrar] Because registrants manage their domain settings through their registrar, the plan can also record evidence that the buyer controls the registrar account, including renewal and account recovery. [^d3-icann-registrant-settings]

Registrar administration and hosting are separate steps; in a hosting move, traffic moves when the domain's DNS settings are changed to point to the new hosting infrastructure. [^d3-google-dns-move] Completion is recorded against the scheduled domain in the buyer's registrar account rather than inferred from the public website staying online, because the registration is maintained under the registrant's contract with its registrar. [^d3-icann-registrar-maintains]

## In what order should control of the app's accounts move at closing? {#account-control-order}

**Short answer.** The buyer's receiving accounts can be set up before any transfer and the seller's leftover access removed after it, because Google Play needs both accounts active before a transfer and GitHub keeps the old owner on as a collaborator afterward. [^play-accounts-active] [^github-original-owner-collaborator]

Google Play requires both developer accounts to be registered and active before a transfer request is submitted. [^play-accounts-active] Vercel requires a valid payment method on the receiving team before project transfer to avoid service interruption. [^vercel-payment-method]

For a business sale, Stripe directs the existing owner to contact Stripe Support first to confirm the information that needs updating. [^stripe-business-sale-contact] Where the new owner is a new individual rather than an existing user, Stripe's process adds that person as a Super Administrator and transfers ownership after the invitation is accepted. [^stripe-new-owner-invite]

For a domain transaction, Escrow.com's instructions require the seller to provide the username, password or authorization code needed for access before funds are released, and the [domain transfer question](#domain-closing) covers the registrar steps. [^escrow-domain-credentials-before-release]

After a GitHub repository transfer, the original owner becomes a collaborator and existing collaborators remain. [^github-original-owner-collaborator] Removing a collaborator does not remove that person's local clones, and GitHub assigns the repository owner responsibility for ensuring former users delete confidential information or intellectual property. [^github-removed-keeps-clones]

## How and when do the business's online accounts migrate around closing? {#migration-timing}

**Short answer.** The steps and timing of an account migration depend on each provider's transfer process and on which accounts the agreement requires to move before or after closing, as Vercel's rule that a project's transferor be an owner of the sending team and a member of the receiving team shows. [^p6-vercel-transfer-roles]

Provider rules can require the receiving account to be ready before a transfer starts; Vercel requires the receiving team to have a valid payment method before a project is transferred, [^p6-vercel-payment-method] and a GitHub repository transfer to another personal account lapses if the new owner does not accept it within one day. [^p6-github-accept-window]

Not every account has to move before closing; a purchase agreement can let some assets keep transferring after a defined migration milestone, as one filed purchase agreement expressly anticipated. [^migration-migration-timing] Later deliveries can be made enforceable obligations of their own; the same agreement made either party's failure to complete the Migration Process a material breach. [^d3-jeffs-migration-breach] It also tied payment to the handover, releasing 92 percent of the price to the seller after its inspection period expired, with the broker retaining 8 percent as a portion of its commission. [^d3-jeffs-release-after-inspection]

Provider-specific prerequisites are covered under [Apple apps](#app-transfer), [website and app projects](#website-migration) and [shared accounts](/practice-guides/selling-an-app-business/us#shared-accounts).

## How should a website or app project migration be planned so the service keeps running? {#website-migration}

**Short answer.** A website or app migration plan can identify the actual hosting platform and include tests of the resulting service, because a platform's project transfer may leave work undone, as when Vercel requires a project's integrations to be added again after the transfer. [^migration-website-migration]

Before the switch, Google suggests uploading a copy of the site to the new host and testing all aspects of how users interact with it, [^d3-google-copy-and-test] which for an online business can include an ordinary customer journey, a payment or subscription test, required notifications, deployment access and recovery access.

For a hosting move that keeps the same web address, Google suggests lowering DNS time-to-live values at least a week before the move so the change propagates faster [^p6-google-hosting-ttl] and shutting down the old hosting only once no one, including Google's crawler, is still using it. [^p6-google-hosting-shutdown] A closing plan that ends the seller's hosting on the closing date can conflict with that sequence, because Google's guide waits for traffic to the old provider to reach zero before shutdown, so the agreement can keep the old hosting available for a short, defined period after the switch. [^d3-google-traffic-zero]

Repository and domain settings move on their own terms; for a GitHub Pages site published from a private repository with a custom domain, GitHub suggests removing or updating the domain's DNS records before transferring the repository to avoid the risk of a domain takeover. [^p6-github-pages-dns] The [domain transfer](#domain-closing) is scheduled separately from the hosting move.

A project can also move through its provider's own transfer route, which carries the original project rather than a copy: Vercel, for example, transfers a project between teams with zero downtime and moves or copies the project's dependencies to the receiving team, [^vercel-zero-downtime-transfer] a transferred GitHub repository keeps its issues and pull requests and stays associated with its secrets and deploy keys, [^github-transfer-keeps-secrets] and a Google Cloud project migration is not a data transfer, and the project's services and databases stay active without downtime. [^gcp-migration-not-data-transfer] The closing record can show which route each project took, and the result is tested as described above.
