# SBA 7(a) loans to buy a business: the 10% equity injection and seller notes[^about]

For a buyer using an SBA 7(a) loan to buy a business: how much equity the buyer must put in, when a seller note can count toward it, what a paying seller note does to the loan, and what changed when SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 took effect on October 1, 2026.

## How much must a buyer put in on an SBA 7(a) loan to buy a business, and how much can a seller note cover? {#at-a-glance}

**Short answer.** When a buyer new to the business takes it over with an SBA 7(a) loan, a deal SBA calls an Initial Acquisition, the buyer must put in at least 10% of the total project cost [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-initial-acquisition-defined][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-ten-percent][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-total-project-cost]. A seller note can cover up to half of that 10%, but only if it is subordinated to the lender and takes no payments of principal or interest for the life of the 7(a) loan, and that half is shared with other standby debt and minority investors [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-seller-debt][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-half-cap].

How long is the life of the loan? A 7(a) loan for a business purchase must have a stated maturity, no balloon payment, and be paid off over no more than 10 years, so on a business-only deal a standby seller note waits up to 10 years [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-loan-term]. The loan can run longer when the deal also buys real estate. On a blended loan, the real estate share may be paid over up to 25 years [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-real-estate-term]. A deal that buys an owner-occupied special purpose property together with the business operating from it may have a maturity of up to 25 years if 85% or more of the project cost is for real estate [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-special-purpose-property-term].

The 10% is SBA's minimum for an Initial Acquisition [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-ten-percent]. This guide does not cover a particular lender's own credit standards, which a buyer learns from the lender's term sheet.

The rule in numbers, for a business bought on a $1,000,000 total project cost (price plus closing costs and working capital):

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Total project cost | $1,000,000 |
| Minimum equity injection: 10%, which the lender cannot reduce when the buyer is new to the business [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-ten-percent][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-total-project-cost] | $100,000 |
| Most that a full-standby seller note can cover: half of the injection, less anything counted from other standby debt or a minority investor [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-half-cap][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-seller-debt] | $50,000 |
| Least that must come from unlimited sources, such as the buyer's own unborrowed cash [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-half-cap][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-cash] | $50,000 |
| The rest: the SBA 7(a) loan plus any paying seller note, with total acquisition debt capped at the business valuation [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-debt-cap] | $900,000 |

What counts toward the injection:

| Source | Counts toward the 10%? |
| --- | --- |
| Cash that is not borrowed, including a gift [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-cash] | Yes, no cap |
| A personal loan to a guarantor, if repayment can be shown to come from outside the business's cash flow (the owner's salary from the business does not count) [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-personal-loan] | Yes, no cap |
| A grant that never has to be repaid during the loan [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-grants] | Yes, no cap |
| Eligible prepaid expenses, verified by the lender through paid invoices, canceled checks or bank statements [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-prepaid] | Yes, no cap |
| What the buyer paid for the business valuation and quality of earnings report [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-diligence-costs] | Yes (our review found no separate cap in the SOP) |
| A seller note, subordinated and on full standby for the whole loan term [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-seller-debt][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-half-cap] | Yes, within the shared half |
| Other debt on full standby, if its provider takes no equity investment in the business [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-standby-debt][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-half-cap] | Yes, within the shared half |
| A minority investor with less than 20% and no control, with no agreement to repay the investment or make distributions to recover it before the SBA guaranty is released [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-minority][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-half-cap] | Yes, within the shared half |
| A seller note that takes payments during the loan [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-debt-cap] | No: it counts as debt |
| A seller earnout [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-earnouts] | Not allowed in these loans |

Other numbers a buyer will meet, each explained below: the loan can be up to $5,000,000 [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-max-loan]; a paying seller note can be refinanced only after 36 months [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-refinance-wait]; the seller can stay on only as a consultant, for up to 24 months in total [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-consultant-limit]; a purchase price of $3 million or more needs a quality of earnings report [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-qoe]; and anyone owning 20% or more after the sale personally guarantees the whole loan [^sba-sop-8-1-glance-guaranty][^sba-sop-8-1-glance-guaranty-unlimited].

## How much equity must a buyer put in to buy a business with an SBA 7(a) loan? {#minimum-equity-injection}

**Short answer.** A first-time buyer taking control of a business with an SBA 7(a) loan must put in at least 10% of the total project cost, and the lender cannot reduce or waive that minimum [^sba-sop-8-1-initial-acquisition-ten-percent][^sba-sop-8-1-equity-total-project-cost].

SBA calls this deal an Initial Acquisition: a purchase that makes someone the new majority or largest individual owner who was not already an owner, or who has worked for the business for fewer than 24 months [^sba-sop-8-1-initial-acquisition-defined].

The 10% is measured against the total project cost, not the headline price. The SOP defines total project cost as all costs required to become operational, whatever the source of funds, other than lines of credit and 504 loans. Closing costs, working capital and other funded costs therefore raise the base along with the purchase price [^sba-sop-8-1-equity-total-project-cost].

The 10% is SBA's floor for the program. This guide does not cover a particular lender's own credit standards, which a buyer learns from the lender's term sheet.

SBA lenders must follow SBA's Standard Operating Procedures, which the regulations list among the Loan Program Requirements lenders must comply with [^cfr-13-120-10-sops-are-loan-program-requirements]. The rules in this guide come from SOP 50 10 8.1, Appendix 15, which governs 7(a) changes of ownership.

## Can a seller note count toward the buyer's equity injection on an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan? {#seller-note-as-equity}

**Short answer.** A seller note can count toward the equity injection on an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan only if it is subordinated to the lender and on full standby, with no principal or interest paid for the whole term of the 7(a) loan [^sba-sop-8-1-seller-debt-full-standby]. Even then, it can supply no more than half of the required injection [^sba-sop-8-1-limited-sources-half-cap].

Full standby means the seller receives nothing on the note until the SBA loan is repaid. A note that pays for the first few years and then pauses, or pays interest only, does not qualify [^sba-sop-8-1-seller-debt-full-standby]. Interest can keep running: the standby debt may accrue interest and be added to the standby debt and amortized after the 7(a) loan is paid in full. The seller, as standby creditor, subordinates any lien to the lender and agrees to take no action against the borrower or the collateral without the lender's consent [^sba-sop-8-1-standby-interest-accrues].

The half cap is shared. Since SOP 50 10 8.1, a seller note on standby, other standby debt and a non-controlling minority equity investment are all limited equity sources [^sba-sop-8-1-seller-debt-full-standby][^sba-sop-8-1-standby-debt-limited-source][^sba-sop-8-1-minority-investor-defined], and "The following sources, whether individually or in the aggregate, may provide no more than half of the required Equity Injection."[^sba-sop-8-1-limited-sources-half-cap] A minority investor's money and the seller's standby note draw on the same half. Someone who provides other standby debt may not also take an equity investment in the business [^sba-sop-8-1-limited-sources-half-cap]. A non-controlling minority investor is one with less than 20% of the equity and no control of the business [^sba-sop-8-1-minority-investor-defined].

The other half must come from unlimited sources, such as "Cash that is not borrowed, whether on the business’s balance sheet or from other sources (e.g. gift)."[^sba-sop-8-1-unlimited-sources-cash]

## How do you calculate the equity injection and the seller-note limit on an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan? {#equity-injection-worked-example}

**Short answer.** For an Initial Acquisition, the minimum equity injection is 10% of total project cost, and limited sources, such as a full-standby seller note, may together supply no more than half of that minimum [^sba-sop-8-1-worked-example-ten-percent][^sba-sop-8-1-worked-example-total-project-cost][^sba-sop-8-1-worked-example-half-cap][^sba-sop-8-1-worked-example-seller-debt].

An illustrative Initial Acquisition, with round figures. It doubles the $1,000,000 deal at the top of this guide to show that the minimum injection and the shared limited-source cap scale with total project cost, and then works through a seller note larger than the limit:

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Total project cost (price, closing costs, working capital) | $2,000,000 |
| Minimum equity injection (10%) | $200,000 |
| Most that limited sources can supply (half of $200,000), including a full-standby seller note | $100,000 |
| Least that must come from unlimited sources, such as the buyer's own unborrowed cash | $100,000 |

If the seller's standby note is $100,000, a qualifying non-controlling minority investment cannot also count toward the minimum; the other $100,000 has to come from unlimited sources, such as the buyer's unborrowed cash or a gift [^sba-sop-8-1-worked-example-half-cap][^sba-sop-8-1-worked-example-minority-investment][^sba-sop-8-1-worked-example-unlimited-sources]. A standby seller note larger than $100,000 can still be part of the deal, but the excess does not count toward the minimum, so it does not reduce the $100,000 that unlimited sources must supply [^sba-sop-8-1-worked-example-half-cap].

A price above the business valuation changes the math. The valuation must support the purchase price, and "If the amount paid for the business exceeds the business valuation, the difference must be made up by equity."[^sba-sop-8-1-price-above-valuation-equity] Extra limited-source money may help fill that gap only if it, too, is on full standby [^sba-sop-8-1-gap-funding-full-standby].

## What happens on an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan when the seller note is paid during the loan term? {#paying-seller-note}

**Short answer.** A seller note that is paid during the SBA loan term does not count toward the buyer's equity injection [^sba-sop-8-1-paying-note-not-equity]. It counts as debt instead, and total acquisition debt, including that note, is capped at the business valuation and must be covered by the business's cash flow [^sba-sop-8-1-debt-capped-at-valuation].

Only seller debt on full standby for the term of the 7(a) loan counts as equity [^sba-sop-8-1-paying-note-not-equity]. A paying note is debt, so its payments are part of the debt service coverage the lender has to see [^sba-sop-8-1-debt-capped-at-valuation].

A paying seller note can later be refinanced into SBA financing, but not soon: "To be eligible for refinancing, any seller-financed Note must have been in place and current (not on standby) for at least 36 months following the change of ownership."[^sba-sop-8-1-seller-note-refinance-36-months] Under the June 2025 version of the SOP the wait was 24 months [^sba-sop-8-seller-note-refinance-24-months].

A seller note is not the only way sellers take part of the price later. An earnout is not available: "Seller earnouts are prohibited; however, buyer rebates based on business performance are allowed because this is a benefit to the Borrower."[^sba-sop-8-1-earnouts-prohibited]

## What changed for seller notes on SBA 7(a) acquisition loans in October 2026? {#october-2026-changes}

**Short answer.** SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026, kept the full-standby rule and the half-of-the-injection cap for seller notes, but made the cap shared with other standby debt and minority equity investments [^sba-sop-8-seller-debt-standby-half][^sba-sop-8-1-changes-seller-debt-full-standby][^sba-sop-8-1-shared-half-cap].

Under SOP 50 10 8, effective June 1, 2025, a complete change of ownership already needed a 10% injection, and seller debt could count toward it only if it was on full standby for the life of the SBA loan and did not exceed half of the required injection [^sba-sop-8-seller-debt-standby-half]. Its list of equity sources, which included other standby debt and an equity investment, set no cap on them; the half limit applied only to seller debt [^sba-sop-8-equity-investment-uncapped][^sba-sop-8-seller-debt-standby-half]. SOP 50 10 8.1 moved non-controlling minority equity, other standby debt and seller debt into one capped bucket [^sba-sop-8-1-shared-half-cap][^sba-sop-8-1-changes-standby-debt][^sba-sop-8-1-changes-seller-debt-full-standby][^sba-sop-8-1-changes-minority-investment].

What changed and what did not, side by side:

| Rule | SOP 50 10 8 (June 1, 2025) | SOP 50 10 8.1 (October 1, 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When a seller note counts toward the 10% | Only on full standby for the life of the SBA loan [^sba-sop-8-seller-debt-standby-half] | Only subordinated and on full standby for the term of the 7(a) loan [^sba-sop-8-1-changes-seller-debt-full-standby] |
| How much a standby seller note can count | Up to half of the injection; the cap applied to seller debt alone [^sba-sop-8-seller-debt-standby-half][^sba-sop-8-equity-investment-uncapped] | Up to half of the injection, shared with other standby debt and minority equity [^sba-sop-8-1-shared-half-cap] |
| Wait before a paying seller note can be refinanced | 24 months [^sba-sop-8-refinance-wait-24-months] | 36 months [^sba-sop-8-1-refinance-wait-36-months] |
| Seller consulting after the sale | Up to 12 months, including extensions [^sba-sop-8-seller-consultant-twelve-months] | Up to 24 months in total [^sba-sop-8-1-consultant-24-months] |
| Quality of earnings report | Our review found no such requirement | Required for Initial Acquisitions and business expansions at a $3 million purchase price or more [^sba-sop-8-1-qoe-three-million] |
| Seller earnouts | Prohibited [^sba-sop-8-changes-earnouts-prohibited] | Prohibited [^sba-sop-8-1-changes-earnouts-prohibited] |
| Which applications it governs | Submitted through September 30, 2026 [^sba-notice-5000-882227-transition-by-application-date] | Received by SBA on or after October 1, 2026 [^sba-notice-5000-882227-transition-by-application-date] |

Which version governs turns on the application date. SBA's notice issuing the updated SOP says "This updated version of the SOP is effective October 1, 2026, and will apply to all applications received … by SBA on or after that date."[^sba-notice-5000-882227-transition-by-application-date] Lenders keep using SOP 50 10 8 for applications submitted through September 30, 2026 [^sba-notice-5000-882227-transition-by-application-date].

## How large can an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan be, and who must personally guarantee it? {#loan-size-and-personal-guaranty}

**Short answer.** A standard SBA 7(a) loan can be up to $5,000,000, and every individual who will own 20% or more of the borrower after the sale must give an unlimited personal guaranty [^sba-sop-8-1-standard-7a-maximum][^sba-sop-8-1-twenty-percent-guaranty][^sba-sop-8-1-guaranty-post-sale-ownership].

The $5,000,000 limit applies to the borrower together with its affiliates, so a buyer who already owns other businesses may have less room [^sba-sop-8-1-maximum-includes-affiliates]. It is a ceiling, not an amount the lender must offer.

The guaranty is unlimited: it covers the whole loan, not the guarantor's ownership percentage. On a change of ownership, the 20% test is applied to ownership after the sale, so a buyer who will own 20% or more signs; a selling owner who keeps less than 20% is subject to a separate guaranty rule [^sba-sop-8-1-guaranty-post-sale-ownership]. That rule does not reach an Initial Acquisition, where the seller may not keep a stake [^sba-sop-8-1-guaranty-seller-may-not-remain]. A spouse who owns less than 20% must also guarantee in full when the spouses and minor children together own 20% or more [^sba-sop-8-1-spouse-guaranty].

## Can the seller keep a stake or stay on after a buyer uses an SBA 7(a) loan to buy the business? {#seller-staying-on}

**Short answer.** In an Initial Acquisition the seller cannot remain an officer, director, owner or employee of the business, but the business may hire the seller as a consultant for up to 24 months in total [^sba-sop-8-1-seller-may-not-remain].

A buyer who wants the seller to keep a minority stake, often called rollover, is outside the Initial Acquisition structure. The SOP lets a seller stay on as an owner when an owner sells less than the whole stake (an owner buyout, partial change) [^sba-sop-8-1-seller-stays-partial-change], or when an ESOP or cooperative buys control [^sba-sop-8-1-seller-stays-esop-cooperative]. An outside buyer cannot use the owner buyout route to take control: someone who has not worked in the business for at least 24 months may buy only less than 50% and may not become the largest shareholder [^sba-sop-8-1-owner-buyout-outsider-limit].

The consulting period is a ceiling that includes any extensions. A transition plan that keeps the seller for longer, or as an employee, does not fit an Initial Acquisition [^sba-sop-8-1-seller-may-not-remain].
