# Employee Expense Reimbursement in New York[^about]

New York has no general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses, but agreed reimbursements, wage-deduction limits, and minimum-wage rules still shift some costs to the employer.

## Must an employer in New York reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** New York has no general statute requiring an employer to reimburse every business expense. The Labor Law's reimbursement provision applies to an employer that is party to an agreement to provide benefits or wage supplements, which include reimbursement for expenses. [^ny-198c-agreement][^ny-198c-definition] The New York State Department of Labor states that an employer that never promised to pay wage supplements is not required by law to pay them. [^ny-dol-never-promised] Separate rules still put some costs on the employer: an employer may not charge work costs against wages or require a separate payment unless the Labor Law permits it as a deduction or a current collective bargaining agreement permits or requires it, and under the miscellaneous-industries minimum wage order, expenses an employee incurs in carrying out assigned duties may not reduce the minimum wage. [^ny-193-charges][^ny-142-2-10-expenses]

"As used in this section, the term ‘benefits or wage supplements’ includes, but is not limited to, reimbursement for expenses; health, welfare and retirement benefits; and vacation, separation or holiday pay."[^ny-198c-definition]

## Which business expenses must a New York employer reimburse? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** A New York employer must reimburse the expenses it has agreed to reimburse, and Labor Law § 198-c does not protect an employee in a bona fide executive, administrative, or professional capacity who earns more than $1,300 a week. [^ny-198c-covered-duty][^ny-198c-covered-agreement][^ny-198c-executive] Beyond agreed reimbursements, the miscellaneous-industries minimum wage order bars any reduction of the minimum wage by expenses incurred in carrying out assigned duties. [^ny-142-2-10-covered] Required uniforms are a named cost: the miscellaneous-industries order requires reimbursement of a required uniform's cost by the next payment of wages, and the hospitality order requires reimbursement of the total cost no later than the next payday. [^ny-142-2-5-uniforms][^ny-146-1-8-uniforms] The minimum-wage rule protects only the wage floor, so an employee paid well above the minimum wage may bear an assigned expense that does not pull pay below that floor. [^ny-142-2-10-covered]

"(a) When an employee purchases a required uniform, he or she shall be reimbursed by the employer for the total cost of the uniform no later than the next payday. Employers may not avoid such costs by requiring employees to obtain uniforms before starting the job."[^ny-146-1-8-uniforms]

## Must a New York employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No New York statute requires an employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone, or other remote-work costs as such. Labor Law § 198-c reaches those costs only when the employer has agreed to reimburse them, because it applies to agreed benefits or wage supplements, which include reimbursement for expenses. [^ny-198c-remote-text][^ny-198c-remote-definition] For an employee covered by the miscellaneous-industries minimum wage order, remote-work expenses incurred in carrying out assigned duties still may not reduce the minimum wage. [^ny-142-2-10-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in New York make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A New York offer letter or expense policy may define which business expenses the employer agrees to reimburse, subject to mandatory uniform-reimbursement, wage-deduction and minimum-wage protections. [^ny-198c-waiver-agreement][^ny-146-1-8-waiver][^ny-193-waiver][^ny-142-2-10-waiver] Labor Law § 198-c enforces agreed expense reimbursements, except for a bona fide executive, administrative, or professional employee earning more than $1,300 a week. [^ny-198c-waiver-agreement][^ny-198c-waiver-definition][^ny-198c-waiver-exclusion] Those protections work as follows: a hospitality employer must reimburse a required uniform an employee buys, the employer may not recover costs by charging them against wages or requiring a separate payment unless the Labor Law permits the charge as a deduction or a current collective bargaining agreement permits or requires it, and assigned expenses may not reduce the minimum wage of an employee covered by the miscellaneous-industries order. [^ny-146-1-8-waiver][^ny-193-waiver][^ny-142-2-10-waiver] The Department of Labor's deduction regulation lists employee purchases of tools, equipment and attire required for work, and recoupment of unauthorized expenses, among the deductions that are not permitted. [^ny-195-4-5-tools] An agreement to work for less than the minimum wage is no defense to a minimum-wage action. [^ny-663-no-defense]

## What happens in New York if an employer does not pay an agreed expense reimbursement? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** Unless the employee is a bona fide executive, administrative, or professional employee earning more than $1,300 a week, a New York employer that fails to pay an agreed expense reimbursement within thirty days after payment is required commits a misdemeanor. [^ny-198c-deadline][^ny-198c-remedy-definition][^ny-198c-remedy-exclusion] When the employer is a corporation, its president, secretary, treasurer or officers with corresponding functions are each guilty of a misdemeanor. [^ny-198c-officers] An employee may recover benefits and wage supplements accrued during the six years before the action, whether the employee or the Commissioner of Labor brings it. [^ny-198-six-years]

## Must a New York City business pay for a delivery cyclist's safety equipment? {#new-york-city-equipment}

**Short answer.** A New York City business that uses bicycles for commercial purposes must provide required safety equipment at no cost to the cyclist: a compliant helmet and working brakes, bells, lights and reflectors when the cyclist lacks them, and a retro-reflective safety vest. [^nyc-dot-equipment-business][^nyc-dot-vest] The city's app-based delivery worker laws do not cover delivery workers classified as employees. [^nyc-dcwp-employees-excluded]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in New York? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse, and New York's minimum wage order applies the same kind of floor to assigned expenses under state law. [^fed-531-35-tools][^ny-142-2-10-federal]
