# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Massachusetts[^about]

Massachusetts has no general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses, but medical-examination, uniform and work-travel rules, the Wage Act's bar on shifting costs into wages, and the minimum wage still put some costs on the employer.

## Must an employer in Massachusetts reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Massachusetts has no general statute requiring an employer to reimburse every business expense. Specific rules reach named costs: an employer that requires a present or prospective employee to undergo a medical examination by a physician it designates, as a condition of employment, must reimburse the medical expenses, and the state's minimum wage regulations require reimbursement of required uniforms and of transportation expenses for work-day travel. [^ma-c149-s159b-medical-exam-duty][^ma-cmr-27-05-uniform-purchase-duty][^ma-cmr-27-04-workday-travel-transport-duty] The Massachusetts Appeals Court has stated that breach of a standard expense reimbursement arrangement ordinarily does not violate the Wage Act, because a reimbursement is not compensation earned by labor, service or performance. [^ma-fraelick-reimbursement-not-earned-duty] The same court stated that an employer may not reduce wages by, or condition full and timely payment of wages on, payments by the employee to or on behalf of the employer. [^ma-fraelick-no-cost-shifting-duty]

## Which business expenses must a Massachusetts employer pay for? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** A Massachusetts employer must reimburse the medical expenses of an examination by a physician it designates when it requests or requires the examination as a condition of securing or continuing employment. [^ma-c149-s159b-medical-exam-covered] Under the minimum wage regulations, an employee or prospective employee required to buy or rent a uniform must be reimbursed the actual purchase or rental cost, and an employee must be reimbursed the actual cost of dry-cleaning, commercial laundering or other special treatment a required uniform needs; wash-and-wear uniforms washed with other personal garments are excluded from the maintenance rule. [^ma-cmr-27-05-uniform-purchase-covered][^ma-cmr-27-05-uniform-cleaning-covered] The regulations also require reimbursement of all transportation expenses when an employee is required or directed to travel from one place to another during the work day, and of associated transportation expenses when an employee who regularly works at a fixed location is required to report somewhere else. [^ma-cmr-27-04-workday-travel-transport-covered][^ma-cmr-27-04-reassigned-site-transport-covered] Ordinary travel between home and work is not compensable working time. [^ma-cmr-27-04-commute-not-work-covered] For other costs, the minimum wage regulations bar deductions from the basic minimum wage other than those required or expressly allowed by law and the listed lodging and meal deductions, and bar an employer from separately billing an employee for amounts it could not deduct. [^ma-cmr-27-05-no-deductions-covered][^ma-cmr-27-05-indirect-deductions-covered]

"(d) An employee required or directed to travel from one place to another after the beginning of or before the close of the work day shall be compensated for all travel time and shall be reimbursed for all transportation expenses."[^ma-cmr-27-04-workday-travel-transport-covered]

## Must a Massachusetts employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Massachusetts statute or minimum wage regulation we reviewed requires an employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs as such. The named reimbursement rules cover medical examinations, uniforms and work travel. [^ma-c149-s159b-medical-exam-remote][^ma-cmr-27-05-uniform-purchase-remote][^ma-cmr-27-04-workday-travel-transport-remote] The Appeals Court has stated that breach of a standard expense reimbursement arrangement ordinarily does not violate the Wage Act, so an employer's promise to cover those costs is ordinarily not enforceable as wages. [^ma-fraelick-reimbursement-not-earned-remote] Remote-work costs still matter at the wage floor: a charge or deduction for them may not take pay below the basic minimum wage, and under federal law an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which their cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^ma-cmr-27-05-no-deductions-remote][^ma-cmr-27-05-indirect-deductions-remote][^ma-fed-tools-of-trade-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Massachusetts make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Massachusetts offer letter or expense policy cannot condition full and timely payment of earned wages on an employee's payments for the employer's benefit. [^ma-fraelick-no-cost-shifting-waiver] The Wage Act provides that no person may exempt itself from its payment and enforcement sections by a special contract with an employee or by any other means, and the only defenses it allows to a failure to pay wages include a valid set-off. [^ma-c149-s148-no-special-contract-waiver][^ma-c149-s150-defenses-waiver] The Supreme Judicial Court has held that the Act prohibits wage deductions based on an employer's unilateral determination of an employee's fault and damages, and the Appeals Court has stated that an employer may not reduce wages by, or condition their full and timely payment on, payments by the employee to or on behalf of the employer. [^ma-camara-unilateral-deductions-waiver][^ma-fraelick-no-cost-shifting-waiver] An agreement to work for less than the minimum wage is no defense to a minimum-wage claim, so an agreement cannot make an employee bear costs that take pay below the minimum wage. [^ma-c151-s20-no-agreement-defense-waiver][^ma-cmr-27-05-no-deductions-waiver] The uniform, work-travel and medical-examination reimbursement rules contain no exception for an employee's agreement. [^ma-cmr-27-05-uniform-purchase-waiver][^ma-cmr-27-04-workday-travel-transport-waiver][^ma-c149-s159b-medical-exam-waiver]

## What happens in Massachusetts if an employer leaves business expenses unreimbursed? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** A Massachusetts employer may not reduce wages by, or condition their full and timely payment on, payments by the employee to or on behalf of the employer, and the Wage Act lets an employee sue over a violation. [^ma-fraelick-no-cost-shifting-remedy][^ma-c149-s150-private-action-remedy] The employee may sue 90 days after filing a complaint with the Attorney General, or sooner with the Attorney General's written assent, and within three years after the violation. [^ma-c149-s150-private-action-remedy] The three-year period is tolled from the filing of a complaint with the Attorney General until the Attorney General authorizes a private action or an Attorney General enforcement action becomes final. [^ma-c149-s150-private-action-remedy] A prevailing employee must be awarded treble damages for lost wages and other benefits, with litigation costs and reasonable attorneys' fees. [^ma-c149-s150-treble-remedy] Where costs take pay below the minimum wage, a prevailing employee must likewise be awarded treble damages for the loss of minimum wage, with costs and reasonable attorneys' fees. [^ma-c151-s20-treble-remedy] Because a standard reimbursement arrangement ordinarily is not wages, a claim on it ordinarily falls outside the Wage Act's remedies; most contract actions must be commenced within six years after the cause of action accrues. [^ma-fraelick-reimbursement-not-earned-remedy][^ma-c260-s2-contract-six-years-remedy] An employer may not penalize an employee for seeking rights under the wage and hour provisions of chapter 149, and in *Fraelick v. PerkettPR* the Appeals Court held that an employee who alleged that her employer abandoned its reimbursement arrangement and required her, under penalty of discharge, to advance expenses indefinitely for its benefit sufficiently alleged a reasonable belief to withstand dismissal of her retaliation claim. [^ma-c149-s148a-retaliation-remedy][^ma-fraelick-retaliation-claim-remedy]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Massachusetts? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^ma-fed-tools-of-trade-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Massachusetts goes further: its minimum wage regulations require reimbursement of required uniforms and work-day travel without asking whether the cost cuts into the minimum wage, and bar deductions from the basic minimum wage other than those required or expressly allowed by law and the listed lodging and meal deductions. [^ma-cmr-27-05-uniform-purchase-federal][^ma-cmr-27-04-workday-travel-transport-federal][^ma-cmr-27-05-no-deductions-federal]
