# Us

## Documents

* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Alabama](alabama.md) - Alabama has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses and no state minimum wage, so the federal minimum wage caps how much of the cost of employer-required tools of the trade an employee can be made to bear, while a few Alabama statutes assign named costs such as required drug tests in the state's drug-free workplace program to the employer.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Alaska](alaska.md) - Alaska has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but a state regulation bars requiring employees to buy certain uniforms and equipment, and deduction rules protect the minimum wage.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Arizona](arizona.md) - Arizona has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but the wage-withholding statute limits deductions from wages and the state minimum wage counts only cash or its equivalent toward the floor.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in California](california.md) - California Labor Code sections 2802 and 2804 govern necessary business expenses, reimbursement agreements, and recovery of unpaid costs.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Colorado](colorado.md) - Colorado has no general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses, but required uniforms, wage-deduction limits, and state and local minimum-wage rules still shift some costs to the employer.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Delaware](delaware.md) - Delaware has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but agreed reimbursements must be paid within 30 days, wage deductions need a statutory ground, and work costs cannot cut into the minimum wage.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Illinois](illinois.md) - Illinois law requires reimbursement of authorized necessary work expenses and sets rules for written policies, documentation, and submission time.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Iowa](iowa.md) - Iowa Code § 91A.3 requires payment of authorized employee expenses and sets the timing for reimbursement and written refusals.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Kentucky](kentucky.md) - Kentucky has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but it bars making employees pay for employer-required medical examinations and bars wage deductions for fines, shared-till cash shortages, breakage and listed losses.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Massachusetts](massachusetts.md) - Massachusetts has no general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses, but medical-examination, uniform and work-travel rules, the Wage Act's bar on shifting costs into wages, and the minimum wage still put some costs on the employer.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Montana](montana.md) - Mont. Code Ann. § 39-2-701 covers necessary job expenses, ordinary business risks, employer-caused losses, remote-work costs, and the limits of the statutory text on waiver.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in New Hampshire](new-hampshire.md) - RSA 275:57 requires reimbursement of qualifying employer-requested expenses and sets a 30-day deadline and remedies.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in New York](new-york.md) - New York has no general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses, but agreed reimbursements, wage-deduction limits, and minimum-wage rules still shift some costs to the employer.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in North Carolina](north-carolina.md) - North Carolina has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but reimbursements an employer promises are wages that must be paid on the regular payday, wage deductions need written authorization and notice, and deductions for the employer's benefit may not cut into the minimum wage.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in North Dakota](north-dakota.md) - North Dakota Century Code chapter 34-02 covers necessary job expenses, shared-use equipment, and limits on employer liability.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Ohio](ohio.md) - Ohio has no general statute requiring employers to reimburse business expenses, but employer-required medical examinations cannot be charged to applicants, agreed reimbursements count as wages for Ohio's pay-timing law, and work costs cannot cut into the minimum wage.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Pennsylvania](pennsylvania.md) - Pennsylvania has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but the Wage Payment and Collection Law enforces reimbursements an employer has agreed to pay, and a state regulation bars deductions for employer-required expenses that take pay below the minimum wage.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in South Dakota](south-dakota.md) - South Dakota's employee indemnity statutes cover necessary job expenses, exclude certain losses, and leave remote-work costs and waiver unaddressed expressly.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Texas](texas.md) - Texas has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but the Payday Law limits deductions from wages and the federal minimum wage still caps how much of a work cost an employee can be made to bear.
* [Employee Expense Reimbursement in Washington](washington.md) - Washington has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but employers must pay for required uniforms and for protective equipment the safety rules require them to provide at no cost, wage deductions and rebates for work costs are tightly limited, and Seattle requires reimbursement of necessary expenses for employees working in the city.
