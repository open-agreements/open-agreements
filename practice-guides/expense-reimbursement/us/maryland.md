# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Maryland[^about]

Maryland has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but it limits wage deductions to the grounds Labor and Employment § 3-503 permits, bars charging tipped employees for customers who leave without paying, and lets a court award up to three times wages withheld without a bona fide dispute.

## Must an employer in Maryland reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Maryland has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. Maryland's main protection works through deductions rather than reimbursement: an employer may not deduct anything from an employee's wage unless a court orders the deduction, the employee expressly authorizes it in writing, the Commissioner of Labor and Industry allows it because the employee received full consideration, or another law or governmental regulation permits it. [^md-le-3-503-deductions-duty] One statute names a specific cost: an employer may not require a tipped employee to pay for food or drinks when a customer leaves without paying. [^md-le-3-713-walkout-duty] Under the Maryland wage and hour regulations, the cost of facilities furnished primarily for the employer's benefit or convenience cannot be counted as part of an employee's wage. [^md-comar-09-12-41-18-employer-benefit-duty] In our review, we found no Maryland statute requiring an employer to pay for a medical examination or a uniform it requires. Maryland defines wage, for the Wage Payment and Collection Law, as all compensation due to an employee for employment, including a fringe benefit and any other remuneration promised for service; in our review we found no Maryland appellate decision deciding whether an agreed expense reimbursement falls within that definition. [^md-le-3-501-wage-definition-duty] In *Roley v. National Professional Exchange, Inc.*, the federal district court for Maryland, applying Maryland law, held that travel reimbursements an employer promised, where fairly construed as remuneration for services, are wages recoverable under the Wage Payment and Collection Law. [^md-roley-npx-reimbursement-wage-duty] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-maryland-duty]

## Which business costs may a Maryland employer not shift onto employees? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Maryland has no general statute listing business expenses an employer must reimburse. An employer may not deduct a business cost from wages unless a court orders it, the employee expressly authorizes it in writing, the Commissioner allows it because the employee received full consideration, or another law or governmental regulation permits it. [^md-le-3-503-deductions-covered] An employer may not require a tipped employee, meaning one who customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips, to reimburse or pay it for a customer's food or beverage charge when the customer leaves the employer's place of business without paying. [^md-le-3-713-tipped-definition-covered][^md-le-3-713-walkout-covered] Subject to § 3-503, an employer also may not deduct that unpaid charge from a tipped employee's wage. [^md-le-3-713-walkout-deduction-covered] An employer may count the reasonable cost of board, lodging or other facilities as wages only if the facilities are customarily available to similarly situated employees, the employee's acceptance is voluntary and uncoerced, and the employee receives the benefit charged for, and the amount counted may not exceed the employer's actual cost or include a profit. [^md-comar-09-12-41-18-conditions-covered][^md-comar-09-12-41-18-actual-cost-covered] The cost of facilities furnished primarily for the employer's benefit or convenience cannot be counted as part of the wage. [^md-comar-09-12-41-18-employer-benefit-covered] Maryland's statewide minimum wage is $15.00 an hour. [^md-le-3-413-minimum-covered] Each pay statement must show the amount and name of every deduction. [^md-le-3-504-deduction-statement-covered] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-maryland-covered]

## Must a Maryland employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Maryland statute specifically requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs. In our review, we found no Maryland regulation specifically requiring reimbursement of those costs. An employer may not recover a remote-work cost by deducting it from wages unless a court orders it, the employee expressly authorizes it in writing, the Commissioner allows it or another law permits it. [^md-le-3-503-deductions-remote] Facilities furnished primarily for the employer's benefit or convenience cannot be counted as part of an employee's wage under the Maryland wage and hour regulations. [^md-comar-09-12-41-18-employer-benefit-remote] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-maryland-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Maryland make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Maryland offer letter or expense policy can support a deduction for a business cost only if the employee's authorization is express and in writing, unless a court order, the Commissioner or another law permits the deduction. [^md-le-3-503-deductions-waiver] No agreement lets an employer make a tipped employee pay for a customer who leaves without paying. [^md-le-3-713-walkout-waiver] An agreement to work for less than the wage required under the Wage Payment and Collection Law is void. [^md-le-3-502-agreement-void-waiver] Under the Wage and Hour Law, an employee's agreement to work for less than the wage the employee is entitled to is not a defense to the employee's action for the shortfall. [^md-le-3-427-no-defense-waiver] Acceptance of board, lodging or other facilities counted as wages must be voluntary and uncoerced. [^md-comar-09-12-41-18-conditions-waiver]

## What remedies does a Maryland employee have for an unlawful deduction or unpaid wages? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** An employee may sue to recover unpaid wages once two weeks have passed since the employer was required to pay them. [^md-le-3-507-2-action-remedies] In *Marshall v. Safeway, Inc.*, the Supreme Court of Maryland, then called the Court of Appeals, held that an unauthorized deduction under § 3-503 is also a failure to pay wages under § 3-502 or § 3-505, so an employee may bring that action for an unlawful deduction. [^md-marshall-safeway-deduction-action-remedies] In *Roley v. National Professional Exchange, Inc.*, the federal district court for Maryland held that promised travel reimbursements fairly construed as remuneration for services are wages recoverable in a Wage Payment and Collection Law claim. [^md-roley-npx-reimbursement-wage-remedies] If the court finds the employer withheld the wage in violation of the Wage Payment and Collection Law and not as a result of a bona fide dispute, it may award up to three times the wage plus reasonable counsel fees and other costs. [^md-le-3-507-2-treble-remedies] For a wage claim of $5,000 or less, the Commissioner must send the complaint to the employer and require a written response within 15 days, and may order payment of the wages with interest at 5 percent a year. [^md-le-3-507-1-order-remedies][^md-le-3-507-1-interest-remedies] An employee paid less than the minimum wage may recover the shortfall, an equal additional amount as liquidated damages, and counsel fees and costs. [^md-le-3-427-liquidated-remedies] A civil action at law must be filed within three years after it accrues unless another provision of the Code sets a different period. [^md-cjp-5-101-limitations-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Maryland? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-maryland-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Maryland's statewide minimum wage is $15.00 an hour. [^md-le-3-413-minimum-federal] Maryland's wage and hour regulations likewise exclude facilities furnished primarily for the employer's benefit from the wages counted toward that minimum. [^md-comar-09-12-41-18-employer-benefit-federal]
