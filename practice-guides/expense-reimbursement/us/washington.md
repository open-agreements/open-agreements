# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Washington[^about]

Washington has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but employers must pay for required uniforms and for protective equipment the safety rules require them to provide at no cost, wage deductions and rebates for work costs are tightly limited, and Seattle requires reimbursement of necessary expenses for employees working in the city.

## Must an employer in Washington reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Washington has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. Under RCW 49.12.450, an employer that requires an employee to wear a uniform must furnish it or pay for it, and that section alone decides the employer's duty for apparel required during work hours. [^wa-uniform-duty-only-source] Under WAC 296-800-16020, an employer must provide personal protective equipment at no cost to employees when it would not normally be worn away from the workplace or is required to comply with a safety and health standard. [^wa-ppe-no-cost-duty] An employer may withhold or divert wages only for a deduction required by state or federal law, specifically agreed orally or in writing by the employee and employer, or for medical, surgical or hospital care under a rule, and it must record the deduction openly in its books. [^wa-deduction-limits-duty] Washington's minimum wage is $17.13 an hour from January 1, 2026, and an agreement to accept less than the Minimum Wage Act requires is no defense to a claim for the shortfall. [^wa-minimum-wage-2026-duty][^wa-minimum-wage-no-defense-duty] Seattle goes further for employees who work in the city: its Office of Labor Standards explains that the Wage Theft Ordinance requires employers to pay all compensation owed, which includes reimbursement for employer expenses that are reasonably necessary to do the job or foreseeably caused by the employer's instructions, and that the ordinance covers employees working within Seattle city limits whatever the employer's location or size. [^wa-seattle-reimbursement-duty][^wa-seattle-expense-scope-duty][^wa-seattle-coverage-duty] The ordinance text itself, as amended by Ordinance 125203, defines that reimbursement duty in Seattle Municipal Code 14.20.010: the employer must indemnify the employee for all necessary expenditures or losses the employee incurs in direct consequence of discharging the employee's duties or obeying the employer's directions, even unlawful directions, unless the employee believed the directions were unlawful when obeying them. [^wa-seattle-ordinance-indemnify-necessary-expenses]

## Which business expenses must a Washington employer pay for? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Required uniforms and the protective equipment Washington's safety rules require the employer to provide at no cost are the costs Washington law puts on the employer by name. [^wa-uniform-duty-covered][^wa-ppe-no-cost-covered] The safety rule's table lists, as non-exhaustive examples, both equipment the employer must provide at no cost and items for which employer payment is not required. [^wa-ppe-payment-table-covered] A uniform is apparel of a distinctive style and quality that identifies the wearer as a specific employer's employee when worn outside the workplace, apparel specially marked with the employer's logo, unique apparel representing a historical period or ethnic tradition, or formal apparel. [^wa-uniform-definition-covered] The employer need not furnish or pay for required apparel of a common color that conforms to a general dress code or style, which means white, tan or blue tops and tan, black, blue or gray bottoms, and it may require two sets for seasonal changes. [^wa-uniform-common-color-covered] An employer that changes the required colors within a two-year period must furnish or pay for the new apparel of the employees the change affects. [^wa-uniform-color-change-covered] Personal protective equipment required under the Washington Industrial Safety and Health Act is not wearing apparel for the uniform rule, and the employer's duty to provide it at no cost comes from the safety rules instead. [^wa-uniform-ppe-excluded-covered][^wa-ppe-no-cost-standard-covered] The Department of Labor and Industries takes the position that an employer may not require deposits or make wage deductions for uniforms, and that normal wear and tear and loss due to workplace conditions are the employer's cost of doing business; its administrative policies state the department's opinions and do not replace the statutes and rules. [^wa-lni-uniform-no-deduction-covered][^wa-lni-policy-status-covered] Outside those named costs, an employer that recovers tools, equipment, mileage, phone or travel costs through payroll may withhold wages only on the statutory grounds: a deduction required by law, one specifically agreed by the employee and employer, or one for medical care under a rule. [^wa-deduction-limits-covered] Under federal law, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which their cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^wa-federal-tools-covered] In Seattle, the Office of Labor Standards explains that an employer must reimburse items or services that are explicitly or implicitly necessary, or required by the employer, to carry out the employee's work, so the employee is made whole for the cost actually incurred, but generally need not reimburse costs an employee chooses to incur for convenience that the job does not require. [^wa-seattle-expense-scope-covered][^wa-seattle-voluntary-costs-covered]

## Must a Washington employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Washington state statute requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs as such. In Seattle, the Office of Labor Standards explains that the Wage Theft Ordinance's required compensation includes reimbursement for employer expenses that are reasonably necessary to do the job, and that the ordinance covers employees who work within Seattle city limits. [^wa-seattle-reimbursement-remote][^wa-seattle-expense-scope-remote][^wa-seattle-coverage-remote] The office gives as an example that an employer requiring teleworking employees to use personal cell phones and home internet must reimburse the reasonably required work portion of those plans and any upgrade the work use requires, and it explains that reimbursement for optional telework depends on whether the telework and its costs were reasonably necessary to do the job. [^wa-seattle-telework-example-remote][^wa-seattle-optional-telework-remote] The office's questions and answers are guidance rather than law, and it explains that employees based outside Seattle are covered once they work two hours in the city within a two-week period. [^wa-seattle-guidance-status-remote][^wa-seattle-occasional-coverage-remote] Under federal law, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which their cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^wa-federal-tools-remote] An employer that wants to recover the cost of a laptop, phone or other equipment it supplies through payroll needs a deduction that law requires or that the employee specifically agreed to, and during ongoing employment the department's rule allows a deduction the employee authorized in writing and in advance only for a lawful purpose for the employee's benefit, with no financial profit or benefit to the employer. [^wa-deduction-limits-equipment-remote][^wa-written-authorization-remote][^wa-no-employer-profit-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Washington make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** An offer letter or expense policy in Washington cannot shift to the employee the cost of a required uniform, protective equipment the safety rules require the employer to provide at no cost, or wages below the minimum wage. [^wa-uniform-duty-waiver][^wa-ppe-no-cost-waiver][^wa-minimum-wage-no-defense-waiver] An agreement to accept less than the Minimum Wage Act requires is no defense to a claim for the shortfall. [^wa-minimum-wage-shortfall-waiver] RCW 49.48.010 allows a deduction specifically agreed orally or in writing by the employee and employer, while the rebate statute's safe harbor covers withholding that state or federal law requires or empowers, a deduction the employee expressly authorized in writing in advance for a lawful purpose accruing to the employee's benefit, and medical-care deductions under a rule, on condition that the employer derives no financial benefit and records the deduction openly; the rebate statute also makes collecting a rebate of wages already paid a misdemeanor. [^wa-agreed-deduction-waiver][^wa-rebate-safe-harbor-waiver][^wa-rebate-offense-waiver] During ongoing employment, the department's rule likewise requires a written, advance authorization for the employee's benefit and bars the employer from deriving any financial profit or benefit from the deduction. [^wa-written-authorization-waiver][^wa-no-employer-profit-waiver] The Department of Labor and Industries takes the position that an employer may not withhold the difference between the minimum wage and an employee's agreed wage to cover a uniform, because the employer bears the full cost of the uniform whatever the employee earns. [^wa-lni-uniform-full-cost-waiver] At the final paycheck, the department's rule lets an employer deduct the cost of employer-provided uniforms the employee does not return only under an oral or written agreement and never below the state minimum wage. [^wa-final-uniform-deduction-waiver]

## What can a Washington employee recover for an unlawful deduction, an unpaid uniform or pay pushed below the minimum wage? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** A Washington employee can recover twice the wages an employer unlawfully rebated or withheld, with costs and attorney's fees, when the employer collected a rebate of wages or wilfully paid less than it owed with intent to deprive the employee of wages, unless the employee knowingly submitted to the violation. [^wa-double-damages-remedies][^wa-rebate-offense-remedies] An employee paid below the minimum wage may recover the shortfall with costs and reasonable attorney's fees, whatever the employee agreed to. [^wa-minimum-wage-recovery-remedies] A court must award reasonable attorney's fees to a person who recovers judgment for wages owed, unless the recovery does not exceed what the employer admitted owing. [^wa-wage-fees-remedies] An employee may instead file a wage complaint with the Department of Labor and Industries, which covers violations of the wage deduction and rebate statutes, may order payment of wages owed with interest of one percent a month and may not investigate violations that occurred more than three years before the complaint. [^wa-wage-payment-requirement-remedies][^wa-lni-interest-remedies][^wa-lni-lookback-remedies] We found no Washington statute or rule that classifies an agreed expense reimbursement as a wage; where a reimbursement agreement is enforceable as a contract, an action on it must be brought within six years if the agreement is in writing or three years if it is not. [^wa-written-contract-limit-remedies][^wa-oral-contract-limit-remedies]

## How do federal and local minimum-wage laws limit unreimbursed expenses in Washington? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Federal law adds a floor below Washington's: under the Fair Labor Standards Act, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade violates the Act in any workweek in which their cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^wa-federal-tools-floor] The federal minimum wage that rule protects is $7.25 an hour, while Washington's minimum wage is $17.13 an hour from January 1, 2026. [^wa-federal-minimum-floor][^wa-minimum-wage-2026-floor] RCW 49.48.010 restricts withholding or diverting wages, WAC 296-126-028 imposes additional conditions on deductions during ongoing employment, and RCW 49.52.050 makes it a misdemeanor for an employer to collect a rebate of wages already paid. [^wa-deduction-limits-floor][^wa-written-authorization-floor][^wa-rebate-offense-floor] Some Washington cities and counties set higher local minimum wages and different labor rules in 2026, and the Minimum Wage Act leaves standards more favorable to employees in local law in force. [^wa-local-rates-floor][^wa-more-favorable-law-floor] Seattle adds a local reimbursement duty: its Office of Labor Standards explains that the Wage Theft Ordinance's required compensation includes reimbursement for employer expenses for employees working in the city. [^wa-seattle-reimbursement-floor][^wa-seattle-coverage-floor]
