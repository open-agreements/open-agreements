# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Alabama[^about]

Alabama has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses and no state minimum wage, so the federal minimum wage caps how much of the cost of employer-required tools of the trade an employee can be made to bear, while a few Alabama statutes assign named costs such as required drug tests in the state's drug-free workplace program to the employer.

## Must an employer in Alabama reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Alabama has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. The Alabama Department of Labor states that Alabama does not have any state laws governing wage and hour issues and that employers must therefore follow the federal guidelines of the U.S. Wage and Hour Division. [^aldol-no-state-wage-law-duty][^aldol-federal-guidelines-duty] Alabama also bars counties and cities from requiring an employer to provide any employment benefit, including a wage, that state or federal law does not require, so no local ordinance can add a reimbursement duty. [^ala-25-7-41-local-mandate-duty] Two other rules still put some costs on the employer. Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-alabama-duty] A few Alabama statutes assign named costs to the employer, such as drug tests the employer requires under the state's drug-free workplace program. [^ala-25-5-335-drug-test-cost-duty]

## Which work costs must an Alabama employer pay for itself? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Alabama has no general statute listing business expenses a private employer must reimburse. The Alabama Department of Labor states that Alabama has no state laws governing wage and hour issues. [^aldol-no-state-wage-law-covered] A few Alabama statutes assign named costs. An employer in the state's drug-free workplace program, which an employer joins to qualify for workers' compensation insurance premium discounts, must pay for all initial and confirmation drug tests it requires of employees, while the employee or applicant pays for additional tests the employer does not require. [^ala-25-5-335-program-covered][^ala-25-5-335-drug-test-cost-covered][^ala-25-5-335-additional-tests-covered] When an employer asks an injured employee to be examined by the employer's physician in a workers' compensation matter, the employer pays that physician, and in a compensable claim the injured employee is not liable for authorized and compensable medical expenses. [^ala-25-5-77-exam-covered][^ala-25-5-77-medical-covered] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-alabama] For other costs, one further limit is the federal wage floor: the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-alabama-covered] An employee may therefore bear the cost of employer-required tools of the trade only if no applicable statute or regulation assigns that cost to the employer and the cost does not cut into the required minimum or overtime wages, although a reimbursement promise in an employment agreement or policy may separately bind the employer. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-alabama][^fed-531-35-tools-alabama-covered][^hoffman-handbook-contract-covered]

## Must an Alabama employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Alabama statute requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs. The Alabama Department of Labor states that Alabama has no state laws governing wage and hour issues and directs employers to federal guidelines. [^aldol-no-state-wage-law-remote][^aldol-federal-guidelines-remote] A county or city may not fill the gap, because Alabama bars local mandates requiring an employer to provide any employment benefit, including a wage, that state or federal law does not require. [^ala-25-7-41-local-mandate-remote] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-alabama-remote]

## Can an Alabama offer letter or expense policy make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** An Alabama offer letter or expense policy may limit which business expenses the employer will reimburse, but it cannot shift a cost that a statute assigns to the employer or let the cost of employer-required tools of the trade cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages. [^ala-25-5-335-drug-test-cost-waiver][^fed-531-35-tools-alabama-waiver] The employer has that latitude because Alabama has no general reimbursement statute, and the Alabama Department of Labor states that Alabama has no state laws governing wage and hour issues. [^aldol-no-state-wage-law-waiver] A reimbursement promise in a policy can bind the employer as a contract. The Alabama Supreme Court has held that the language in an employee handbook can be sufficient to constitute an offer to create a binding unilateral contract, and the first step in deciding whether it does is to examine whether the language is specific enough to constitute an offer. [^hoffman-handbook-unilateral-contract][^hoffman-handbook-specific-offer] The same court said that an employer that does not want handbook policies read as an offer is free to say so in the handbook. [^hoffman-handbook-disclaimer] An employer that wants its reimbursement policy to be a firm commitment should therefore state the covered expenses, limits and submission steps specifically, and an employer that wants flexibility should say so in the policy.

## How long does an Alabama employee have to sue over an unpaid expense reimbursement? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** An Alabama employee suing on a reimbursement promise generally has six years, because an action founded on a promise in writing not under seal, or on a simple contract not otherwise listed, must be commenced within six years. [^ala-6-2-34-six-years][^ala-6-2-34-simple-contract] A claim that the cost of employer-required tools of the trade pushed pay below the federal minimum or overtime wage is a federal wage claim: the employer is liable for the unpaid minimum wages or overtime plus an equal amount as liquidated damages, unless the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds, in which case the court may award less or none; the court must allow a reasonable attorney's fee and costs, and the action must be brought within two years, or three years for a willful violation. [^usc-29-216-b-liquidated-alabama][^usc-29-216-b-fees-alabama][^usc-29-260-good-faith-alabama][^usc-29-255-a-alabama] Alabama's own two-year limit applies to actions for wages, overtime, damages, fees or penalties accruing under laws respecting the payment of wages. [^ala-6-2-38-m-wages] The Alabama Department of Labor directs wage and hour questions to the U.S. Wage and Hour Division, because Alabama has no state laws governing wage and hour issues. [^aldol-no-state-wage-law-remedies][^aldol-federal-guidelines-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Alabama? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-alabama-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Alabama has no state minimum wage of its own, and the Alabama Department of Labor directs employers to the federal guidelines, so for a covered employee the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour is the wage the expense may not cut into. [^aldol-no-state-wage-law-federal][^aldol-federal-guidelines-federal][^usc-29-206-a-alabama-federal]
