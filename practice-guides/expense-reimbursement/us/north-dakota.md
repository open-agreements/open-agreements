# Employee Expense Reimbursement in North Dakota[^about]

North Dakota Century Code chapter 34-02 covers necessary job expenses, shared-use equipment, and limits on employer liability.

This note covers employee expense reimbursement in North Dakota. It addresses the state indemnity rule and the federal wage floor. The broader topic appears in the [expense reimbursement practice guide](/practice-guides/expense-reimbursement).

## Must an employer in North Dakota reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** North Dakota employers must cover what an employee necessarily spends or loses as a direct consequence of job duties or employer directions, subject to the statute's exceptions. [^nd-34-02-01-indemnify-necessary-expenses] Section 34-02-02 limits liability for losses from ordinary business risks and coworker negligence, unless the employer failed to use ordinary care in selecting the coworker. [^nd-loss-limits]

"An employer shall indemnify the employer's employee, except as prescribed in section 34-02-02, for all that the employee necessarily expends or loses in direct consequence of the discharge of the employee's duties as such or of the employee's obedience to the directions of the employer even though such directions were unlawful, unless the employee at the time of obeying such directions believed them to be unlawful."[^nd-34-02-01-indemnify-necessary-expenses]

## Which business expenses must a North Dakota employer reimburse? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** North Dakota employers must cover necessary expenditures directly caused by an employee's duties or employer directions. [^nd-covered-duty] The duty excludes buying or renting tools of a trade or other equipment that the employee also uses outside work. [^nd-covered-equipment] Separate limits apply to losses from ordinary business risks and coworker negligence. [^nd-covered-losses]

## Must a North Dakota employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** North Dakota's indemnity duty does not cover buying or renting equipment, such as a personal phone or computer, that the employee also uses outside the scope of employment. [^nd-remote-equipment] The exclusion addresses equipment, not a non-equipment cost such as an internet service bill. [^nd-remote-equipment] Such a cost falls under the general duty if it is necessary and directly caused by job duties or employer directions. [^nd-remote-duty]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in North Dakota make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** North Dakota's indemnity provision requires employers to cover necessary job expenditures and does not address waiver by an offer letter, policy, or agreement. [^nd-waiver-duty] Its express equipment exclusion applies when the employee also uses the tools or equipment outside employment. [^nd-waiver-equipment] The statutory text supplies no basis to treat a general employee-pays clause as an additional exclusion. [^nd-waiver-duty] [^nd-waiver-equipment]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in North Dakota? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools] The regulation rests on the rule that wages count as paid only when they are paid free and clear, with no kick-back to the employer. [^fed-531-35-free-and-clear][^fed-531-35-kickback]

The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. It is breached only when an employer-required cost pushes the week's pay below the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools]

The federal rule does not depend on state law. North Dakota's statute excludes tools of a trade and other equipment the employee also uses outside the scope of employment [^nd-federal-equipment-exclusion], but an employer-required tool that the employee buys still breaches the federal rule in any workweek in which its cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools]
