# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Connecticut[^about]

Connecticut has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but it limits wage deductions to listed grounds, bars charging employees fees or sums of money to get or keep a job, bars making employees cover customer losses, requires employers to supply protective garments, and lets an employee recover twice wages withheld unlawfully.

## Must an employer in Connecticut reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Connecticut has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. Connecticut instead limits how an employer may shift costs onto employees. An employer may not withhold or divert any part of an employee's wages unless law requires or empowers it, the employee authorizes it in writing on a form approved by the Labor Commissioner, or another listed ground applies. [^ct-31-71e-deductions-duty] An employer may not demand, request or receive a fee or sum of money from any person, or deduct agreed wages, on the representation or understanding that the payment is necessary to get or keep a job. [^ct-31-73-no-fee-for-employment-duty] That rule does not apply to deductions made in accordance with a law or a governmental agency's rule or regulation. [^ct-31-73-legal-deductions-excepted-duty] An employer also may not request or require an employee to reimburse a loss or shortage in its business caused by a customer's wrongdoing. [^ct-31-51hh-customer-losses-duty] Under the Labor Commissioner's minimum wage regulations, protective garments such as gloves, boots or aprons that are needed to protect the worker or required for sanitation must be provided, paid for and maintained by the employer without charge to the employee. [^ct-31-60-9-protective-garments-duty] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-connecticut-duty]

## Which business costs may a Connecticut employer not shift onto employees? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Connecticut has no general statute listing business expenses an employer must reimburse. Rules for particular costs come from statutes and from the Labor Commissioner's minimum wage regulations. An employer may not make an employee repay a loss or shortage caused by a customer's wrongdoing, such as a customer who leaves without paying. [^ct-31-51hh-customer-losses-covered] Necessary protective garments, such as gloves, boots or aprons, must be provided, paid for and maintained by the employer without charge. [^ct-31-60-9-protective-garments-covered] Uniforms and other clothing the employer supplies are treated differently: an allowance of no more than $1.50 a week, or the actual cost if lower, may count toward the minimum fair wage for maintaining or laundering that clothing when the service has been performed. [^ct-31-60-9-apparel-allowance-covered] In the mercantile trade, the cost of uniforms or other facilities the employer requires, and their reasonable upkeep, may not be charged to an employee if the charge would bring pay below the minimum wage. [^ct-31-62-d11-mercantile-uniforms-covered] In restaurants and hotel restaurants, an employer may not require a deposit for a uniform or any other purpose without the labor department's permission. [^ct-31-62-e8-restaurant-deposits-covered] A restaurant employee required or permitted to travel between establishments during the work day must be paid for the travel time and reimbursed for the cost of transportation. [^ct-31-62-e10-restaurant-travel-covered] Outside that restaurant rule, the general minimum wage regulations require the employer to pay expenses directly incidental to travel an employee makes for the employer's benefit only when the employee's paying them would bring earnings below the minimum fair wage. [^ct-31-60-10-travel-expenses-covered] By statute, the minimum wage regulations recognize deductions and allowances for meals at 85 cents for a full meal and 45 cents for a light meal, and for lodging, apparel or other items or services the employer supplies. [^ct-31-60-board-lodging-covered] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-connecticut-covered]

## Must a Connecticut employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Connecticut statute specifically requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs. In our review, we found no Connecticut regulation specifically requiring reimbursement of those costs. An employer may not recover a remote-work cost by withholding it from wages unless law requires or empowers the deduction, the employee authorizes it in writing on a form approved by the Labor Commissioner, or another listed ground applies. [^ct-31-71e-deductions-remote] An employer also may not demand a sum of money from an employee on the representation or understanding that paying it is necessary to keep the job. [^ct-31-73-no-fee-for-employment-remote] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-connecticut-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Connecticut make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Connecticut offer letter or expense policy cannot authorize the employer to deduct business costs from wages except on a ground the statute lists, such as the employee's written authorization on a form approved by the Labor Commissioner. [^ct-31-71e-deductions-waiver] An agreement to be paid wages other than as the wage payment sections require is no defense to a wage action. [^ct-31-72-agreement-no-defense-waiver] No employer may require a person to agree to pay a fee, contribution or deduction from wages in order to get or keep a job. [^ct-31-73-no-fee-for-employment-waiver] That rule does not apply to deductions made in accordance with a law or a governmental agency's rule or regulation. [^ct-31-73-legal-deductions-excepted-waiver] The Connecticut Supreme Court held in *Dunn v. Northeast Helicopters Flight Services, LLC* that the sums of money the statute covers include any amount of money or similar measure of value and need not be directly related to the job. [^ct-dunn-sum-of-money-waiver] No agreement can make an employee repay a loss caused by a customer's wrongdoing, because the statute bars an employer from even requesting that reimbursement. [^ct-31-51hh-customer-losses-waiver] In a dispute over wages, accepting a payment of the conceded amount does not release the rest of the claim, and a release the employer requires as a condition of payment is void. [^ct-31-71d-no-release-waiver]

## What remedies does a Connecticut employee have for an unlawful deduction or unpaid wages? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** An employee whose employer fails to pay wages as the wage payment sections require, including by withholding wages on a ground § 31-71e does not allow, may sue for twice the full amount of the wages, with costs and reasonable attorney's fees. If the employer proves a good faith belief that the underpayment complied with law, the recovery is the full amount of the wages with costs and fees. [^ct-31-72-double-damages-remedies] The Labor Commissioner may also collect unpaid wages with interest from the date they should have been paid. [^ct-31-72-commissioner-collection-remedies] An employee whose unreimbursed costs leave pay below the minimum fair wage owed under a statute or wage order may sue for twice the shortfall with costs and reasonable attorney's fees, or the shortfall with costs and reasonable attorney's fees if the employer proves a good faith belief in compliance. [^ct-31-68-minimum-wage-action-remedies] A suit for periodically payable remuneration must be brought within two years after the claim accrues, and the period is tolled when a wage complaint is filed with the Labor Commissioner. [^ct-52-596-two-years-remedies] A violation of the wage payment part is a class D felony when more than $2,000 in wages is owed to an employee, with lesser penalties for smaller amounts. [^ct-31-71g-criminal-remedies] Demanding a fee or sum of money as a condition of employment is punishable by a fine of up to $100 or 30 days in jail for a first offense and up to $500, six months, or both for later offenses. [^ct-31-73-penalty-remedies] Connecticut defines wages, for the wage payment sections, as compensation for labor or services rendered by an employee. [^ct-31-71a-wages-definition-remedies] The Connecticut Appellate Court noted in *Matteson v. Great Eastern Development, Ltd.* that an employee's out-of-pocket expenses were due under the employment contract, not § 31-72, and upheld the expense award on the contract. [^ct-matteson-expenses-contract-remedies] Under that reasoning, an unpaid agreed reimbursement is recovered as a breach of the employment agreement rather than as wages subject to double damages, while a deduction from wages that § 31-71e does not allow remains a claim for unpaid wages under § 31-72. [^ct-31-72-double-damages-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Connecticut? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-connecticut-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Connecticut applies a similar wage floor through its own regulations: in the mercantile trade, required uniforms and their upkeep may not be charged to an employee if the charge would bring pay below the minimum wage. [^ct-31-62-d11-mercantile-uniforms-federal] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-connecticut-federal]
