# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Nevada[^about]

Nevada has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but it requires employers to furnish and specially clean distinctive uniforms and accessories at no cost, limits wage deductions to specific written authorizations, and bars employers from taking employees' tips.

## Must an employer in Nevada reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Nevada has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. One statute names a specific cost: an employer must furnish, without cost to employees, all uniforms or accessories distinctive as to style, color or material, and must clean at no cost any such uniform or accessory that needs a special cleaning process and cannot be easily laundered by the employee. [^nv-nrs-608-165-uniforms-duty] Nevada's other protections work through deductions and wage payment rules rather than reimbursement. Under the Labor Commissioner's regulation, an employer may not deduct an amount from wages, other than amounts required by law and benefit contributions, unless it reasonably believes the employee is responsible for the amount, the deduction is for a specific purpose, pay period and amount, and the employee voluntarily authorizes it in writing. [^nv-nac-608-160-deduction-conditions-duty] An employer also may not require an employee to rebate, refund or return any part of wages earned and paid. [^nv-nrs-608-100-no-rebate-duty] Nevada defines wages as the amount an employer agrees to pay for time worked, computed in proportion to time, plus commissions and amounts due at separation, excluding bonuses and profit sharing. [^nv-nrs-608-012-wage-definition-duty] In our review, we found no Nevada appellate decision deciding whether an agreed expense reimbursement falls within that definition. Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-nevada-duty]

## Which business costs may a Nevada employer not shift onto employees? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Nevada has no general statute listing business expenses an employer must reimburse. An employer must furnish distinctive uniforms and accessories without cost and must clean at no cost those that need a special cleaning process and cannot be easily laundered by the employee. [^nv-nrs-608-165-uniforms-covered] The Labor Commissioner interprets uniform, for that statute, to mean distinctive clothing an employee is required to wear that clearly identifies the employee with the business. [^nv-nac-608-090-uniform-meaning-covered] An employer may not deduct a business cost from wages unless it reasonably believes the employee is responsible for the amount, the deduction is for a specific purpose, pay period and amount, and the employee voluntarily authorizes it in writing. [^nv-nac-608-160-deduction-conditions-covered] Each payment of wages must come with an itemized list of the deductions made. [^nv-nrs-608-110-itemized-deductions-covered] An employer may not take any part of the tips given to its employees or count those tips toward the State minimum wage. [^nv-nrs-608-160-tips-covered] Meals may count as part of wages only if the employee and employer mutually agree in the contract of employment, and their value is capped by reference to the statutory minimum hourly wage, except that the caps do not apply to agricultural employees. [^nv-nrs-608-155-meals-covered] Training the employer requires counts as time worked, which must be paid at no less than the minimum wage. [^nv-nac-608-130-required-training-covered] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-nevada-covered]

## Must a Nevada employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Nevada statute specifically requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs. In our review, we found no Nevada regulation specifically requiring reimbursement of those costs. An employer may not recover a remote-work cost by deducting it from wages unless it reasonably believes the employee is responsible for the amount, the deduction is for a specific purpose, pay period and amount, and the employee voluntarily authorizes it in writing. [^nv-nac-608-160-deduction-conditions-remote] A blanket authorization the employee gave in advance does not support a deduction. [^nv-nac-608-160-no-blanket-authorization-remote] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-nevada-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Nevada make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Nevada offer letter or expense policy containing only a blanket advance authorization cannot support later deductions for business costs, because an employer may not use a blanket authorization the employee made in advance to withhold any amount from wages. [^nv-nac-608-160-no-blanket-authorization-waiver] A deduction requires the employee's voluntary written authorization for a specific purpose, pay period and amount that the employer reasonably believes the employee owes. [^nv-nac-608-160-deduction-conditions-waiver] Chapter 608 does not bar deductions authorized by the employee's written order. [^nv-nrs-608-110-written-order-waiver] Wages must be paid in money or a negotiable check payable to the employee unless the employee agrees in writing to another disposition or a court or government agency directs one. [^nv-nrs-608-120-payment-manner-waiver] An employer may not require an employee to rebate, refund or return earned wages that have been paid. [^nv-nrs-608-100-no-rebate-waiver] An employer may not pay less than the amount a statute, regulation or the employment contract requires. [^nv-nrs-608-100-agreed-pay-waiver] A contract, or the employee's acceptance of a lesser wage, does not bar an action for unpaid minimum wages. [^nv-nrs-608-260-minimum-wage-action-waiver] No statute we found lets an agreement shift the cost of distinctive uniforms or their special cleaning onto the employee. [^nv-nrs-608-165-uniforms-waiver]

## What remedies does a Nevada employee have for an unlawful deduction or unpaid wages? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** The Nevada Supreme Court held in *Neville v. Eighth Judicial District Court* that chapter 608 provides a private right of action for unpaid wages. [^nv-neville-private-action-remedies] An employee may bring a civil action within two years after the employer fails to pay wages as NRS 608.020 to 608.050 require. [^nv-nrs-608-135-civil-action-remedies] If an employer does not pay a discharged employee within 3 days after the wages become due, or an employee who quits on the day they are due, the employee's wages continue at the same rate until paid or for 30 days, whichever is less. [^nv-nrs-608-040-waiting-time-remedies] An employee discharged or laid off without being paid wages then due may collect the agreed daily wage for each day of default, for up to 30 days. [^nv-nrs-608-050-default-wages-remedies] A court must award a reasonable attorney fee to an employee who proves the wages sued for are justly due and that a written demand for no more than that amount was made at least 5 days before suit. [^nv-nrs-608-140-attorney-fee-remedies] A violation of NRS 608.005 to 608.195, which include the uniform and tip rules, or a regulation adopted under them is a misdemeanor, and the Labor Commissioner may impose an administrative penalty of up to $5,000 for each violation. [^nv-nrs-608-195-penalties-remedies] An employee paid less than the minimum wage may sue within 2 years, and a prevailing employee is entitled to appropriate legal and equitable remedies and must be awarded reasonable attorney's fees and costs. [^nv-nrs-608-260-minimum-wage-action-remedies][^nv-nrs-608-260-minimum-wage-remedies-remedies] A violation of the minimum wage provisions is also a misdemeanor carrying an administrative penalty of up to $5,000 for each violation. [^nv-nrs-608-290-minimum-wage-penalties-remedies] In our review, we found no Nevada appellate decision deciding whether an unpaid agreed expense reimbursement is recoverable as wages under these provisions; Nevada's statutory definition of wages covers pay for time worked computed in proportion to time, commissions and amounts due at separation. [^nv-nrs-608-012-wage-definition-remedies] Until a court decides the point, the conservative course is to pay an agreed expense reimbursement on the same schedule as wages, including with final pay at separation.

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Nevada? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-nevada-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Nevada separately bars employers from counting employees' tips toward the State minimum wage. [^nv-nrs-608-160-tips-federal] Meals may count toward wages in Nevada only by mutual agreement in the employment contract and, except for agricultural employees, within caps tied to the statutory minimum hourly wage. [^nv-nrs-608-155-meals-federal]
