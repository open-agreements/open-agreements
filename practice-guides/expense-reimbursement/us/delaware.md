# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Delaware[^about]

Delaware has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but agreed reimbursements must be paid within 30 days, wage deductions need a statutory ground, and work costs cannot cut into the minimum wage.

## Must an employer in Delaware reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Delaware has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. The Wage Payment and Collection Act defines a wage as compensation due by reason of employment, payable in legal tender or a check convertible into cash at full face value, and the definition does not name expense reimbursement. [^de-1101-wage-definition] The Act instead treats reimbursement for expenses as a benefit or wage supplement, which is compensation other than wages, and an employer that is party to an agreement to pay such benefits or supplements must pay them within 30 days after payment is required, unless the employer is subject to Part I of the Interstate Commerce Act. Disputed amounts handled under collective-bargaining dispute procedures fall outside the definition. [^de-1109-agreed-reimbursement][^de-1109-supplement-definition] Separate rules still put some costs on the employer. Under 19 Del. C. § 1107, an employer may not withhold or divert any part of an employee's wages unless law requires or empowers it, the deduction is for medical, surgical or hospital care without financial benefit to the employer and is openly recorded in the employer's books, or the employee signed an authorization for deductions for a lawful purpose accruing to the employee's benefit. [^de-1107-withholding-rule] The Department of Labor may prohibit authorized deductions by regulation, and its deduction rule, as amended effective June 11, 2026, bars deducting cash or inventory shortages from an employee's pay in any case. [^de-1107-department-prohibition][^de-1328-shortages][^de-1328-amendment-effective-date] Delaware's minimum wage is $15.00 an hour from January 1, 2025. [^de-902-minimum-wage] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-delaware-floor]

## Which business expenses must a Delaware employer reimburse? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** A Delaware employer not subject to Part I of the Interstate Commerce Act must pay agreed expense reimbursements within 30 days after payment is required. [^de-1109-agreed-covered][^de-1109-definition-covered] The Wage Payment and Collection Act counts reimbursement for expenses among the benefits or wage supplements that an employer party to an agreement must pay, and the definition leaves out disputed amounts handled under collective-bargaining dispute procedures. Beyond agreed reimbursements, Delaware has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse employee business expenses. Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions such as non-specialty safety-toe footwear and non-specialty prescription safety eyewear that the employer permits employees to wear off the job site. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-delaware][^osha-1910-132-footwear-delaware] For other costs, the main limits are the wage floor and the withholding statute. Delaware requires at least $15.00 an hour, and the minimum-wage chapter defines a wage as compensation payable in legal tender or a check convertible into cash at full face value, subject only to deductions, charges or allowances permitted by Department of Labor regulations. [^de-902-minimum-wage-covered][^de-901-wage-covered] The minimum-wage chapter does not cover an individual employed in a bona fide executive, administrative or professional capacity, or as an outside commission-paid salesperson, other than a route driver, who customarily works away from the employer's premises taking orders for goods or services. [^de-901-exemptions-covered] Federal law bars a required tools-of-the-trade cost from cutting into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-delaware-covered] An employer that wants to recover a work cost from wages also needs one of the grounds in 19 Del. C. § 1107, and a signed authorization supports a deduction only for a lawful purpose accruing to the employee's benefit. [^de-1107-withholding-covered][^de-1107-signed-authorization-covered] The Department of Labor's deduction rule, as amended effective June 11, 2026, adds that cash or inventory shortages and an employer's loss from damage to its own or a customer's or client's property may not be deducted from an employee's pay in any case. [^de-1328-shortages-covered][^de-1328-damage-covered][^de-1328-amendment-effective-covered] The amended rule also allows an authorized deduction only if the authorization is voluntary and given after the employee receives written notice of all terms and conditions of the payment or its benefits and of how the deductions are made. [^de-1328-notice-covered][^de-1328-amendment-effective-covered] The statute does not say whether a deduction for an item the employer requires for its own work accrues to the employee's benefit, so the conservative course is not to deduct required work costs from wages.

## Must a Delaware employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Delaware statute requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs as such. Under 19 Del. C. § 1109, an employer not subject to Part I of the Interstate Commerce Act must pay agreed remote-work expense reimbursements within 30 days after payment is required, because agreed benefits or wage supplements include reimbursement for expenses. [^de-1109-agreed-remote][^de-1109-definition-remote] The Delaware minimum wage of $15.00 an hour still applies to remote employees covered by the minimum-wage chapter. [^de-902-minimum-wage-remote] Federal law applies the same kind of floor to required tools of the trade an employee buys. [^fed-531-35-tools-delaware-remote] Any deduction of a remote-work cost from wages must also satisfy 19 Del. C. § 1107, and the Department of Labor's deduction rule bars deducting an employer's loss from damage to its property, such as a damaged laptop, in any case. [^de-1107-withholding-remote][^de-1328-damage-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Delaware make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Delaware offer letter or expense policy can decide which expenses the employer agrees to reimburse, because the reimbursement duty in 19 Del. C. § 1109 applies to an employer that is party to an agreement to pay benefits or wage supplements. [^de-1109-agreed-waiver] Once the employer has agreed, the agreement cannot set aside the Wage Payment and Collection Act: except as the Act provides, no provision of the Act may be contravened or set aside by private agreement. [^de-1110-no-waiver] An agreement also cannot waive the minimum wage, because an agreement to work for less than the minimum wage is no defense to an employee's action for the shortfall. [^de-911-agreement-no-defense-waiver] For work covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, a policy cannot let required tools-of-the-trade costs cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages. [^fed-531-35-tools-delaware-waiver] A policy that recovers a cost by deduction from wages needs a ground under 19 Del. C. § 1107, and a signed authorization supports a deduction only for a lawful purpose accruing to the employee's benefit. [^de-1107-withholding-waiver][^de-1107-signed-authorization-waiver] Under the Department of Labor's deduction rule, as amended effective June 11, 2026, an authorization counts only if it is voluntary and given after the employee receives written notice of all terms and conditions of the payment or its benefits and of how the deductions are made. [^de-1328-notice-waiver][^de-1328-amendment-effective-waiver] No agreement can permit deducting cash or inventory shortages or an employer's loss from damage to its own or a customer's or client's property, because the rule bars those deductions in any case. [^de-1328-shortages-waiver][^de-1328-damage-waiver] A cash advance or a charge for goods or services may be repaid through payroll deductions only under a written agreement signed by both sides that states the amount, repayment schedule and method, and the schedule may not exceed 15 percent of gross wages per pay period. [^de-1328-advances-waiver][^de-1328-repayment-cap-waiver] The deduction rule does not prevent an employee from voluntarily reimbursing an employer. [^de-1328-voluntary-waiver]

## What remedies does a Delaware employee have for unreimbursed expenses, an unlawful deduction or pay pushed below the minimum wage? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** A Delaware employee may bring a civil action to recover unpaid wages and liquidated damages, and a judgment for the employee must include costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^de-1113-civil-action] The Department of Labor may also bring an action to collect unpaid wages. [^de-1113-civil-action] An employer that fails to pay wages as the Act requires, without reasonable grounds for dispute under § 1104 or § 1107, also owes liquidated damages equal to the lower of 10 percent of the unpaid wages for each day, excluding Sundays and legal holidays, that the failure continues or an amount equal to the unpaid wages. [^de-1103-liquidated-damages] An employer that violates any requirement of the Act, including the 30-day duty to pay agreed reimbursements, is subject to a civil penalty of $1,000 to $5,000 for each violation. [^de-1112-civil-penalty] Although § 1109 classifies reimbursement for expenses as compensation other than wages, the Delaware Superior Court stated in *Nye v. University of Delaware* that § 1113 gives employees a civil remedy when payments due under § 1109 are not timely made, so an agreed reimbursement left unpaid past its 30-day deadline can be pursued under § 1113. [^de-1109-definition-remedies][^nye-1113-remedy-for-1109-supplements] An employer that pays less than the minimum wage owes the shortfall plus costs, expenses of prosecution and reasonable attorney's fees. [^de-911-minimum-wage-remedy] An action to recover wages, related damages or penalties, or other benefits arising from work performed must be brought within 2 years after the cause of action accrues. [^de-8111-limitations]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Delaware? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-delaware-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Delaware's own minimum wage is $15.00 an hour, and the state rate rises to match any higher federal minimum wage. [^de-902-minimum-wage-federal] The Delaware minimum-wage chapter defines a wage as compensation payable in legal tender or a check convertible into cash at full face value, subject only to deductions, charges or allowances permitted by Department of Labor regulations. [^de-901-wage-federal]
