# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Alaska[^about]

Alaska has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but a state regulation bars requiring employees to buy certain uniforms and equipment, and deduction rules protect the minimum wage.

## Must an employer in Alaska reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Alaska has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. A Department of Labor and Workforce Development regulation still bars an employer from requiring an employee to buy a uniform or equipment that federal, state or local safety or health codes require; one the business requires that cannot be worn or used during the employee's normal social activities; or one the business requires that is distinctive and advertises or is associated with the employer's products or services, except that clothing forming the uniform may advertise those products or services if the employer customarily sells the uniform to the public. [^ak-8aac-15-165-uniform-purchase-duty] Two other rules limit how far an employer can shift work costs onto pay. A written agreement for deductions payable to the employer is not valid if it would take the employee's wage rate below the statutory minimum wage or overtime rates, or if it would make the employee repay listed losses such as cash shortages or breakage without the rule's written admission or willful-conduct conditions. [^ak-8aac-15-160-deduction-limits-duty] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-alaska-duty]

## Which business expenses must an Alaska employer pay for? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Alaska has no general statute requiring reimbursement of employee business expenses. An employer may not require an employee to purchase a uniform or equipment that federal, state or local safety or health codes require; one the business requires that cannot be worn or used during the employee's normal social activities; or one the business requires that is distinctive and advertises or is associated with the employer's products or services, except that clothing forming the uniform may advertise those products or services if the employer customarily sells the uniform to the public. [^ak-8aac-15-165-uniform-purchase-covered] Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-alaska-covered] An employer may take a security deposit from wages for uniforms or equipment it issues only under a written agreement, only up to the item's cost, and only if the deduction does not take wages below the statutory minimum or overtime below one and one-half times the contractual rate. [^ak-8aac-15-160-uniform-deposit-covered] A deduction to repay the employer for transportation from the place of hire to the place of employment is allowed under a signed written agreement only on the same minimum-wage and overtime condition. [^ak-8aac-15-160-transport-deduction-covered] Other work costs fall under the wage floor: except as otherwise provided by law, an Alaska employer must pay each employee the state minimum wage for hours worked, and that rate is $14.00 per hour from July 1, 2026. [^ak-23-10-065-minimum-wage-duty-covered][^ak-23-10-065-minimum-wage-rates-covered] The scheduled rise to $15.00 per hour on July 1, 2027 is future law and does not apply to work performed before that date. [^ak-23-10-065-minimum-wage-rates-covered]

## Must an Alaska employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Alaska statute requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs, and our review of the state wage-and-hour regulations found no rule addressed to them. The state rule on employee purchases can still reach remote-work equipment: an employer may not require an employee to buy equipment that the business requires and that cannot be used during the employee's normal social activities. [^ak-8aac-15-165-uniform-purchase-remote] The Alaska minimum wage still applies to remote employees covered by it, so an employer must pay each covered employee at least $14.00 per hour for hours worked in a pay period from July 1, 2026. [^ak-23-10-065-minimum-wage-duty-remote][^ak-23-10-065-minimum-wage-rates-remote] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-alaska-remote] A written agreement to deduct a remote-work cost payable to the employer is not valid if it would take the employee's wage rate below the statutory minimum wage or overtime rates. [^ak-8aac-15-160-deduction-limits-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Alaska make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** An Alaska employer and employee may authorize deductions in writing, subject to wage-floor protections and restrictions on deductions for employer losses. [^ak-8aac-15-160-written-agreement-waiver][^ak-8aac-15-160-deduction-limits-waiver] A written agreement for deductions payable to the employer is not valid if it would take the employee's wage rate below the statutory minimum wage or overtime rates, or would make the employee repay customer checks returned for any reason or non-payment for goods or services resulting from theft or credit default. The same agreement may not make the employee repay cash or cash register shortages, or lost, missing or stolen property, unless the employee admits, willingly and in writing, to having personally taken the specific cash or property alleged to be missing. It may not make the employee repay damage or breakage costs unless the damage was clearly due to the employee's willful conduct and the employee has acknowledged responsibility in writing. [^ak-8aac-15-160-deduction-limits-waiver] Requiring or inducing an employee to give back any part of the compensation to which the employee is entitled, by threat of dismissal or any other means, is prohibited. [^ak-8aac-15-160-no-kickback-waiver] A policy also cannot require an employee to buy a uniform or equipment that the regulation places on the employer. [^ak-8aac-15-165-uniform-purchase-waiver]

## What remedies does an Alaska employee have when work costs push pay below the minimum wage? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** An Alaska employer that violates the state minimum-wage or overtime provisions is liable for the unpaid minimum wages or overtime and, except as AS 23.10.110(d) provides, an additional equal amount as liquidated damages. [^ak-23-10-110-liquidated-damages-remedies] The court must also allow costs and, except as subsections (e) through (h) provide, reasonable attorney fees paid by the defendant. [^ak-23-10-110-attorney-fees-remedies] An action for unpaid minimum wages, unpaid overtime or liquidated damages under the Alaska Wage and Hour Act must be started within two years after the cause of action accrues. [^ak-23-10-130-two-year-limit-remedies] For a reimbursement claim enforceable in contract, AS 09.10.053 generally provides a three-year limitation period, subject to its statutory exceptions and contractual waiver. [^ak-09-10-053-contract-three-years-remedies]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Alaska? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-alaska-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Alaska's own minimum wage, $14.00 per hour from July 1, 2026, is the higher floor for most Alaska work, and a written deduction agreement payable to the employer is not valid under the state regulation if it would take the wage rate below the statutory minimum wage or overtime rates. [^ak-23-10-065-minimum-wage-rates-federal][^ak-8aac-15-160-deduction-limits-federal]
