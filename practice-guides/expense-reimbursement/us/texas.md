# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Texas[^about]

Texas has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but the Payday Law limits deductions from wages and the federal minimum wage still caps how much of a work cost an employee can be made to bear.

## Must an employer in Texas reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Texas has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. The Texas Workforce Commission states that employers do not have to reimburse an employee's out-of-pocket business-related expenses. [^twc-expense-no-duty] The Texas Payday Law defines wages as compensation for labor or services and for vacation, holiday, sick leave, parental leave or severance pay owed under a written agreement or policy, and the definition does not name expense reimbursement. [^tx-61-001-wages-definition] The Texas Workforce Commission's Payday Law rule adds that, for purposes of the written-agreement category of wages, expense reimbursements paid to employees are not wages. [^tx-821-25-not-wages] Two separate rules still put some costs on the employer. Under Labor Code § 61.018, an employer may not withhold or divert any part of an employee's wages without a court order, authority under state or federal law, or the employee's written authorization for a lawful purpose. [^tx-61-018-deduction-rule] Under federal law, which the Texas Minimum Wage Act adopts as the Texas rate, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^tx-62-051-federal-rate][^fed-531-35-tools-texas-floor]

## Which business expenses must a Texas employer reimburse? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Texas has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse employee business expenses. The Texas Workforce Commission states that employers do not have to reimburse out-of-pocket business-related expenses. [^twc-expense-no-duty-covered] Federal rules that require an employer to pay for a named item still apply: under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions such as non-specialty safety-toe footwear and non-specialty prescription safety eyewear that the employer permits employees to wear off the job site. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-texas][^osha-1910-132-footwear-texas] For other costs, a key limit is the wage floor. The Texas Workforce Commission treats almost all costs of providing a workplace, of complying with workplace regulations that impose a duty on the employer (such as supplying safety equipment required under OSHA regulations), and of running the business as normal costs of doing business that may not be deducted from wages to the extent the deduction takes pay below the minimum wage or overtime pay below one and one-half times the regular rate. [^twc-normal-cost-of-business] Federal law applies the same limit to required tools of the trade an employee buys. [^fed-531-35-tools-texas-covered] An employee paid well above the minimum wage may therefore bear a work cost, provided the cost does not reduce the minimum wage or required overtime compensation and no other rule requires the employer to pay for it; a deduction of that cost from wages still needs a court order, authority under state or federal law, or the employee's written authorization for a lawful purpose. [^twc-normal-cost-of-business][^tx-61-018-covered]

## Must a Texas employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No general Texas statute requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs. The Texas Workforce Commission states that employers do not have to reimburse an employee's out-of-pocket business-related expenses, and it recognizes that an employer must reimburse what is needed to keep the employee's pay at least at the minimum wage. [^twc-expense-no-duty-remote][^twc-expense-minimum-wage-remote] The Commission treats the expenses of an ongoing business operation as normal costs of doing business that may not be deducted from wages to the extent the deduction takes pay below the minimum wage or overtime pay below one and one-half times the regular rate, and federal law applies the same floor to required tools of the trade an employee buys. [^twc-normal-cost-remote][^fed-531-35-tools-texas-remote] Any deduction of a remote-work cost from wages must also satisfy Labor Code § 61.018. [^tx-61-018-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Texas make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** A Texas offer letter or expense policy may specify which business expenses the employer will reimburse, subject to minimum-wage, overtime and other applicable employer-payment requirements. [^twc-expense-no-duty-waiver][^twc-normal-cost-waiver][^fed-531-35-tools-texas-waiver] The Texas Workforce Commission advises employers to set a clear written policy stating what will be reimbursed, under what conditions and when, and to be careful with the minimum wage when listing expenses that will not be reimbursed. [^twc-expense-written-policy][^twc-expense-not-reimbursed] A policy that recovers a cost by deduction from wages must meet the Payday Law's written-authorization rule: under the Commission's rule, the authorization must be specific as to the lawful purpose, give the employee a reasonable expectation of the amount to be withheld, and clearly indicate that the deduction will come from wages. [^tx-61-018-waiver][^tx-821-28-written-authorization] A signed acknowledgment of a handbook can serve as that authorization only if it meets those requirements, specifically informs the employee of the deduction, and states that the employee agrees to abide by or be bound to it. [^tx-821-28-handbook][^tx-821-28-handbook-agreement] The Commission's guidance treats a deduction the employee authorized in writing as still unlawful if it violates the minimum wage or overtime laws. [^twc-authorized-but-unlawful]

## Can a Texas employee file a Payday Law wage claim for an unpaid expense reimbursement? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** The Texas Workforce Commission states that, other than what an employer must reimburse to keep the employee's pay at least at the minimum wage, expense reimbursements are not wages and may not be the subject of a Texas Payday Law wage claim. [^twc-expense-not-wages][^tx-821-25-remedy] A wage claim for an unlawful deduction or for pay pushed below the minimum wage must be filed with the Commission no later than the 180th day after the wages became due, and that deadline is jurisdictional. [^tx-61-051-deadline] Where the Texas Minimum Wage Act applies, an employer that violates the minimum-wage rule is liable for the unpaid wages plus an equal amount as liquidated damages, and the action must be brought within two years after the wages are due; that Act does not apply to a person covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act. [^tx-62-201-liquidated][^tx-62-202-limitations][^tx-62-151-flsa-covered]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Texas? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-texas-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Texas sets its own minimum wage at the federal rate, and the Texas Minimum Wage Act does not apply to a person the Fair Labor Standards Act covers, so for an employee the Fair Labor Standards Act covers, the federal rule is the operative limit. [^tx-62-051-federal-rate-federal][^tx-62-151-flsa-covered-federal]
