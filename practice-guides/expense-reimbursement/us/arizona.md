# Employee Expense Reimbursement in Arizona[^about]

Arizona has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse business expenses, but the wage-withholding statute limits deductions from wages and the state minimum wage counts only cash or its equivalent toward the floor.

## Must an employer in Arizona reimburse employee business expenses? {#reimbursement-duty}

**Short answer.** Arizona has no general statute requiring a private employer to reimburse an employee's business expenses. The Arizona wage-payment statute defines wages as nondiscretionary compensation due an employee in return for labor or services, and the definition does not name expense reimbursement. [^az-23-350-wages-definition] Three separate rules still put some costs on the employer. Under A.R.S. § 23-352, an employer may not withhold or divert any part of an employee's wages unless state or federal law requires or empowers it, the employee gave prior written authorization, or there is a reasonable good-faith dispute over the wages due. [^az-23-352-withholding-rule] For the Arizona minimum wage, the Industrial Commission's rule directs an employer to consider only monetary compensation in computing the minimum wage. [^az-r20-5-1206-monetary-only] Under federal law, the employee's cost of employer-required tools of the trade may not cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages due in a workweek. [^fed-531-35-tools-arizona-floor]

## Which business expenses must an Arizona employer reimburse? {#covered-expenses}

**Short answer.** Arizona has no general statute requiring private employers to reimburse employee business expenses. [^az-23-350-wages-covered] Some rules require an employer to pay for a named item. Under the general-industry OSHA standard, protective equipment used to comply with that part must be provided at no cost to employees, subject to listed exceptions such as non-specialty safety-toe footwear and non-specialty prescription safety eyewear that the employer permits employees to wear off the job site. [^osha-1910-132-ppe-arizona][^osha-1910-132-footwear-arizona] Arizona's drug-testing statute requires an employer that wants the benefits of that article to pay all actual costs of drug and alcohol impairment testing it requires of employees, but the article makes compliance voluntary. [^az-23-493-02-testing-costs][^az-23-493-08-voluntary] For other costs, the main limits are the wage floor and the withholding statute. Arizona requires employers to pay no less than the state minimum wage, and the Industrial Commission's rule directs that only monetary compensation, meaning cash or its equivalent, counts in computing it. [^az-23-363-minimum-wage-covered][^az-r20-5-1206-monetary-covered][^az-r20-5-1202-monetary-defined] Federal law bars a required tools-of-the-trade cost from cutting into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-arizona-covered] An employer that wants to recover a work cost from wages also needs one of the grounds in A.R.S. § 23-352. [^az-23-352-covered] In Tucson, the city ordinance adds that, except as required by law or court order, an employer may not deduct from wages if the deduction would leave the employee below the Tucson minimum wage, including deductions for employer-provided meals and damaged, lost or spoiled goods. [^tucson-17-83-deduction-floor-covered]

## Must an Arizona employer reimburse remote-work costs such as home internet or a personal phone? {#remote-work-costs}

**Short answer.** No Arizona statute requires a private employer to reimburse home internet, a personal phone or other remote-work costs, and the wage-payment statute's definition of wages does not name expense reimbursement. [^az-23-350-wages-remote] The Arizona minimum wage still applies to remote employees, and the Industrial Commission's rule requires an employer to pay at least the difference whenever an employee's combined wages fall below the minimum wage for a workweek. [^az-23-363-minimum-wage-remote][^az-r20-5-1206-shortfall-remote] Federal law applies the same kind of floor to required tools of the trade an employee buys. [^fed-531-35-tools-arizona-remote] Any deduction of a remote-work cost from wages must also satisfy A.R.S. § 23-352. [^az-23-352-remote]

## Can an offer letter or expense policy in Arizona make the employee bear business expenses? {#waiver}

**Short answer.** An Arizona offer letter, expense policy or employment contract cannot waive the state minimum wage: no verbal or written agreement or employment contract may waive any rights under Arizona's minimum-wage article. [^az-23-364-no-waiver] An expense policy therefore cannot leave an employee below the state minimum wage, and for work covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act it cannot let required tools-of-the-trade costs cut into the federal minimum or overtime wages. [^az-23-363-minimum-wage-waiver][^fed-531-35-tools-arizona-waiver] A policy that recovers a cost by deduction from wages needs a ground under A.R.S. § 23-352, such as the employee's prior written authorization. [^az-23-352-waiver] The employee may revoke that authorization in writing, and the employer may not keep withholding past the date the employee specifies unless the withholding resolves a debt or obligation to the employer or a court orders otherwise. [^az-23-352-revocation] Without an authorization, withholding is allowed only under state or federal law or during a reasonable good-faith dispute over the wages due, including an employer claim of reimbursement or set-off. [^az-23-352-waiver][^az-23-352-dispute-waiver] For work in Tucson, an employee's authorization does not permit a deduction that would leave the employee below the Tucson minimum wage, because the ordinance excepts only deductions required by law or court order. [^tucson-17-83-deduction-floor-waiver] Flagstaff's minimum-wage chapter likewise provides that no verbal or written agreement or employment contract may waive any rights under that chapter. [^flagstaff-15-01-no-waiver]

## What remedies does an Arizona employee have for an unlawful deduction or pay pushed below the minimum wage? {#deadlines-and-remedies}

**Short answer.** An Arizona employee may recover treble the unpaid wages in a civil action when an employer fails to pay wages due in violation of the wage-payment chapter. [^az-23-355-treble] Instead of suing, the employee may file a written claim with the Industrial Commission's labor department for unpaid wages of up to $12,000 within one year after the claim accrues. [^az-23-356-wage-claim] A civil action upon a liability created by statute, other than a penalty or forfeiture, must be brought within one year after the cause of action accrues, and the same one-year period applies to an action for breach of an oral or written employment contract, including a contract action based on an employee handbook or policy manual that does not specify a time period. [^az-12-541-statutory-liability] An employer that fails to pay the state minimum wage owes the balance with interest plus an additional amount equal to twice the underpaid wages, and a civil action to enforce the minimum-wage article may be brought within two years after the violation last occurs, or three years for a willful violation. [^az-23-364-twice-underpaid][^az-23-364-limitations]

## How does federal minimum-wage law limit unreimbursed expenses in Arizona? {#federal-floor}

**Short answer.** Under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, an employer that requires an employee to supply tools of the trade for the job violates the Fair Labor Standards Act in any workweek in which the employee's cost cuts into the federal minimum or overtime wages due. [^fed-531-35-tools-arizona-federal] The federal rule is a floor, not a general duty to reimburse. Arizona sets its own minimum wage, and the Industrial Commission's rule counts only monetary compensation toward it. [^az-23-363-minimum-wage-federal][^az-r20-5-1206-monetary-federal] A county, city or town may set a higher local minimum wage by ordinance but may not set one lower than the state minimum wage. [^az-23-364-local-minimum] Flagstaff and Tucson have their own minimum-wage ordinances: Flagstaff's covers an individual who works or is expected to work 25 or more hours in a calendar year within the city, and Tucson's bars deductions, other than those required by law or court order, that would leave the employee below the Tucson minimum wage. [^flagstaff-15-01-employee-coverage][^tucson-17-83-deduction-floor-federal]
