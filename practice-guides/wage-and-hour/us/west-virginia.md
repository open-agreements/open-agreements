# Wage and Hour Law in West Virginia[^about]

How West Virginia sets an $8.75 minimum wage for employers with six or more nonexempt employees at one location, requires weekly overtime, a 20-minute meal break and twice-monthly pay, and sets final-pay, deduction, tip-credit, worker-classification and wage-claim rules.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** West Virginia generally requires $8.75 an hour for nonexempt employees at a location with six or more nonexempt employees, while state-government employees are covered regardless of headcount. [^wv-minimum-wage-rate] [^wv-employer-six-employees] [^wv-dol-six-employee-rule] [^wv-state-employees-covered] The state rate is above the $7.25 federal rate. [^wv-federal-minimum-wage-rate] If the federal minimum wage ever equals or exceeds the state rate, the state rate rises to match it. [^wv-federal-rate-tracking] Outside state government, the state rate applies only to an employer that employs six or more employees in any one separate, distinct and permanent location or business establishment during a calendar week. [^wv-employer-six-employees] The state minimum wage applies to everyone employed by the State of West Virginia and its agencies and departments, regardless of that definition or federal coverage. [^wv-state-employees-covered] The Division of Labor applies that count location by location, so employees of the same employer can be covered at one site and not at another. [^wv-dol-six-per-location] Where the six-employee count is not met, the federal minimum wage may still apply. [^wv-dol-six-employee-rule]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The state law's definition of employee leaves out several groups. It excludes, among others, federal employees, volunteers, outside and traveling salespeople, family members employed by a parent, child or spouse, bona fide professional, executive and administrative employees, on-the-job trainees, summer camp workers and people aged 62 or older who receive Social Security old-age or survivors benefits. [^wv-employee-exclusions] The Division of Labor states that employees in an excluded group are exempt from the state minimum wage. [^wv-dol-nonexempt]

An employer may pay an employee under 20 years old a training wage of at least $6.40 an hour, for a cumulative period of no more than 90 days, with an extra 90 days available for a new business. [^wv-training-wage] [^wv-training-wage-limits] That is a state-law allowance. For an employee also covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, the federal $4.25 youth rate may be paid only during the first 90 consecutive calendar days after the employee is first employed, so after that period the federal $7.25 minimum applies, including during any state-law extension. [^wv-federal-youth-wage] [^wv-federal-minimum-wage-rate]

An agreement by an employee to work for less than the applicable rate is unenforceable, and the employer is liable for the unpaid wages. [^wv-agreement-for-less]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** West Virginia requires overtime by the week, not by the day, but the state rule does not reach every employer. [^wv-overtime-forty-hours] [^wv-dol-overtime-eighty-percent] Where it applies, an employer may not employ an employee for more than 40 hours in a workweek unless the employee receives at least one and one-half times the regular rate for the excess hours, [^wv-overtime-forty-hours] although county and municipal governments may give compensatory time off instead. [^wv-comp-time-rate] There is no state daily overtime rule.
The state overtime rule does not reach an employer when 80 percent or more of its workforce is covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, so the Division of Labor expects most employers to be covered by state law for minimum wage and by federal law for overtime. [^wv-dol-overtime-eighty-percent] [^wv-dol-state-mw-federal-ot] The federal act requires time and one-half for hours over 40 in a workweek for covered employees, except as that section otherwise provides. [^wv-federal-overtime]

Some groups are excluded from the state maximum-hours rule alone, such as seasonal employees of a commercial whitewater outfitter or amusement park who work less than seven months a year. [^wv-seasonal-overtime-exclusion]

County and municipal employees may receive compensatory time off at no less than one and one-half hours for each overtime hour, [^wv-comp-time-rate] but only under a written agreement made before the work is performed and recorded in the employer's hours records, and only while the employee has not exceeded the accrual limit. [^wv-comp-time-agreement] The limit is 480 hours for public safety, emergency response or seasonal work and 240 hours for other work, and overtime beyond the limit must be paid in cash. [^wv-comp-time-limits]

Federal overtime exemptions and hours-worked rules are discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** West Virginia requires a meal break in some situations but not a rest break. [^wv-meal-break] During a workday of six or more hours, every employer must make available at least 20 minutes for meal breaks, at times the employer reasonably designates, and the statute applies that requirement where employees are not afforded necessary breaks or permitted to eat lunch while working. [^wv-meal-break] No state statute requires a separate rest break for adults.
For an employer covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, short rest periods that are given must be counted as hours worked, [^wv-federal-rest-periods-paid] while a meal period may be unpaid only if the employee is completely relieved from duty to eat. [^wv-federal-meal-relieved] Under the Division of Labor's minimum wage rule, as the Division describes it, a meal or rest break of 20 minutes or less must be paid, while a bona fide meal period, typically 30 minutes or longer, may be paid or unpaid at the employer's choice. [^wv-dol-short-breaks-paid]

Federal law separately requires an employer, subject to statutory exemptions, to provide reasonable break time, and a private place other than a bathroom, for an employee to express breast milk for a nursing child for one year after the child's birth. [^wv-federal-pump-break]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** West Virginia requires payment of earned wages by the next regular payday on which they would otherwise be due, whether the employer discharges the employee or the employee quits or resigns, subject to an exception for deferred fringe benefits governed by an agreement. [^wv-final-pay-next-payday] The same next-payday deadline applies when an employee is laid off or work stops because of a labor dispute. [^wv-final-pay-layoff] Fringe benefits that an agreement makes payable at a future date or on other ascertainable conditions are paid under the agreement rather than by the next payday. [^wv-final-pay-next-payday] If the employee asks for payment by mail, payment is made on the date the mailed payment is postmarked. [^wv-final-pay-by-mail]
An employer that fails to pay final wages on time owes the unpaid amount plus two times that amount as liquidated damages, [^wv-final-pay-liquidated-damages] subject to a safe harbor. If the employer, at separation or with the final paycheck, tells the employee in writing who its authorized representative is and where to send a written demand by e-mail and regular mail, the employee must make a written demand before seeking liquidated damages or attorney's fees, [^wv-safe-harbor-demand] and the employer then has seven calendar days to correct the underpayment. [^wv-safe-harbor-cure] Those liquidated damages are not available to an employee who claims to have been misclassified as exempt from overtime. [^wv-final-pay-liquidated-damages]

An employer may withhold from final wages up to the replacement cost of property worth more than $100 that the employer provided to the employee in the course of, and for use in, the employer's business and that the employee has not returned, but only if the employee signed a written agreement when the property was obtained, or signed and ratified an agreement covering property provided before the provision took effect, that itemizes the property with a replacement cost, states that it must be returned immediately on separation and includes the employee's acknowledgment that the replacement cost may be recovered from final wages, and only if the employer gives the employee written notice of the replacement cost and a demand for return within no more than 10 business days, by personal service at separation or as soon as practicable afterward by personal service or certified mail with return receipt requested. [^wv-final-pay-property-withholding] [^wv-final-pay-property-written-agreement] [^wv-final-pay-property-agreement] The employer must release the withheld wages if the property is returned in suitable condition by the deadline; uniforms returned within three years of issue are acceptable in their current condition, replacement tools are treated as the employee's property and are outside the rule, and the property rule does not apply to employees covered by a collective bargaining agreement. [^wv-final-pay-property-return] [^wv-final-pay-property-cba] An employee who objects in writing to the replacement cost by the return deadline can require the employer to place the disputed amount in an interest-bearing escrow account, but the employee forfeits that amount to the employer if the employee does not sue for it within three months. [^wv-final-pay-property-escrow]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** West Virginia generally requires employers other than railroad companies to pay employees at least twice a month, unless the Commissioner of Labor approves a special agreement permitting less frequent paydays. [^wv-twice-monthly-pay] [^wv-special-agreement] Every employer doing business in the state, other than a railroad, must settle with its employees at least twice every month, with no more than 19 days between paydays, unless a special agreement provides otherwise. [^wv-twice-monthly-pay] A special agreement must be filed with and approved by the Commissioner of Labor on a compelling showing of good cause, and even then employees must be paid in full at least once each calendar month on a regular schedule. [^wv-special-agreement]

Wages may be paid in cash, by check or money order, by payroll card or by direct deposit to an account the employee identifies. [^wv-payment-cash-check] [^wv-payment-methods] An employer that pays by payroll card must disclose the card's fees in writing, allow at least one free withdrawal or transfer each pay period and offer the option of direct deposit. [^wv-payroll-card] [^wv-payroll-card-option]

At hiring, an employer must tell employees in writing the rate of pay and the day, hour and place of payment. [^wv-notice-of-pay]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** West Virginia requires an itemized earnings statement on each payday. [^wv-earnings-statement-rule] Under the Division of Labor's wage payment rule, the statement must show the hourly rate or salary, the hours being paid, any overtime rate, any bonus and incentive pay and itemized deductions, and it may be on paper or electronic if the employee has direct, immediate and convenient electronic access. [^wv-earnings-statement-rule] The statute itself requires an itemized statement of the deductions made from wages for each pay period in which deductions are made. [^wv-deduction-statement]
Wages are paid less authorized deductions and authorized wage assignments. [^wv-deduction-twice-monthly] The statute defines deductions to include amounts the law requires to be withheld and amounts authorized for union, labor organization or club dues or fees, pension plans, payroll savings plans, credit unions, charities and insurance the employer offers, but for public employees, other than certain municipal employees under a collective bargaining agreement, deductions do not include union, labor organization or club dues or fees. [^wv-deductions-definition] An assignment of future wages must be in writing, last no more than one year, state the total amount due, leave three-fourths of the employee's periodic wages exempt, apart from a separate rule for repaying state-employee overpayments, [^wv-wage-assignment-state-overpayment] and carry the employer's written acceptance, though a private employer and its employees may agree between themselves on payroll deductions. [^wv-wage-assignment]

An employer may withhold the replacement cost of unreturned employer-provided property from final wages only under the written-agreement and notice conditions described under [final pay](#final-pay). [^wv-deduction-property] [^wv-deduction-property-agreement] [^wv-deduction-property-notice]

For an employer covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, an employee's cost of tools of the trade the employer requires violates federal law in any workweek in which it cuts into the minimum or overtime wages. [^wv-federal-tools-cut-into-wages]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** For wage payment, workers' compensation, unemployment and Human Rights Act purposes, West Virginia uses a statutory multi-factor test, and applies the test in Internal Revenue Service Revenue Ruling 87-41 to workers who do not meet it. [^wv-contractor-written-contract] [^wv-contractor-irs-fallback] Under the statutory test, a person is an independent contractor if the person signs a written independent-contractor contract containing specified acknowledgments, [^wv-contractor-written-contract] has filed or is contractually required to file business or self-employment tax returns for the fees, or works through a business entity or a registered sole proprietorship, [^wv-contractor-tax-entity] actually controls the manner and means of the work, [^wv-contractor-manner-means] and meets three or more of the listed criteria, [^wv-contractor-control] or if the person qualifies as a direct seller under the Internal Revenue Code. [^wv-contractor-direct-seller] A worker who does not meet those criteria is classified under the test in Internal Revenue Service Revenue Ruling 87-41. [^wv-contractor-irs-fallback]

The wage payment article defines employee to include any person suffered or permitted to work, except a person classified as an independent contractor under that statute. [^wv-wage-payment-employee] The minimum wage article defines employ as to hire or permit to work, [^wv-employ-hire-or-permit] and federal law defines employ to include to suffer or permit to work. [^wv-federal-suffer-or-permit] For federal minimum wage and overtime, the economic-realities test asks whether the worker is economically dependent on the potential employer for work or is in business for themself. [^wv-federal-economic-realities]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** West Virginia allows a tip credit of 70 percent of the minimum wage. In deciding whether an employer has paid the minimum wage and overtime, the employer receives a credit of 70 percent of the hourly rate for an employee customarily receiving gratuities, and the employer must give the Commissioner of Labor documentary evidence on request that the employee receives at least 70 percent of the minimum wage in tips. [^wv-tip-credit-seventy] [^wv-tip-credit-evidence] The Division of Labor states that this lets an employer pay a tipped employee a cash wage of $2.62 an hour, as long as the cash wage plus reported tips equals $8.75 an hour. [^wv-dol-tipped-cash-wage] The reduced cash wage applies only to eligible tipped hours: the Division states that an employee who spends more than 20 percent of a workweek on duties for which the employee does not receive tips must be paid the full $8.75 for that time, [^wv-dol-twenty-percent-rule] and that a dual job employee must be paid the full $8.75 for all hours worked in a non-tipped job. [^wv-dol-dual-job] The Division also states that the employer must have written tip records completed by the employee to take the credit, and without them must pay the full $8.75 minimum wage. [^wv-dol-tip-records]

The same section allows a reasonable credit for board and lodging furnished to an employee. [^wv-tip-credit-seventy]

For an employer covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act, an employer may not keep employees' tips, including by letting managers or supervisors keep any portion, whether or not it takes a tip credit. [^wv-federal-tip-retention]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employee may sue, and the Commissioner of Labor may sue on the employee's request, under both the wage payment article and the minimum wage article. [^wv-wage-payment-suit] [^wv-minimum-wage-suit] Under either article, a court that awards judgment to the employee may assess costs, including reasonable attorney fees, against the employer. [^wv-wage-payment-fees] [^wv-minimum-wage-fees] A late final paycheck carries liquidated damages of two times the unpaid amount, [^wv-enforcement-liquidated-damages] but where the employer gave the required notice, the employee must first make a written demand before seeking liquidated damages or attorney's fees for final pay, [^wv-enforcement-safe-harbor-demand] and the employer has seven calendar days to cure. [^wv-enforcement-safe-harbor-cure]
In an action under West Virginia's minimum wage and maximum hours article, recovery is limited to wages that should have been paid within the two years before the action began, although that limit does not restrict recovery on a contract of employment. [^wv-minimum-wage-two-years]

If an employer refuses for five days to pay wages at the intervals the pay-frequency statute requires, and the employee sues, judgment is entered for the unpaid amount with legal interest until paid. [^wv-refusal-five-days]
