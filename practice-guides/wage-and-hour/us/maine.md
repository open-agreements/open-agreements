# Wage and Hour Law in Maine[^about]

How Maine sets a $15.10 minimum wage indexed to inflation, requires weekly overtime after 40 hours, requires a 30-minute break after six consecutive hours, allows a 50 percent tip credit, and sets final-pay, pay-frequency, pay-statement, deduction, worker-classification and wage-claim rules.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Maine's minimum wage is $15.10 an hour in 2026. [^me-minimum-wage-2027-announcement] Since January 1, 2021, the rate has risen each January 1 by the increase in the cost of living, measured by the Northeast consumer price index for urban wage earners and rounded to the nearest 5 cents. [^me-minimum-wage-annual-cost-of-living] If the federal minimum wage rises above the Maine rate, the Maine rate rises to match it on the same date. [^me-minimum-wage-annual-cost-of-living] The federal minimum wage, which binds employers covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, is $7.25 an hour. [^me-federal-minimum-wage-rate] The Maine Department of Labor has announced that the state rate rises to $15.70 an hour on January 1, 2027. [^me-minimum-wage-2027-announcement]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The minimum wage subchapter reaches any individual an employer suffers or permits to work, but it exempts several groups. [^me-employee-definition-exemptions] The exemptions include agricultural workers, other than workers at a farm with more than 300,000 laying birds. [^me-employee-definition-exemptions] They also include home workers who make and sell their own articles without supervision, and family members who live with and depend on the employer. [^me-fishing-homeworker-family-exemptions]

Agricultural workers now have a separate minimum wage. Beginning January 1, 2026, an agricultural employer must pay at least $14.65 an hour, increased by the change in the same price index from August 2024 to August 2025. [^me-agricultural-minimum-wage] The Department of Labor describes agricultural workers as entitled to the state minimum wage under that 2025 law. [^me-agricultural-workers-state-minimum]

Maine does not allow subminimum wages for workers with disabilities. An employer may not pay less than the minimum wage because a person has a mental or physical disability, and a state or federal certificate authorizing a lower wage has no effect. [^me-no-subminimum-wage-disability] An employer cannot avoid the minimum wage subchapter by a special contract with an employee. [^me-no-contract-waiver-minimum-wage]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Maine requires overtime at one and one-half times the regular hourly rate for all hours actually worked over 40 in a week. [^me-overtime-forty-hours-regular-rate] No Maine statute requires daily overtime. The regular rate includes earnings, bonuses, commissions and other pay based on work performed, and excludes the sums left out of the federal regular rate. [^me-overtime-forty-hours-regular-rate]

Executive, administrative and professional employees are exempt only if they are salaried and their regular pay, converted to an annual rate, exceeds 3,000 times the state minimum wage or the federal salary level, whichever is higher. [^me-salaried-exemption-3000-times] At the 2026 minimum wage of $15.10, that is more than $45,300 a year. [^me-minimum-wage-2026-rate-overtime] [^me-salaried-exemption-3000-times] The Department of Labor has announced that the threshold rises to $905.79 a week on January 1, 2027. [^me-salary-threshold-2027] The federal salary level for those exemptions is generally $684 a week. [^me-federal-white-collar-salary-684]

The overtime rule does not apply to mariners, to most public employees outside the executive and judicial branches of state government, or to the canning, processing, preserving, freezing, drying, marketing, storing, packing for shipment or distributing of agricultural produce, meat and fish products and perishable foods, although workers at an egg processing facility with more than 300,000 laying birds must be paid overtime. [^me-overtime-exemptions-list] It also excludes certain drivers and driver's helpers who are not paid hourly and are subject to federal motor carrier rules. [^me-overtime-driver-exemption] An employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act must still pay overtime after 40 hours in a workweek unless a federal exemption applies. [^me-federal-overtime-forty-hours]

Separately, an employer may not require an employee to work more than 80 hours of overtime in any two consecutive weeks. [^me-mandatory-overtime-80-hour-cap] That limit has exceptions, including work in an emergency declared by the Governor and work by employees who perform essential services for the public. [^me-mandatory-overtime-exceptions]

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Maine generally requires a 30-minute break after six consecutive hours of work. Unless a collective bargaining agreement or other written agreement provides otherwise, an employee may not work more than six consecutive hours without the opportunity to take at least 30 consecutive minutes of rest, except in an emergency involving danger to property, life, public safety or public health. [^me-rest-break-six-hours-thirty-minutes] The break may be unpaid mealtime only if the employee is completely relieved of duty. [^me-rest-break-six-hours-thirty-minutes] No Maine statute requires separate short paid rest breaks.

The break rule does not apply where fewer than three employees are on duty at one time and the nature of the work allows frequent, shorter paid breaks during the day. [^me-rest-break-small-business-exception] A violation is a civil violation with a forfeiture of $100 to $500 for each violation, and discharging or discriminating against an employee for complaining about a violation to the director, the district attorney or the Attorney General is also a civil violation with a forfeiture of $100 to $500. [^me-rest-break-penalty-retaliation]

An employer must provide adequate unpaid break time, or let the employee use paid break or meal time, to express breast milk for up to three years after childbirth, and must make reasonable efforts to provide a private location other than a bathroom. [^me-nursing-break-three-years]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** An employee who leaves employment, whether by quitting or by discharge, must be paid in full no later than the next established payday. [^me-final-pay-next-established-payday] An employer that violates the final-pay rule owes the unpaid wages and accrued vacation pay, plus a reasonable rate of interest, an additional amount equal to twice those unpaid wages and vacation pay as liquidated damages, and costs of suit including a reasonable attorney's fee. [^me-final-pay-double-liquidated-damages]

From final wages, an employer may withhold an overpayment only as the overcompensation statute allows, and may deduct a loan or advance against future wages only if the employee signed a written statement evidencing it. [^me-final-pay-next-established-payday] When the employee quits voluntarily, the employer may deduct from wages due the full recoverable overpayment made through employer error, limited to overcompensation paid during the three years before discovery. [^me-overcompensation-five-percent-limit]

Vacation pay owed under the terms of employment or the employer's established practice has the same status as wages on separation. Unused paid vacation accrued on and after January 1, 2023 under the employer's policy must be paid out, unless the employer has 10 or fewer employees or is a public employer, and a collective bargaining agreement that addresses vacation payout controls instead. [^me-final-pay-vacation-payout]

In a wage suit under this subchapter, an employer may not set off or counterclaim for damage the employee allegedly caused to the employer's property or for money the employee allegedly owes, subject to the statutory exceptions for overcompensation and documented loans or advances, though it keeps the right to sue for that money separately. [^me-no-setoff-wage-action] The final-pay statute covers any person who performs services for compensation other than an independent contractor. [^me-final-pay-employee-definition] The Law Court has held that its coverage does not depend on the salaried-employee exemption that limits the pay-frequency statute. [^me-madore-final-pay-salaried-employees]

When a business is sold, the seller must pay employees their earned wages within two weeks after the sale, unless the buyer agrees in a specific agreement to pay those wages and honor earned vacation. [^me-sale-of-business-two-weeks]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Subject to statutory exceptions, Maine employers must pay wages earned at regular intervals of no more than 16 days, and each payment must include all wages earned up to eight days before the payment date. [^me-pay-frequency-sixteen-days] The rule excludes family members who live with and depend on the employer, and salaried employees described in the executive, administrative and professional exemption of the minimum wage law. [^me-pay-frequency-sixteen-days] [^me-pay-frequency-family-salaried-definitions] [^me-pay-frequency-sixteen-days]

Wages must be paid on an established day or date at regular intervals made known to the employee, and the interval may not be lengthened without at least 30 days' written notice. [^me-pay-day-established-notice] An employer may lower a rate of pay effective the next working day only after notifying the affected employees. [^me-pay-rate-decrease-notice] Employees of a school administrative unit, or of a school in an unorganized territory, who work the school year schedule may, by written agreement, be paid for the school year over 12 months or a shorter period. [^me-school-personnel-twelve-month-pay] A municipal fire department may pay volunteer firefighters at intervals of up to six months. [^me-volunteer-firefighter-six-months] An employer may not charge a fee for paying wages by direct deposit. [^me-no-direct-deposit-fee]

Every employer must keep a daily record of the time each employee works, except exempt salaried employees. [^me-payroll-daily-time-records] Violating the pay-frequency, recordkeeping or final-pay rules carries a fine of $100 to $500 for each violation. [^me-wage-payment-fines]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** With each payment of wages, an employer subject to the minimum wage subchapter must give the employee a statement that clearly shows the date of the pay period, the hours, total earnings and itemized deductions. [^me-pay-statement-contents-records] An employer paying by direct deposit must provide the same information when the transfer is made, and an electronic statement must be accessible and printable at no cost to the employee. [^me-pay-statement-direct-deposit] The employer must keep records of hours worked and wages paid for at least three years. [^me-pay-statement-contents-records]

Maine limits what an employer may take back from wages. Outside agricultural work and work in or about a private home, an employer may not require an employee, as a condition of employment, to return part of the employee's pay, except for a loan, debt or advance, merchandise bought from the employer, sick or accident benefits or life or group insurance premiums (not compensation insurance) that the employee agreed to pay, or rent, light or water for company housing. [^me-unfair-agreements-return-wages] A debt, for this purpose, must benefit the employee. It does not include cash shortages, inventory shortages, dishonored checks or credit cards, damage to the employer's property, or uniforms, protective equipment and tools of the trade that mainly benefit the employer. [^me-debt-excludes-shortages-uniforms] An employer is liable to the employee for any amount returned in violation of these limits. [^me-unfair-agreements-liability]

An employer that overpays an employee through its own error may not withhold more than 5 percent of the net amount of any later paycheck without the employee's written permission, except that it may deduct the full overpayment from wages due when the employee voluntarily quits, and may recover overpayments only from the three years before discovery. [^me-overcompensation-five-percent-limit-deductions]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** Maine uses a single statutory employment standard to decide whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor, and the Maine Department of Labor applies it to wage and hour coverage as well as unemployment insurance and workers' compensation. [^me-dol-common-employment-standard] Under that standard, services for pay are employment unless the worker is free from the essential direction and control of the business and meets all five listed criteria plus at least three of seven others. [^me-unemployment-independent-contractor-test] The employment-practices subchapter that contains the misclassification penalty defines an independent contractor by cross-reference to the same test. [^me-independent-contractor-cross-reference]
The minimum wage subchapter defines employ as to suffer or permit to work, and the final-pay statute covers any person who performs services for compensation but excludes an independent contractor. [^me-employ-suffer-or-permit] [^me-final-pay-excludes-independent-contractor]

The five required criteria are that the worker has the essential right to control the means and progress of the work except as to final results, is customarily engaged in an independently established business, has the opportunity for profit and loss, hires and pays any assistants and supervises the work of assistants who are employees, and makes services available to a client or customer community even if that right is voluntarily not exercised or is temporarily restricted. [^me-unemployment-five-required-criteria] The seven further criteria include a substantive investment in tools and materials, freedom to work for others, responsibility for satisfactory completion with possible contractual responsibility for failing to complete the work, a contract that defines the relationship and gives rights if the other party terminates it before completion, payment based on factors related to the work and not solely on time spent, work outside the usual course of the business, and an Internal Revenue Service determination of independent contractor status. [^me-unemployment-three-of-seven-criteria]

An employer that intentionally or knowingly misclassifies an employee as an independent contractor commits a civil violation with a fine of $2,000 to $10,000 for each violation, and a misclassification finding can lead to penalties under the unemployment and workers' compensation laws. [^me-misclassification-civil-fine]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Maine permits a tip credit of up to half of the minimum wage. [^me-tip-credit-fifty-percent] An employer may count tips toward the wages of a service employee, but the tip credit may not exceed 50 percent of the minimum wage. [^me-tip-credit-fifty-percent] In 2026 the minimum direct wage for a service employee is $7.55 an hour, rising to $7.85 an hour on January 1, 2027. [^me-tip-wage-2027-announcement] The employer must be able to show that direct wages and tips together reach at least the minimum wage within the seven-day workweek, and must make up any shortfall. [^me-tip-credit-fifty-percent]

A service employee is an employee in an occupation who customarily and regularly receives more than a monthly amount in tips that was $175 in 2023 and rises each January 1 with the cost of living. [^me-service-employee-tip-threshold] [^me-service-employee-tip-indexing] The Department of Labor reports that the threshold is $191 a month in 2026 and rises to $199 a month in 2027. [^me-tip-wage-2027-announcement]

Before taking the credit, the employer must tell the employee, orally or in writing, the direct wage, the amount of tips credited, that the credit cannot exceed tips actually received, that the employee keeps all tips except under a valid tip pool, that the credit does not apply to an employee who has not been informed, and any required tip-pool contribution. [^me-tip-credit-advance-notice] Tips belong to the employee and may not be shared with the employer. Tips charged to a credit card must be paid by the next regular payday, without deducting card processing fees. [^me-tips-property-of-employee]

A tip pool that does not violate the federal Fair Labor Standards Act is allowed among service employees when the employer takes the tip credit, or among a group of employees all paid the full minimum wage when it does not. The employer, supervisors and managers may not receive tips from the pool. [^me-tip-pooling-rules] In a banquet or private club setting, an employer that adds a service charge must tell the customer the charge is not a tip for service employees. [^me-banquet-service-charge-notice]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** Maine employees may sue to recover unpaid wages under the wage payment subchapter. [^me-department-of-labor-may-sue] An employer is liable for unpaid wages under the wage payment subchapter, and a judgment for the employee must include the unpaid wages, a reasonable rate of interest, costs of suit including a reasonable attorney's fee, and an additional amount equal to twice the unpaid wages as liquidated damages. [^me-unpaid-wages-double-liquidated-damages] For unpaid minimum wages, the minimum wage subchapter separately provides the unpaid wages, an equal additional amount as liquidated damages, and costs including a reasonable attorney's fee. [^me-minimum-wage-equal-liquidated-damages]

Wage payment remedies become available eight days after the due date when wages are clearly due without a bona fide dispute. When there is a bona fide dispute, they become available eight days after a demand for wages that are in fact due and remain unpaid. [^me-remedies-eight-days-after-due] The Department of Labor may also sue for unpaid wages, supervise and collect the judgment for the employee, and collect fines. [^me-department-of-labor-may-sue]

The Director of the Bureau of Labor Standards may inspect and copy an employer's wage records, and on a written complaint has authority to enter the place of employment for that purpose. [^me-director-inspect-records] A violation of the minimum wage subchapter, and retaliation against an employee who complains to the director or county attorney, are punishable by a fine of $50 to $200. [^me-minimum-wage-penalties-retaliation]
