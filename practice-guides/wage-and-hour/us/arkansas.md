# Wage and Hour Law in Arkansas[^about]

How Arkansas sets an $11.00 minimum wage for employers with four or more employees, requires weekly overtime after 40 hours, allows a tip credit down to a $2.63 cash wage, requires semimonthly pay from corporations, and sets final-pay, deduction, worker-classification and wage-claim rules.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Arkansas's minimum wage is $11.00 an hour for covered employees of employers with four or more employees. [^ar-minimum-wage-eleven-dollars] [^ar-four-employee-coverage-threshold] The 2018 ballot measure known as Issue 5 amended Arkansas Code § 11-4-210(a) to require every employer to pay each employee at least $11.00 an hour beginning January 1, 2021. [^ar-minimum-wage-eleven-dollars] Arkansas voters approved the measure on November 6, 2018. [^ar-voters-approved-wage-increase] The state rate is above the federal minimum wage of $7.25/hr, and the federal Fair Labor Standards Act does not excuse compliance with a higher state minimum wage. [^ar-federal-minimum-wage-rate] [^ar-flsa-higher-state-wage-savings]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The Arkansas Minimum Wage Act does not reach a small employer. Its definition of employer excludes any employer for a workweek in which it employs fewer than four employees, and exempt employees count toward the four. [^ar-four-employee-coverage-threshold] An employer below that threshold may still be covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act and owe the federal minimum wage of $7.25/hr. [^ar-federal-minimum-wage-rate]

The Act also does not apply to bona fide executive, administrative or professional employees or to outside commission-paid salespeople, and the Department of Labor and Licensing's rules define those exemptions by incorporating the federal regulations at 29 C.F.R. part 541 as they stood on July 1, 2005. [^ar-white-collar-exemption-rule]

Every covered employer must keep payroll records for each employee showing, among other items, the hours worked each workday and each workweek, the deductions from wages and the total wages paid each pay period, and must keep those records for at least three years. [^ar-payroll-records-contents] [^ar-payroll-records-three-years]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Arkansas requires covered employers to pay covered employees overtime at one and one-half times the regular rate of pay for all hours worked over 40 in a week. [^ar-overtime-forty-hours-weekly] The overtime statute measures hours by the week, and no Arkansas statute requires daily overtime. An employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act owes the same weekly overtime under federal law unless a federal exemption applies. [^ar-federal-overtime-forty-hours]

The executive, administrative and professional exemption removes an employee from both the minimum wage and overtime rules. [^ar-overtime-white-collar-exemption] For charitable and religious organizations and employers with gross annual sales under $500,000, the rules set the salary level for those exemptions at $360 a week. [^ar-small-employer-salary-level]

Under the rules on hours worked, short rest periods of about five to 20 minutes count as hours worked, while a bona fide meal period during which the employee is completely relieved from duty is not work time. [^ar-overtime-rest-periods-hours-worked]

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** No Arkansas statute sets a general meal-period or rest-break requirement for adult employees of private employers. The Department of Labor and Licensing's minimum wage rules instead govern pay for breaks an employer chooses to give. Rest periods of about five to 20 minutes must be counted as hours worked, and that paid time may not be offset against other compensable time. [^ar-breaks-rest-periods-paid] A bona fide meal period is not work time if the employee is completely relieved from duty, and 30 minutes or more is ordinarily long enough. [^ar-breaks-meal-period-relieved]

Arkansas does have narrower break statutes for particular workers. For example, each school district must give each classified school employee who works more than 20 hours a week at least two paid 15-minute breaks during each regular workday. [^ar-school-classified-paid-breaks]

An employee who must perform any duties while eating, such as an office worker required to eat at a desk, is not relieved from duty and is working during the meal. The employee need not be allowed to leave the premises if otherwise completely freed from duties. [^ar-breaks-eating-at-desk-is-work]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** An Arkansas employer that discharges an employee must pay all wages due by the next regular payday. [^ar-final-pay-next-regular-payday] An employer that does not pay within seven days after that next regular payday owes the employee double the wages due. [^ar-final-pay-double-wages] Act 853 of 2019 put these rules into Arkansas Code § 11-4-405. [^ar-final-pay-double-wages]

The statute addresses an employer that discharges an employee and does not separately address an employee who resigns. [^ar-final-pay-next-regular-payday]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Subject to a monthly-pay exception for certain highly paid exempt managers, a corporation doing business in Arkansas that employs salespersons, mechanics, laborers or other workers must pay their wages at least semimonthly. Under subsection (c) of § 11-4-401, a corporation with annual gross income of $500,000 or more may pay management-level and executive employees who are exempt from federal minimum wage and overtime and paid more than $25,000 a year at least once each calendar month. [^ar-semimonthly-pay-corporations] [^ar-monthly-pay-exempt-managers] As amended by Act 475 of 2017, the statute requires payment no less frequently than semimonthly, so an employer may pay more often, such as weekly or every two weeks. [^ar-semimonthly-pay-corporations]

An employer may pay wages by automatic deposit or by a debit card preloaded with the amount of wages. [^ar-direct-deposit-paycard] If it uses a preloaded debit card, at least one free withdrawal must be available for each deposit of wages. [^ar-paycard-free-withdrawal] An employee may opt out of electronic direct deposit by giving the employer a written statement requesting payment by check. [^ar-direct-deposit-opt-out]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** No Arkansas statute requires a private employer to give employees an itemized pay statement. The Arkansas Minimum Wage Act rules require an employer to keep payroll records of each employee's deductions and total wages paid each pay period. [^ar-records-deductions-each-period] The rules limit deductions from the minimum wage and overtime wages to deductions authorized by the rules, deductions authorized or required by law, and deductions not otherwise prohibited that are for the employee's benefit and authorized by the employee in writing. [^ar-deductions-three-permitted-kinds]

An employer may not deduct from the applicable minimum wage for spoilage or breakage, cash or inventory shortages, or fines for lateness, misconduct or quitting without notice. [^ar-deductions-prohibited-items] A payment the employer is legally obliged by court order to make to a third party for the employee, such as a wage garnishment or child support withholding order, counts as payment to the employee, provided neither the employer nor anyone acting on its behalf or in its interest derives any profit or benefit from the transaction. [^ar-deductions-court-ordered-payments]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** Arkansas decides whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor under its Minimum Wage Act by applying the factors in the Empower Independent Contractors Act. The Minimum Wage Act excludes bona fide independent contractors, and the Department of Labor and Licensing's rules require contractor status to be determined by the factors the Empower Independent Contractors Act requires. [^ar-classification-empower-act-rule] As amended by Act 743 of 2025, that Act directs an employer or agency determining employment status to consider factors including whether the person for whom services are performed can require compliance with instructions on when, where and how the worker works, whether the worker must receive training, and whether the worker's services are integrated into the business and subject to its direction and control. [^ar-classification-control-training-integration]

The statute lists 20 factors in all. The last ones ask whether the person for whom services are performed keeps the right to discharge the worker and whether the worker may end the relationship at any time without liability. [^ar-classification-discharge-termination-factors]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Arkansas allows a tip credit, but the cash wage must be at least $2.63 an hour. [^ar-tip-credit-cash-wage-floor] With the $11.00 minimum wage, the maximum tip credit is therefore $8.37 an hour, and the employee's tips must actually make up the difference. [^ar-tipped-voters-approved-rate] [^ar-tip-question-eleven-dollar-rate]

The tip credit is available only in occupations where tips are customarily recognized as part of pay, such as waiters, bellhops, beauty operators and barbers who actually receive and keep tips; for any other occupation, tips must exceed $20 a month. [^ar-tip-credit-eligible-occupations] If an employer requires tips to be turned over to it as part of its gross receipts, the employee is not a tipped employee and must be paid the full minimum wage. [^ar-tip-credit-turnover-to-employer]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employee may sue an employer under the Arkansas Minimum Wage Act for unpaid minimum wages, including overtime wages. [^ar-private-action-minimum-overtime] The employee need not first exhaust administrative remedies. [^ar-no-exhaustion-required] The employer is liable for the unpaid wages and for costs and reasonable attorney's fees allowed by the court. [^ar-employer-liability-wages-fees] [^ar-enforcement-costs-attorney-fees] Liquidated damages, up to an amount equal to the unpaid wages, are available only if the employee proves the violation was willful. [^ar-liquidated-damages-willful-only] The statute of limitations for these claims is two years. [^ar-two-year-limitations-period]

The two-year period was added by Act 853 of 2019. Before that amendment, the Arkansas Supreme Court had held that the three-year limitations period for statutory liabilities applied to private actions under the Act. [^ar-former-three-year-limitations-holding] [^ar-two-year-limitations-period] Act 853 also requires each employee who joins a collective action to give written consent and file it in the court where the action is brought. [^ar-written-consent-to-join]

The Minimum Wage Act rules also provide for a civil money penalty of $50 to $1,000 for each violation, and each day a violation continues is a separate offense. [^ar-civil-money-penalty-range]
