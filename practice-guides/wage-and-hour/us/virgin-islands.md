# Wage and Hour Law in the U.S. Virgin Islands[^about]

How the U.S. Virgin Islands sets a $12.00 minimum wage scheduled to rise to $15.00, requires daily, weekly and sixth- and seventh-day overtime, mandates paid rest periods and meal periods, allows a $4.80 cash wage for tipped tourist-service and restaurant workers and enforces wage claims through the Department of Labor.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** The U.S. Virgin Islands minimum wage is $12.00 an hour, beginning April 24, 2026, according to the Virgin Islands Department of Labor's official minimum wage poster. [^vi-poster-minimum-wage] The Department's employee rights page gives the same rate. [^vi-dol-minimum-wage] Act No. 9069 amends 24 V.I.C. § 4(a) by replacing $8.35 with $12.00, $9.50 with $14.00, June 1, 2017 with June 1, 2027, $10.50 with $15.00 and June 1, 2018 with June 1, 2028, and by adding the words whichever is greater at the end of the subsection. [^vi-act-9069-minimum-wage] The amended text of § 4(a) could not be read on a free official host, so the pairing of the $14.00 rate with June 1, 2027 and the $15.00 rate with June 1, 2028 has not been checked against the Code text itself.
The federal Fair Labor Standards Act applies in the Virgin Islands, because it defines a State to include any territory or possession of the United States. [^vi-flsa-territory] The federal minimum wage under that Act is $7.25/hr an hour. [^vi-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The federal Act does not excuse an employer from paying a higher minimum wage set by territorial law, so an employer covered by both laws must pay the Virgin Islands rate. [^vi-flsa-higher-state-standard] The coverage provisions and exemptions of the Virgin Islands Wage and Hour Act, 24 V.I.C. chapter 1, could not be checked against the Code text on a free official host.

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Outside the tourist and restaurant industries, the Virgin Islands Department of Labor states that time and a half the regular rate is owed for work over 8 hours a day, over 40 hours a week and for any hours on the sixth or seventh consecutive day of work. [^vi-dol-overtime] [^vi-poster-overtime-variation] In the tourist and restaurant industries, the Department's poster says the sixth consecutive day is exempt from overtime if the employee does not exceed 40 hours that week, while overtime on the seventh consecutive day is still owed. [^vi-poster-overtime-variation] For employees covered by the federal Act, federal law separately requires time and a half after 40 hours in a workweek. [^vi-federal-overtime]

The Department's labor relations brochure describes the same rules, including the narrower sixth-day rule for the tourist service and restaurant industries. [^vi-brochure-overtime] These statements come from Department of Labor publications. The overtime section of the Virgin Islands Wage and Hour Act, and any exemptions from it, could not be checked against the Code text on a free official host.

Federal overtime exemptions and hours-worked rules are discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Unless a written contract or collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise, Virgin Islands employers must give paid rest periods and meal periods under 24 V.I.C. §§ 20a and 20b, added by Act No. 6843 of 2006. [^vi-break-contract-exception] [^vi-meal-period] Section 20a requires an employer to allow a paid rest period of at least 10 minutes within each four hours of continuous work. [^vi-rest-period] Section 20b requires an employer to give an employee who works a continuous period of seven hours or longer either an uninterrupted meal period of at least 30 minutes, relieved of all duties and beginning no later than five hours after work starts, or an on-duty meal period when the employer shows that the nature or circumstances of the work prevent relief from duty. [^vi-meal-period] An employee may voluntarily agree to give up a rest period or meal period, and the employer bears the burden of proving the agreement. [^vi-break-waiver]

If an employer fails to provide a rest period, the employee must make it up within the same workday or pay period, or the employer must pay one-half hour at the employee's regular rate for each missed rest period. [^vi-missed-rest-period] The rest-period rule does not apply to an employee who is 18 or older, works alone in a retail or service establishment and may leave the assigned station to use the restroom. [^vi-rest-period-exception] The Commissioner of Labor may grant an employer an exemption if business necessity precludes providing the benefit and the exemption would not materially affect employees' welfare or comfort. [^vi-break-exemptions] On the same two findings, the Commissioner may also exempt a defined category of employers by regulation, after a hearing on each application. [^vi-break-category-exemptions]

These rules are quoted from Act No. 6843 as passed in 2006, in the copy the Department of Labor publishes. [^vi-break-contract-exception] As of November 24, 2026, whether §§ 20a through 20c have since been amended could not be checked against the current Code text on a free official host.

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** No free official source reviewed for this guide sets a Virgin Islands final-pay deadline or late-pay penalty. The Code text of 24 V.I.C. chapter 1 could not be read on a free official host, so a Code rule could not be checked, and the Department of Labor publications reviewed do not address final pay. Under federal law, overtime earned in a workweek must generally be paid on the regular payday for the period in which that workweek ends. [^vi-federal-overtime-payday] When the correct amount cannot be determined until later, payment may not be delayed beyond the next payday after the computation can be made. [^vi-federal-overtime-delay]

Unpaid minimum wages or overtime owed at separation are recoverable under the federal Act, ordinarily with an additional equal amount as liquidated damages, [^vi-federal-liability] unless the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds for believing it was complying, in which case the court may reduce or deny them. [^vi-federal-good-faith]

The Department's brochure describes 24 V.I.C. § 472, which requires advance notice of a plant closing or mass layoff, and says the severance pay owed to affected employees, one week's pay for every year of service, is due no later than one pay period after the end of work. [^vi-brochure-severance]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** No free official source reviewed for this guide sets a Virgin Islands pay-frequency rule. The Code text of 24 V.I.C. chapter 1 could not be read on a free official host, so a Code rule could not be checked, and the Department of Labor publications reviewed do not address pay frequency. The federal Fair Labor Standards Act does not set a pay frequency, but overtime earned in a workweek must generally be paid on the regular payday for the period in which that workweek ends. [^vi-pf-federal-overtime-payday]

For employees covered by the federal Act, wages are not treated as paid unless they are paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear. [^vi-federal-free-and-clear]

## What records must employers keep, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** The Virgin Islands Department of Labor states that every employer must keep, at its place of business for three years, each employee's name, address and occupation, rate of pay, hours worked each day and each week, and the amount paid each pay period. [^vi-brochure-records] The Department's poster adds that these records must be made available to the Department on request. [^vi-poster-records] Whether the Virgin Islands Code requires employers to give employees a pay statement, and which deductions from wages it allows, could not be verified, because the text of 24 V.I.C. chapter 1 could not be read on a free official host.

For employees covered by the federal Act, wages are not treated as paid unless they are paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear. [^vi-ws-federal-free-and-clear]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** No free official source reviewed for this guide sets a Virgin Islands employee-versus-contractor test. The Code text of 24 V.I.C. chapter 1 could not be read on a free official host, so any definition in the Wage and Hour Act could not be checked. Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, to employ includes to suffer or permit to work, [^vi-federal-employ] and federal regulations decide employee status by looking at the economic realities of the relationship, asking whether the worker is economically dependent on the potential employer for work or in business for themself. [^vi-federal-economic-realities] That federal test governs federal coverage.

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Tipped employees in tourist services and restaurants may be paid $4.80 an hour in cash wages, according to the Virgin Islands Department of Labor's poster, and the employer must make sure the $12.00 minimum wage is paid if wages and tips fall short. [^vi-tip-poster] The Department's employee rights page states the same $4.80 direct-wage floor and $12.00 combined minimum. [^vi-dol-tipped-wage] The territorial provision behind the $4.80 rate could not be checked against the Code text on a free official host.

Under the federal Act, a covered employer may count tips toward the minimum wage only if it has informed the employee of the tip-credit rules and the employee keeps all tips, apart from a pool among employees who customarily receive tips. [^vi-federal-tip-credit-conditions] A tipped employee is one who customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips. [^vi-federal-tipped-employee] An employer may not keep employees' tips for any purpose, including letting managers or supervisors share them, whether or not it takes a tip credit. [^vi-federal-tips-kept] Because the federal Act does not excuse noncompliance with a higher territorial minimum wage, an employer covered by both laws must pay the higher territorial cash wage. [^vi-tip-flsa-higher-state-standard]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** The Virgin Islands Department of Labor states that an employer that pays an employee less than the applicable wage rate is liable to the employee for the full amount, and that an agreement to work for less is no defense. [^vi-dol-wage-claim] The Department takes these claims on its wage claim form. [^vi-dol-wage-claim] The Department's Division of Labor Relations investigates to enforce the wage laws, and an employer may be fined up to $2,500. [^vi-poster-enforcement] The Department's brochure says the $2,500 fine applies to an employer that willfully violates Title 24 or a regulation or order issued under it. [^vi-brochure-penalty]

The territorial provisions on private suits, damages beyond the unpaid wage, attorney's fees and limitation periods could not be checked against the Code text on a free official host. Under the federal Act, an employer that violates the minimum wage or overtime sections owes the unpaid wages plus an equal amount as liquidated damages, [^vi-enf-federal-liability] although a court may reduce or deny them if the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds for believing it was complying. [^vi-enf-federal-good-faith] A federal claim must be brought within two years, or three years for a willful violation. [^vi-federal-limitations]
