# Wage and Hour Law in North Dakota[^about]

How North Dakota sets a $7.25 minimum wage, requires weekly overtime after 40 hours, requires a meal period on longer shifts when two or more employees are on duty, and sets final-pay, pay-frequency, pay-statement, deduction, worker-classification, tip-credit and wage-claim rules.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** North Dakota's minimum wage is $7.25 an hour. Section 34-06-22 of the North Dakota Century Code requires every employer to pay each employee at least $7.25 an hour, effective July 24, 2009, except as the chapter and the labor commissioner's rules otherwise provide. [^nd-minimum-wage-seven-twenty-five] The state rate is fixed in the statute rather than tied to the federal rate, but the commissioner may adopt a rule setting a different state rate. [^nd-minimum-wage-rule-authority] An employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act must also pay the federal minimum wage, which is currently the same $7.25. [^nd-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

North Dakota's wage-and-hour chapter covers a broad set of employers, including the state and its political subdivisions. [^nd-employer-definition-includes-state] Its definition of employee excludes people in a ridesharing arrangement, people engaged in firefighting and sworn law enforcement officers of a political subdivision. [^nd-employee-definition-exclusions] The Minimum Wage and Work Conditions Order lists groups exempt from the minimum wage and its working conditions, including golf course caddies [^nd-exemption-golf-caddies] and a person babysitting on a casual basis for fewer than 20 hours a week for less than three consecutive weeks. [^nd-exemption-golf-caddies] [^nd-minimum-wage-order-exemptions] An individual who provides family home care, as the statute defines it, is exempt from the chapter's minimum wage and hour standards. [^nd-companionship-family-home-care-exemption] Family home care means providing room, board, supervisory care and personal services to an eligible elderly or disabled person by the person's spouse or by a listed relative, such as a parent, adult child or adult sibling, or that relative's current or former spouse. [^nd-family-home-care-definition]

The commissioner may license a subminimum wage for a worker whose productive capacity is impaired by disability and for a student or learner in a career and technical education program. [^nd-special-license-subminimum-wage] A vocational education student's subminimum wage may not fall below 85 percent of the current state minimum wage. [^nd-student-subminimum-eighty-five-percent]

A city, county, school district or other political subdivision may not set a living wage mandate higher than the state minimum wage. [^nd-living-wage-mandate-preemption]

An employer may count the reasonable value of board, lodging and other facilities, not exceeding its actual cost, as wages, up to $18 a day, only under a written agreement and only if the employee's acceptance is in fact voluntary. [^nd-board-lodging-eighteen-dollars] An employer may require an employee to buy uniforms only if the cost does not bring the employee's wage below the minimum wage for all hours worked in that pay period. [^nd-uniform-cost-minimum-wage]

Every employer must keep a register of the names of all its employees and let the commissioner inspect it on request. [^nd-employee-register] The employer must post the commissioner's summary of the wage-and-hour rules in a conspicuous, commonly frequented area where employees work. [^nd-posting-rule-summary]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** North Dakota generally requires overtime pay at one and one-half times the regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a week. [^nd-overtime-forty-hours-weekly] Overtime is computed weekly regardless of the length of the pay period, and hours may not be averaged over the pay period or used to offset shorter weeks; paid holidays, paid time off and sick leave do not count toward overtime hours. [^nd-overtime-weekly-computation] An employer must generally count all hours an employee works in more than one job under its control. [^nd-overtime-multiple-jobs] For a state or political subdivision employee who, solely at the employee's option, works occasionally or sporadically part time for the same agency in a different capacity, the hours in the different jobs are not combined for overtime. [^nd-public-occasional-sporadic-conditions] [^nd-public-occasional-sporadic-not-combined]

Two industries have different overtime periods. Taxicab drivers must be paid overtime for hours over 50 in a week. [^nd-overtime-taxicab-fifty-hours] A hospital or residential care establishment may, by agreement with its employees, use a 14-day overtime period if it pays time and one-half for hours over eight in a day or 80 in the 14-day period. [^nd-overtime-hospital-fourteen-day]

The order exempts listed employees from overtime, including bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees and agricultural workers. [^nd-overtime-exemptions-list] Other exempt groups include live-in domestic workers, [^nd-overtime-live-in-domestic] outside salespeople whose unrelated work does not exceed 20 percent of weekly hours, [^nd-overtime-outside-sales] teachers [^nd-overtime-teachers] and highly compensated employees. [^nd-overtime-highly-compensated-exempt] A computer professional is exempt if the professional exercises discretion and independent judgment in designing, developing, analyzing, testing or modifying programs, or is paid at least $27.63 an hour. [^nd-overtime-computer-professional] A highly compensated employee is one paid total annualized compensation of $100,000 or more, including at least $455 a week on a salary or fee basis, whose primary duty includes office or nonmanual work. [^nd-highly-compensated-employee] The order interprets the salary or fee basis used in those executive, administrative and professional exemptions according to the federal Fair Labor Standards Act. [^nd-salary-basis-follows-flsa]

The state and its political subdivisions may provide compensatory time and a work period for overtime calculation if they comply with the Fair Labor Standards Act and the federal Department of Labor's rules. [^nd-public-employer-compensatory-time]

The order also governs what counts as hours worked. Standby time on the employer's premises, or being on call in an engaged-to-wait manner, is compensable work time, while waiting to be engaged need not be paid. [^nd-on-call-work-time] Travel from jobsite to jobsite, or from office to jobsite, is compensable work time. [^nd-travel-jobsite-work-time]

A retail business may not require an employee to work seven consecutive days and must allow at least 24 consecutive hours off for rest or worship in each seven-day period. [^nd-retail-day-of-rest]

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** North Dakota requires a meal period of at least 30 minutes in each shift longer than five hours when two or more employees are on duty, unless the employee and employer agree to waive it. [^nd-meal-period-two-employees-on-duty] [^nd-meal-period-waiver-unpaid] The meal period may be unpaid if the employee is completely relieved of duties and the period is ordinarily 30 minutes long, and an employee who must perform any duties during the period is not completely relieved. [^nd-meal-period-waiver-unpaid] A collective bargaining agreement prevails over the meal-period rule. [^nd-meal-period-collective-bargaining]

A shift of five hours or less, or a shift on which only one employee is on duty, does not trigger the state meal-period requirement. [^nd-meal-period-two-employees-on-duty] The review for this guide of chapters 34-06 and 34-14 of the Century Code and the Minimum Wage and Work Conditions Order found no provision requiring separate rest breaks for adults.

When an employee is on duty for 24 hours or more, the employer and employee may agree to exclude bona fide meal periods and regularly scheduled sleeping periods of up to eight hours from hours worked, if the employer furnishes adequate sleeping facilities and the employee can usually sleep without interruption. [^nd-twenty-four-hour-sleep-exclusion] Only eight hours may be deducted for a longer sleeping period, an interruption for a call to duty counts as hours worked, and the entire period counts as work time if interruptions prevent a reasonable night's sleep. [^nd-twenty-four-hour-sleep-limits]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** In North Dakota, final wages are due on the next regular payday. Whether an employee is discharged, quits or is suspended because of an industrial dispute, the unpaid wages become due at the regular paydays the employer set in advance for the periods worked. [^nd-final-pay-regular-payday] When the employer discharges or terminates the employee, it must pay those wages by certified mail to an address the employee designates, or as both parties otherwise agree. [^nd-final-pay-certified-mail] If the employer does not pay on time, the employee may collect wages at the contract rate for each day the employer is in default until it pays in full, for up to 30 days. [^nd-final-pay-daily-wage-penalty]

When the amount is disputed, the employer must give the employee written notice of the amount it concedes is due, less what the employee owes the employer, and pay that amount without condition within the statutory time. Accepting the payment does not release the rest of the claim. [^nd-undisputed-wages-unconditional]

Earned paid time off is treated as wages at separation. Under the Minimum Wage and Work Conditions Order, paid time off that is available for use at separation must be paid at the employee's regular rate, and no contract or policy may provide for forfeiture of earned paid time off on separation. [^nd-paid-time-off-wages-at-separation] A use-it-or-lose-it policy is allowed if the employee has a reasonable opportunity to take the vacation and the employer can show the employee had notice of the policy. [^nd-use-it-or-lose-it-notice]

A private employer may withhold accrued paid time off from an employee who quits only if it gave written notice of that limitation at hiring, the employee worked for it less than one year and the employee gave less than five days' notice. [^nd-pto-withholding-voluntary-quit] A private employer may also withhold paid time off that it awarded but the employee had not yet earned, if it gave written notice of that limitation before awarding the time. [^nd-pto-withholding-awarded-unearned]

Earned bonuses and commissions can also be owed. Under the order, an earned bonus is compensable when the employee meets the requirements of a contract or agreement between the parties. [^nd-earned-bonus-compensable] At separation the employer's past practices, policies and entire employment relationship determine whether a commission is earned. [^nd-earned-bonus-commission]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** North Dakota requires pay at least once a month. Every employer must pay all wages due at least once each calendar month on regular paydays it designates in advance. [^nd-pay-frequency-monthly] Wages may be paid in cash, by check drawn on a bank or credit union convenient to the workplace, by direct deposit at the employee's chosen financial institution or, if the employer offers it and the employee elects it, by a stored value card. [^nd-pay-methods] A stored value card used for wages must be issued by a federally insured bank or credit union, [^nd-stored-value-card-insured-issuer] and the employer must first deposit funds at least equal to the wages due and any fees the issuer charges the employer. [^nd-stored-value-card-funding]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** North Dakota requires a basic pay statement. Every employer must give each employee, each pay period, a check stub or pay voucher showing hours worked, the rate of pay, required state and federal deductions and authorized deductions. [^nd-pay-stub-contents]
Apart from deductions required by state or federal law or ordered by a court, an employer may withhold from wages only advances other than undocumented cash, recurring deductions authorized in writing, and nonrecurring deductions authorized in writing that cite the source of the deduction specifically. [^nd-deductions-allowed] A nonrecurring deduction for damage, breakage, shortage or negligence must be authorized by the employee at the time of the deduction. [^nd-deduction-damage-shortage]

Time clocks are not required. An employer that uses them may round to the nearest five minutes or quarter hour, using the total minutes for the day, as long as the employee is paid over time for all time actually worked. [^nd-time-clock-rounding]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** North Dakota uses the common-law control test for wage-and-hour purposes. The Minimum Wage and Work Conditions Order adopts the common-law test in the job service rule, N.D. Admin. Code § 27-02-14-01(5)(a) and (b), to decide whether an individual is an employee or an independent contractor. [^nd-wage-order-adopts-common-law-test] Under that rule, an employment relationship generally exists when the person for whom services are performed has the right to control and direct the worker not only as to the result but also as to the details and means of accomplishing it. [^nd-common-law-right-to-control] The rule identifies 20 factors as guides for deciding whether enough control is present, and the weight of each factor varies with the occupation and the facts. [^nd-common-law-twenty-factors]

A worker may apply to the labor commissioner for verification of independent contractor status. When the information supports a finding under the common-law test, the commissioner issues a determination and an identification number, which becomes invalid if the worker's job changes. [^nd-independent-contractor-verification]

Transportation network company drivers have a separate statutory rule. A driver is an independent contractor, not an employee, of a transportation network company if the company meets conditions listed in the statute, including an agreement establishing independent-contractor status and no unilateral prescription of specific hours during which the driver must be available. [^nd-tnc-driver-independent-contractor]

That verification limits back-pay exposure. If the commissioner later finds that a worker holding a valid identification number is an employee for wage purposes, the commissioner may not require wages beyond those in the contract, or interest or penalties, retroactive to the start of the relationship, unless the employer willfully and intentionally entered the relationship to avoid paying wages. [^nd-verified-contractor-no-retroactive-wages] [^nd-verified-contractor-wages-from-final-order]

The commissioner may require wages for that employee from the date the order declaring the employment relationship becomes final. [^nd-verified-contractor-wages-from-final-order]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** North Dakota allows a partial tip credit. An employer may count tips toward the wages of tipped employees, but the tip credit may not exceed 33 percent of the minimum wage. [^nd-tip-credit-thirty-three-percent] An employer that uses the tip credit must tell the employee in advance and keep written records showing that direct wages plus the tip credit equal at least the minimum wage. [^nd-tip-credit-notice-records] A tipped employee is a service employee in an occupation in which the employee customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips. [^nd-tipped-employee-definition]

At the $7.25 minimum wage, a 33 percent credit is about $2.39 an hour, which leaves a cash wage of about $4.86 an hour. [^nd-tip-credit-base-minimum-wage] [^nd-tip-credit-thirty-three-percent] Service employees are those who regularly provide personal face-to-face service to customers, and cooking and dishwashing do not count. [^nd-service-employee-definition]

Tip pooling is allowed only among tipped employees, and it requires a vote approved by 50 percent plus one of all tipped employees, with a written record of each vote kept by the employer. [^nd-tip-pooling-vote] Licensed nonprofit gaming organizations have exceptions: a gaming site that regularly has four or fewer tipped employees on duty may require tip pooling among all tipped employees at the site, and a licensed organization may require tip pooling by blackjack dealers. [^nd-gaming-tip-pooling-exceptions]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** North Dakota enforces wage claims through the Department of Labor and Human Rights and the courts. [^nd-wage-claim-filing-limits] [^nd-wage-claim-court-referral] An employee may file a wage claim with the department for wages due under the wage collection chapter or the minimum wage and hours chapter if the claim is filed within two years after the wages are due and the amount claimed is at least $125 but not more than $15,000. [^nd-wage-claim-filing-limits] If the department denies a claim as too small, it must tell the claimant about small claims court, and if it denies a claim as too large, it must tell the claimant about district court. [^nd-wage-claim-court-referral] If the labor commissioner finds unpaid wages that are an enforceable claim, the commissioner may, on the employee's request, take an assignment of the wages or a liquidated damages claim and sue to collect it. [^nd-commissioner-assignment-and-suit]
Besides unpaid wages, an employee may recover interest from the date the wages were due. [^nd-interest-on-unpaid-wages] In addition to the unpaid wages and interest, an employer found liable for two previous wage claims on separate occasions within the year before the wages were due owes an amount equal to double the unpaid wages, and one found liable for three or more owes an amount equal to treble the unpaid wages. [^nd-interest-on-unpaid-wages] [^nd-double-treble-repeat-violators]

The doubling and trebling provision does not apply to wage claims under the Railway Labor Act or to claims arising from a contested application of a collective bargaining agreement. [^nd-double-treble-exceptions]

Lawsuits for overtime, damages, fees or penalties under laws on paying wages, including the Fair Labor Standards Act, must be brought within two years after the claim accrues under North Dakota law. [^nd-two-year-limitations] Filing a claim with the commissioner tolls that state limitation period until the commissioner finds the claim unenforceable or reassigns it to the employee. [^nd-limitations-tolled-by-claim] Federal law separately allows a Fair Labor Standards Act action for a willful violation to be brought within three years. [^nd-flsa-limitations-willful-three-years]

The commissioner enforces the wage collection chapter, may hold hearings on the merits of a claim [^nd-commissioner-enforcement-powers] and may require witnesses and records, by subpoena if needed. [^nd-commissioner-subpoena-power] The commissioner may consider an employer's offsets, deductions and counterclaims, and the employer must disclose their basis and amount within the time the commissioner directs. [^nd-employer-offsets-disclosure] Questions of fact under the minimum wage and hours chapter are decided by the commissioner without appeal, but rulings on questions of law may be appealed to the district court of Burleigh County. [^nd-appeal-questions-of-law]

Violations also carry criminal exposure. Violating the minimum wage and hours chapter or its rules is a class B misdemeanor. [^nd-minimum-wage-violation-misdemeanor] An employer that willfully refuses to pay wages due on demand, or falsely denies them with intent to secure a discount or to harass, delay or defraud, is guilty of an infraction. [^nd-willful-nonpayment-infraction]

An employer may not discharge or discriminate against an employee for testifying, or being about to testify, in a proceeding under the minimum wage and hours chapter. [^nd-minimum-wage-anti-retaliation] North Dakota's general anti-retaliation statute separately protects an employee who in good faith reports a violation or suspected violation of law to an employer, a governmental body or a law enforcement official. [^nd-general-anti-retaliation-protected-report] The employee may sue for injunctive relief or actual damages within 180 days after the violation, after department proceedings or after any available grievance procedure, whichever is later. [^nd-general-anti-retaliation-civil-action] An employee whose collective bargaining agreement, employment contract or public employee rights provide a recourse process must complete it before suing, and the civil action is unavailable if that process provides judicial review by statutory appeal. [^nd-anti-retaliation-exhaustion]
