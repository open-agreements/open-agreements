# Wage and Hour Law in New Mexico[^about]

How New Mexico sets a $12.00 minimum wage with a $3.00 cash wage for tipped employees, requires weekly overtime after 40 hours, sets no general break rule, requires final pay within five or ten days of a discharge, and sets pay-timing, pay-receipt, deduction, worker-coverage and wage-claim rules.

## What is New Mexico's minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** New Mexico's minimum wage is $12.00 an hour, the rate that has applied since January 1, 2023. [^nm-minimum-wage-twelve-dollars] The Minimum Wage Act sets that figure as the last step of a fixed schedule and contains no provision that adjusts it for inflation. [^nm-minimum-wage-twelve-dollars] The state rate is above the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour. [^nm-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The Minimum Wage Act's definition of employee leaves out several groups, so the state minimum wage and overtime rules do not reach them. The exclusions include bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees, forepersons, superintendents and supervisors, and salespersons or employees paid on piecework, flat rate schedules or a commission basis. [^nm-employee-exclusions] [^nm-employee-piecework-commission-exclusion-mw] Most public employees are also outside the Act, but the state and its political subdivisions must still pay the minimum wage rate. [^nm-employer-definition-public-minimum-wage]

Local governments may set higher rates. The state's temporary bar on local minimum wage laws expired on January 1, 2010, and a local ordinance in effect on January 1, 2007 that sets a higher rate continues until repealed. [^nm-local-minimum-wage-saving-clause] Albuquerque, for example, lists a city minimum wage of $13.00 an hour from January 1, 2027, or $12.00 an hour if the employer provides healthcare or childcare benefits costing at least $2,500 a year. [^nm-albuquerque-minimum-wage-2027] An employer with workers in a city or county should check that local government's ordinance for a higher rate.

## When is overtime owed in New Mexico? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** New Mexico requires overtime at one and one-half times the employee's regular hourly rate for all hours worked over 40 in a week of seven days. [^nm-overtime-forty-hours] The rule counts hours by the week, not the day, so there is no daily overtime threshold. [^nm-overtime-forty-hours] Separately, most employees may not be required to work more than 16 hours in a 24-hour day except in emergencies. [^nm-sixteen-hour-daily-maximum]

The overtime rule does not apply to employers of workers engaged in agriculture, as the federal Fair Labor Standards Act defines it. [^nm-overtime-agriculture-exemption] Because the overtime rule sits in the Minimum Wage Act, the Act's exclusions from the definition of employee, including executive, administrative and professional employees and employees paid by piecework, flat rate or commission, also take those workers out of state overtime. [^nm-employee-exclusions-ot] [^nm-employee-piecework-commission-exclusion-ot]

## Does New Mexico require meal or rest breaks? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** New Mexico's statutes do not require employers to give adult employees general meal or rest breaks. The review for this guide of Article 4 of Chapter 50 of the New Mexico Statutes, which holds the wage payment law and the Minimum Wage Act, found no such provision. Section 50-4-30(A) separately limits required daily work to 16 hours in a 24-hour day, subject to exceptions for emergencies and for firefighters, law enforcement officers, farm or ranch hands whose duties require longer hours, and stand-by employees. [^nm-sixteen-hour-daily-maximum-breaks]

When an employer does give breaks to an employee covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, federal rules treat short rest periods of about 5 to 20 minutes as paid working time. [^nm-federal-rest-periods-paid] A bona fide meal period is not worktime, but the employee must be completely relieved from duty, and 30 minutes or more is ordinarily long enough. [^nm-federal-meal-period]

New Mexico requires every employer, including the state and its political subdivisions, to give a nursing mother flexible break times and a clean, private space near her workspace, other than a bathroom, for using a breast pump. [^nm-breast-pump-workplace] Under the state statute, the employer is not liable to pay for the nursing mother's break time beyond established employee breaks, or to pay overtime while she is using a breast pump. [^nm-breast-pump-workplace]

Federal law adds its own protection: it requires an employer to provide reasonable break time to express breast milk for one year after the child's birth, and a place other than a bathroom that is shielded from view. [^nm-federal-lactation-break] Federal law does not require an employer to pay for that break time unless other law requires it, but the break counts as hours worked, and so must be paid, if the employee is not completely relieved from duty during the entire break. [^nm-federal-lactation-pay] [^nm-federal-lactation-hours-worked]

## When is final pay due in New Mexico? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** When an employer discharges an employee, unpaid wages that are a fixed and definite amount become due immediately on demand and must be paid within five days of the discharge. [^nm-final-pay-discharge] Wages based on a task, piece, commission or other method of calculation must be settled and paid within ten days of the discharge. [^nm-final-pay-discharge] An employee who quits, and who does not have a written contract for a definite period, is paid at the next succeeding payday. [^nm-final-pay-quit]
If the employer misses the deadline after a discharge, the employee's wages continue at the same rate from the date of discharge until paid, for no more than 60 days. [^nm-final-pay-late-continuation] To recover those continued wages, the employee must plead and prove a demand made within a reasonable time at the place designated for payment, and a refusal to pay. [^nm-final-pay-late-continuation]

The Court of Appeals of New Mexico has held that accrued vacation pay is a fixed and definite amount under this rule, and that failing to pay any component of what is due triggers continued payment of both vacation pay and wages for up to 60 days. [^nm-wolf-vacation-fixed-definite] [^nm-wolf-vacation-pay-penalty]

In a dispute over wages, the employer must give the employee written notice of the amount it concedes is due and pay that amount without condition within the statutory time; accepting that payment does not release the rest of the claim. [^nm-conceded-wages-written-notice]

## How often must New Mexico employers pay wages? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** A New Mexico employer must designate regular paydays no more than 16 days apart, with two exceptions that allow monthly pay. [^nm-paydays-sixteen-days] An employer may pay professional, administrative, executive and outside sales employees, as those categories are defined under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, once a month unless a collective bargaining agreement covers their wages, and a task, piece or commission employee may agree in writing at hiring to be paid monthly, on or before the 10th day of the following calendar month. [^nm-monthly-pay-exempt-employees] [^nm-wage-receipt-and-deductions-freq] Work from the 1st through the 15th of a month must be paid by the 25th of that month, and work from the 16th through the end of the month must be paid by the 10th of the next month. [^nm-paydays-sixteen-days] An employer that computes earnings and amounts due, prepares payrolls and issues paychecks at a central location outside New Mexico has until the last day of the month and the 15th of the next month instead. [^nm-paydays-sixteen-days]

## What must a New Mexico pay receipt show, and which deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** A New Mexico employer must give each employee a written receipt that identifies the employer and shows the employee's gross pay, the number of hours worked, the total wages and benefits earned, and an itemized list of all deductions withheld. [^nm-wage-receipt-and-deductions] Wages must be paid in full, less lawful deductions and payroll deductions authorized by the employer and employee, and without any other reduction except as stated in a written contract of hiring made at the time of hiring. [^nm-wage-receipt-and-deductions]

The Department of Workforce Solutions' rules define the written authorization an employer needs before taking a deduction, and require one for deducting an advance or overpayment of wages, while keeping pay at no less than the minimum wage for the hours worked. [^nm-written-authorization-deductions] An employer furnishing food, utilities, supplies or housing to an agricultural employee may deduct their reasonable value from wages. [^nm-agriculture-furnished-items-deduction]

The wage payment law requires every employer to keep a true and accurate record of hours worked and wages paid to each employee, and to keep it for at least one year. [^nm-records-one-year] The unemployment compensation rules require an employing unit to preserve its payroll records for longer: at least four years in addition to the current calendar year. [^nm-ui-payroll-records-duty] [^nm-ui-records-four-years]

## How does New Mexico decide who is an employee? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** New Mexico courts decide whether a worker is an employee under the Minimum Wage Act by the economic reality of the total employment situation, looking at facts such as pay, contract, control and whether the work was voluntary. [^nm-garcia-economic-reality] The Act itself sets out no independent contractor test; it defines employ to include suffer or permit to work. [^nm-employ-suffer-permit] The Department of Workforce Solutions' wage rules adopt the same definition of employ. [^nm-nmac-employ-definition] A different statute, New Mexico's paid sick leave law, the Healthy Workplaces Act, uses a right-to-control definition for its own purposes: it defines an independent contractor as a person whom the hiring party may direct as to the result but who is not subject to the hiring party's right to control the details of how the work is done. [^nm-hwa-independent-contractor]

Whether a worker is an employee matters less under the Minimum Wage Act for some pay methods, because the Act excludes salespersons and employees compensated on piecework, flat rate schedules or a commission basis from its definition of employee. [^nm-employee-piecework-commission-exclusion] The wage payment rules on paydays, final pay and deductions use their own broader definition of employer, which reaches every person employing anyone in New Mexico except employers of livestock and agricultural labor. [^nm-wage-payment-employer-definition]

## Can tips count toward New Mexico's minimum wage? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** New Mexico allows a partial tip credit for qualifying tipped employees, so long as cash wages and tips together reach the full minimum wage. [^nm-tipped-cash-wage] An employee who customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips must be paid a cash wage of at least $3.00 an hour, and the employer may count tips toward the rest of the wage only if tips plus the cash wage equal at least the full $12.00 minimum wage. [^nm-tipped-cash-wage] [^nm-minimum-wage-twelve-dollars-tip] That leaves a maximum tip credit of $9.00 an hour. [^nm-tipped-cash-wage] [^nm-minimum-wage-twelve-dollars-tip]

Tips belong to the employee, although the statute does not bar pooling tips among wait staff. [^nm-tipped-cash-wage] A local minimum wage ordinance may also set its own rates, so employers in a city or county with a local minimum wage should check it. [^nm-local-minimum-wage-saving-clause-tip]

## How is New Mexico wage and hour law enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employer that violates the minimum wage or overtime rule is liable to the affected employees for the unpaid or underpaid wages plus interest and an additional amount equal to twice the unpaid wages. [^nm-treble-damages-attorney-fees] One or more employees may sue on behalf of themselves and other employees similarly situated, and the court must award costs and reasonable attorney fees to a successful plaintiff. [^nm-treble-damages-attorney-fees] A civil action to enforce any provision of the wage article may reach all violations that were part of a continuing course of conduct, whenever they occurred. [^nm-continuing-course-of-conduct]

An employee may instead file a wage claim with the Labor Relations Division of the Department of Workforce Solutions; the claim must be filed within three years of the employer's last violation as to the claimant, and if any part of the claim falls within that period, the division investigates back to the start of the continuing course of conduct. [^nm-wage-claim-three-years] The division can take an assignment of a wage claim and sue the employer to collect it. [^nm-wage-claim-assignment]

The Minimum Wage Act bars retaliation against a person for asserting a claim or right under the Act, assisting another person to do so, or informing another person about employment rights. [^nm-retaliation-prohibited]
