# Wage and Hour Law in Guam[^about]

How Guam sets a $9.25 minimum wage, requires weekly overtime and a meal period, makes final pay due immediately on discharge, limits deductions, excludes tips from wages and imposes treble damages for unpaid wages.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Guam's minimum wage is $9.25 an hour, the last step of the schedule in 22 GCA § 3105, which took effect on September 1, 2021. [^gu-minimum-wage-rate] The Guam Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division gives the same current rate. [^gu-dol-minimum-wage-faq] If federal law sets a higher minimum wage, Guam's minimum wage rises automatically to the federal level on the same effective date. [^gu-minimum-wage-federal-escalator] The federal Fair Labor Standards Act applies in Guam, because it defines a State to include any territory or possession of the United States. [^gu-flsa-territory] The federal minimum wage under that Act is $7.25/hr an hour. [^gu-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The federal Act does not excuse an employer from paying a higher minimum wage set by territorial law, so an employer covered by both laws must pay Guam's $9.25 rate. [^gu-flsa-higher-state-standard] Guam's minimum wage and overtime rules do not apply to an employee who works for the employee's own child, spouse or parent, and they exclude certain occupations, including commercial fishing, seamen, taxi drivers working from a fixed stand, golf caddies and newspaper carriers. [^gu-exemption-family-executive] [^gu-exemptions-family-occupations] The Guam statute also excludes from its definition of employee anyone employed in domestic work in or about a private home, and anyone employed in agriculture during a workweek when the employer has fewer than ten employees. [^gu-employee-definition]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Guam generally requires overtime after 40 hours in a week, at no less than one and one-half times the employee's regular rate, and has no general daily overtime rule. [^gu-weekly-overtime] The weekly rule does not apply to employees covered by section 207(k) of the federal Fair Labor Standards Act. [^gu-weekly-overtime] Employers that operate institutions caring for sick, aged or mentally ill persons may instead use a 14-day work period, with overtime owed after eight hours in a day or 80 hours in the 14 days. [^gu-hospital-fourteen-day]

Split shifts must all fall within 14 consecutive hours in any 24-hour period, except in an extraordinary emergency. [^gu-split-shifts] The minimum wage and overtime rules do not apply to bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees paid on a salary or fee basis of at least $455 a week who meet the statutory duties tests, or to outside salespersons. [^gu-executive-exemption] [^gu-administrative-professional-exemption] [^gu-outside-sales-exemption] For an employee covered by the federal Act, federal law separately requires one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. [^gu-federal-overtime] Except for government employees, employees of tax-exempt nonprofit organizations and employers that operate those care institutions, an employee cannot do volunteer work that benefits the employer at less than regular pay or without any overtime that applies. [^gu-no-volunteer-work]

Federal overtime exemptions and hours-worked rules are discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Guam requires a meal period of at least 30 minutes for a covered, nonexempt employee who works more than five hours, subject to a limited mutual-waiver exception. [^gu-meal-period] [^gu-meal-exemption-coverage] The employer and the employee may agree to skip the meal period only when a work period of no more than six hours will complete the day's work. [^gu-meal-period] The meal period is unpaid and does not count as time worked, unless the nature of the work prevents the employee from being relieved of duty. [^gu-meal-period] No Guam statute reviewed for this guide requires separate rest breaks.

For an employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, short rest periods that are given must be counted as hours worked, [^gu-federal-rest-periods-paid] while a meal period may be unpaid only if the employee is completely relieved from duty. [^gu-federal-meal-relieved]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** An employee discharged with or without cause must be paid all earned wages immediately on discharge. [^gu-final-pay-timing] An employee who quits voluntarily must be paid earned wages by the next regular payday, and employees whose work is suspended by a labor dispute must be paid wages earned to that date on the next regular payday. [^gu-final-pay-timing] If the amount of wages is disputed at termination, the employer must give the employee and the Director of Labor notice of the amount it concedes is due and pay that amount unconditionally by the same deadline; accepting it does not release the disputed balance. [^gu-disputed-wages] An employer other than a government entity that fails to pay wages when due must pay the employee punitive damages of three times the wages due, unless it proves every element of a statutory affirmative defense. [^gu-final-pay-treble-damages]

The Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division describes the discharge deadline as immediately, within 24 hours. [^gu-dol-final-pay-faq] Failing to pay wages when due, by an employer that has the ability to pay and intentionally refuses after a demand, is also a misdemeanor, as is any violation of the payment-timing and deduction sections. [^gu-wage-payment-misdemeanor] An employer that cannot or does not pay wages when due must, within five working days, give each employee and the Director of Labor a statement of the correct hours, wages, deductions and wages payable. [^gu-unpaid-wage-statement]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** No Guam statute reviewed for this guide fixes how often an employer must pay wages, such as weekly or semimonthly. Instead, earned wages are due within seven days after the end of each pay period the employer sets. [^gu-pay-timing-seven-days] All wage payments must be made in cash or by check. [^gu-cash-or-check]

An employer of non-immigrant alien employees may not deduct from their pay any money to be sent off-island, for any purpose. [^gu-cash-or-check]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** Every deduction from an employee's pay, and every sum paid to the employer from that pay, must be shown on a pay statement or check stub given to the employee when the employee is paid, and the employer must keep a copy for three years. [^gu-pay-statement] An employer may deduct wages only when federal or Guam law or court process requires it, or when the employee authorizes the deduction in writing. [^gu-deductions-limited] No fine may be collected, deducted or retained from wages, and an employee cannot authorize a deduction for a fine. [^gu-no-fines] [^gu-deductions-limited]

Employers must also keep records of each employee's name, address, Social Security number or, for alien workers, passport number, occupation, pay for each pay period and hours worked each day and each workweek. [^gu-payroll-records-kept] An improper deduction from wages counts as a failure to pay wages, as does failing to pass on sums deducted for payment to someone else within five days of the employee's payday. [^gu-failure-to-pay-defined]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** No Guam statute reviewed for this guide sets out a separate test for deciding whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor. Guam's minimum wage law treats permitting or suffering someone to work as employing that person, [^gu-employ-definition] which follows the federal Fair Labor Standards Act's definition. [^gu-federal-employ-definition] Guam has also adopted the federal Fair Labor Standards Act Field Operations Handbook in effect when the provision was enacted as the basic procedures and guidelines for applying fair labor standards in Guam, to the extent they are consistent with Guam law. [^gu-federal-handbook-adopted]

Guam's wage law also treats as wages the sums payable for work under a contract to perform personal services, and declares it against public policy to avoid wage, overtime or child labor laws by inducing employees to sign employment contracts. [^gu-personal-services-wages]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Guam does not allow a tip credit. The minimum wage law defines wages to exclude tips and gratuities of any kind, so tips cannot count toward the minimum wage. [^gu-wage-excludes-tips] That minimum wage is $9.25 an hour. [^gu-tip-minimum-wage-rate] Because federal law does not excuse an employer from paying a higher territorial minimum wage, [^gu-tip-flsa-higher-state-standard] a tipped employee in Guam must receive at least $9.25 an hour from the employer. [^gu-tip-minimum-wage-rate]

The same definition lets an employer count toward wages the reasonable cost, as determined by the Wage and Hour Commissioner, of board, lodging or other facilities it customarily furnishes, except to the extent a bona fide collective bargaining agreement excludes that cost. [^gu-wage-excludes-tips]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** Employees may sue in the Superior Court of Guam to recover unpaid minimum wages, overtime compensation and applicable liquidated damages for themselves and similarly situated employees. [^gu-liquidated-damages] [^gu-collection-suits] An employer that violates the minimum wage or overtime sections is liable for the unpaid minimum wages or overtime and, for a willful violation, an additional equal amount as liquidated damages. [^gu-liquidated-damages] Separately, an employer other than a government entity that fails to pay or underpays wages must pay the employee punitive damages of three times the wages due, unless it proves each element of a statutory affirmative defense. [^gu-treble-damages] A prevailing employee recovers a reasonable attorney's fee and costs, [^gu-collection-suits] and the court must award fees of at least $125 an hour to the government or a private attorney who represents employees. [^gu-attorney-fees-floor]

The elements of the affirmative defense include that, for non-payment, the employer did not have the ability to pay, that it complied with all other provisions of the chapter and that it committed no fraud in computing wages. [^gu-treble-damages] Anyone who participates in or knowingly benefits from fraud or intentional non-payment or underpayment is jointly and severally liable with the employer. [^gu-joint-liability] An employee may not waive or compromise earned wages, and may waive penalties, costs and attorney's fees only if all wages are paid in full and the employee is represented by an attorney, and the waiver or compromise is approved in writing by both the employee and the employee's attorney. [^gu-no-waiver] An employer that fails to pay wages when due must pay the attorney's fees and costs of collecting them, and the Department of Labor may charge a 12% collection fee on wages it collects. [^gu-collection-fee] Intentional violations of the minimum wage and hour law, and retaliation against an employee who complains or testifies, are misdemeanors. [^gu-criminal-retaliation]
