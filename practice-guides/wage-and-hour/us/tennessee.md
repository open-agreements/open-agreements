# Wage and Hour Law in Tennessee[^about]

How Tennessee, which has no state minimum wage or overtime law, relies on the federal Fair Labor Standards Act for the $7.25 wage floor, weekly overtime, tip credit and wage claims, bars cities and counties from setting a higher wage, requires a 30-minute unpaid meal break in a six-hour shift, pays final wages by the later of the next regular payday or 21 days, requires employers with five or more employees to pay at least once a month, and limits wage offsets for money an employee owes the employer.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Tennessee has no state minimum wage law. Under federal law, a covered, nonexempt employee generally must be paid at least $7.25 an hour. [^tn-dol-no-state-minimum-wage] [^tn-federal-minimum-wage-rate] The federal rate applies to an employee who, in any workweek, is engaged in commerce or in the production of goods for commerce, or who is employed in an enterprise engaged in commerce or in the production of goods for commerce. [^tn-federal-minimum-wage-rate] Tennessee law also bars a local government from requiring a private employer, as a condition of doing business within its boundaries or contracting with it, to pay an hourly wage above the minimum required by federal or state law, so no Tennessee city or county can set a higher minimum wage for private employers. [^tn-local-wage-mandate-preemption]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The 2023 Protecting Tennessee Businesses and Workers Act broadened that preemption to any wage or employment benefit mandate a local government imposes on private employers, unless state or federal law requires it, and barred local governments from using their purchasing or contracting procedures to control or affect the wages or benefits their vendors and contractors provide. [^tn-local-wage-mandate-preemption] [^tn-local-contracting-wage-preemption] A local government may still set wages and benefits for its own employees. [^tn-local-own-employees-wages] The same act bars a local government from regulating the hours, schedules or work output that private employers require of their employees, except as state or federal law provides. [^tn-local-hours-scheduling-preemption]

An employee who is outside federal coverage has no Tennessee minimum wage to fall back on. [^tn-dol-no-state-minimum-wage] Federal law allows an employer to pay an employee under age 20 not less than $4.25 an hour during the first 90 consecutive calendar days of employment. [^tn-federal-youth-wage] [^tn-federal-youth-wage-age] An employer may not displace other employees, including by reducing their hours, wages or benefits, to hire workers at that wage. [^tn-federal-youth-wage-displacement]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** A covered, nonexempt Tennessee employee generally earns overtime of at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek under federal law. [^tn-federal-overtime-forty-hours] The federal rule counts hours by the workweek, not by the day. [^tn-federal-overtime-forty-hours] Tennessee has no state minimum wage law, and so no state wage floor from which a separate overtime rate is computed. [^tn-dol-no-state-minimum-wage-overtime] One federal alternative uses a daily threshold: a hospital, or an institution primarily engaged in caring for sick, aged, or mentally ill or disabled people who reside on its premises, may, by agreement with the employee before the work is performed, use a 14-day work period if it pays time and a half for work over eight hours in a workday and over 80 hours in the period. [^tn-federal-hospital-fourteen-day]

A federal executive, administrative or professional exemption generally requires a salary of at least $684 a week. [^tn-federal-white-collar-salary-684] An employer may make deductions from an exempt employee's salary only in the circumstances the federal salary-basis rule allows, and an exempt employee generally must receive the full salary for any week in which the employee performs any work. [^tn-federal-salary-basis-full-week]

Because a Tennessee city or county may not regulate the hours or schedules private employers set, no local overtime or predictable-scheduling ordinance can add to the federal rule. [^tn-local-hours-scheduling-preemption-overtime]

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Tennessee generally requires a meal break for employees scheduled to work six consecutive hours, subject to a workplace exception and a waiver for tipped food and beverage servers, but requires no separate rest break. [^tn-meal-break-six-hours] [^tn-dol-meal-break-guidance] [^tn-meal-break-tipped-waiver] Each employee scheduled to work six consecutive hours must have a 30-minute unpaid meal break, except in workplaces whose nature of business gives ample opportunity to take an appropriate meal break, and the break may not be scheduled during or before the first hour of scheduled work. [^tn-meal-break-six-hours] The Tennessee Department of Labor and Workforce Development gives food and beverage work and security guards as examples of that exception, treats a failure to provide the break as a violation of state law, and states that there are no state requirements for additional breaks. [^tn-dol-meal-break-guidance]

An employee who is principally employed serving food or beverages to customers, and who receives and reports tips, may waive the meal break at the employer's discretion. [^tn-meal-break-tipped-waiver] The waiver must be requested in writing on the employer's form, knowingly and voluntarily, and both sides must consent. [^tn-meal-break-tipped-waiver] An employer that intends to accept waivers must adopt a written policy, posted in at least one conspicuous place in the workplace, that includes a waiver form acknowledging the employee's right to the 30-minute break, the length of time the waiver lasts and procedures for rescinding it. [^tn-meal-break-waiver-policy] Either side may rescind a waiver on at least seven calendar days' notice, and an employer may not coerce an employee into waiving a meal break. [^tn-meal-break-waiver-policy]

When an employer gives short rest periods, federal regulations treat breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes as customarily paid and counted as hours worked. [^tn-federal-rest-periods-paid]

Federal law generally requires an employer to provide a covered employee reasonable break time, and a private place other than a bathroom, for an employee to express breast milk for one year after the child's birth. [^tn-federal-lactation-break] The break time need not be paid unless other law requires it, but it counts as hours worked if the employee is not completely relieved from duty during the break. [^tn-federal-lactation-unpaid] [^tn-federal-lactation-hours-worked] Under the federal law, an employer with fewer than 50 employees is exempt from the federal break requirement if compliance would impose an undue hardship, and other federal exemptions are covered in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us). [^tn-federal-lactation-small-employer]

Tennessee also has its own lactation statute, enacted in 1999, which requires reasonable unpaid break time each day for an employee who needs to express breast milk for her infant child, running concurrently with existing breaks if possible, unless providing the break time would unduly disrupt the employer's operations, and it requires reasonable efforts to provide a private room or other location near the work area, other than a toilet stall. [^tn-state-lactation-break-room]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** Except for an employee paid on a piece-work or commission basis, an employee who quits or is discharged in Tennessee must be paid in full all wages or salary earned by the later of the next regular payday after separation or 21 days after separation. [^tn-final-pay-twenty-one-days] For an employee paid on a piece-work or commission basis, compensation earned is due by the last day of the month after the month of separation, and an employer may not secure an exemption from the final-pay rule by any means. [^tn-final-pay-twenty-one-days] The piece-work and commission rule took effect July 1, 2026 and applies to wages and compensation earned on or after that date. [^tn-final-pay-effective-date] Tennessee law does not require final wages to include unused vacation, sick pay or other fringe benefits unless the employer's policy or a labor agreement requires it. [^tn-dol-unused-fringe-benefits]

The federal Fair Labor Standards Act still applies to the final paycheck. Overtime earned in a workweek generally must be paid on the regular payday for the period in which that workweek ends, and payment may not be delayed beyond the next payday after the amount can be computed. [^tn-federal-overtime-regular-payday] [^tn-federal-overtime-payment-delay] An employer that fails to pay federal minimum wages or overtime generally owes the unpaid amount plus an equal amount as liquidated damages, although a court may reduce or deny liquidated damages if the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds for believing it was not violating the law. [^tn-federal-unpaid-wages-liquidated-damages] [^tn-federal-good-faith-fp]

An employer that wants to recover an advance, loan or personal credit-card charge from a final paycheck must follow Tennessee's wage-offset statute, including a written agreement signed before the advance or loan and written notice 14 days before the wages are paid. [^tn-wage-offset-conditions-fp] [^tn-wage-offset-notice-fp] Federal law also requires wages to be paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear. [^tn-federal-free-and-clear]

An employee whose final wages are not paid on time can file a wage claim with the Department of Labor and Workforce Development, which may forward an unresolved claim for review and possible penalty assessment after 20 calendar days. [^tn-dol-wage-claim-process-fp]

## How often must workers be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Tennessee requires private employers with five or more employees to establish regular paydays and to pay wages at least once a month. [^tn-dol-regular-paydays-five-employees] [^tn-dol-payday-once-monthly] An employer that pays once a month must pay all wages earned before the first day of a month by the fifth day of the next month, except that piece-work and commission compensation is due by the last day of the next month. [^tn-monthly-payday-fifth-day] An employer that pays in two or more periods a month must pay wages earned before the first day of a month by the 20th day of that month, and wages earned before the 16th day of a month by the fifth day of the next month. [^tn-dol-payday-semimonthly]

The piece-work and commission rule for monthly payers was added by a 2026 act that took effect July 1, 2026 and applies to wages and compensation earned on or after that date. [^tn-monthly-payday-fifth-day] [^tn-final-pay-effective-date-frequency] For an employee covered by federal law, overtime earned in a workweek generally must be paid on the regular payday for the period in which that workweek ends. [^tn-federal-overtime-regular-payday-frequency] The federal Fair Labor Standards Act itself does not require overtime to be paid weekly. [^tn-federal-overtime-not-weekly]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** Tennessee has no general statute requiring a private employer to give employees a pay statement. Tennessee does limit when an employer may offset wages for money the employee owes it. An employer may offset wages for a wage advance, a loan or personal items charged to a business credit card only if the employee signed a written agreement allowing the offset before the advance, loan or charge, and the employer has a copy of that signed agreement at the time of the offset. [^tn-wage-offset-conditions] The employer must also notify the employee in writing 14 days before the wages are paid that money is owed, that wages may be offset if it is not repaid first, and that the employee may submit an affidavit contesting the amount, and the offset is allowed only if the employee has not repaid the amount. [^tn-wage-offset-notice] If the employee sends a sworn affidavit contesting the amount to the employer, with a copy to the Department of Labor and Workforce Development, within seven days after the notice, the employer may not offset and must instead sue to recover the amount. [^tn-wage-offset-affidavit]

The Department of Labor and Workforce Development advises that, generally, an employer cannot deduct the cost of uniforms, equipment, company loans or shortages from a paycheck without a signed written agreement. [^tn-dol-deductions-written-agreement]

Federal law adds its own limits. Wages count as paid only if they are paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear. [^tn-federal-free-and-clear-statements] A deduction for tools or similar items that are not board, lodging or other facilities is illegal to the extent it cuts the employee's wages below the federal minimum in a non-overtime workweek. [^tn-federal-tool-deductions-statements] In a non-overtime workweek, a deduction for board, lodging or other facilities may reduce the cash wage below the minimum wage only if the price charged does not exceed the reasonable cost of the facilities. [^tn-federal-facilities-reasonable-cost]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** Because Tennessee has no state minimum wage law, a Tennessee worker's status as an employee or independent contractor for minimum wage and overtime purposes turns on the federal economic-realities test. [^tn-dol-no-state-minimum-wage-classification] [^tn-federal-economic-realities] That test asks whether the worker is economically dependent on the potential employer for work or is in business for themself. [^tn-federal-economic-realities] The federal law defines employ to include to suffer or permit to work. [^tn-federal-suffer-or-permit]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Because Tennessee has no state minimum wage, a covered employer may take the federal tip credit, paying a tipped employee a cash wage of at least $2.13 an hour, if tips make up the rest of the $7.25 minimum and the employer meets the federal notice and tip-retention conditions. [^tn-dol-no-state-minimum-wage-tips] [^tn-federal-tip-credit-formula] [^tn-federal-tip-credit-notice] The employer must first inform the employee of the federal tip-credit provisions, and the employee must keep all tips, except that tips may be pooled among employees who customarily and regularly receive tips. [^tn-federal-tip-credit-notice] Under the federal formula, a tipped employee's cash wage must be at least $2.13 an hour, and the tip credit makes up the difference between that cash wage and the $7.25 minimum. [^tn-federal-tip-credit-formula] [^tn-federal-minimum-wage-rate-tips] The tip credit may not exceed the tips the employee actually receives, and if tips fall short, the employer must pay the balance so the employee receives at least the minimum wage. [^tn-federal-tip-credit-cap] [^tn-federal-tip-shortfall]

Tennessee's own Tip Statute governs gratuities and tips that a business adds automatically to a customer's bill. A restaurant or club that automatically adds a gratuity or tip to a customer's bill must distribute the amount collected among the employees who rendered the service, and a violation is a misdemeanor. [^tn-tip-statute-service-charges] In 2017 the Tennessee Supreme Court held that an employee may not sue an employer for violating the Tip Statute, because the statute does not give a private party a right to sue. [^tn-tip-statute-no-private-suit]

A tipped employee is an employee engaged in an occupation in which the employee customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips. [^tn-federal-tipped-employee-definition] Whether or not it takes a tip credit, an employer may not keep its employees' tips, including by letting managers or supervisors keep any portion. [^tn-federal-employer-may-not-keep-tips] An employer that keeps tips is liable under federal law for the tip credit taken and the tips kept, plus an equal amount as liquidated damages. [^tn-federal-tip-retention-damages]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** Unpaid minimum wages and overtime in Tennessee are enforced under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, because Tennessee has no state minimum wage law. [^tn-dol-no-state-minimum-wage-enforcement] [^tn-federal-unpaid-wages-liquidated-damages-enforcement] An employer that violates the federal minimum wage or overtime rules generally owes the unpaid amount plus an equal amount as liquidated damages, although a court may reduce or deny liquidated damages if the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds for believing it was not violating the law. [^tn-federal-unpaid-wages-liquidated-damages-enforcement] [^tn-federal-good-faith-enf] One or more employees may sue in any federal or state court of competent jurisdiction, for themselves and other employees similarly situated, and the court must award a reasonable attorney's fee and costs to a successful plaintiff. [^tn-federal-employee-action] [^tn-federal-attorney-fees] A federal wage claim must be filed within two years after the cause of action accrued, or three years for a willful violation. [^tn-federal-limitations]

In a federal collective action, no employee becomes a party plaintiff without filing written consent in the court. [^tn-federal-written-consent] The U.S. Secretary of Labor may also sue to recover unpaid minimum wages or overtime and an equal amount as liquidated damages, and a person who repeatedly or willfully violates the minimum wage or overtime rules faces a civil penalty. [^tn-federal-secretary-action] [^tn-federal-civil-penalty]

Tennessee's own pay rules come from its Wage Regulations Act, which the Department of Labor and Workforce Development describes as protecting wage earners from unfair pay practices and covering breaks and meal periods, final paychecks, payday regulations and deductions. [^tn-dol-meal-break-guidance-enforcement] An employee can file a wage claim with the department: an inspector reviews the claim and contacts the employer, and if the employer does not resolve it within 20 calendar days, the claim goes to the department's Central Office for review and possible penalty assessment. [^tn-dol-wage-claim-process] The department handles wage claims only for W-2 employees and does not investigate claims against employers with fewer than five employees. [^tn-dol-wage-claim-jurisdiction] An employee has no private lawsuit under the Tip Statute for automatically added gratuities or tips that are distributed to the wrong employees. [^tn-tip-statute-no-private-suit-enforcement]
