# Wage and Hour Law in Alaska[^about]

How Alaska sets a minimum wage rising to $15 in 2027 with no tip credit, requires daily and weekly overtime, limits required breaks to minors, and sets final-pay, pay-frequency, pay-statement, deduction, worker-classification and wage-claim rules, including the paid sick leave added by 2024 Ballot Measure 1.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Alaska's minimum wage is $14.00 an hour for work performed since July 1, 2026. [^ak-minimum-wage-fourteen-dollars-july-2026] The rate applies to hours worked in a pay period whether the work is measured by time, piece, commission or otherwise. [^ak-minimum-wage-all-pay-methods] The statute also keeps the Alaska rate at least two dollars above the federal minimum wage, with the two-dollar margin itself adjusted for inflation. [^ak-minimum-wage-two-dollars-over-federal]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The current schedule comes from 2024 Ballot Measure 1, a voter initiative effective July 1, 2025, that raised the minimum wage in steps. [^ak-ballot-measure-1-wage-and-sick-leave] [^ak-ballot-measure-1-wage-schedule] The rate rises to $15.00 an hour on July 1, 2027. [^ak-minimum-wage-fifteen-dollars-july-2027] After that step, the department adjusts the rate each year for Anchorage consumer-price inflation, calculated each September 30, rounded up to the nearest 10 cents, and applied to work performed from January 1 through December 31 of the year for which it is effective. [^ak-minimum-wage-annual-inflation-adjustment]

Public school bus drivers must be paid at least two times the Alaska minimum wage. [^ak-school-bus-driver-double-minimum] A contractor providing school bus service need not adjust drivers' wages under that rule except when it enters into or renews its contract. [^ak-school-bus-contractor-timing]

The Alaska Wage and Hour Act does not cover every worker. Its exclusions include, among others, agricultural workers, workers catching or taking fish and other aquatic life, and domestic workers in or about a private home. [^ak-wage-hour-act-exemptions-list] It also excludes federal, state and local government employees, except public school bus drivers. [^ak-wage-hour-act-public-employees-excluded] A worker under 18 employed part time for not more than 30 hours a week is excluded as well. [^ak-minors-part-time-exemption] The commissioner may allow wages below the minimum for approved apprentices and learners and for certain nonprofit residential summer camp employees. [^ak-sub-minimum-apprentices-learners-camps]

The department decides which hours count toward the minimum wage under listed federal hours-worked regulations. [^ak-compensable-hours-federal-rules] An employer may deduct the reasonable cost of board or lodging from minimum wage or overtime pay only if the board or lodging is customarily furnished, the cost is reasonable and without profit, the employee received prior written notice describing it, stating the weekly deduction and stating that acceptance is voluntary, and the employee accepted it in a signed writing. [^ak-board-lodging-deduction]

Ballot Measure 1 also established paid sick leave. [^ak-ballot-measure-1-wage-and-sick-leave] Employees accrue at least one hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked, up to 56 hours a year at an employer with 15 or more employees and 40 hours a year at a smaller employer, unless the employer sets a higher limit. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-accrual] The employer's size is measured in full-time equivalents worked during the previous calendar year. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-fte-count] Accrual began at the start of employment or on July 1, 2025, whichever is later, and leave may be used as it accrues. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-start-date] Sick leave is paid at the employee's regular rate or the applicable minimum wage, whichever is greater. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-pay-rate]

A paid time off policy satisfies the requirement if it provides enough leave that may be used for the same purposes and under the same conditions. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-pto-policy] Unused sick leave need not be paid out at separation unless other law requires it. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-no-payout] Executive, administrative, professional, outside sales and computer employees are excluded from the Wage and Hour Act's minimum wage and overtime rules, [^ak-sick-leave-white-collar-categories] but they are covered by the sick leave requirements. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-exempt-workers-covered] An employer may not retaliate against an employee who uses or tries to use paid sick leave. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-uses-and-retaliation] The rights cannot be waived by agreement, policy or condition of employment, except by an express, clear and unambiguous waiver in a bona fide collective bargaining agreement. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-no-waiver] An employer that violates the sick leave sections is liable for the employee's lost wages or other damages appropriate and allowable under state law. [^ak-paid-sick-leave-remedy]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Unless an exemption applies, such as the one for employers with fewer than four employees, Alaska requires overtime for hours worked over eight in a day and over 40 in a week. [^ak-overtime-daily-and-weekly] [^ak-overtime-small-employer-exemption] Hours already paid as daily overtime are not counted again toward the weekly total. [^ak-overtime-daily-and-weekly] The overtime rate is one and one-half times the employee's regular rate of pay. [^ak-overtime-rate-time-and-a-half] The overtime section is treated as part of every employment contract. [^ak-overtime-in-all-contracts]
Federal overtime law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The overtime section does not apply to an employer that employs fewer than four employees in the regular course of business. [^ak-overtime-small-employer-exemption] In counting toward four, corporate officers who actively engage in the business and all part-time employees count, regardless of the days or hours they work. [^ak-overtime-employee-count-includes-officers]

The overtime section lists other exempt work, including casual employees as defined by regulation and hospital employees whose employment includes providing medical services. [^ak-overtime-hospital-medical-exemption] The Act's minimum wage and overtime requirements also exclude employees in a bona fide executive, administrative or professional capacity, outside and straight-commission salespeople, and computer systems analysts, programmers, software engineers and similarly skilled workers. [^ak-white-collar-exemption] The executive, administrative and professional duties tests follow the Fair Labor Standards Act and its regulations. [^ak-exempt-duties-federal-meaning]

Alaska sets its own salary floor for those executive, administrative and professional employees: a salary or fee of at least two times the state minimum wage for the first 40 hours each week, excluding board or lodging. [^ak-exempt-salary-two-times-minimum] At the $14.00 rate in effect since July 1, 2026, that is at least $1,120 a week. [^ak-exempt-salary-fourteen-dollar-base] [^ak-exempt-salary-two-times-minimum] An exempt employee must receive the full salary for any week in which the employee does any work, subject to the federal salary-deduction exceptions and a daily-rate option. [^ak-exempt-full-salary-any-work-week] Under that option, an exempt employee may instead be paid a daily rate of at least $300 if the two-times-minimum salary floor is met and the full daily salary is paid for any day on which work is done. [^ak-exempt-daily-rate-option]

Paying a salary does not by itself make an employee exempt. A salaried employee whose work is not exempt must be paid overtime under a written employment contract that states the expected straight-time and overtime hours and the corresponding rates. [^ak-salaried-nonexempt-overtime-contract] If the contract does not fix the hours the salary covers, the salary is treated as pay for an eight-hour day and a 40-hour week. [^ak-salary-default-eight-forty] A Belo guaranteed-pay plan and compensatory time off instead of money do not satisfy Alaska's overtime requirement. [^ak-comp-time-not-overtime]

An employer and employee may adopt a voluntary flexible work hour plan of 40 hours a week and not more than 10 hours a day. The plan must be in a signed written agreement filed with the department and certified by it, and overtime is still owed for work over 40 hours a week or 10 hours a day. [^ak-overtime-voluntary-flex-plan] The request must state that the plan is voluntary and not a condition of employment. [^ak-flex-plan-voluntary-not-condition]

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Alaska's break rule applies only to workers under 18, and the Department of Labor and Workforce Development states that employers are not required to give breaks to employees 18 and over. [^ak-dol-no-adult-breaks] A minor scheduled to work six consecutive hours or more is entitled to a break of at least 30 minutes, which the employer may schedule at its convenience after the first hour and a half of work and before the last hour. [^ak-minor-thirty-minute-break]

A minor who works five consecutive hours without a break is entitled to a 30-minute break before continuing. A collective bargaining agreement may modify the rule, and so may occasional mutual agreement between the employer and the minor. [^ak-minor-five-hours-without-break]

The minor's break is unpaid, but failing to provide it, or providing it late, creates a minimum wage liability for the missed break that is enforceable under the Wage and Hour Act's remedies section. That liability applies even to part-time minors otherwise excluded from the Act. [^ak-minor-missed-break-minimum-wage-liability] The break rule does not apply to minors employed in fishing and related aquatic harvesting or to minors employed by a listed family member. [^ak-minor-break-exclusions]

When an employer does allow breaks, the department's guidance is that a break lasting less than 20 minutes must be paid. [^ak-dol-short-breaks-paid] The same guidance says a meal period need not be paid if it lasts more than 20 minutes and the employee does no work during it. [^ak-dol-meal-period-unpaid]

Federal break rules are discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** When an employer ends the employment, for any reason, final pay is due within three working days after the termination. [^ak-final-pay-three-working-days] When the employee quits, final pay is due at the next regular payday that is at least three days after the employer received notice. [^ak-final-pay-resignation-next-payday] An employer that pays late may be required to pay a penalty of the employee's regular wages from the time of demand to the time of payment, or for 90 working days, whichever is less. [^ak-penalty-wages-ninety-days]

On termination all wages become due immediately and must be paid where the employee is usually paid or at a location the employer and employee agree on. [^ak-final-pay-place-of-payment]

The penalty is discretionary in a private action, but in an action brought by the department the employer must pay it, calculated at the employee's straight-time rate for an eight-hour day. [^ak-penalty-wages-mandatory-department-action] In a private action for unpaid overtime that results in liquidated damages under the Wage and Hour Act, the late-final-pay penalty does not apply. [^ak-penalty-wages-not-stacked-on-overtime-damages]

If the amount of wages is disputed, the employer must give written notice of the amount it concedes and pay that amount without condition within the time the statute sets. [^ak-wages-in-dispute-pay-conceded] Accepting that payment does not release the rest of the claim, and a release required as a condition of payment is void. [^ak-wages-in-dispute-release-void]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Unless an annual initial employment contract establishes monthly pay periods, an Alaska employee may elect monthly or semi-monthly pay periods, and the employer must establish the periods the employee elects. [^ak-pay-periods-monthly-or-semimonthly]

At hiring, the employer must notify the employee in writing of the payday, the place of payment and the rate of pay, and must give notice of any change on the payday before the change. [^ak-notice-of-payday-and-rate]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** An Alaska employer must give each employee a written or electronic statement of earnings and deductions for each pay period. The statement must show the rate of pay, gross and net wages, the pay-period dates, federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare and Alaska unemployment deductions, board or lodging deductions, advances, straight-time and overtime hours worked, other authorized deductions, and sick leave used and remaining. [^ak-pay-statement-contents]

A written agreement can authorize deductions for an employee's monetary obligations, but an employer may not require or induce an employee to give back any part of the compensation due. [^ak-deductions-no-forced-kickbacks] A written deduction agreement payable to the employer is invalid if it would reduce the employee's pay below the minimum wage or overtime rate, or if it would make the employee reimburse the employer for listed losses. [^ak-deductions-written-agreement-limits] Such an agreement can never make the employee repay returned customer checks or losses from customer theft or credit default. Cash shortages and lost, missing or stolen property may be charged only if the employee willingly admits in writing to having personally taken the specific cash or property, and damage or breakage only if it was clearly due to the employee's willful conduct and the employee acknowledged responsibility in writing. [^ak-deductions-shortages-and-damage]

Employers must keep payroll records for at least three years, including each employee's name, address, occupation, rate of pay, amount paid each pay period, and hours worked each day and each week. [^ak-records-three-years] Those records belong at the employer's central office in Alaska, and duplicates may be kept at work sites. [^ak-records-central-office]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** The Alaska Wage and Hour Act borrows the Fair Labor Standards Act's definitions for terms it does not define itself, and the Alaska Supreme Court has applied the federal economic-realities test to decide whether a worker is an employee for Alaska minimum wage and overtime. [^ak-wage-hour-act-flsa-definitions] [^ak-classification-jeffcoat-dependence-opening] Federal classification is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The Alaska Supreme Court applied that federal approach in *Jeffcoat v. State, Department of Labor*, adopting a superior court decision as its own opinion. [^ak-jeffcoat-adopts-trial-court-opinion] Finding no Alaska decision on the employee-contractor line under the state counterpart to the federal Act, the decision turned to federal case law. [^ak-jeffcoat-federal-authorities] The decision used a six-part federal inquiry: the right to control the work, the opportunity for profit or loss, investment in equipment or helpers, special skill, permanence of the relationship, and whether the work is an integral part of the business. [^ak-jeffcoat-six-part-inquiry] No single factor controls. [^ak-jeffcoat-single-factor-not-controlling] The factors are weighed with the worker's economic dependence on the business as the focus. [^ak-jeffcoat-economic-reality-focus]

Alaska unemployment insurance uses a three-part statutory test. Service is employment unless the department is shown that the worker is free from control and direction, the service is outside the usual course or places of the business, and the worker is customarily engaged in an independently established business of the same kind. [^ak-unemployment-abc-test] That test is limited by its own text to the unemployment insurance chapter, so by its terms it does not govern minimum wage or overtime claims under the Wage and Hour Act. [^ak-unemployment-definition-chapter-scope]

The Wage and Hour Act also excludes one category of driver by statute: a taxicab driver paid only by customers under flat-rate written arrangements that place no limits on hours or areas worked beyond local ordinances. [^ak-taxicab-driver-exclusion]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Alaska does not allow a tip credit. An employer may not count tips or gratuities toward the minimum wage, and the federal tip credit does not apply to the Alaska minimum wage. [^ak-no-tip-credit] A tipped employee must therefore receive the full $14.00 an hour in effect since July 1, 2026, from the employer before tips. [^ak-tipped-minimum-fourteen-dollars]

An employer may not handle or take possession or control of an employee's tips except as the department's tip rule allows. [^ak-employer-may-not-take-tips] One allowed purpose is a tip pool among service employees, which requires written notice to all service employees, written notice of any change by the payday before the payday on which it takes effect, and no retroactive pooling. [^ak-tip-pool-written-notice] Service employees include hosts, order-takers, servers, bussers, dishwashers and cooks, but not employees whose primary duty is managerial or administrative. [^ak-tip-pool-service-employees]

An employer that pays a percentage fee on credit card charges may reduce a credit card tip by no more than that percentage. [^ak-credit-card-tip-fee] A compulsory service charge that is part of the employer's gross receipts is not a tip. [^ak-service-charge-not-tip]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An Alaska employer owes unpaid minimum wages or overtime plus equal liquidated damages, but in overtime actions not brought by the commissioner, a court may reduce them on clear and convincing proof of good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the employer complied. [^ak-liquidated-damages-equal-amount] [^ak-enforcement-overtime-good-faith-local] [^ak-commissioner-actions-no-good-faith-reduction] An employee may sue personally and for similarly situated employees, or through a designated agent. [^ak-private-and-collective-action] The court must award costs and, subject to the overtime fee rules, reasonable attorney fees against the employer. [^ak-costs-and-attorney-fees] The action must be filed within two years after the claim accrues. [^ak-wage-hour-two-year-limitation]

That overtime defense requires the employer to prove that it acted in good faith and had reasonable grounds to believe it was complying. [^ak-overtime-good-faith-defense] Failing to look into Alaska law is inconsistent with a claim of good faith. [^ak-good-faith-requires-inquiry] If an overtime defendant wins after making an offer of judgment, the court must award it attorney fees unless the plaintiff proves the case was brought and pursued in good faith on reasonable grounds. [^ak-overtime-fee-shifting-offer-of-judgment]

An employee keeps the right to liquidated damages in an overtime settlement that the department or a court does not supervise, unless the employee expressly waives them in a written settlement agreement. That private agreement is not valid unless it is submitted to the department, which approves it only if it is fair to the parties, and a waiver of liquidated damages may not be made a condition of employment. [^ak-private-overtime-settlement-review]

The Department of Labor and Workforce Development also enforces wage claims. At an underpaid employee's request, the commissioner may take an assignment of the claim in trust and sue to collect it. [^ak-commissioner-assignment-in-trust] The department may hold hearings on wage claims and cooperate with an employee in enforcing a claim it considers just and valid. [^ak-department-wage-claim-hearings] It may not accept assignment of a claim larger than the district court's small-claims limit for wage claims. [^ak-department-assignment-small-claims-cap] When an investigation finds probable cause of a Wage and Hour Act violation, the division first tries to correct it through an informal conference. [^ak-division-conference-and-persuasion]

An employer violates the Act if it discharges or discriminates against an employee for filing a complaint, starting a proceeding or testifying. [^ak-retaliation-violation] A violation of the Act or its regulations is a crime punishable by a fine of $100 to $2,000, 10 to 90 days in jail, or both, and each day of violation is a separate offense. [^ak-criminal-penalty]
