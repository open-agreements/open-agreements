# Wage and Hour Law in Connecticut[^about]

How Connecticut law sets the indexed minimum wage, weekly overtime, the meal period, final-pay deadlines, pay frequency, pay statements and deductions, worker classification, the tip credit, and double-damages wage claims.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Connecticut's minimum wage is $16.94 per hour for 2026 and will rise to $17.48 per hour on January 1, 2027. [^ct-minimum-wage-2027-announcement]
The statute adjusts the rate each January 1 by the percentage change in the federal employment cost index for wages and salaries over the 12 months ending the previous June 30. [^ct-minimum-wage-annual-adjustment] The Labor Commissioner must announce each adjustment by October 15, effective the following January 1. [^ct-minimum-wage-commissioner-announcement] The Governor's office announced the 2027 rate on August 5, 2026. [^ct-minimum-wage-2027-announcement]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The state rate can never fall below the scheduled statutory amount or one-half of one percent above the highest federal minimum wage, whichever is greater. [^ct-minimum-wage-federal-plus-half-percent]

Employees under 18, other than emancipated minors, may be paid not less than 85% of the minimum wage or $10.10 per hour, whichever is greater, for their first 90 days of employment, and must be paid the full minimum wage after that, except in institutional training programs the commissioner specifically exempts. [^ct-minor-ninety-day-training-rate] An employer may not displace an employee, including by cutting the employee's hours, wages or benefits, to hire workers under 18 at the lower rate, and the commissioner must suspend the right to pay the reduced rate when that rule is violated. [^ct-minor-rate-anti-displacement]

The minimum wage law's definition of an employee excludes, among others, individuals employed in camps or resorts open no more than six months a year, domestic service in or about a private home other than domestic service employment as federal Fair Labor Standards Act regulations define it, individuals employed in a bona fide executive, administrative or professional capacity as the Labor Commissioner's regulations define it, babysitters, outside salespeople as federal regulations define them, and members of the state armed forces performing military duty. [^ct-minimum-wage-employee-exclusions] Since October 1, 2026, the definition also excludes an individual who has contracted to play minor league baseball and is paid under a collective bargaining agreement. [^ct-minor-league-baseball-exclusion] [^ct-pa-26-12-section-40-effective-date]

Each employer subject to the minimum wage part must keep a true and accurate record of each employee's hours and wages at the place of employment for three years, unless a regulation exempts it. [^ct-minimum-wage-three-year-records] The employer must also keep the applicable minimum fair wage order and the commissioner's regulations posted where employees can easily read them. [^ct-minimum-wage-records-and-posting]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Connecticut's general overtime rule is weekly: unless an exemption applies, a Connecticut employer must pay at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. [^ct-overtime-forty-hour-workweek] [^ct-overtime-exemptions]
A hospital may instead use a 14-day overtime period agreed with the employee before the work is performed, but it must then pay at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over eight in any workday and over 80 in the 14-day period. [^ct-hospital-fourteen-day-overtime]
The overtime sections use the minimum wage law's definition of an employee, so the exclusions in that definition, including bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees, carry over to overtime. [^ct-overtime-employee-definition] [^ct-minimum-wage-employee-exclusions-overtime]
The overtime sections also do not apply to listed categories, including drivers and helpers subject to federal hours-of-service rules, seamen, certain radio and television employees, outside salespeople, certain commissioned inside salespeople and automobile salespeople, certain taxicab drivers and route salespeople, and agricultural employees. [^ct-overtime-exemptions]

Hours worked include all time an employee is required to be on the employer's premises, on duty or at the prescribed workplace, and all time the employee is employed or permitted to work, whether or not required to do so; meal time is excluded unless the employee is required or permitted to work during it. [^ct-hours-worked-definition] Under the Labor Department's regulation, working time is computed to the nearest unit of 15 minutes. [^ct-hours-worked-fifteen-minute-unit] Time an employee is required to spend on call for emergency service at a location the employer designates is working time and must be paid, whether or not the employee is called to work. [^ct-on-call-at-designated-location]

An employee paid less than the overtime wage to which the employee is entitled under the minimum wage sections or a minimum fair wage order recovers twice the unpaid amount, with costs and reasonable attorney's fees, in a civil action, or the unpaid amount alone if the employer proves a good faith belief that its pay complied with the law. [^ct-minimum-overtime-civil-action]

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Subject to statutory exceptions, no person in Connecticut may be required to work seven and one-half or more consecutive hours without at least 30 consecutive minutes for a meal, given after the first two hours of work and before the last two hours. [^ct-meal-period-seven-and-half-hours] The exceptions cover employers that give 30 or more total minutes of paid rest or meal periods in each seven-and-one-half-hour period, written agreements for a different schedule, commissioner exemptions, pre-July 1, 1990 collective bargaining agreements, and certified school professionals working directly with children. [^ct-meal-period-paid-break-alternative] [^ct-meal-period-written-agreement] [^ct-meal-period-commissioner-exemptions] [^ct-meal-period-cba-preserved] [^ct-meal-period-teacher-exclusion]
No Connecticut statute requires rest breaks for adult employees, and the meal period may be unpaid, because hours worked exclude meal time unless the employee is required or permitted to work during it. [^ct-hours-worked-definition-meal-rest-breaks]

The meal-period rule does not apply to an employer that provides 30 or more total minutes of paid rest or meal periods within each seven-and-one-half-hour work period. [^ct-meal-period-paid-break-alternative] An employer and employee may also agree in writing to a different schedule of meal periods. [^ct-meal-period-written-agreement]

The Labor Commissioner must exempt an employer when compliance would be adverse to public safety, when a position's duties can be performed by only one employee, for employees on a shift with fewer than five employees at a single place of business, or when continuous operations require employees to be available at all times and those employees are paid for break and meal periods. [^ct-meal-period-commissioner-exemptions] The rule also does not alter a collective bargaining agreement in effect on July 1, 1990, and does not apply to certified professional employees of a board of education who work directly with children. [^ct-meal-period-cba-preserved] [^ct-meal-period-teacher-exclusion]

An employer that violates the meal-period rule may be subject to the Labor Department's civil penalty. [^ct-meal-period-civil-penalty]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** A Connecticut employer that discharges an employee must pay the employee's wages in full by the next business day after the discharge. [^ct-final-pay-discharge-next-business-day] An employee who quits must be paid in full no later than the next regular payday, and an employee who is laid off, or whose work is suspended by a labor dispute, must be paid wages earned no later than the next regular payday. [^ct-final-pay-voluntary-quit] [^ct-final-pay-layoff-labor-dispute]
Late or unpaid final wages can be recovered in a civil action for twice the unpaid amount, with costs and reasonable attorney's fees, unless the employer proves a good faith belief that its pay complied with the law, in which case the recovery is the unpaid amount with costs and fees. [^ct-wage-claim-double-damages]

Accrued vacation, holiday, sick and other leave is payable at termination only if an employer policy or collective bargaining agreement provides for payment of accrued fringe benefits on termination. When the policy or agreement does, the employer must pay those benefits, other than normal pension benefits, as wages under the policy or agreement and at no less than the earned average rate for the accrual period. [^ct-accrued-fringe-benefits-at-termination] The double-damages civil action covers a failure to pay those fringe benefits. [^ct-wage-claim-double-damages] Each employer must make its policies on wages, vacation pay, sick leave and similar matters available to employees in writing or through a posted notice. [^ct-hiring-notice-and-policies]

When the amount of wages is disputed, the employer must pay without condition, and on time, all wages it concedes are due, and the employee keeps every remedy for the balance claimed. [^ct-disputed-wages-pay-conceded-amount] An employee's acceptance of that payment does not release the balance, and any release the employer demands as a condition of payment is void. [^ct-disputed-wages-no-release]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Unless a statutory exception or a schedule the Labor Commissioner approves applies, a Connecticut employer must pay all wages weekly or once every two weeks on a regular payday designated in advance, by cash, negotiable check, direct deposit on the employee's written or electronic request, or a qualifying payroll card. [^ct-pay-weekly-or-biweekly] [^ct-pay-frequency-commissioner-waiver] [^ct-pay-schedule-school-agreements]
Subject to the same exceptions, the pay period paid on a regular payday must end no more than eight days before that payday, and when the payday falls on a nonworkday, payment is due on the preceding workday. [^ct-pay-period-eight-day-lag]

The statutory exceptions allow a local or regional board of education or a state-aided institution to set a different wage payment schedule in a collective bargaining agreement with its employees' exclusive bargaining representative, and allow a private or parochial school to set one by written agreement with its employees. [^ct-pay-schedule-school-agreements]

On application, the Labor Commissioner may waive the weekly or biweekly schedule for particular weeks, or allow an employer to set pay periods less often than every two weeks, but each affected employee must still be paid in full at least once each calendar month on a regular schedule. [^ct-pay-frequency-commissioner-waiver]

At hiring, each employer must tell employees in writing their rate of pay, hours of employment and wage payment schedule. [^ct-hiring-notice-and-policies-pay-frequency]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** Subject to a statutory exemption from reporting hours and separating straight-time and overtime earnings, with each wage payment a Connecticut employer must give each employee a record of hours worked, gross earnings with straight time and overtime shown separately, itemized deductions and net earnings. [^ct-pay-statement-contents]
The record may be electronic with the employee's explicit consent, and the hours and the straight-time and overtime split are not required for an employee for whom the employer is specifically exempt from timekeeping and overtime under state or federal law. [^ct-pay-statement-contents]
An employer may withhold or divert part of an employee's wages only when state or federal law requires or permits it, when the employee has given written authorization on a form the Labor Commissioner approves, or in the other limited cases the statute lists. [^ct-wage-deductions-limited]

Since October 1, 2026, an employer with 100 or more employees must also create a guide to its pay codes for overtime and its most commonly used pay differentials. [^ct-pay-code-guide-employer-threshold] [^ct-pay-code-guide-required] [^ct-pa-26-12-section-38-effective-date] The guide must include at least ten pay codes where applicable, be posted on the employer's website in English, Spanish and the other most common languages of its employees, include contact information for the designated office or individual who handles disputes about hours and pay-differential calculations, and be updated whenever a new overtime or differential pay code is added. [^ct-pay-code-guide-posting] [^ct-pay-code-guide-updates] The employer must give the guide's web address to each employee at hire and include it on each pay record, or may instead give a written copy of the guide at hire in English and the employee's primary language. [^ct-pay-code-guide-delivery] An employer that uses a third-party payroll company providing a compliant pay-code guide is deemed to comply. [^ct-pay-code-guide-payroll-vendor]

The other permitted deductions are those the employee authorizes in writing for medical, surgical or hospital care without financial benefit to the employer and recorded in the employer's wage record book, automatic-enrollment retirement contributions, and income tax another state requires the employer to withhold. [^ct-wage-deductions-limited]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** For a wage payment claim under § 31-72, the Connecticut Supreme Court has held that the ABC test used for unemployment compensation also applies to decide whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor. [^ct-tianti-abc-test-applies]
Under that ABC test, service is employment unless the worker is free from control and direction, the service is outside the usual course of the business or outside all its places of business, and the worker is customarily engaged in an independently established business of the same nature. [^ct-unemployment-abc-test]

Because the test is conjunctive, the court found employment once it concluded that the business had the right to control the workers, without reaching the other two prongs. [^ct-tianti-conjunctive-right-to-control] The court treated commissioned real estate salespeople as employees within § 31-72, rather than independent contractors, even though they received 1099 forms and no medical benefits. [^ct-tianti-salespeople-employees] [^ct-tianti-1099-not-decisive]

The wage payment law defines an employee to include any person suffered or permitted to work by an employer, and wages to include compensation calculated by time, task, piece, commission or another basis. [^ct-wage-payment-employee-suffered-permitted] [^ct-wages-definition-any-basis] The minimum wage law separately covers any individual employed or permitted to work by an employer, subject to listed exclusions. [^ct-minimum-wage-employee-exclusions-worker-classification] The Labor Department states that Connecticut agencies use different tests to determine employment status for different laws. [^ct-dol-agencies-use-different-tests]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Connecticut allows a partial tip credit for hotel and restaurant employees who customarily and regularly receive tips, with a cash wage of at least $6.38 per hour, or $8.23 per hour for bartenders. [^ct-tip-credit-employer-share] Tips count toward the difference between that cash wage and the minimum wage. [^ct-tip-credit-hotel-restaurant] [^ct-tip-credit-bartenders] [^ct-tip-credit-employer-share]
Tips count toward the minimum wage only when the employee works in an occupation where tips have customarily been part of pay, the tips claimed are recorded in a wage record, and the employer has substantial evidence that the employee received at least the amount claimed. [^ct-tip-credit-regulation-conditions]

The Labor Department's regulation accepts, as that substantial evidence, an electronic or written attestation or statement showing that wages, tips and other authorized allowances together equal at least the minimum wage for each hour of the pay period. [^ct-tip-credit-attestation] A claim about tips as part of the minimum wage filed after September 24, 2022, is decided solely under that regulation as effective September 24, 2020, and its amendments. [^ct-tip-claims-governed-by-regulation]

Outside the hotel and restaurant industry, the statute limits the tip allowance to no more than 35 cents per hour. [^ct-tip-allowance-other-industries]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** Subject to the employer's statutory good-faith defense, a Connecticut employee who is not paid wages as the wage payment statutes require may sue and recover twice the unpaid amount, with costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^ct-wage-claim-double-damages-enforcement] The same action covers accrued fringe benefits that an employer policy or collective bargaining agreement provides for payment on termination [^ct-accrued-fringe-benefits-at-termination-enforcement], and recovery is limited to the unpaid amount with costs and fees if the employer proves a good faith belief that it complied with the law. [^ct-wage-claim-double-damages-enforcement] An employee paid less than the minimum fair wage or overtime wage due under the minimum wage sections has a parallel civil action with the same double recovery and good-faith limit. [^ct-minimum-overtime-civil-action-enforcement]
An action for wages payable periodically must be brought within two years after the right of action accrues, and that period is tolled when the employee files a wage complaint with the Labor Commissioner. [^ct-wage-action-two-year-limit]

An agreement between employee and employer to be paid other than as the wage payment statutes require is no defense to a wage action. [^ct-wage-agreement-no-defense] The Labor Commissioner may collect unpaid wages with interest from the date they were due [^ct-commissioner-collects-wages-interest], and may sue to recover twice the unpaid wages, with the employer paying costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^ct-commissioner-collects-double-wages] On a complaint of nonpayment, the commissioner or the director of Wage and Workplace Standards may enter the place of business during usual business hours, examine payroll records, interview employees and hold hearings. [^ct-commissioner-wage-investigation]

An employer may not discharge, discipline, penalize or discriminate against an employee for filing a wage claim, taking part in a proceeding, or exercising rights under the wage statutes, and an employee who prevails on a retaliation complaint before the commissioner is awarded reasonable attorney's fees and costs. [^ct-wage-retaliation-prohibited] [^ct-wage-retaliation-remedies]

Violations of the wage and employment-regulation chapters generally also carry a civil penalty of $300 per violation payable to the Labor Department, with different amounts for listed violations such as stop-work-order and certain child-labor violations. [^ct-labor-civil-penalty-per-violation] An employer, officer or agent who violates the wage payment part faces criminal fines and imprisonment scaled to the unpaid amount, and a violation is a class D felony when more than $2,000 is owed to an employee. [^ct-wage-payment-criminal-penalty]

For a construction contract entered into on or after January 1, 2027, other than a public works or government contract or a covered home improvement contract, a contractor will be jointly and severally liable for a subcontractor's unpaid wages for labor within the contract's scope. [^ct-construction-contractor-joint-liability] [^ct-construction-contract-definition] [^ct-construction-contract-home-improvement-exclusion] The excluded contracts are public works and other contracts with a Connecticut, other-state or federal agency or department, and home improvement contracts for an owner-occupied residence or a one- or two-family dwelling, unless the contract is to build more than fifteen one- or two-family units at one project site. [^ct-construction-contract-definition] [^ct-construction-contract-home-improvement-exclusion] An employee who sues the contractor must give the contractor notice describing the subcontractor's alleged violation at least 30 days before suing, unless the employee already gave the contractor notice of the same violation or a prior violation by the same subcontractor. [^ct-construction-wage-claim-notice] [^ct-construction-notice-exception]
