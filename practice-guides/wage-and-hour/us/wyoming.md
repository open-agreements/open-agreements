# Wage and Hour Law in Wyoming[^about]

How Wyoming's $5.15 state minimum wage sits below the $7.25 federal rate, so most Wyoming workers are owed the federal wage, how Wyoming leaves most overtime and breaks to federal law, requires semimonthly paydays for specified industries and itemized deduction statements for nonagricultural employers, generally pays final wages by the next regular payroll date, allows 18% interest and attorney fees in a suit that establishes wages justly due, lets an employer offset final wages for debts incurred during employment, and enforces wage claims through the Department of Workforce Services.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Wyoming's ordinary minimum wage is $5.15 an hour, below the $7.25 federal minimum, subject to special tipped-employee and introductory youth rates. [^wy-minimum-wage-rate] [^wy-federal-minimum-wage-rate] [^wy-tipped-cash-wage-mw] [^wy-youth-wage] The lower state figure does not reduce what federal law requires: the U.S. Department of Labor states that Wyoming employers subject to the federal Fair Labor Standards Act must pay the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour. [^wy-dol-federal-rate-applies] The federal act says it does not excuse noncompliance with a state minimum wage that is higher than the federal one, and Wyoming's is not higher. [^wy-federal-higher-wage-controls] For an employee covered by both laws, the ordinary minimum wage is therefore the federal $7.25. [^wy-federal-minimum-wage-rate] [^wy-federal-higher-wage-controls] The $5.15 state wage is the ordinary floor for an employee who falls outside federal coverage but within Wyoming's own definition of employee. [^wy-minimum-wage-rate] [^wy-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

Wyoming's definition of employee leaves out several groups, so the state minimum wage does not reach them at all. It excludes individuals employed in agriculture, in domestic service in a private home, in a bona fide executive, administrative or professional capacity, by the United States, the state or a political subdivision, and volunteers or others in educational, charitable, religious or nonprofit organizations where no employment relationship in fact exists. [^wy-employee-definition-exclusions] It also excludes an outside salesman paid solely by commission on sales and an on-call ambulance or other vehicle driver. [^wy-employee-definition-salesmen-drivers] An employee in one of those groups who is covered by the federal act is still owed the federal minimum wage unless a federal exemption applies. [^wy-federal-minimum-wage-rate]

Wyoming allows an employer to pay an employee under age 20 at least $4.25 an hour during the first 90 consecutive days of employment, and bars displacing other employees to hire workers at that wage. [^wy-youth-wage] Federal law has a parallel $4.25 rate for employees under 20 during their first 90 consecutive calendar days with an employer. [^wy-federal-youth-wage] [^wy-federal-youth-wage-age]

Every employer subject to the state minimum wage must keep, for at least two years, a record of each employee's name, address, occupation, rate of pay, amount paid each pay period and hours worked each day and each workweek. [^wy-minimum-wage-records]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** No Wyoming statute sets a general overtime requirement for private employers. One Wyoming overtime statute covers state and county employees: it sets their lawful day's and week's work at eight hours and 40 hours, and allows them to be paid one and one-half times their regular compensation for hours required beyond those limits. [^wy-state-county-overtime] Another limits laborers, workmen and mechanics on public works of a public entity to eight hours a day and 40 hours a week unless they agree to work more, in which case they must be paid one and one-half times the regular hourly rate for hours over 40 in a week. The limit does not apply in an emergency caused by fire, flood or danger to life or property, or to work on public or military works or defenses in time of war. [^wy-public-works-overtime] A covered, nonexempt private employee in Wyoming earns overtime under federal law, at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. [^wy-federal-overtime-forty-hours]
Federal law on this point is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The federal rule counts hours by the workweek, not by the day. [^wy-federal-overtime-forty-hours] One federal alternative uses a daily threshold: a hospital, or a residential institution primarily engaged in caring for the sick, the aged or the mentally ill, may, by agreement with the employee before the work is performed, use a 14-day work period if it pays time and a half for work over eight hours in a workday and over 80 hours in the period. [^wy-federal-hospital-fourteen-day]

A federal executive, administrative or professional exemption generally requires a salary of at least $684 a week. [^wy-federal-white-collar-salary-684] An exempt employee generally must receive the full salary for any week in which the employee performs any work, subject to the exceptions in the federal salary-basis rule. [^wy-federal-salary-basis-full-week]

## Does Wyoming require meal or rest breaks? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** No Wyoming statute requires a private employer to give adult employees meal or rest breaks. When an employer does give short rest periods, federal regulations treat breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes as customarily paid and counted as hours worked. [^wy-federal-rest-periods-paid]

Federal law generally requires an employer to provide a covered employee reasonable break time, and a private place other than a bathroom, to express breast milk for one year after the child's birth. [^wy-federal-lactation-break] The break time need not be paid unless other law requires it, but it counts as hours worked if the employee is not completely relieved from duty during the break. [^wy-federal-lactation-unpaid] [^wy-federal-lactation-hours-worked] An employer with fewer than 50 employees is exempt from the federal break requirement if compliance would impose an undue hardship. [^wy-federal-lactation-small-employer]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** Except for certain commissioned sales agents, when a Wyoming employee quits or is discharged, the employee must be paid the wages due no later than the employer's usual practice on regularly scheduled payroll dates, or at a time set by a collective bargaining agreement. [^wy-final-pay-next-payroll] The statute lets the employer offset from the final wages sums the employee owes it that were incurred during employment, but Department of Workforce Services rules allow only the offsets they list, such as repayment of acknowledged advances and loans. [^wy-final-pay-next-payroll] [^wy-offset-rules-only-listed-fp] [^wy-offset-advances-loans-fp] The rule does not apply to a commissioned sales agent who has custody of the principal's accounts, money or goods where the net amount due cannot be determined until after an audit or verification. [^wy-final-pay-next-payroll] An employee who sues for wages earned and due and establishes in court the amount justly due recovers interest on the past-due wages at 18% a year, a reasonable attorney fee and all costs of suit. [^wy-final-pay-interest-fees] A willful violation of the final-pay rule is also a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of $500 to $750 for each offense. [^wy-final-pay-misdemeanor]

The interest and fee remedy depends on proving wages, not just any contract damages. In *Jensen v. Fremont Motors Cody, Inc.*, the Wyoming Supreme Court found that the employee had won contract damages without establishing that they were wages justly due, and stated that proceedings outside the scope of the wage claim statute, although related to employment, do not allow recovery of attorney fees. [^wy-jensen-wages-justly-due] [^wy-jensen-no-fees-outside-statute] The court also held that interest under the statute could be denied where the amount of past-due wages was uncertain. [^wy-jensen-unliquidated-interest]

For a claim the Department of Workforce Services handles, wages include fringe benefits, but not accrued vacation that the employer's written policy says is forfeited on termination, if the employee has acknowledged that policy in writing. [^wy-wages-definition-vacation] An employee whose work stops because of a labor dispute or a temporary layoff must be paid wages earned to that point on the next regular payday. [^wy-layoff-next-payday]

The federal Fair Labor Standards Act still applies to the final paycheck. Overtime earned in a workweek generally must be paid on the regular payday for the period in which that workweek ends. [^wy-federal-overtime-regular-payday] Wages count as paid under federal law only if they are paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear. [^wy-federal-free-and-clear-fp]

## How often must workers be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Wyoming requires employers operating railroads, mines, refineries, oil and gas work, factories, mills or workshops to pay wages semimonthly on statutory deadlines. [^wy-semimonthly-paydays] Those employers must pay wages earned in the first half of a month by the first day of the next month, and wages earned in the second half by the 15th of the next month, and, for personnel regularly paid at one location, a payday that falls on a nonworking day moves to the last preceding working day. [^wy-semimonthly-paydays] The same sentence directs every employer to establish and maintain regular paydays as the statute provides and to post copies of the law in at least two conspicuous places. [^wy-semimonthly-paydays] Agricultural operations are exempt. [^wy-agricultural-exemption] Violating the payday statute is a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of up to $750, up to six months in jail, or both. [^wy-payday-misdemeanor]

An employee absent on a payday may collect the wages due at any later time on demand to the paymaster or at the usual place of payment. [^wy-semimonthly-paydays] An employer may deposit wages directly into an employee's account at a bank, savings and loan association, credit union or other authorized financial institution only if the employee has voluntarily authorized the deposit. [^wy-itemized-deductions-statement-freq]

For an employee covered by federal law, overtime earned in a workweek generally must be paid on the regular payday for the period in which that workweek ends. [^wy-federal-overtime-regular-payday-frequency]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** Except for agricultural operations, each time a Wyoming employer pays wages, it must give each employee a detachable part of the check, draft or voucher, or a slip attached to the payment, with an itemized written statement of all deductions made from the wages. [^wy-itemized-deductions-statement] [^wy-agricultural-exemption-statements] The statute requires an itemized list of deductions, not a full list of hours, rates and gross pay, although an employer subject to the state minimum wage must keep its own records of each employee's rate of pay, amount paid each pay period and hours worked. [^wy-itemized-deductions-statement] [^wy-minimum-wage-records-statements]

Department of Workforce Services rules list the only sums an employer may offset from wages, and an agreement contrary to those rules is void. [^wy-offset-rules-only-listed] [^wy-offset-improper-agreements] The listed offsets include repayment of cash advances, loans, or payments of expenses for optional benefits such as tuition assistance, relocation and training, if paid during employment and acknowledged in writing. [^wy-offset-advances-loans] A cash-shortage offset requires a written acknowledgment at hire, written cash counts at the start and end of the work period, and the employee's sole access to the register or cash box. [^wy-offset-cash-shortages] By statute, an employer may not withhold money from an employee's wages because a check the employee accepted for the employer was dishonored, unless the employee failed to follow the employer's written check-acceptance procedures or the employer reasonably believes the employee took part in a fraud or other wrongdoing in taking the check. [^wy-dishonored-check-deduction] At separation, the final-pay statute lets the employer offset sums the employee owes it that were incurred during employment, within the limits of those rules. [^wy-final-pay-offset-statements] It is also unlawful for an employer to pay less than the wage set by a collective bargaining agreement or by the employer's contract with the employee. [^wy-agreed-wage-unlawful]

Federal law adds its own limits. Wages count as paid only if they are paid finally and unconditionally, or free and clear. [^wy-federal-free-and-clear-statements] A deduction for tools or similar items that are not board, lodging or other facilities is illegal to the extent it cuts the employee's wages below the federal minimum in a non-overtime workweek. [^wy-federal-tool-deductions-statements]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** For wage claims under Wyoming's wage-payment article, a worker is an employee if the usual common-law rules for deciding the employer-employee relationship make the worker one. [^wy-wage-claim-common-law-employee] Wyoming's unemployment and workers' compensation statutes use a different, statutory test: a worker who performs services for wages is an employee for unemployment purposes unless shown to be free from control or direction over the details of the work by contract and in fact, to represent the services to the public as self-employed or an independent contractor, and to be able to substitute another individual to do the work. [^wy-unemployment-contractor-test] [^wy-workers-comp-contractor-definition] For federal minimum wage and overtime, the economic-realities test asks whether the worker is economically dependent on the potential employer for work or is in business for themself. [^wy-federal-economic-realities]

Wyoming's minimum wage article defines employ to include to suffer or to permit to work, the same phrase federal law uses. [^wy-employ-suffer-or-permit] [^wy-federal-suffer-or-permit]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Wyoming law sets a $2.13 cash wage for tipped employees and requires the employer to pay the difference if the cash wage plus tips in a pay period does not reach the $5.15 state minimum wage. [^wy-tipped-cash-wage] [^wy-minimum-wage-rate-tips] For an employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, the employer must also satisfy the federal tip-credit requirements, because the state's lower figure does not excuse the federal minimum: the employer may pay a cash wage of at least $2.13 an hour only if tips make up the rest of the $7.25 federal minimum and it meets the federal notice and tip-retention conditions. [^wy-dol-federal-rate-applies-tips] [^wy-federal-tip-credit-formula] [^wy-federal-tip-credit-notice] If tips fall short, the employer must pay the balance so the employee receives at least the minimum wage. [^wy-federal-tip-shortfall]

Under Wyoming law, a tip employee is one who customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips, and tip employees must give their employers each month the daily record of tips required by federal tax law, which is prima facie proof of the tips received. [^wy-tip-employee-records] Wyoming makes tips and gratuities the sole property of the employees who receive them, not payable in whole or in part to the employer or anyone else. [^wy-tips-employee-property]

Federal law separately bars an employer from keeping its employees' tips, including by letting managers or supervisors keep any portion, whether or not it takes a tip credit. [^wy-federal-employer-may-not-keep-tips]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employer that pays less than the Wyoming minimum wage is liable to the employee for the unpaid minimum wage, and the employee may sue to recover it with reasonable attorney fees and costs. [^wy-minimum-wage-civil-action] The Department of Workforce Services takes claims for unpaid wages under the payday and final-pay statutes, generally capped at two months' wages per employee per claim outside bankruptcy. [^wy-dws-wage-claims] The department investigates a written claim and decides whether it is valid; either side may request a fair hearing in writing within 15 calendar days after receiving the determination, and an employer that does not comply with the resulting order faces a civil fine of up to $200 a day. [^wy-dws-claim-process] [^wy-dws-order-fine] For an employee covered by federal law, unpaid federal minimum wages and overtime are recoverable with an equal amount as liquidated damages, attorney fees and costs, and one or more employees may sue in federal or state court. [^wy-federal-unpaid-wages-liquidated-damages] [^wy-federal-attorney-fees] [^wy-federal-employee-action]

An employer may not discharge, harass, discipline or otherwise discriminate against an employee for filing a wage claim or taking part in a wage-claim proceeding, and an employer that does is liable for appropriate relief, including reinstatement, lost wages and an equal amount as liquidated damages. [^wy-wage-claim-anti-retaliation] A departing employee who sues and establishes the wages justly due also recovers 18% annual interest, attorney fees and costs under the final-pay statute. [^wy-final-pay-interest-fees-enforcement] A federal wage claim must be filed within two years after the cause of action accrued, or three years for a willful violation. [^wy-federal-limitations]
