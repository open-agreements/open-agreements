# Wage and Hour Law in Idaho[^about]

A plain-language guide to Idaho wage-and-hour law covering the $7.25 minimum wage that Idaho's Minimum Wage Law ties to the federal minimum wage, the $3.35 direct-wage floor for tipped employees, the absence of any Idaho overtime statute for private employers and of any meal or rest break statute for adult employees, final-pay deadlines and the late-pay penalty, monthly paydays and deduction statements, how Idaho statutes define employees, and how wage claims are enforced through the Idaho Department of Labor and the courts.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Idaho's standard minimum wage is $7.25 an hour for employees covered by its Minimum Wage Law [^idaho-minimum-wage-hourly-rate]. The statute says this amount conforms to, and tracks with, the federal minimum wage [^idaho-minimum-wage-federal-tracking].
No Idaho political subdivision may set a higher minimum wage by ordinance or other action [^idaho-local-minimum-wage-preemption].
The Minimum Wage Law does not apply to certain groups of workers. The excluded groups include bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees; people in domestic service; outside salespeople; seasonal employees of a nonprofit camping program; children under sixteen working part time or at odd jobs for no more than four hours a day with any one employer; and employees under eighteen who work for an immediate family member or that family member's business [^idaho-minimum-wage-act-exemptions].
For workers under twenty, an employer may pay an employee who is not yet twenty years old at least $4.25 an hour during the first ninety consecutive calendar days after the employee is first hired [^idaho-under-twenty-opportunity-wage]. An employer may not displace employees to hire people at that wage. That ban covers partial displacements such as cuts to hours, wages or employment benefits [^idaho-opportunity-wage-displacement-bar].
For apprentices and learners, the director of the Idaho Department of Labor may issue an apprentice or learner a special license. The license allows employment below the minimum wage, for the time and under the conditions the director sets [^idaho-apprentice-learner-special-license].
For workers with disabilities, the minimum-wage payment requirement does not apply to a worker with disabilities if the employer is issued a special certificate under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act [^idaho-disability-special-certificate-exception].

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** No Idaho statute requires private employers to pay overtime, daily or weekly, and Idaho's state-personnel overtime statute for state employees does not reach private employers [^idaho-state-employee-overtime-compensation]. Under it, the appointing authority of a state department decides when overtime work is necessary and provides cash pay or compensatory time off for that work to eligible classified officers and employees, subject to applicable federal law [^idaho-state-employee-overtime-compensation].
Federal law on this point is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** No Idaho statute requires employers to give adult employees meal or rest breaks, and Idaho's working-hours rule for children under sixteen does not require a break either [^idaho-under-sixteen-hours-limit].
A person under sixteen may not work more than fifty-four hours in a week or more than nine hours in a day, and may not work before 6 a.m. or after 9 p.m. [^idaho-under-sixteen-hours-limit].
Children under fourteen face tighter limits. They may not work in or in connection with listed workplaces, including mines, factories, workshops, stores, laundries, restaurants and hotels [^idaho-under-fourteen-workplace-bar]. They also may not be employed in any business while the local public schools are in session, or before 6 a.m. or after 9 p.m. [^idaho-under-fourteen-school-hours]. A student may, however, be employed by the public schools of the district for up to ten hours a week if the employment is voluntary and the student's legal guardian consents [^idaho-under-fourteen-school-district-exception]. A child over twelve may also work in the occupations the chapter mentions during public school vacations of two weeks or more [^idaho-under-fourteen-school-hours].
These child-labor sections do not address meal periods, rest breaks, or whether break time is paid [^idaho-under-sixteen-hours-limit] [^idaho-under-fourteen-workplace-bar] [^idaho-under-fourteen-school-hours]. Federal law on this point is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** On layoff or termination by either side, Idaho employers must pay hourly or salaried employees all wages due by the earlier of the next regularly scheduled payday or within ten days, weekends and holidays excluded, unless an employee's written request or a director's extension applies [^idaho-final-pay-deadline]. A written request from the employee shortens the deadline to forty-eight hours after the employer receives it, weekends and holidays excluded, and the director of the Idaho Department of Labor may grant an employer a temporary extension on an application showing good and sufficient reasons [^idaho-final-pay-written-request] [^idaho-final-pay-director-extension]. The wages must be paid or made available at the usual place of payment [^idaho-final-pay-deadline].
Some employees are not paid on an hourly or salary basis. Unless they are exempt from the Minimum Wage Law, they must receive at least the applicable minimum wage for all hours worked in the pay period just before the layoff or termination [^idaho-final-pay-nonhourly-minimum]. Any additional wages owed to those employees must be paid by the next regularly scheduled payday [^idaho-final-pay-additional-wages-payday].
**Late-pay penalty.** If an employer fails to pay all wages due by these deadlines, the employee's wages continue at the same rate, as if the employee were still working in the same way, until the wages are paid in full or for fifteen days, whichever is less [^idaho-late-final-pay-penalty]. The penalty can never exceed $750. If the full wages are paid before a wage lien is filed, the cap is $500 [^idaho-late-pay-penalty-caps].
No penalty may be assessed when the employer pays all undisputed wages within the final-pay deadlines, unless the remaining balance was withheld willfully, arbitrarily and without just cause [^idaho-undisputed-wages-penalty-bar].
If an employee accepts a check that carries a restrictive endorsement, that does not release the employee's claim to the disputed amount and does not count as an accord and satisfaction [^idaho-restrictive-endorsement-no-release].

An employee who hides or stays away to avoid payment, or who refuses to receive payment made available under the final-pay rules, is not entitled to any penalty [^idaho-penalty-lost-avoiding-payment].

"Any employee who secretes or absents himself to avoid payment, or refuses to receive payment when made available as provided for in section 45-606, Idaho Code, shall not be entitled to any penalty under this chapter."[^idaho-penalty-lost-avoiding-payment]

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> A separation or settlement clause that delays undisputed final wages beyond the final-pay deadline forfeits the protection available for timely payment of undisputed wages [^idaho-undisputed-wages-penalty-bar].

## How often must workers be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Idaho employers must pay all wages due at least once each calendar month, on regular paydays the employer designates in advance [^idaho-monthly-regular-payday]. Payment may be made in lawful money of the United States, or by check if the employee can cash it without charge [^idaho-monthly-regular-payday].
The pay period covered by a regular payday must end no more than fifteen days before that payday. If the payday falls on a nonworkday, payment must be made on an earlier workday [^idaho-pay-period-fifteen-day-lag]. The director of the Idaho Department of Labor may allow an employer to pay later than that fifteen-day period on an application showing good and sufficient reasons [^idaho-director-pay-lag-permission]. The director may levy a civil penalty on an employer that failed to obtain that exemption and has been determined to have undertaken a consistent pattern of untimely payment of wages; the penalty may not exceed $500 for the employer per pay period [^idaho-payday-penalty-per-period].
**Deductions.** An employer may not withhold or divert any part of an employee's wages unless one of two conditions is met. Either state or federal law requires or empowers the employer to do so, or the employee has given written authorization for deductions for a lawful purpose [^idaho-wage-deduction-conditions].
**What the employee must receive.** For each pay period in which deductions are made, the employer must give each employee a statement of those deductions [^idaho-deduction-statement-required]. The cited sections do not list other items a pay stub must show [^idaho-deduction-statement-required].
At hiring, employers must tell employees their rate of pay and the usual payday, and must put that information in writing if the employee asks [^idaho-hiring-pay-rate-notice]. Employers must also give notice of any wage reduction before the work is performed, in writing if the employee asks [^idaho-wage-reduction-advance-notice].

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> A payroll statement form that omits deductions can expose the employer to misdemeanor liability when the required statement is willfully withheld [^idaho-deduction-statement-misdemeanor].

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** For Idaho wage-payment claims, the Idaho Court of Appeals in *Hill* treats the employer's retained right to control the work as the general test of employee status [^idaho-hill-right-to-control-test] [^idaho-hill-actual-indicia-over-labels]. *Hill* looks not to the labels the parties apply but to the actual indicia of the relationship [^idaho-hill-actual-indicia-over-labels]. Under that test, a person is deemed an employee when the employer retains the right to control and direct the details of the work and to set the hours and the times to start and stop [^idaho-hill-right-to-control-test].
Idaho's wage-payment chapter defines an employee as any person suffered or permitted to work by an employer [^idaho-wage-payment-employee-definition].
The Minimum Wage Law uses similar language. To employ includes to suffer or permit to work, and an employee includes any individual employed by an employer [^idaho-minimum-wage-employ-definition]. Under that law, an employer does not include the United States, any state or political subdivision of a state, or a labor organization except when it acts as an employer [^idaho-minimum-wage-employer-definition].
For purposes of the employment security law, services an individual performs for pay are covered employment unless both of two conditions are shown. First, the worker has been and will continue to be free from control or direction in doing the work, both under the contract and in fact. Second, the worker is engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, profession or business [^idaho-employment-security-control-test].
For worker's compensation purposes, title 72, chapter 1 defines an independent contractor as a person who performs services for a specified payment toward a specified result. The principal controls, or has the right to control, only the result of the work, not the means of achieving it [^idaho-workers-comp-contractor-definition].

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Idaho employers may count tips a tipped employee actually receives toward the employee's wage, but the direct wage the employer pays must be at least $3.35 an hour [^idaho-tipped-direct-wage-floor].
If the employee's tips plus the employer's direct wages do not at least equal the minimum wage, the employer must make up the difference [^idaho-tipped-wage-make-up-duty].
A tipped employee is one who works in an occupation in which the employee customarily and regularly receives more than $30 a month in tips [^idaho-tipped-employee-definition]. Any tips an employee shares with other employees through a tip pool or similar arrangement do not count as tips that employee actually received [^idaho-tip-pool-share-excluded]. If the employer and employee disagree about how much the employee received in tips, the employer bears the burden of showing the amount [^idaho-tip-dispute-employer-burden].

## How is it enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** Idaho workers owed wages under the wage-payment chapter may file a wage claim with the Idaho Department of Labor or, as an alternative, sue in any court of competent jurisdiction [^idaho-wage-claim-court-alternative].
**Department wage claims (title 45, chapter 6).** The director may hold hearings, investigate actual or alleged violations, and issue orders for the administrative remedies the chapter authorizes [^idaho-director-wage-hearings-orders]. Not counting potential penalties, wage claims filed with the department are capped at the same dollar amount that caps small claims cases in magistrate court [^idaho-department-claim-dollar-limit]. Once a wage claim is properly filed with the department, the department-claim section provides the exclusive remedy for resolving it [^idaho-department-claim-exclusive-remedy]. The department may award a penalty only if it specifically finds that wages were withheld willfully, arbitrarily and without just cause [^idaho-department-penalty-willful-finding].
**Lawsuits (title 45, chapter 6).** A court judgment for the employee may include costs and attorney's fees reasonably incurred. The employee recovers whichever is greater: the unpaid wages plus the late-pay penalties, or three times the unpaid wages [^idaho-wage-suit-fees-treble-damages]. An employee who sues on a claim the employee knew was false when filing is liable for the employer's attorney's fees and costs in defending against that claim [^idaho-false-wage-claim-fee-liability]. Knowingly making a false wage claim is also a misdemeanor, punishable by up to six months in county jail, a fine of up to $1,000, or both [^idaho-false-wage-claim-misdemeanor].
**Filing deadlines (title 45, chapter 6).** Claims for wages, penalties and liquidated damages must be filed with the department or in court within two years after the claim accrues. If wages were paid for a pay period and the employee claims more for work in that period, the deadline is twelve months from accrual [^idaho-wage-claim-limitation-periods].
**Wage liens (title 45, chapter 6).** Once a lien notice is properly filed, all amounts due become a lien on the person's entire legal or equitable interest in any nonexempt property in Idaho, real or personal [^idaho-wage-claim-property-lien].
**Minimum Wage Law (title 44, chapter 15).** When the director has reason to believe an employer is violating, or will violate, the Minimum Wage Law, the director may sue to stop the conduct and enforce compliance [^idaho-director-minimum-wage-injunction]. A claim for unpaid minimum wages may be treated as a wage claim under the wage-payment chapter [^idaho-minimum-wage-claim-chapter-six]. Such a claim is not subject to the dollar cap on department wage claims [^idaho-minimum-wage-claim-no-dollar-limit]. A court action for those wages must be filed within two years after the claim accrues [^idaho-minimum-wage-action-two-years].
**Retaliation.** The wage-payment chapter bars employers from firing or otherwise retaliating against an employee for complaining of unpaid wages to the employer or the department, for suing over unpaid wages, or for testifying, or being about to testify, in a department investigation or hearing [^idaho-wage-payment-retaliation-bar]. Separately, the Minimum Wage Law bars employers from firing or otherwise discriminating against an employee for complaining of not being paid as that law requires [^idaho-minimum-wage-retaliation-bar].
**Records and posting.** Under the wage-payment chapter, employment records must be kept for at least three years after the employee's last day of service [^idaho-employment-records-three-years]. Under the Minimum Wage Law, every covered employer must post a summary of the law, which the director furnishes free of charge, in a conspicuous place where employees work or in a place employees can reach [^idaho-minimum-wage-summary-posting].

The wage-payment chapter states what happens when those deadlines pass.

"In the event an action is not commenced as herein provided, any remedy on the cause of action shall be forever barred."[^idaho-untimely-wage-claim-barred]
