# Wage and Hour Law in Montana[^about]

How Montana sets a $10.85 indexed minimum wage that rises to $11.20 in 2027, requires weekly overtime after 40 hours, sets no general adult break rule, requires prompt final pay, bars a tip credit, and sets pay-timing, deduction, worker-classification and wage-claim rules.

## What is the minimum wage? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Montana's minimum wage is $10.85 an hour in 2026, and it rises to $11.20 an hour on January 1, 2027. [^mt-dli-minimum-wage-page] Section 39-3-409 of the Montana Code Annotated adjusts the rate each year by the increase, if any, in the consumer price index from August to August, rounded to the nearest 5 cents, with the new rate taking effect the following January 1. [^mt-minimum-wage-indexing] The Department of Labor and Industry lists the current $10.85 rate and the $11.20 rate for 2027. [^mt-dli-minimum-wage-page] The state rate is above the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour. [^mt-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

A business whose annual gross sales are $110,000 or less may pay $4 an hour. [^mt-small-business-four-dollar-rate] That lower rate does not apply to an employee covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, because Montana's minimum wage applies to a federally covered employee whenever the state rate is higher than the federal rate. [^mt-flsa-covered-employee-state-rate] The Department of Labor and Industry reads the two provisions together the same way: a business not covered by the federal Act may pay $4.00, but an individual employee who is covered must be paid the higher of the federal or Montana rate. [^mt-dli-small-business-guidance]

Several groups are excluded from both the minimum wage and overtime. The exclusions include bona fide executive, administrative and professional employees as defined by the commissioner's regulations, computer systems analysts, programmers, software engineers, network administrators and similarly skilled computer employees who earn at least $27.63 an hour under the federal computer-employee regulations, 29 CFR 541.400 or 541.402, and outside salespeople under 29 CFR 541.500. [^mt-white-collar-exclusion] They also include immediate family members of the employer and persons employed in private homes for menial chores such as babysitting and mowing lawns. [^mt-family-household-exclusion]

A farm worker employed for part of a year that includes periods requiring more than 8 hours of work a day and other seasonal periods requiring substantially less than 8 hours a day may instead be paid a fixed rate, either with total pay at least equal to the minimum wage times all hours worked or as a monthly wage of at least $635. [^mt-farm-worker-monthly-wage]

## When is overtime owed? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Montana requires overtime at one and one-half times the hourly wage rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. [^mt-overtime-forty-hours] The general overtime rule counts hours by the workweek, not the workday; a daily threshold of 8 hours appears only in the optional 14-day work period for hospitals and care establishments. [^mt-overtime-forty-hours] [^mt-overtime-hospital-eight-eighty] Farm workers are not covered by the overtime provision. [^mt-overtime-farm-workers]

A seasonal amusement or recreational area that furnishes students with board, lodging or other facilities owes those students overtime only after 48 hours in a workweek. [^mt-overtime-seasonal-students]

The overtime rule has its own list of exclusions on top of the exclusions that apply to both minimum wage and overtime. The list includes employees for whom the U.S. Secretary of Transportation has power to set qualifications and maximum hours of service. [^mt-overtime-motor-carrier-dealer-exclusion] It also includes a salesperson, parts person or mechanic paid on a commission or contract basis who is primarily engaged in selling or servicing automobiles, trucks, mobile homes, recreational vehicles or farm implements, if employed by a nonmanufacturing establishment primarily engaged in selling those vehicles or implements to ultimate purchasers. [^mt-overtime-motor-carrier-dealer-exclusion] It also includes guides, cooks, camp tenders, outfitter's assistants and livestock handlers employed by a licensed outfitter. [^mt-overtime-outfitter-exclusion]

A hospital or other establishment primarily engaged in caring for the sick, disabled, aged or mentally ill may set an 80-hour, 14-day work period, through a collective bargaining agreement when a bargaining unit represents the employee or by mutual agreement of the employer and employee when no bargaining unit is recognized, but it must then pay overtime for work over 8 hours a day or 80 hours in the 14-day period. [^mt-overtime-hospital-eight-eighty]

## Are breaks required? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Montana's statutes do not require private employers to give adult employees meal or rest breaks. The review for this guide of parts 2 and 4 of chapter 3 and part 2 of chapter 2 of Title 39 of the Montana Code Annotated found no such provision. The only statutory break rule found applies to public employers: state and county governments, municipalities, school districts and the university system must give reasonable unpaid break time each day to an employee who needs to express breast milk, if breaks are already allowed. [^mt-public-employer-lactation-break]

Private-sector nursing employees have federal protection instead: federal law requires an employer to provide reasonable break time to express breast milk for one year after the child's birth, and a place other than a bathroom that is shielded from view. [^mt-federal-lactation-break] An employer with fewer than 50 employees is exempt if the requirement would impose an undue hardship. [^mt-federal-lactation-small-employer]

When an employer chooses to give breaks, the Department of Labor and Industry treats rest breaks as work time. [^mt-dli-break-pay-guidance] It treats a meal period as non-work time only if the employee is completely relieved of duty and the period lasts at least 30 minutes. [^mt-dli-meal-period-guidance]

Those public employers must also make reasonable efforts to provide a room or other location near the work area, other than a toilet stall, for expressing milk. [^mt-public-employer-lactation-space] A public employer need not provide the break time if doing so would unduly disrupt its operations. [^mt-public-employer-lactation-break]

## When is final pay due? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** When an employer discharges an employee for cause or lays an employee off, all unpaid wages are due immediately on separation, unless a written personnel policy extends the deadline to the next regular payday for the pay period or 15 days after separation, whichever comes first. [^mt-final-pay-discharge] An employee who separates in any other way, such as by quitting, must be paid by the next regular payday for the pay period in which the separation happened or within 15 days, whichever comes first. [^mt-final-pay-quit]

The Department of Labor and Industry reads immediately to mean within four hours or by the end of the business day, whichever comes first. [^mt-dli-immediate-four-hours]

When an employee is discharged because of an allegation of theft of property or funds connected to the employee's work, the employer may hold back from the final paycheck the value of the alleged theft only if the employee agrees in writing, or if the employer reports the theft to local law enforcement within 7 business days of separation. When the withholding rests on a police report, the withheld wages become due when the 30-day period after the report expires if no theft charges have been filed. [^mt-final-pay-theft-withholding]

Late final pay carries the general wage-payment penalty. An employer that fails to pay wages as part 2 requires owes the employee a penalty of up to 110% of the unpaid wages, in addition to the wages, and commits a misdemeanor. [^mt-late-pay-penalty]

## How often must wages be paid? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** Montana's general wage payment law does not set a minimum pay frequency, but it requires an employer to pay wages no later than 10 business days after they are due and payable. [^mt-wages-ten-business-days] If the employer has not established when wages are due, the pay period is presumed to be semimonthly. [^mt-semimonthly-presumption]

On public works construction contracts covered by Montana's prevailing wage law, a contractor or subcontractor must pay hourly employees weekly. [^mt-public-works-weekly-pay]

If an employee turns in a timesheet after the employer's processing deadline and is not paid within the 10-day period, the employer may pay those wages in the next pay period, but not later. [^mt-semimonthly-presumption]

Wages may be paid by direct deposit only if the employee consents in writing, or electronically with a record retained, and an employee may not be required to accept direct deposit. [^mt-direct-deposit-consent]

On written demand before work begins, an employer must tell each employee in writing, or by a conspicuous posted notice, the rate of wages and the paydays. [^mt-pay-rate-notice-on-demand]

## What must a pay statement show, and what deductions are allowed? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** Montana requires every employer, when paying wages, to give the employee an itemized statement of the money deducted for state and federal income taxes, social security or any other deduction, with the amount of each deduction. [^mt-itemized-deduction-statement] If nothing is deducted, the employer must give a statement saying so. [^mt-itemized-deduction-statement] The Department of Labor and Industry describes the same duty as an itemized pay statement given at the time of payment. [^mt-dli-pay-statement]
Montana's wage payment statute allows reasonable deductions for board, room and other incidentals supplied by the employer when they are part of the conditions of employment, or as otherwise provided by law. [^mt-deductions-board-room-incidentals] The department, relying on opinions of the Montana Attorney General, states that wages cannot be withheld for shortages, damages or mistakes, even when the employee has agreed to the deduction as a condition of employment. [^mt-dli-no-deductions-for-damages]

Any contract or agreement that violates, evades or circumvents the wage payment part is unlawful and void, and the employee may still sue for the wages and the penalty. [^mt-contracts-violating-part-void]

## Employee or independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** Montana's Department of Labor and Industry applies a two-part test to decide whether an individual is an independent contractor or an employee, subject to rules that can make independent contractor certificate status conclusive. [^mt-arm-two-part-test] [^mt-arm-certificate-conclusive] The certificate rule works this way: except as provided in ARM 24.35.204, a worker required by section 39-71-417 to hold an independent contractor exemption certificate who does not hold one is conclusively an employee for wage and hour purposes, and a worker who holds a certificate and works under it as that section requires is conclusively an independent contractor. [^mt-arm-certificate-conclusive] Montana's wage payment law excludes independent contractors from the definition of employee but does not define independent contractor in that part. [^mt-employee-excludes-independent-contractor] A dispute involving an employer, a worker or the Department of Labor and Industry over whether a worker is an independent contractor or an employee, when no workers' compensation benefits are at issue, must be brought before the department's independent contractor central unit. [^mt-independent-contractor-central-unit] Separately, section 39-71-417 rebuttably presumes a worker without a certificate to be an independent contractor when the worker represents in writing to the hiring entity that the worker has a certificate, provides a forged or fraudulent certificate, or has a certificate that expires while the worker is performing the contract and before full performance, for no more than 120 days after the expiration. [^mt-certificate-rebuttable-presumption] The department evaluates those cases using the two conditions a certificate applicant must swear to. [^mt-certificate-presumption-evaluation]

Both wage parts define employ broadly. Part 2 says to employ means to permit or suffer to work. [^mt-employ-suffer-permit-part-2] Part 4, which sets the minimum wage and overtime, says to employ means to suffer or permit to work. [^mt-employ-suffer-permit-part-4]

The workers' compensation chapter states two conditions a person must swear to in order to obtain an independent contractor exemption certificate: freedom from control or direction over the performance of the services, both under contract and in fact, and engagement in an independently established trade, occupation, profession or business. [^mt-independent-contractor-certificate-conditions]

A decision of the independent contractor central unit is final unless a party requests mediation within 15 days. [^mt-independent-contractor-central-unit] If mediation does not resolve the dispute, a party may appeal by petition to the workers' compensation court within 30 days after the mediator's report is mailed. [^mt-iccu-appeal-workers-comp-court]

## Is a tip credit allowed? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Montana does not allow an employer to count an employee's tips toward the minimum wage. [^mt-minimum-wage-excludes-tips] The minimum wage is set excluding the value of tips received by the employee. [^mt-minimum-wage-excludes-tips] The definition of wage for the minimum wage and overtime part also leaves out tips and gratuities received at businesses licensed to provide food, beverage or lodging. [^mt-wage-excludes-tips-part-4] The Department of Labor and Industry states that no tip credit, meal credit or training wage is allowed. [^mt-dli-no-tip-credit]

An employer may impose a tip pool that includes employees involved in customer service or food preparation, such as servers, bussers, dishwashers and cooks, but employees may not be required to contribute more than they received in tips. [^mt-tip-pool-participants] The employer must notify employees of any required tip pool, may not take part in it, and may not include exempt salaried supervisors or managers. [^mt-tip-pool-employer-limits] Federal law adds a broader limit for employers covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act: an employer may not keep employees' tips for any purpose, including letting managers or supervisors keep any portion of them, whether or not it takes a tip credit. [^mt-federal-no-manager-tips]

## How are wage claims enforced? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** The Commissioner of Labor and Industry enforces Montana's wage payment law, and minimum wage and overtime claims are pursued as wage claims under the same procedure. [^mt-commissioner-enforces] [^mt-minimum-wage-enforced-as-wage-claim] An employee may file a complaint to recover wages and penalties within 180 days of the default or delay in payment. [^mt-wage-claim-180-days] An employer that fails to pay wages as required owes a penalty of up to 110% of the unpaid wages, except on minimum wage and overtime claims subject to the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, where federal liquidated damages apply instead. [^mt-late-pay-penalty-enforcement] [^mt-flsa-claims-liquidated-damages] When an employee must sue to collect wages, the judgment must include a reasonable attorney fee in favor of the successful party. [^mt-attorney-fees-successful-party]

An employee may recover wages and penalties for the two years before the claim is filed, or before the last day of employment if the employee has left, and for three years if the employer has engaged in repeated violations. [^mt-wage-claim-lookback]

The 110% penalty does not apply to minimum wage and overtime claims that are subject to the federal Fair Labor Standards Act; for those claims, liquidated damages under the federal Act apply instead. [^mt-flsa-claims-liquidated-damages]

If the department finds a wage claim valid and the employer does not appeal, the department may enter a default order for the wages and any penalty. [^mt-wage-claim-default-order] A party that disputes the determination before a contested case goes to mediation, and a party may appeal the determination to a hearing within 15 days after it is mailed. [^mt-wage-claim-mediation-hearing]

On the employee's written request, the commissioner must take an assignment of an unpaid wage claim and may pursue it. [^mt-commissioner-wage-assignment]
