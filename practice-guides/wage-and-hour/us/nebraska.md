# Wage and Hour Law in Nebraska[^about]

How Nebraska sets a $15.00 minimum wage that rises 1.75 percent a year from 2027, leaves overtime to federal law, requires a lunch period only in assembling plants, workshops and mechanical establishments, allows a $2.13 cash wage for tipped employees, and sets final-pay, payday, pay-statement, deduction, worker-classification, paid sick time and wage-claim rules.

## What is Nebraska's minimum wage, and how does it compare with the federal rate? {#minimum-wage}

**Short answer.** Nebraska's minimum wage is $15.00 an hour from January 1 through December 31, 2026. [^ne-minimum-wage-fifteen-dollars-schedule] That rate is the last step of a statutory schedule that raised the wage from $9.00 in 2022 through $10.50, $12.00 and $13.50 to $15.00. [^ne-minimum-wage-fifteen-dollars-schedule] Starting January 1, 2027, the rate rises by 1.75 percent each January 1, and the Nebraska Department of Labor must publish the coming rate by October 15 of each year. [^ne-minimum-wage-annual-increase] A 1.75 percent increase on $15.00 is about $15.26 an hour. The federal minimum wage, which binds employers covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, is $7.25 an hour. [^ne-federal-minimum-wage-rate]
Federal minimum wage law is discussed in the [federal wage and hour guide](/practice-guides/wage-and-hour/us).

The state minimum wage binds only an employer with four or more employees at any one time, and it excludes seasonal employment of not more than 20 weeks in a calendar year and all public employers. [^ne-employer-four-or-more-employees] The definition of employee leaves out, among others, agricultural workers, babysitters in private homes, and employees in a bona fide executive, administrative or professional capacity or working as a superintendent or supervisor. [^ne-employee-exclusions-agriculture-executive]

Two lower rates apply to young workers. A 2026 law lets an employer pay a youth minimum wage of $13.50 an hour to an employee aged 14 or 15 who is not an emancipated minor, effective July 18, 2026, and that youth rate rises by 1.5 percent every fifth year starting January 1, 2030. [^ne-youth-minimum-wage-fourteen-fifteen] [^ne-youth-wage-effective-july-2026] A new employee aged 16 through 19 who is not a seasonal or migrant worker or an emancipated minor may be paid a training wage of $13.50 an hour through December 31, 2026, for the first 90 days after hire. [^ne-training-wage-ninety-days] The training wage rises by 1.5 percent each January 1 starting in 2027. [^ne-training-wage-ninety-days] No more than one-fourth of an employer's total paid hours may be at the training wage, and an employer may not use it to displace or cut the hours of another employee. [^ne-training-wage-ninety-days] [^ne-training-wage-limits-displacement] Student-learners in a bona fide vocational training program must receive at least 75 percent of the minimum wage. [^ne-student-learner-seventy-five-percent]

The Nebraska Supreme Court has held that the parties cannot waive the protections of the Wage and Hour Act by agreement. [^ne-mays-no-waiver-wage-hour-act]

## Does Nebraska law require overtime pay? {#overtime}

**Short answer.** Nebraska has no generally applicable statute requiring private employers to pay overtime, daily or weekly. The Wage and Hour Act sets minimum wages, and its private remedy makes employers liable to affected employees for unpaid minimum wages. [^ne-wage-hour-act-minimum-wage-remedy] Overtime therefore comes from the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, which requires a covered employer to pay at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek unless a federal exemption applies. [^ne-federal-overtime-forty-hours]

The Nebraska Department of Labor explains that an employee can claim overtime under the state Wage Payment and Collection Act only if the employer and employee previously agreed to it, and that overtime for hours over 40 can otherwise be claimed under the federal Act. [^ne-dol-overtime-agreed-or-federal] That follows from the state definition of wages, which covers compensation, including fringe benefits, that was previously agreed to once the stipulated conditions are met. [^ne-wages-defined-previously-agreed]

## Must Nebraska employers give meal or rest breaks? {#meal-rest-breaks}

**Short answer.** Nebraska requires a 30-minute lunch period in each eight-hour shift at assembling plants, workshops and mechanical establishments, except where employment is covered by a valid collective bargaining agreement or another written agreement between employer and employee. [^ne-lunch-period-assembling-plants] An employer that owns or operates an assembling plant, workshop or mechanical establishment employing one or more people must allow every employee at least 30 consecutive minutes for lunch in each eight-hour shift, and may not require the employee to stay in the building or on the premises during that time. [^ne-lunch-period-assembling-plants] The rule does not apply to employment covered by a valid collective bargaining agreement or another written agreement between employer and employee. [^ne-lunch-period-assembling-plants] No Nebraska statute generally requires rest breaks or a meal break in other workplaces, or says whether breaks are paid. One exception is the Nebraska Fair Employment Practice Act, under which reasonable accommodation for pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions includes more frequent or longer breaks, periodic rest, and break time for breast-feeding or expressing breast milk, unless the accommodation would pose an undue hardship. [^ne-pregnancy-accommodation-breaks] For employers covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, short rest periods are customarily paid and must be counted as hours worked, [^ne-federal-short-rest-breaks-paid] while a meal period may be unpaid only if the employee is completely relieved from duty. [^ne-federal-meal-period-relieved-duty]

A violation of the lunch-period statute is a Class III misdemeanor. [^ne-lunch-period-misdemeanor] The Department of Labor's guidance confirms that only mechanical establishments, workshops and assembling plants must give the 30-minute break, and that other breaks are at the employer's discretion. [^ne-dol-lunch-period-only-mechanical] [^ne-dol-breaks-employer-discretion]

## When is a final paycheck due in Nebraska? {#final-pay}

**Short answer.** Except for commissions, when a private employer separates an employee from the payroll, the unpaid wages are due on the next regular payday or within two weeks of the date of termination, whichever is sooner. [^ne-final-pay-next-payday-two-weeks] [^ne-final-commissions-after-customer-payment] The Department of Labor reads the rule to apply whether the employee is fired or quits. [^ne-dol-final-pay-fired-or-quit] When a political subdivision separates an employee, final wages are due within two weeks of the governing body's next regularly scheduled meeting if the separation is at least one week before that meeting, and otherwise within two weeks of the following regularly scheduled meeting. [^ne-final-pay-political-subdivision] Commissions owed at separation are due on the next regular payday after the employer receives the customer's payment, and the employer must give a periodic accounting of outstanding commissions until they are paid. [^ne-final-commissions-after-customer-payment]

Remedies for late final pay are covered under [enforcement](#enforcement).

Earned but unused vacation is part of the wages due at separation, but other paid leave provided as a fringe benefit is not, unless the employer and employee, or the employer and a union, have specifically agreed otherwise. [^ne-final-pay-vacation-other-leave] The Nebraska Supreme Court has held that paid time off an employee earns simply by working and may use for any purpose must be treated as vacation and paid out at separation. [^ne-fisher-pto-treated-as-vacation] Unless a contract in effect at the start of employment, or at least 90 days before separation, says otherwise, wages at separation include commissions on all orders delivered and on file, less orders returned or canceled when suit is filed. [^ne-commissions-orders-on-file]

Paid sick time owed under the Nebraska Healthy Families and Workplaces Act does not have to be paid out at separation. [^ne-paid-sick-time-no-payout-rehire] An employee rehired by the same employer within 12 months gets back accrued paid sick time that was not used or paid out. [^ne-paid-sick-time-no-payout-rehire]

## How often must Nebraska employers pay wages, and what may they deduct? {#pay-frequency}

**Short answer.** No Nebraska statute sets a minimum pay frequency. Each employer must pay all wages due on regular paydays that the employer designates or that the employer and employee agree on, and must give employees 30 days' written notice before changing regular paydays. [^ne-deductions-written-agreement-wage-statement] An employer may deduct, withhold or divert part of an employee's wages only when state or federal law requires or allows it, a court orders it, or the employer has a written agreement with the employee authorizing it. [^ne-deductions-written-agreement-wage-statement]

The Department of Labor reads the deduction rule to require written authorization for deductions such as cash shortages, breakage and tools, and says such deductions may not reduce wages below the minimum wage. [^ne-dol-deductions-shortages-breakage] An employer that pays wages with a payroll debit card must follow the federal compulsory-use rule, give the employee at least one free withdrawal of up to the full net wages each pay period, though not more often than once a week, and may not pass its card fees to the employee. [^ne-payroll-debit-card-free-withdrawal]

## What must a Nebraska pay statement show? {#wage-statements}

**Short answer.** On each regular payday, an employer must deliver or make available to each employee a wage statement showing, at a minimum, the employer's identity, the hours for which the employee was paid (subject to an exception for some overtime-exempt employees), the wages earned and the deductions made. [^ne-wage-statement-minimum-contents] Hours need not be shown for employees exempt from overtime under the federal white-collar rules, unless the employer has a policy or practice of paying them overtime, a bonus or a payment based on hours worked, in which case the statement must show the hours worked or the payments made, as applicable. [^ne-wage-statement-minimum-contents] The statement may be mailed, sent electronically or provided at the normal workplace during working hours for all shifts. [^ne-wage-statement-minimum-contents]

An employer covered by the Healthy Families and Workplaces Act must also record, in or attached to the regular paycheck, the paid sick time available to the employee, the paid sick time used so far that year, and the pay received as paid sick time. [^ne-paid-sick-time-paycheck-notice]

## How does Nebraska decide who is an employee rather than an independent contractor? {#worker-classification}

**Short answer.** For wage payment claims, Nebraska uses an ABC test. Services an individual performs for an employer are employment unless the individual has been and will continue to be free from control or direction both under the contract and in fact, the service is outside the usual course of the business or is performed outside all of the business's places of business, and the individual is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, profession or business. [^ne-wage-payment-abc-test] The statute says that test is not a codification of the common law and is complete as written. [^ne-wage-payment-abc-test] The same definition also covers a person who has contracted to sell an employer's goods or services for commission. [^ne-wage-payment-abc-test]

The Employee Classification Act adds a presumption for construction and delivery work. An individual performing construction labor for a contractor is presumed to be an employee unless the individual meets the unemployment insurance test for independence, registered as a contractor before starting work, and has an unemployment tax rate or an exemption. [^ne-construction-delivery-employee-presumption] An individual performing delivery services for a contractor is presumed to be an employee unless the individual meets that unemployment insurance test or is exempt from unemployment coverage. [^ne-construction-delivery-employee-presumption] The Commissioner of Labor may fine a contractor up to $500 per misclassified individual for a first offense and up to $5,000 per individual for later offenses. [^ne-misclassification-penalty-per-individual] The Wage and Hour Act defines employ to include permitting work but does not adopt the ABC test. [^ne-wage-hour-act-employ-definition]

## Can Nebraska employers count tips toward the minimum wage? {#tip-credit}

**Short answer.** Under Nebraska law, an employer may pay employees compensated by gratuities, such as servers, hotel bellhops and porters, a cash wage of $2.13 an hour plus all tips they receive, as long as cash wages and tips together reach the applicable minimum wage. [^ne-tipped-cash-wage-two-thirteen] The cash wage and tips together must equal or exceed the applicable minimum wage, and the employer bears the burden of proving that an employee is compensated by gratuities. [^ne-tipped-cash-wage-two-thirteen]
For an employer covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, the federal tip credit applies only if the employee has been informed of the federal tip-credit provisions and keeps all tips received, apart from a pool among employees who customarily and regularly receive tips. [^ne-federal-tip-credit-notice-retention]

The Nebraska Supreme Court has held that, unlike the federal rule, the Nebraska statute does not require an employer to notify an employee in advance that the employee will be paid as a tipped employee. [^ne-mays-no-tip-notice-required] The Department of Labor says the employer must make up the difference whenever wages plus tips fall short of the minimum wage. [^ne-dol-tips-make-up-difference] No Nebraska statute addresses tip pooling.

## How are unpaid wages recovered in Nebraska, and what penalties apply? {#enforcement}

**Short answer.** An employee whose wages are not paid within 30 days of the regular payday may sue for them. [^ne-wage-claim-suit-attorney-fees] An employee who wins a judgment recovers the full judgment and the costs of suit, including reasonable attorney's fees, unless the judgment does not exceed what the employer tendered within 30 days of the payday, in which case the employee does not recover attorney's fees. [^ne-wage-claim-suit-attorney-fees] After a judgment, an additional amount equal to the judgment may be recovered from the employer, and if the nonpayment is found to be willful, an amount equal to twice the unpaid wages must be recovered; either amount goes to the State Treasurer for the public schools rather than to the employee. [^ne-additional-recovery-school-fund]

For minimum wage violations, the employer owes affected employees their unpaid minimum wages, one or more employees may sue for themselves and others similarly situated, and the court must award costs and reasonable attorney's fees. [^ne-minimum-wage-employer-liability-misdemeanor] [^ne-minimum-wage-collective-action-fees] A minimum wage violation is also a Class IV misdemeanor. [^ne-minimum-wage-employer-liability-misdemeanor]

The Commissioner of Labor must issue a citation when an investigation shows that an employer may have violated the Wage Payment and Collection Act, with an administrative penalty of up to $500 for a first violation and up to $5,000 for a later violation. [^ne-wage-payment-citation-penalty] An employer with an unpaid citation is barred from contracting with the state or any political subdivision until it pays, but a contested citation is not treated as unpaid until the contest is resolved. [^ne-wage-payment-unpaid-citation-contract-bar] An employer may not retaliate against an employee for filing a suit or complaint under the Act or for taking part in an investigation or proceeding. [^ne-wage-payment-anti-retaliation]

Since October 1, 2025, a private employer with 11 or more employees must provide paid sick time to its covered employees. [^ne-paid-sick-time-begins-october-2025] [^ne-paid-sick-time-employer-eleven-employees] Covered employees do not include owner-operators, independent contractors, individuals who work in Nebraska fewer than 80 hours in a calendar year, seasonal or temporary agricultural workers, railroad employees covered by the federal Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act, or individuals under 16. [^ne-paid-sick-time-employee-exclusions] Employees begin accruing after 80 hours of consecutive employment at a rate of at least one hour for every 30 hours worked, and a small business may cap earning and use at 40 hours a year while any other covered employer may cap them at 56 hours. [^ne-paid-sick-time-accrual-caps] [^ne-paid-sick-time-employer-eleven-employees] A small business is an employer with at least 11 but fewer than 20 employees in a given week, but an employer that kept 20 or more employees on its payroll in each of 20 or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year is not a small business. [^ne-paid-sick-time-small-business-definition] An employer with a paid time off policy that meets those amounts and allows use for sick-time purposes need not provide more. [^ne-paid-sick-time-pto-policy-satisfies] The Department of Labor enforces the Healthy Families and Workplaces Act through citations with the same $500 and $5,000 penalty caps, and the Act bars retaliation for requesting or using paid sick time. [^ne-paid-sick-time-citation-penalty] [^ne-paid-sick-time-anti-retaliation]
