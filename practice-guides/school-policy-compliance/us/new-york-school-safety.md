# School Policy Compliance in New York: School Safety, Health and Security[^about]

The safety and health rules a New York independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: fire, evacuation and lockdown drills, safety plans, AEDs, medication and emergency epinephrine, and firearms on school grounds.

## How many fire, evacuation and lockdown drills must a New York private school hold each year? {#fire-and-lockdown-drills}

**Short answer.** The principal or person in charge of every public or private school in New York, other than a college, must hold at least twelve drills each school year, eight of them between September 1 and December 31, with eight evacuation drills and four lockdown drills. [^edn-807-drill-count]

Four of the evacuation drills must use fire escapes where provided or identified secondary means of egress, and drills must be held at different times of the school day. [^edn-807-drill-count] A school with sleeping accommodations must hold four additional drills between sunset and sunrise, and a school with summer sessions must hold at least two summer drills, one in the first week. [^edn-807-boarding-and-summer] Neglect to comply is a misdemeanor. [^edn-807-misdemeanor]

## Must a New York independent school adopt a district-wide safety plan and building-level emergency response plan? {#school-safety-plans}

**Short answer.** The safety-plan mandate in Education Law § 2801-a is imposed on school district boards, boards of cooperative educational services, county vocational education and extension boards and the New York City chancellor, and it does not name nonpublic schools. [^edn-2801-a-plan-mandate]

The drill requirements of Education Law § 807 do apply to every private school, including lock-down drills, as explained in the [question on fire and lockdown drills](#fire-and-lockdown-drills). [^edn-807-private-school-drills] The § 2801-a plan format is therefore not a statutory requirement for a nonpublic school. [^edn-2801-a-plan-mandate] That conclusion is limited to § 2801-a: it does not address fire-code, building-code or local emergency-planning obligations, which this guide does not cover. Section 807 also requires the person in charge of a school building to have the leader of an after-school program, event or performance that includes outside attendees tell those present, at the start, the procedures to follow in an emergency. [^edn-807-after-school-notice]

## Must a New York independent or religious school keep an AED on site and have staff trained to use it? {#aed-requirements}

**Short answer.** Since July 1, 2026, Education Law § 917-a has required every nonpublic school to provide and maintain AED equipment in each instructional school facility and to maintain and test it to the applicable standards. [^edn-917-a-nonpublic-aed] [^s2590-effective-date]

When the school's facilities are used for school-sponsored or school-approved events or activities, and at any school-sponsored athletic contest, at least one staff member trained in AED use must be present, and the school must provide an AED on site at an off-campus competitive athletic event it sponsors. [^edn-917-a-trained-staff-events] Only a person who has completed an approved AED training course may operate the device, except a licensed health care practitioner acting within scope, a person acting under a lawful prescription, or a good-faith volunteer rescuer. [^edn-917-a-operators] Each use must be reported immediately to the local emergency medical services system, the school must register each AED with the Department of Health, and a sign at the main entrance must show where the AED is kept. [^edn-917-a-report-register-sign]

Section 917-a is a separate mandate from Education Law § 917, whose on-site defibrillator requirement names school districts, boards of cooperative educational services, county vocational education and extension boards and charter schools, not nonpublic schools. [^edn-917-aed-mandate]

## Who may give students medication, including emergency epinephrine, at a New York independent or religious school? {#medication-and-epinephrine}

**Short answer.** The State Education Department's medication guidelines apply to nonpublic as well as public schools and treat medication administration as the work of licensed health professionals, with unlicensed staff limited to helping students take their own medication and, in limited circumstances, giving certain emergency medications. [^nysed-medication-nonpublic] [^nysed-medication-unlicensed-personnel]

Education Law § 921 authorizes, but does not oblige, a nonpublic school to have registered nurses, nurse practitioners, physician assistants or physicians train unlicensed staff to give prescribed glucagon or epinephrine in an emergency when an appropriately licensed health professional is not available. [^edn-921-epinephrine-training] The authority extends to pupils with written permission from a physician or other authorized provider and written parental consent, and the training must be given by a physician or other licensed health care professional and completed in the form and manner the Commissioner prescribes by regulation. [^edn-921-epinephrine-training] [^edn-921-training-form] A nonpublic school and its employees incur no liability for harm caused by reasonable, good-faith compliance with § 921. [^edn-921-liability-shield]

Outside the student-assistance and emergency situations described above, the guidelines reserve medication administration to licensed health professionals whose scope of practice includes it, and emergency administration by trained unlicensed staff applies when a licensed health professional is not available. [^nysed-medication-unlicensed-personnel]

## What limits does New York law place on firearms on nonpublic school grounds? {#firearms-on-school-grounds}

**Short answer.** New York makes it a class E felony to knowingly possess a firearm in or on a school's buildings or grounds without the school's written authorization, and a school may not give that authorization to a teacher, administrator or other person employed at the school unless the person is primarily employed as a school resource officer, police officer, peace officer or security guard holding a special armed guard registration card. [^pen-265-01-a-school-grounds]

The offense reaches the buildings and grounds of any school, college or university used for educational purposes, and school buses. [^pen-265-01-a-school-grounds] The limit on authorizing school employees refers to a school as defined in Education Law § 1125(10), which includes a nonpublic school, so a nonpublic school may not authorize a teacher, administrator or other employee who is not primarily employed in one of the listed security roles to carry a firearm. [^pen-265-01-a-school-grounds] [^edn-1125-school-includes-nonpublic-safety]
