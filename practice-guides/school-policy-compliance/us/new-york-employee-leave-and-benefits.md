# School Policy Compliance in New York: Employee Leave, Benefits and Tuition Remission[^about]

The leave and benefit rules a New York independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: paid sick, prenatal and safe-time leave, lactation breaks, disability and paid family leave for teachers, jury, voting and blood-donation leave, FMLA and the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act, unemployment claims, tuition remission and repayment on departure.

## What paid sick leave and paid prenatal leave must a New York private school provide? {#paid-sick-and-prenatal-leave}

**Short answer.** A New York private school with five to ninety-nine employees must provide up to forty hours of paid sick leave a year, and one with one hundred or more must provide up to fifty-six hours. [^lab-196-b-sick-leave-tiers] Since January 1, 2025, every employer must also provide twenty hours of paid prenatal personal leave in any fifty-two-week period, in addition to sick leave. [^lab-196-b-prenatal-leave]

Sick leave accrues at no less than one hour for every thirty hours worked. [^lab-196-b-accrual-rate] An employer may not require disclosure of confidential health information, or of information about an absence related to domestic violence, a sexual offense, stalking or human trafficking, as a condition of sick leave or prenatal leave. [^lab-196-b-confidentiality] An existing paid time off policy satisfies the statute only if it meets the hours and also the accrual, carryover and use requirements. [^lab-196-b-existing-policy] An employer with four or fewer employees owes unpaid sick leave unless its net income exceeded one million dollars in the prior tax year. [^lab-196-b-sick-leave-tiers] Unused sick leave carries over to the next calendar year, although an employer with fewer than one hundred employees may cap use at forty hours a year. [^lab-196-b-carryover] The employer must provide accrued sick leave on the employee's oral or written request for the statutory purposes. [^lab-196-b-on-request]

## What lactation breaks and space must a New York school provide to nursing employees? {#lactation-breaks}

**Short answer.** A New York employer must provide thirty minutes of paid break time each time an employee reasonably needs to express breast milk, for up to three years after childbirth, and must permit existing paid break or meal time to be used beyond thirty minutes. [^lab-206-c-paid-break]

On request, the employer must designate a room or other location near the work area that is well lit, shielded from view and free from intrusion, with a chair, a working surface, nearby clean running water and, where available, an electrical outlet; a restroom or toilet stall does not qualify. [^lab-206-c-room-requirements] The employer's written policy must tell employees how to request a room and require a response within five business days. [^lab-206-c-policy-contents] Federal law separately requires reasonable break time and a non-bathroom space for one year after the child's birth. [^usc-218d-pump-break-lactation]

## Do New York paid family leave and disability benefits cover teachers at a nonprofit independent school? {#paid-family-leave-teachers}

**Short answer.** Teachers at a nonprofit religious, charitable or educational institution are generally not employees for New York disability and paid family leave benefits, because Workers' Compensation Law § 201(5) excludes persons engaged in a professional or teaching capacity for such an institution, an exclusion the Workers' Compensation Board limits to teachers performing only teaching duties. [^wkc-201-teaching-capacity-exclusion] [^wcb-teaching-duties-only]

The same subdivision excludes an executive officer of an incorporated religious, charitable or educational institution and volunteers, and defines such an institution as one organized and operated exclusively for those purposes with no private inurement. [^wkc-201-teaching-capacity-exclusion] [^wkc-201-institution-defined] Staff outside those categories, such as business-office or facilities staff, are not within that exclusion. [^wkc-201-teaching-capacity-exclusion] The Workers' Compensation Board reads the exclusion narrowly: teachers must be performing only teaching duties to be exempt from coverage. [^wcb-teaching-duties-only] A covered employer must give an eligible employee a written statement of rights within five business days after the seventh consecutive day of disability or family leave absence. [^wkc-229-written-statement-of-rights]

## What jury duty, voting and blood-donation leave must a New York school's policies allow? {#jury-voting-blood-donation-leave}

**Short answer.** A New York employer may not penalize an employee for jury service after notice, must allow up to two paid hours to vote when working hours leave too little time, and, with twenty or more employees at a site, must provide blood-donation leave. [^jud-519-juror-protection] [^eln-3-110-paid-voting-time] [^lab-202-j-blood-donation]

An employer with more than ten employees may not withhold the first seventy-two dollars of a juror's daily wages for the first three days of service. [^jud-519-juror-protection] Four consecutive free hours between the opening of the polls and the start of a shift, or between the end of a shift and the closing of the polls, count as sufficient time to vote. [^eln-3-110-four-hours-sufficient] An employee who needs time off must give the employer two to ten working days' notice. [^eln-3-110-sufficient-time-notice] The blood-donation duty may be met either by three hours of leave in a twelve-month period or by allowing donation during work hours at least twice a year. [^lab-202-j-blood-donation]

## Which federal leave and accommodation laws apply to a New York private school's handbook? {#federal-leave-and-accommodation}

**Short answer.** The Family and Medical Leave Act covers a private elementary or secondary school regardless of its size, but an employee is eligible for leave only if the school employs at least fifty employees within seventy-five miles of the employee's worksite. [^cfr-825-600-school-coverage] The Department of Labor's regulation states that the Act's fifty-employee coverage test does not apply to these schools while the usual eligibility requirements, including the seventy-five-mile test, still do. [^cfr-825-600-school-coverage] [^usc-2611-fmla-75-mile]

For other private employers the Act applies only at fifty or more employees in twenty or more workweeks, a threshold the school regulation displaces for elementary and secondary schools. [^usc-2611-fmla-employer] [^cfr-825-600-school-coverage] An eligible employee must also have worked for the employer for at least twelve months and for at least 1,250 hours in the preceding twelve months. [^cfr-825-110-eligibility] The same subpart sets special rules for instructional employees, such as teachers and coaches, on intermittent leave, reduced schedules and leave near the end of an academic term. [^cfr-825-600-instructional-employees]

The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act separately requires an employer with fifteen or more employees to reasonably accommodate the known limitations related to pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions of a qualified employee unless the accommodation would impose an undue hardship. [^usc-2000gg-1-pwfa-accommodation] [^usc-2000gg-pwfa-covered-entity] The Act is subject to Title VII's religious-employer provision in 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-1(a), so a religious school's ability to prefer co-religionists carries over to it. [^usc-2000gg-5-religious] The federal PUMP Act provision requires reasonable break time and a private non-bathroom space for expressing breast milk for one year after birth, alongside New York's broader paid-break rule. [^usc-218d-pump-federal]

## Can a New York school penalize an employee for applying for unemployment insurance? {#unemployment-claim-during-leave}

**Short answer.** New York Labor Law § 215(1)(a) bars an employer from penalizing or retaliating against an employee for instituting a proceeding under the Labor Law, for otherwise exercising rights the Labor Law protects, or for using a legally protected absence. [^lab-215-retaliation] Unemployment insurance benefits are paid under the Labor Law's own unemployment insurance article. [^lab-591-unemployment-benefits]

Penalizing an employee for seeking benefits that the Labor Law provides falls within the conduct § 215(1)(a) forbids. [^lab-215-retaliation] [^lab-591-unemployment-benefits] Whether an employee on a leave of absence is eligible for benefits at all is a separate question for the unemployment insurance system; apart from listed exceptions, benefits are paid only to a claimant who is totally or partially unemployed. [^lab-591-unemployment-benefits]

## Is tuition remission for employees' children at a New York K–12 school tax-free? {#employee-tuition-remission}

**Short answer.** Internal Revenue Code § 117(d) excludes from gross income an educational organization's tuition reduction for below-graduate education of an employee or the employee's dependent child, but a highly compensated employee's reduction qualifies only if offered on nondiscriminatory terms. [^irc-117-d-qualified-reduction] [^irc-132-h-dependent-child] [^irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination]

The exclusion is limited to tuition reductions given by an organization described in § 170(b)(1)(A)(ii), which is an educational organization that normally maintains a regular faculty and curriculum and an enrolled student body at the place where it teaches. [^irc-117-d-qualified-reduction] [^irc-170-b-educational-organization] A K–12 independent school that meets that description falls within the definition, and the education may be at the employer's own school or at another such organization. [^irc-117-d-qualified-reduction]

A reduction that does not qualify, such as one offered only to highly compensated employees, is not excluded by § 117(d) and falls within gross income as compensation for services, including fringe benefits. [^irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination] [^irc-61-compensation-fringe] The highly compensated employee test uses the definition in Code § 414(q), [^irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination] whose compensation threshold remains $160,000 for 2026 under IRS Notice 2025-67. [^irs-notice-2025-67-hce] IRS Publication 970 describes a qualified tuition reduction as one for education below the graduate level, including primary and secondary school. [^irs-pub-970-primary-secondary] Neither the statute nor the publication addresses a standalone pre-kindergarten or child care program, so whether a reduction for that program qualifies depends on whether it is part of a qualifying school's educational program. [^irs-pub-970-primary-secondary] [^irc-170-b-educational-organization]

## Can a New York school require a teacher to repay a signing bonus, tuition or training costs if the teacher leaves early? {#repayment-on-departure}

**Short answer.** From December 19, 2026, a New York employer may not require an employee to sign, as a condition of employment, a promissory note to repay money if employment ends before a stated period, unless the agreement fits an exception listed in Labor Law § 1052(2). [^trapped-at-work-1052-note-void] [^trapped-at-work-effective-date-commentary] The exceptions cover a bonus not tied to job performance, a capped tuition repayment for a transferable credential, and sabbatical-leave terms for educational personnel. [^trapped-at-work-1052-bonus-and-tuition] [^trapped-at-work-1052-sabbatical]

A repayment term for a financial bonus, relocation assistance or other non-educational payment not tied to specific job performance stays outside the prohibition only if repayment is not owed when the employee was terminated for any reason other than misconduct or the job was misrepresented. [^trapped-at-work-1052-bonus-and-tuition] A tuition repayment for a transferable credential must fix the amount in advance, not exceed the employer's cost, be prorated without acceleration and be excused if the employee is terminated other than for misconduct. [^trapped-at-work-1052-bonus-and-tuition] An agreement requiring educational personnel to comply with the terms of a sabbatical leave is a separate exception. [^trapped-at-work-1052-sabbatical] An employee who successfully defends an employer's suit on a note made void by Article 37 may recover attorney's fees. [^trapped-at-work-1053-fees]

The 2026 amendment changed the Act's effective clause to one year after it became law. [^trapped-at-work-s8822-effective-clause] Commentary had noted that this clause could be read to run from the February 13, 2026 amendment rather than the December 19, 2025 enactment, [^trapped-at-work-effective-date-ambiguity] but the Senate's consolidated text of Labor Law § 1050 now marks the article effective December 19, 2026. [^lab-1050-definitions-effective] That section defines an employment promissory note as any instrument or contract provision requiring an employee to pay the employer if employment with that employer ends before a stated period, and it excludes employer-specific training and legally mandated safety and compliance training from the transferable credentials that the tuition exception covers. [^lab-1050-definitions-effective]
