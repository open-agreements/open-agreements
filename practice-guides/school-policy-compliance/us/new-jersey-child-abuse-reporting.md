# School Policy Compliance in New Jersey: Child Abuse Reporting and Prevention[^about]

What a New Jersey nonpublic K–12 school's child-protection policies must reflect: the universal duty to report child abuse to the Division of Child Protection and Permanency, investigations of allegations against school staff, the pass-the-trash employment history review, agreements that suppress abuse information, criminal history checks and the anti-bullying law.

## Who must report suspected child abuse at a New Jersey nonpublic school, and to whom? {#mandated-reporting}

**Short answer.** Every person in New Jersey who has reasonable cause to believe that a child has been subjected to child abuse, including every teacher and staff member at a nonpublic school, must report it immediately to the Division of Child Protection and Permanency. [^njsa-9-6-8-10-duty]

The duty applies to any person, not to a list of professions, so it does not turn on a staff member's certification, title or role. [^njsa-9-6-8-10-duty] The report should, where possible, identify the child and the parent or other person with custody, the child's age, the nature and extent of the injuries or abuse, any evidence of earlier abuse and anything else that may help identify the perpetrator. [^njsa-9-6-8-10-contents] The Department of Children and Families' teacher desk reference lists the Child Abuse/Neglect Hotline at the State Central Registry, 1-877 NJ ABUSE, as the number to call, including for help deciding whether a situation should be reported. [^dcf-desk-ref-hotline]

Because § 9:6-8.10 names the Division as the recipient, telling a principal, head of school or supervisor is not by itself the report the statute requires. [^njsa-9-6-8-10-duty] For public schools, the Department of Education's rule requires district policies under which an employee immediately notifies child welfare authorities, may tell the principal first only if that will not delay the notification, and otherwise tells the principal afterward unless the employee believes that notice would likely endanger the reporter or student or lead to retaliation or employment discrimination. [^njac-6a-16-11-1-district-reporting] That rule, like N.J.S.A. 18A:36-25, is addressed to district boards of education and school districts. [^njac-6a-16-11-1-district-reporting] [^njsa-18a-36-25-districts] Notice to the head of school after the report is consistent with the statutory duty, but the statute requires the report itself to be made immediately, so internal approval cannot come first. [^njsa-9-6-8-10-duty]

Knowingly failing to report child abuse when there is reasonable cause to believe it occurred is a disorderly persons offense, and knowingly failing to report sexual abuse of a child is a crime of the fourth degree. [^njsa-9-6-8-14-penalties] A person who reports under the statute is immune from civil and criminal liability, and a person discharged or discriminated against in employment for a good-faith report may sue in the Family Part for reinstatement with back pay or other relief. [^njsa-9-6-8-13-immunity] [^njsa-9-6-8-13-retaliation]

## What happens when a New Jersey nonpublic school employee is accused of abusing a student? {#allegations-against-staff}

**Short answer.** Anyone with reasonable cause to believe a New Jersey private school employee abused a student must report it immediately to the Division of Child Protection and Permanency, and Title 9 treats day-school teaching staff and other employees as a parent or guardian. [^njsa-9-6-8-10-duty-staff] [^njsa-9-6-8-21-parent-day-school] [^njsa-9-6-8-21-day-school-defined]

A day school is a public or private school that provides educational services to day students in kindergarten through grade 12, and it excludes a residential facility providing care on a 24-hour basis. [^njsa-9-6-8-21-day-school-defined] A boarding program falls instead within the definition of an institution, which names residential schools, and the definition of parent or guardian also covers a teacher, employee or volunteer of an institution and any other institution staff person. [^njsa-9-6-8-21-parent-day-school] [^njsa-9-6-8-21-institution-defined] The duty to report such an allegation is the same universal duty described in the [question on who must report](#mandated-reporting). [^njsa-9-6-8-10-duty-staff]

Within the Department of Children and Families, the Institutional Abuse Investigation Unit investigates allegations of abuse and neglect in out-of-home settings, including schools, issues a final report within 60 days of the initial report, and notifies each appropriate entity of its findings. [^dcf-iaiu-schools] The Department's rules require the unit's investigator to assess the need to contact and cooperate with law enforcement or a prosecutor based on the allegations. [^njac-3a-10-4-1-law-enforcement]

From January 1, 2027, P.L.2025, c.281 extends the Title 9 definition of child to a person under 21 who is alleged to have been abused or neglected, or subjected to specified exploitation, by a teacher, employee, contractor or volunteer of an institution responsible for that person's care or supervision, as regulated by the Department of Children and Families, and it expressly includes employees of day schools. [^pl-2025-c281-older-child-definition] [^pl-2025-c281-effective] For a day school, the amended text names employees; it does not separately name day-school contractors or volunteers. [^pl-2025-c281-older-child-definition]

What a school may and may not agree to when an accused employee leaves is addressed in the [question on resignation and severance agreements](#agreements-suppressing-abuse-information).

## What child abuse and sexual misconduct history review must a New Jersey nonpublic school complete before hiring staff who work with students? {#employment-history-review}

**Short answer.** A New Jersey nonpublic school may not employ for pay, or contract for the paid services of, anyone in a position involving regular contact with students unless it collects the applicant's required disclosures about child abuse and sexual misconduct and reviews the applicant's history with past employers. [^njsa-18a-6-7-7-review]

The applicant lists the current employer, every school employer in the last 20 years and every employer in that period where the applicant had direct contact with children, signs an authorization and release, and states whether the applicant has been the subject of a child abuse or sexual misconduct investigation by an employer, State licensing agency, law enforcement agency or the Department of Children and Families, unless the investigation found the allegations false or unsubstantiated. [^njsa-18a-6-7-7-review] The statement also covers whether the applicant was disciplined, discharged, nonrenewed, asked to resign, or resigned or otherwise separated from employment, or had a license, professional license or certificate suspended, surrendered or revoked, while such allegations were pending or under investigation or because of an adjudication or finding of child abuse or sexual misconduct. [^njsa-18a-6-7-7-review] The school then asks each listed employer for the dates of employment and the same information, and it may do so by telephone, electronic or written communication, documenting a telephone review in writing. [^njsa-18a-6-7-7-employer-request] Child abuse here means any conduct within the reporting requirements of Title 9 directed toward a child or student, whatever the student's age. [^njsa-18a-6-7-6-child-abuse] The Department of Education reads the review as applying to substitute teachers and aides, and not to current employees who remain with the same school. [^njdoe-pttfaq-who]

An employer that has or had an employment relationship with the applicant within the last 20 years, whether current or former, must disclose the requested information within 20 days after receiving the request, and its failure to do so may be grounds for automatically disqualifying the applicant; the hiring school is not liable to an applicant it declines or terminates because of information received or an incomplete review. [^njsa-18a-6-7-9-twenty-days] [^njsa-18a-6-7-9-no-liability] For an out-of-state applicant, the school must make, and document with specificity, diligent efforts to verify the applicant's information and to obtain the required information from out-of-state employers. [^njsa-18a-6-7-9-out-of-state] An affirmative answer requires further inquiry before the application proceeds, and the school may employ an applicant provisionally for up to 90 days only when the applicant has made the required disclosures, the school knows of nothing the applicant had to disclose, and special or emergent circumstances justify it. [^njsa-18a-6-7-10-provisional] If disqualifying information surfaces after hiring, the school may immediately terminate the employee or rescind the offer, outside any grievance, appeal or tenure procedure. [^njsa-18a-6-7-9-termination]

Every application for a position involving regular contact with students must notify applicants of the penalties for willfully giving false information or failing to disclose, which include discipline up to termination or denial of employment, possible violation of N.J.S.A. 2C:28-3(a) and a civil penalty of up to $500. [^njsa-18a-6-7-8-penalties-notice] A school that provides information about a current or former employee is immune from civil and criminal liability unless the information was knowingly false. [^njsa-18a-6-7-11-immunity] The Department of Education publishes forms for the review, which a school may use but need not. [^njsa-18a-6-7-13-forms] [^njdoe-pttfaq-forms]

## Can a New Jersey nonpublic school agree to keep a child abuse or sexual misconduct allegation confidential when an employee resigns or is let go? {#agreements-suppressing-abuse-information}

**Short answer.** A New Jersey nonpublic school may not enter into an employment, resignation, severance or other agreement that suppresses or destroys information about an investigation of suspected child abuse or sexual misconduct by a current or former employee, or that affects the school's ability to report it. [^njsa-18a-6-7-12-prohibition]

The same section bars an agreement requiring the school to expunge allegations or findings from its records unless the allegations are found false or unsubstantiated after investigation, and it makes a contrary provision in an employment contract, resignation or termination agreement, or severance agreement executed, amended or entered into after the act took effect void and unenforceable. The prohibition reaches any action with those effects, not only a written agreement. [^njsa-18a-6-7-12-prohibition] [^njsa-18a-6-7-12-void] The Department of Education's guidance identifies June 1, 2018 as the date after which such agreements are void. [^njdoe-pttfaq-agreements]

The prohibition works alongside the school's duty, for anyone it employed within the last 20 years, to answer another school's employment history request within 20 days after receiving it, described in the [question on the employment history review](#employment-history-review), and alongside the immunity the statute gives a school for providing information that is not knowingly false. [^njsa-18a-6-7-9-twenty-days-agreements] [^njsa-18a-6-7-11-immunity-agreements] A promise of a neutral reference that would require the school to withhold an abuse or sexual misconduct investigation from a requesting school has the suppressing effect § 18A:6-7.12 forbids. [^njsa-18a-6-7-12-prohibition] Separately, N.J.S.A. 9:6-8.10 places its reporting duty on any person with reasonable cause to believe a child was abused, so a staff member's duty to report does not depend on what the school has agreed with a departing employee. [^njsa-9-6-8-10-duty-agreements]

## Must a New Jersey nonpublic school run fingerprint criminal history checks on staff, and when may it ask about criminal records? {#criminal-history-checks}

**Short answer.** New Jersey's general nonpublic school statute permits, but does not require, a nonpublic school to make final candidates for teaching, staff and other positions involving regular contact with pupils show that no criminal history record exists that would disqualify them from public school employment. [^njsa-18a-6-4-13-permissive]

A nonpublic school that uses the check must apply it consistently and without discrimination among candidates, and the statute defines a nonpublic school as a K–12 school other than a public school where a child may legally fulfill compulsory attendance requirements. [^njsa-18a-6-4-13-permissive] The applicant submits fingerprints to the Commissioner of Education, must give written consent and bears the cost. [^njsa-18a-6-4-14-fingerprints] The Commissioner applies the public school disqualification standards, informs the applicant in writing and notifies the school's chief administrator, and later reports pending charges for listed offenses against an employee of a school that requires the check. [^njsa-18a-6-4-15-determination] At a school that requires the check, a substitute rehired annually is checked only on initial employment. [^njsa-18a-6-4-17-substitutes] The Department of Education's Office of Student Protection runs these checks for nonpublic schools. [^njdoe-crimhist-osp] A separate statute, N.J.S.A. 18A:39-19.1, requires a school bus driver to submit fingerprints to the Commissioner of Education before employment as a school bus driver and on renewal of the driver's school bus license, with written consent and at the driver's cost. [^njsa-18a-39-19-1-bus-drivers]

New Jersey's Opportunity to Compete Act applies to an employer with 15 or more employees over 20 calendar weeks and bars criminal-record questions on an application, or orally or in writing, during the initial employment application process, which ends when the employer has conducted a first interview. [^njsa-34-6b-13-definitions] [^njsa-34-6b-14-timing] The Act also permits questions during that process when the applicant voluntarily discloses a criminal record. [^njsa-34-6b-14-timing] Its exceptions cover a position where a criminal history background check is required by law, rule or regulation, which includes a school bus driver position, and a position where a conviction would or may preclude employment as required by law. [^njsa-34-6b-16-exceptions] [^njsa-18a-39-19-1-bus-drivers] Neither exception clearly reaches a teaching or staff position at a nonpublic school, whose check under § 18A:6-4.13 is optional. [^njsa-34-6b-16-exceptions] [^njsa-18a-6-4-13-permissive] Because § 18A:6-4.13 already applies to final candidates, the conservative course is for a covered nonpublic school to hold criminal-record questions and the fingerprint check until after a first interview. [^njsa-18a-6-4-13-permissive] [^njsa-34-6b-14-timing]

After that point the Act permits criminal-record questions, and under § 34:6B-14(c) a school may refuse to hire because of a criminal record unless the record has been expunged or erased through executive pardon, provided the refusal is consistent with other applicable law. [^njsa-34-6b-14-timing] A violation carries a civil penalty of up to $1,000 for the first violation, $5,000 for the second and $10,000 for each later violation. [^njsa-34-6b-19-penalties]

## Must a New Jersey nonpublic school adopt a student anti-bullying policy under the Anti-Bullying Bill of Rights Act? {#anti-bullying-policy}

**Short answer.** New Jersey's Anti-Bullying Bill of Rights Act requires each school district to adopt a harassment, intimidation and bullying policy, while it only encourages nonpublic schools to comply with the Act. [^njsa-18a-37-15-district-policy] [^njsa-18a-37-31-encouraged]

The Act's policy mandate is addressed to each school district, which must include at least a prohibition, a definition no less inclusive than the statutory one, expected behavior, consequences and a reporting procedure. [^njsa-18a-37-15-district-policy] Separately, the Department of Education's rule requires each approved private school for students with disabilities to develop, adopt and implement a policy prohibiting harassment, intimidation or bullying on school grounds. [^njac-6a-16-7-8-apssd-policy] The statutory definition reaches gestures, written, verbal or physical acts and electronic communications reasonably perceived as motivated by an actual or perceived characteristic, such as race, religion, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression or disability, that take place on school property, at a school-sponsored function, on a school bus or off school grounds as the Act provides, and that substantially disrupt the school or the rights of other students. [^njsa-18a-37-14-definition] The conduct must also meet one of three tests: a reasonable person should know it will physically or emotionally harm a student or damage the student's property, or place the student in reasonable fear of that harm; it insults or demeans a student or group of students; or it creates a hostile educational environment. [^njsa-18a-37-14-definition] For a faith-based nonpublic school that follows the Act, the Act may not be interpreted to prohibit or abridge the legitimate statement, expression or free exercise of the faith's beliefs or tenets by the school's religious organization, faculty, staff or students. [^njsa-18a-37-31-faith-based]

A pending bill, S4467, titled the Jack Reid Law: Protect All Students Act, would prohibit harassment or bullying of nonpublic school students and require each nonpublic school to adopt a plain-language anti-harassment and bullying policy, publish it online and share it annually with employees, students and parents. [^nj-s4467-bill-synopsis] [^nj-s4467-nonpublic-policy] As of the November 22, 2026 review, the most recent record cited here is the version reported by the Senate Education Committee on October 1, 2026, which provides that a school is not exempt solely because a religious or sectarian institution operates it. [^nj-s4467-committee-status] [^nj-s4467-religious-school-coverage] Until a bill of that kind is enacted, a nonpublic school other than an approved private school for students with disabilities is encouraged, but not required, to follow the Anti-Bullying Bill of Rights Act. [^njsa-18a-37-31-encouraged] That conclusion is limited to Title 18A; whether the Law Against Discrimination reaches a school's treatment of students is addressed in the [question on student admissions and the Law Against Discrimination](/practice-guides/school-policy-compliance/us/new-jersey-religious-employers#student-admissions-public-accommodation).
