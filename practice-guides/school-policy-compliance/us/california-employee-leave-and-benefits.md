# School Policy Compliance in California: Employee Leave, Benefits and Tuition Remission[^about]

The leave and benefit rules a California independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: paid sick leave, pregnancy disability leave and the religious-employer exclusion, lactation accommodation and policy, CFRA and FMLA, State Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave for school staff, bereavement and reproductive loss leave, crime-victim leave, jury, witness and voting time, school-activity leave, final pay and vacation, tuition remission and stay-or-pay repayment.

## What paid sick leave must a California private or religious school provide, and which employees does it cover? {#paid-sick-leave}

**Short answer.** California requires every employer, including a private or religious school, to provide paid sick leave to an employee who works in California for it for thirty or more days within a year of starting, accruing at no less than one hour per thirty hours worked. [^ca-psl-employer-defined] [^ca-psl-eligibility] [^ca-psl-accrual-rate]

The employer definition reaches "any person employing another"[^ca-psl-employer-defined] with no minimum number of employees and no religious or nonprofit exclusion. [^ca-psl-employer-defined] The employee exclusions are limited to employees under a valid collective bargaining agreement that expressly sets wages, hours and working conditions, provides paid sick days or a qualifying paid leave policy, final and binding arbitration of sick-leave disputes, premium pay for all overtime and a regular rate at least 30 percent above the state minimum wage, construction workers under qualifying agreements, airline flight deck and cabin crew, certain retired public employees and employees described in a cross-referenced federal provision, so teachers and staff at a private school are covered. [^ca-psl-cba-exclusion] A teacher or administrator who is exempt from overtime as a professional, administrative or executive employee is deemed to work forty hours per workweek for accrual, unless the normal workweek is shorter. [^ca-psl-exempt-forty-hours]

Accrued sick days carry over, but an employer may limit use to forty hours or five days in each year of employment, calendar year or twelve-month period, and no accrual or carryover is required if the full amount of five days or forty hours is given at the start of each such period. [^ca-psl-carryover-and-use-cap] An employer need not let total accrual exceed eighty hours or ten days. [^ca-psl-accrual-cap] The Labor Commissioner reads the use limit as at least five days or forty hours, whichever is more, so the larger figure controls for an employee whose regular workday is longer than eight hours. [^ca-dlse-psl-whichever-more] The employee may begin using accrued sick days on the ninetieth day of employment. [^ca-psl-90th-day] A paid leave or paid time off policy satisfies the law if it makes the same amount of leave available for the same purposes and under the same conditions and meets the accrual, carryover and use requirements. [^ca-psl-pto-policy] The employer is not required to pay out unused sick days at separation, but sick days must be reinstated if the employee is rehired within one year. [^ca-psl-separation-rehire]

The permitted uses are diagnosis, care or treatment of, or preventive care for, the employee or a family member, and the purposes of crime-victim and court-related time off listed in Government Code § 12945.8. [^ca-psl-uses] [^ca-12945-8-jury-and-witness] An employer may not require the employee to find a replacement worker as a condition of using sick days, and may not deny, discharge, threaten, demote, suspend or otherwise discriminate against an employee for using them. [^ca-psl-no-replacement-and-retaliation] The available balance must appear on the itemized wage statement or in a separate writing on each payday. [^ca-psl-balance-notice]

## Must a California private or religious school provide pregnancy disability leave and pregnancy accommodation? {#pregnancy-disability-leave}

**Short answer.** California requires an employer of five or more persons to allow up to four months of pregnancy disability leave, but the Fair Employment and Housing Act excludes a religious association or corporation not organized for private profit from its definition of employer. [^ca-pdl-four-months] [^ca-feha-employer-religious-exclusion]

The pregnancy disability leave section has no employer definition of its own, so the Fair Employment and Housing Act's definitions, which apply throughout that part of the Government Code unless a different meaning clearly appears, supply both the five-employee threshold and the religious exclusion. [^ca-pdl-four-months] [^ca-feha-employer-religious-exclusion] Where the section applies, the employer must also maintain group health coverage during the leave for up to four months, provide reasonable accommodation requested on the advice of the employee's health care provider, and grant a requested temporary transfer to a less strenuous or hazardous position on the advice of the employee's physician where the transfer can be reasonably accommodated, or where the employer's policy, practice or collective bargaining agreement provides for transferring temporarily disabled employees. [^ca-pdl-health-coverage] [^ca-pdl-accommodation-transfer]

Whether a religiously affiliated school falls within the exclusion does not turn on its form of incorporation alone. In *McKeon v. Mercy Healthcare Sacramento*, the California Supreme Court held that the exclusion also benefits an organization of the requisite religious character that organized itself as a nonprofit public benefit corporation rather than a religious corporation. [^ca-mckeon-religious-character] A separate provision allows a nonprofit public benefit corporation formed by or affiliated with a particular religion that operates an educational institution as its sole or primary activity to restrict employment to members of that religion, and makes such a corporation subject to the Act "in all other respects"[^ca-feha-12926-2-f-religious-school]. [^ca-feha-12926-2-f-religious-school] For a religiously affiliated school organized as a public benefit corporation, *McKeon*'s reading of the exclusion and § 12926.2(f)'s subjection of such a corporation to the Act in all other respects point in different directions, so the conservative course for such a school is to treat the pregnancy disability leave and accommodation duties as applying. [^ca-mckeon-religious-character] [^ca-feha-12926-2-f-religious-school]

Federal law applies whatever the state exclusion's reach. The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act requires an employer with fifteen or more employees to reasonably accommodate the known limitations related to pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions of a qualified employee unless the accommodation would impose an undue hardship. [^usc-2000gg-1-pwfa-accommodation-ca] [^usc-2000gg-pwfa-covered-entity-ca] That Act is subject to Title VII's religious-employer provision in 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-1(a). [^usc-2000gg-5-religious-ca] Whether the school's staff are covered by State Disability Insurance is addressed in the [question on State Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave](#state-disability-and-paid-family-leave).

## What lactation breaks, lactation space and written lactation policy must a California school provide? {#lactation-accommodation}

**Short answer.** Every California employer must provide reasonable break time each time an employee needs to express breast milk and a private location other than a bathroom, subject to a limited undue-hardship exemption for employers with fewer than fifty employees. [^ca-leave-lab-1030-break-time] [^ca-leave-lab-1031-location] [^ca-lab-1031-small-employer]

The break-time duty is addressed to "Every employer, including the state and any political subdivision"[^ca-leave-lab-1030-break-time], with no employee-count threshold or religious exclusion, and it states no fixed end date for the right. [^ca-leave-lab-1030-break-time] Break time runs concurrently with existing break time where possible, and time that does not run concurrently with authorized rest time is unpaid. [^ca-leave-lab-1030-break-time] The location must be near the employee's work area, shielded from view and free from intrusion, and must be safe and clean, with a surface for a breast pump, a place to sit and access to electricity, and the employer must provide access to a sink with running water and a refrigerator or other cooling device nearby. [^ca-leave-lab-1031-location] [^ca-lab-1031-room-contents] An employer with fewer than fifty employees may be exempt from a location requirement if it demonstrates undue hardship, measured by significant difficulty or expense in relation to the size, financial resources, nature or structure of its business, and must then make reasonable efforts to provide a private location other than a toilet stall near the work area. [^ca-lab-1031-small-employer]

The employer must develop and implement a written lactation accommodation policy stating the right to request accommodation, the request process, the employer's obligation to respond and the right to complain to the Labor Commissioner, include it in an employee handbook or set of policies, and distribute it to new employees and to an employee who asks about or requests parental leave. [^ca-leave-lab-1034-policy] An employer that cannot provide compliant break time or space must respond to the employee in writing. [^ca-leave-lab-1034-policy]

The federal PUMP Act provision separately requires reasonable break time each time the employee needs to express milk, for one year after the child's birth, and a private place other than a bathroom. [^usc-218d-pump-break-lactation-ca] An employer with fewer than fifty employees is exempt from the federal provision if compliance would impose an undue hardship by causing significant difficulty or expense in relation to the size, financial resources, nature or structure of its business. [^usc-218d-small-employer-hardship-ca]

## Which job-protected family and medical leave laws apply to a California private or religious school? {#cfra-and-fmla}

**Short answer.** The California Family Rights Act requires an employer of five or more persons to grant an employee with more than twelve months of service and 1,250 hours in the previous twelve months up to twelve workweeks of family care and medical leave in a twelve-month period. [^ca-cfra-entitlement] [^ca-cfra-employer-defined]

The Act supplies its own definition of employer for that section, any person who directly employs five or more persons to perform services for a wage or salary, and that definition contains no exclusion for a religious association or nonprofit corporation, unlike the general Fair Employment and Housing Act definition. [^ca-cfra-employer-defined] [^ca-feha-employer-religious-exclusion-cfra] The Civil Rights Council's regulation likewise defines a covered employer by the number of persons it directly employs, without requiring the five employees to work at the same location or full-time. [^ca-cfra-reg-covered-employer] The eligibility test in the Act contains no minimum number of employees within seventy-five miles of the worksite. [^ca-cfra-entitlement]

Family care and medical leave covers the birth, adoption or foster placement of a child, care for a child, parent, grandparent, grandchild, sibling, spouse, domestic partner or designated person with a serious health condition, the employee's own serious health condition other than pregnancy disability, and a qualifying military exigency. [^ca-cfra-purposes] The leave carries a guarantee of employment in the same or a comparable position, and the employer must maintain group health plan coverage for up to twelve workweeks. [^ca-cfra-entitlement] [^ca-cfra-health-coverage] The leave need not be paid, but the employee may elect, or the employer may require, substitution of accrued vacation or other accrued time off. [^ca-cfra-pay-and-substitution] Leave under the Act runs concurrently with FMLA leave, except FMLA leave for pregnancy disability, and an employee may take pregnancy disability leave in addition to this leave. [^ca-cfra-fmla-concurrent]

The federal Family and Medical Leave Act covers a private elementary or secondary school regardless of its size, but an employee is eligible only if the school employs at least fifty employees within seventy-five miles of the employee's worksite. [^cfr-825-600-school-coverage-ca] [^usc-2611-fmla-75-mile-ca] A federally eligible employee must also have worked for the employer for at least twelve months and 1,250 hours in the preceding twelve months, [^cfr-825-110-eligibility-ca] and the federal regulations set special rules for instructional employees, such as teachers and coaches, on intermittent leave, reduced schedules and leave near the end of an academic term. [^cfr-825-600-instructional-employees-ca] Wage replacement during family leave comes from Paid Family Leave where the school's staff are covered, as explained in the [question on State Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave](#state-disability-and-paid-family-leave).

## Do California State Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave cover staff at a nonprofit or church-run school? {#state-disability-and-paid-family-leave}

**Short answer.** California State Disability Insurance, including Paid Family Leave, covers service that counts as employment under the Unemployment Insurance Code, which reaches staff of a secular nonprofit school but excludes service for a church or for a primarily religious organization that a church operates, supervises, controls or principally supports. [^ca-uic-2606-di-employment] [^ca-uic-3301-pfl-within-sdi] [^ca-uic-608-nonprofit] [^ca-uic-634-5-church]

Disability insurance coverage follows the definition of employment in the unemployment insurance part of the Code, and Paid Family Leave is a family temporary disability insurance program established within the State Disability Insurance program. [^ca-uic-2606-di-employment] [^ca-uic-3301-pfl-within-sdi] Service for a religious, charitable or educational organization described in Internal Revenue Code § 501(c)(3), which federal unemployment tax excludes only by reason of section 3306(c)(8), is employment except as § 634.5 provides. [^ca-uic-608-nonprofit] Section 634.5 excludes service in the employ of a church or convention or association of churches, or of an organization operated primarily for religious purposes and operated, supervised, controlled or principally supported by a church or convention or association of churches, as well as service by an ordained, commissioned or licensed minister in the exercise of the ministry or a member of a religious order performing required duties. [^ca-uic-634-5-church] The exclusion is framed in two parts, a primarily religious purpose and church operation, supervision, control or principal support, so a school with a religious affiliation that is neither operated, supervised or controlled by a church nor principally supported by one does not fall within its words. [^ca-uic-634-5-church]

The "operated primarily for religious purposes"[^ca-uic-634-5-church] test has a constitutional limit. In *Catholic Charities Bureau, Inc. v. Wisconsin Labor & Industry Review Commission*, the Supreme Court held that Wisconsin violated the First Amendment by applying a similarly worded exemption to deny it to church-controlled charities because they neither proselytized nor served only Catholics. [^ca-catholic-charities-bureau-2025] A government that distinguishes among religions based on theological differences in their provision of services imposes a denominational preference that must satisfy the highest level of judicial scrutiny. [^ca-catholic-charities-bureau-scrutiny] The exclusion describes automatic coverage only: an employing unit whose services are not employment may file a written election to have those services in one or more establishments deemed employment for all purposes of the Code for at least two calendar years. [^ca-uic-702-election]

Paid Family Leave provides up to eight weeks of wage replacement in a twelve-month period to care for a seriously ill family member, to bond with a new child or for a qualifying military exigency. [^ca-uic-3301-pfl-within-sdi] It pays benefits but does not itself guarantee reinstatement, and the statute states that it does not abridge the rights and responsibilities under the California Family Rights Act or pregnancy disability leave. [^ca-uic-3301-pfl-within-sdi] On July 1, 2028, SB 590 (Stats. 2025, ch. 772) replaces § 3301 with a version that adds a designated person to the people a worker may take time off to care for, [^ca-sb-590-pfl-designated-person] and from that date defines a designated person as any care recipient related by blood or whose association with the individual is the equivalent of a family relationship. [^ca-sb-590-designated-person-defined]

## What bereavement and reproductive loss leave must a California school give its employees? {#bereavement-and-reproductive-loss-leave}

**Short answer.** A California employer of five or more persons must grant an employee who has worked for it at least thirty days up to five days of bereavement leave upon the death of a family member. [^ca-bereavement-five-days] [^ca-bereavement-definitions]

The bereavement section defines employer for itself as a person who employs five or more persons for a wage or salary, without a religious exclusion, and defines a family member as a spouse, child, parent, sibling, grandparent, grandchild, domestic partner or parent-in-law. [^ca-bereavement-definitions] The section does not apply to an employee covered by a valid collective bargaining agreement that expressly provides equivalent bereavement leave, sets wages, hours and working conditions, pays premium rates for all overtime where applicable and pays at least 30 percent above the state minimum wage. [^ca-bereavement-cba-exclusion] The days need not be consecutive and must be completed within three months of the death. [^ca-bereavement-timing] The leave follows the employer's existing bereavement policy; without one it may be unpaid, and where a policy gives fewer than five days the employee is still entitled to five, with the remainder unpaid, but may use available vacation, personal leave, sick leave or compensatory time off. [^ca-bereavement-pay] The employer may request documentation of the death within thirty days of the first day of leave, and must keep the request and the documentation confidential. [^ca-bereavement-documentation] [^ca-bereavement-confidentiality] SB 1149 (Stats. 2026, ch. 679), approved September 27, 2026, amends § 12945.7 to add a designated person to the family members whose death qualifies: any individual related to the employee by blood or whose association with the employee is the equivalent of a family relationship, whom the employee may identify when requesting the leave. "An employer may limit an employee to one designated person per 12-month period for bereavement leave."[^ca-sb-1149-designated-person] [^ca-sb-1149-designated-person] The act contains no urgency clause and no operative date of its own, so the amendment takes effect on January 1, 2027, as a statute enacted at the 2026 regular session. [^ca-sb-1149-designated-person] As chaptered, the amended definition lists a spouse, child, parent, sibling, grandparent, grandchild or domestic partner and no longer lists the parent-in-law that the current text includes, so the conservative course is for a school to keep granting bereavement leave on the death of a parent-in-law. [^ca-sb-1149-designated-person] [^ca-bereavement-definitions]

California also requires a separate reproductive loss leave, in Government Code § 12945.6. A private school that employs five or more persons must grant an employee who has worked for it at least 30 days up to five days of reproductive loss leave following a reproductive loss event, which the section defines as the day, or for a multiple-day event the final day, of a failed adoption, failed surrogacy, miscarriage, stillbirth or unsuccessful assisted reproduction. [^ca-gov-12945-6-leave] [^ca-gov-12945-6-definitions] Like the bereavement section, § 12945.6 defines employer for itself as a person who employs five or more persons for a wage or salary, without a religious exclusion. [^ca-gov-12945-6-definitions] Where an employee has more than one event in a 12-month period, the statute provides that "an employer shall not be obligated to grant a total amount of reproductive loss leave time in excess of 20 days within a 12-month period."[^ca-gov-12945-6-leave] [^ca-gov-12945-6-leave] The days may be nonconsecutive and must be completed within three months of the event, or, if the employee is on or chooses to take another state or federal leave before or immediately after the event, within three months of the end of that leave. [^ca-gov-12945-6-leave] The leave follows the employer's existing applicable leave policy; without one it may be unpaid, but the employee may use available vacation, personal leave, accrued sick leave or compensatory time off. [^ca-gov-12945-6-leave] The employer may not retaliate against an employee for taking the leave or for giving information or testimony about it, or interfere with the right, and must keep the request and any information provided confidential except as necessary for internal personnel or counsel or as required by law. [^ca-gov-12945-6-protections]

## What time off and accommodation must a California school give an employee who is a victim of domestic violence or another violent crime? {#crime-victim-leave}

**Short answer.** No California employer may retaliate against a crime victim for taking time off to seek a restraining order, or against a victim or family member for attending related court proceedings, and an employer with twenty-five or more employees also may not penalize time off for listed medical, counseling, safety and relocation purposes. [^ca-victim-leave-all-employers] [^ca-victim-leave-court-proceedings] [^ca-victim-leave-definitions] [^ca-victim-leave-25-employees]

The section defines an employer as any person who directly employs one or more persons, with no religious exclusion, and treats domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking and other listed acts of violence as qualifying acts of violence whether or not anyone is arrested, prosecuted or convicted. [^ca-victim-leave-definitions] The larger-employer purposes extend to time off when a family member is the victim. [^ca-victim-leave-25-employees] An employer may limit total leave for those purposes to twelve weeks, and where only a family member who did not die as a result of the crime is the victim, to ten days in total and five days for relocation, and the leave runs concurrently with FMLA and CFRA leave if the employee would have been eligible for them. [^ca-victim-leave-limits] Unless a collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise, the employee may use available vacation, personal leave, paid sick leave or compensatory time off, and paid sick leave expressly covers these purposes. [^ca-victim-leave-paid-time] [^ca-psl-uses-victim]

The employee must give reasonable advance notice unless that is not feasible, and after an unscheduled absence the employer may request a certification, which may be a police report, court order, documentation from a listed professional or a signed written statement; the employer must keep the employee's request confidential to the extent the law allows. [^ca-victim-leave-notice-certification] The employer must provide reasonable accommodations for the safety of an employee who is, or whose family member is, a victim and who requests one, such as a transfer, modified schedule or installed lock, through a timely, good faith interactive process, but need not take an action that constitutes an undue hardship. [^ca-victim-leave-accommodation] The employer must inform each employee of these rights in writing at hire, annually, on request and when an employee discloses victim status, using the Civil Rights Department's form or a substantially similar notice. [^ca-victim-leave-written-notice]

## Can a California school penalize an employee for missing work for jury duty, a witness subpoena or voting? {#jury-witness-and-voting-time}

**Short answer.** A California employer may not discharge or discriminate against an employee for taking time off to serve on an inquest or trial jury, or to appear in court as a witness under a subpoena or other court order. [^ca-12945-8-jury-witness]

The protection reaches any person who directly employs one or more persons, with no religious exclusion. [^ca-12945-8-employer-one-or-more] The section bars discharge and discrimination and does not itself require pay for jury or witness days, but unless a collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise the employee may use available vacation, personal leave, paid sick leave or compensatory time off for that time. [^ca-12945-8-jury-witness] [^ca-12945-8-paid-time-jury] Paid sick leave expressly covers jury and witness time taken or requested on or after January 1, 2025. [^ca-psl-uses-jury]

For voting, a voter who does not have sufficient time outside working hours to vote at a statewide election may take enough working time off to vote, and up to two hours of that time is without loss of pay. [^ca-elec-14000-voting-time] The time off is taken at the beginning or end of the regular shift, whichever allows the most free time for voting and the least time off, unless otherwise agreed, and an employee who knows by the third working day before the election that time off will be needed must give at least two working days' notice. [^ca-elec-14000-voting-time]

## Must a California school give employees who are parents time off for their own children's school or child care activities? {#school-activities-leave}

**Short answer.** A California employer with twenty-five or more employees at one location may not discharge or discriminate against a parent of a K–12 child, or of a child in licensed child care, for taking up to forty hours a year for school or child care activities and emergencies. [^ca-lab-230-8-forty-hours]

Time off for planned activities, such as enrolling a child or participating in school activities, requires reasonable advance notice and may not exceed eight hours in a calendar month, while time off for a child care provider or school emergency requires notice. [^ca-lab-230-8-forty-hours] A parent includes a guardian, stepparent, foster parent, grandparent or person in loco parentis, and an emergency includes a request that the child be picked up, behavioral or discipline problems, an unexpected closure or a natural disaster. [^ca-lab-230-8-definitions]

Unless a collective bargaining agreement entered into before January 1, 1995 and in effect on that date provides otherwise, the employee must use existing vacation, personal leave or compensatory time off for a planned absence, or unpaid time off to the extent the employer makes it available. [^ca-lab-230-8-use-of-vacation] If all permanent, full-time employees take vacation during the same period of the year, an employee may not use that accrued vacation at another time for a planned absence under this section. [^ca-lab-230-8-use-of-vacation] On request, the employee must provide documentation from the school or child care provider of the activity, and an employee penalized for taking protected time is entitled to reinstatement and reimbursement of lost wages and benefits. [^ca-lab-230-8-documentation-remedies]

## When must a California school pay a departing teacher's final wages, and must it pay out unused vacation? {#final-pay-and-vacation}

**Short answer.** A California employer must pay a discharged employee's earned and unpaid wages immediately, and must pay an employee without a written contract for a definite period who quits within seventy-two hours, or at quitting if the employee gave seventy-two hours' notice. [^ca-lab-201-discharge] [^ca-lab-202-quit]

The seventy-two-hour rule for a resignation is written for an employee "not having a written contract for a definite period"[^ca-lab-202-quit], so a teacher who resigns during the term of a written contract for a definite period falls outside that sentence's words. [^ca-lab-202-quit] If an employer willfully fails to pay the final wages of an employee who is discharged or quits, the wages continue as a penalty from the due date at the same rate, for no more than thirty days. [^ca-lab-203-waiting-time]

Unless a collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise, when a contract or policy provides paid vacation, all vested vacation must be paid as wages at the final rate when employment ends, and the contract or policy may not provide for forfeiture of vested vacation on termination. [^ca-lab-227-3-vacation] Unused paid sick leave is treated differently: the employer is not required to pay it out at separation. [^ca-psl-separation-rehire-final-pay]

## Is tuition remission for employees' children at a California K–12 school excluded from federal and California income tax? {#employee-tuition-remission}

**Short answer.** Internal Revenue Code § 117(d) excludes from gross income an educational organization's tuition reduction for below-graduate education of an employee or the employee's dependent child, but a highly compensated employee's reduction qualifies only if offered on nondiscriminatory terms. [^irc-117-d-qualified-reduction-ca] [^irc-132-h-dependent-child-ca] [^irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination-ca]

The exclusion is limited to tuition reductions given by an organization described in § 170(b)(1)(A)(ii), which is an educational organization that normally maintains a regular faculty and curriculum and an enrolled student body at the place where it teaches. [^irc-117-d-qualified-reduction-ca] [^irc-170-b-educational-organization-ca] The Treasury regulation describes such an organization as one whose primary function is the presentation of formal instruction and says the term includes primary, secondary and preparatory schools, so a standalone pre-kindergarten or child care provider falls within it only if formal instruction, rather than custodial care, is its primary function. [^cfr-1-170a-9-c-1-formal-instruction-ca] IRS Publication 970 describes a qualified tuition reduction as one for education below the graduate level, including primary and secondary school. [^irs-pub-970-primary-secondary-ca]

A reduction that does not qualify, such as one offered only to highly compensated employees, falls within gross income as compensation for services, including fringe benefits. [^irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination-ca] [^irc-61-compensation-fringe-ca] The highly compensated employee test uses Code § 414(q), which covers a 5-percent owner in the current or preceding year and an employee whose preceding-year compensation exceeded an inflation-adjusted threshold, limited, if the employer so elects, to the top-paid group. [^irc-414-q-hce-definition-ca] IRS Notice 2025-67 states that the threshold remains $160,000. [^irs-notice-2025-67-hce-ca]

For California personal income tax, Revenue and Taxation Code § 17131 applies Part III of Subchapter B of Chapter 1 of the Internal Revenue Code, the part that contains § 117, "except as otherwise provided"[^ca-rtc-17131-conformity]. [^ca-rtc-17131-conformity] This answer does not trace whether a separate California provision modifies the § 117(d) exclusion.

## Can a California school deduct or recover a signing bonus, tuition or training cost from a teacher who leaves? {#repayment-on-departure}

**Short answer.** A California employer may not collect or receive back from an employee any part of wages it has already paid, and may withhold from wages only as state or federal law requires or empowers or as the employee expressly authorizes in writing for listed purposes. [^ca-lab-221-no-kickback] [^ca-lab-224-authorized-deductions]

The written-authorization category covers insurance premiums, hospital or medical dues and other deductions not amounting to a rebate or deduction from the standard wage set by collective bargaining, wage agreement or statute. [^ca-lab-224-authorized-deductions] Final wages remain due on the schedule in the [question on final pay](#final-pay-and-vacation).

A separate rule governs the repayment promise itself. As amended by AB 1697 (Stats. 2026, ch. 889), an urgency statute that took effect immediately, Business and Professions Code § 16608 makes it unlawful, for contracts entered into on or after January 1, 2027, to include in an employment contract, or require a worker to sign, a term requiring the worker to pay a debt, or imposing a penalty, fee or cost, if employment with a specific employer ends. [^ca-leave-ab-1697-urgency] [^ca-16608-ab-1697-prohibition] The section as it existed on January 1, 2026 is inoperative from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026. [^ca-16608-ab-1697-void-and-inoperative]

The prohibition does not apply to a contract for repayment of tuition for a transferable credential that is offered separately from the employment contract, is not a condition of employment, fixes a repayment amount no greater than the employer's cost, prorates repayment without acceleration and requires no repayment after a termination other than for misconduct. [^ca-16608-ab-1697-tuition-exception] It also does not apply to a contract for a discretionary or unearned payment, including a bonus not tied to specific job performance, if the repayment terms are in a separate agreement, the employee is told of the right to consult an attorney and given at least five business days, repayment is prorated over a retention period of no more than two years without interest, the worker may defer the payment to the end of the retention period, and separation was the employee's choice or a termination for misconduct. [^ca-16608-ab-1697-bonus-exception] A further exception covers repayment of advanced paid time off in excess of accrued time on voluntary separation, capped at forty hours, disclosed separately and without interest. [^ca-16608-ab-1697-pto-exception] A violating term is void, and a worker may sue for actual damages or $5,000 per worker, whichever is greater, plus injunctive relief and reasonable attorney's fees and costs.
