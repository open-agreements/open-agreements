# School Policy Compliance in Texas: School Safety, Health and Security[^about]

The safety and health rules a Texas independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: which drill, emergency-plan and panic-alert mandates stop at public schools, school marshals and armed staff, firearms on school premises, AEDs and cardiac emergency plans, epinephrine, asthma medication and opioid antagonists, concussion rules, immunizations and health screenings, and hazing.

## Must a Texas private school follow the state's school drill, emergency operations plan and silent panic alert requirements? {#drills-plans-and-panic-alerts}

**Short answer.** The Texas Education Code imposes its multihazard emergency operations plan, mandatory drill, emergency response map, breaching tool and silent panic alert requirements on school districts and other public entities, and none of those sections names a private school. [^tx-ed-37-108-eop-districts] [^tx-ed-37-117-map-public] [^tx-ed-37-1171-breaching-tool] [^tx-ed-37-118-panic-alert]

The Education Code states its own reach: it applies to all educational institutions supported in whole or in part by state tax funds unless the code specifically excludes them. [^tx-ed-1-001-applicability] Section 37.108 requires each school district or public junior college district to adopt and implement a multihazard emergency operations plan, and the plan's mandatory school drills and exercises, including the drills required under Section 37.114, apply where the plan applies to a school district. [^tx-ed-37-108-eop-districts] [^tx-ed-37-108-drills] Section 37.114 directs the commissioner of education to designate by rule the number and type of mandatory school drills each semester, not to exceed eight in total. [^tx-ed-37-114-drill-rules]

The 2023 and 2025 school safety additions follow the same pattern. Section 37.117, added by H.B. 3 in 2023, requires each school district and open-enrollment charter school to give the Department of Public Safety and local law enforcement and first responders an accurate campus map and an opportunity for a walk-through. [^tx-ed-37-117-map-public] Section 37.1171, added by H.B. 33 in 2025, requires each school district and open-enrollment charter school to have a breaching tool and a ballistic shield at each campus. [^tx-ed-37-1171-breaching-tool] Section 37.118, enacted by S.B. 838 in 2023, requires each school district and open-enrollment charter school to provide every classroom with silent panic alert technology. [^tx-ed-37-118-panic-alert]

Chapter 37 does give a private school one route into the public system's safety training: Section 37.119 allows a school district, from money available for the purpose, to offer school safety training courses, including active shooter training, at no cost to employees of accredited private schools located in the district, and it defines an accredited private school as one accredited by an organization recognized by the Texas Private School Accreditation Commission or the Texas Education Agency. [^tx-ed-37-119-private-training] This answer is limited to these Education Code provisions: it does not address fire-code, building-code or local emergency-planning obligations, which this guide does not cover.

## May a Texas private school appoint school marshals or let employees carry firearms on campus? {#school-marshals-and-armed-staff}

**Short answer.** A Texas private school's governing body may appoint as school marshals employees certified as eligible under Occupations Code section 1701.260, and Penal Code section 46.03 lets a school permit firearms on its premises by written regulations or written authorization. [^tx-ed-37-0813-appointment] [^tx-pc-46-03-school-premises]

A private school marshal may carry or possess a handgun on school premises only as the governing body's written regulations provide. [^tx-ed-37-0813-appointment] Those regulations must allow concealed carry, open carry while in a uniform identifying the marshal, or storage in a locked and secured safe or location, and must require frangible duty ammunition approved by the Texas Commission on Law Enforcement; the marshal may use the handgun only in circumstances that would justify deadly force under the Penal Code. [^tx-ed-37-0813-regulations] A marshal's status becomes inactive when the marshal license expires, the employee's handgun license is suspended or revoked, employment ends or the governing body gives notice that the services are no longer required. [^tx-ed-37-0813-inactive-and-notice] The marshal's identity is confidential, but if a parent or guardian asks in writing, the school must say in writing whether any employee is currently appointed a marshal. [^tx-ed-37-0813-inactive-and-notice] Section 37.0813 "does not apply to a school whose students meet the definition provided by Section 29.916(a)(1)"[^tx-ed-37-0813-inactive-and-notice], which defines a home-schooled student. [^tx-ed-37-0813-inactive-and-notice] [^tx-ed-29-916-home-schooled]

Occupations Code section 1701.260 opens the Texas Commission on Law Enforcement's marshal training to an employee of a private school who holds a license to carry a handgun, and the program runs 80 hours. [^tx-oc-1701-260-training] [^tx-oc-1701-260-psych] The commission may license a trainee only if a psychological examination shows the trainee is fit to carry out a marshal's duties. [^tx-oc-1701-260-psych] Under Education Code section 37.08131, a private school may enter a memorandum of understanding with another public or private school so that its marshal can act as a marshal at the other school's campus for the duration of an event there in which both schools are participating; the memorandum must comply with the written-regulation requirements for marshals and may satisfy the written-authorization requirement of Penal Code section 46.03(a)(1). [^tx-ed-37-08131-mou] [^tx-pc-46-03-school-premises] Separately, Section 37.0818 lets the governing body of a private school with an enrollment of at least 1,500 students commission peace officers who meet the commission's minimum standards. [^tx-ed-37-0818-peace-officers]

Section 37.087 makes a private school immune from liability for damages resulting from any reasonable action by its security personnel to maintain campus safety, and for reasonable action by an employee who has the governing body's written permission to carry a firearm on campus. [^tx-ed-37-087-immunity] By contrast, the armed-officer mandate added by H.B. 3 in 2023, Section 37.0814, requires the board of trustees of each school district to have at least one armed security officer present during regular school hours at each district campus and does not reach private schools. [^tx-ed-37-0814-armed-officer]

## What limits does Texas law place on firearms at a private school? {#firearms-on-school-grounds}

**Short answer.** Texas Penal Code section 46.03 makes it a third-degree felony to intentionally, knowingly or recklessly possess or go with a firearm on the premises of an accredited public or private primary or secondary school unless the school's written regulations or written authorization permit it. [^tx-pc-46-03-firearms-school] [^tx-pc-46-03-definitions] [^tx-pc-46-03-penalty]

The offense also reaches grounds or buildings the school owns and controls while a school-sponsored activity is being conducted there, and the school's passenger transportation vehicles, and it covers location-restricted knives, clubs and other listed prohibited weapons as well as firearms. [^tx-pc-46-03-firearms-school] The exception for a person licensed to carry a concealed handgun is written only for postsecondary institutions, so a handgun license does not by itself authorize carrying on a primary or secondary school's premises. [^tx-pc-46-03-license-exception] Two definitions narrow the offense: *premises* means a building or portion of a building and excludes driveways, sidewalks, parking lots and parking garages, and *school* means an accredited primary or secondary school. [^tx-pc-46-03-definitions] An unaccredited private school's buildings therefore fall outside this particular offense. [^tx-pc-46-03-definitions] Separately, the principal of a public or private primary or secondary school, or the principal's designee, must notify the municipal police department, or the county sheriff outside a municipality, and any school district police department when the principal has reasonable grounds to believe that a listed weapon was possessed in school, on school property or at a school-sponsored or school-related activity. [^tx-ed-37-015-weapons-report] This answer does not address other weapons laws that may apply there.

Texas's employee parking-lot statute, Labor Code section 52.061, bars a public or private employer from prohibiting a licensed or otherwise lawful employee from transporting or storing a lawfully possessed firearm or ammunition in a locked, privately owned vehicle in employer-provided parking. [^tx-lc-52-061-parking] Section 52.062 states that Section 52.061 does not apply to "a private school, as defined by Section 22.081, Education Code"[^tx-lc-52-062-private-school], and Education Code section 22.081 defines a private school as one offering instruction in any of prekindergarten through grade 12 that is not operated by a governmental entity. [^tx-lc-52-062-private-school] [^tx-ed-22-081-private-school]

Federal law adds the Gun-Free School Zones Act, 18 U.S.C. § 922(q)(2), which makes it unlawful for an individual knowingly to possess a firearm that has moved in or affects interstate or foreign commerce at a place the individual knows, or has reasonable cause to believe, is a school zone. [^tx-usc-922-q-2-school-zone] A school zone is in or on the grounds of, or within 1,000 feet of the grounds of, a public, parochial or private school providing elementary or secondary education as determined under state law. [^tx-usc-921-a-26-school-zone-definition] The federal exceptions include possession on private property that is not part of school grounds, possession by a person licensed by the State where the law requires law enforcement to verify the person's qualification before licensing, an unloaded firearm in a locked container or locked vehicle rack, use in a program the school approves, possession under a contract with the school, and possession by a law enforcement officer acting in an official capacity. [^tx-usc-922-q-2-school-zone]

## Must a Texas private school keep an AED, train staff in CPR or adopt a cardiac emergency response plan? {#aed-and-cardiac-emergency-plans}

**Short answer.** Texas requires a private school to keep an AED, adopt a CPR and AED training policy and implement a cardiac emergency response plan only if the school receives an AED, or funding to buy or lease one, from the Texas Education Agency. [^tx-ed-38-017-g-private-aed] [^tx-ed-22-902-e-cpr-policy] [^tx-ed-38-018-d-private-trigger]

A private school that receives an AED or AED funding from the agency must make at least one AED available at the school and, in coordination with the Texas Association of Private and Parochial Schools, adopt a policy on AED availability at athletic competitions and practices consistent with the section's requirements, including its training and maintenance requirements. [^tx-ed-38-017-g-private-aed] Its CPR and AED instruction policy must comply with Section 22.902(c), which requires each school nurse, assistant school nurse, athletic coach or sponsor, physical education instructor, marching band director, cheerleading coach and student athletic trainer to receive and maintain CPR and AED certification from the American Heart Association, the American Red Cross or a similar nationally recognized association. [^tx-ed-22-902-e-cpr-policy] [^tx-ed-22-902-c-certification]

The cardiac emergency response plan in Section 38.018, rewritten by S.B. 865 in 2025, must be developed with local emergency services providers and must include a cardiac emergency response team, activation procedures, campus-wide dissemination, ongoing first aid, CPR and AED training for appropriate employees including coaches, nurses and athletic trainers, annual practice drills, and annual review. [^tx-ed-38-018-plan-contents] S.B. 865 requires each private school to which the plan requirement applies to implement the plan no later than the first instructional day of the 2027–2028 school year. [^tx-sb-865-implementation]

Any private school that owns or leases an AED, whether or not it came from the agency, is subject to Health and Safety Code chapter 779: it must maintain and test the device to the manufacturer's guidelines and inspect it monthly, notify the local emergency medical services provider of the device's existence, location and type when it acquires one, and promptly notify that provider after using it on a person in cardiac arrest. [^tx-hs-779-003-maintenance] [^tx-hs-779-004-005-notice] A person or entity that acquires an AED, or is responsible for the location where it is placed, is not liable for civil damages related to its use, attempted use or non-use unless the conduct is wilfully or wantonly negligent. [^tx-hs-779-006-immunity] Civil Practice and Remedies Code section 74.151 separately shields a person who in good faith administers emergency care, including with an AED, from civil damages unless the act is wilfully or wantonly negligent, but the protection does not cover care given for or in expectation of payment or by a person at the scene to solicit business. [^tx-cprc-74-151-good-samaritan] For comparison, the AED mandate in Section 38.017(a) requires each school district to make at least one AED available at each campus. [^tx-ed-38-017-a-district-aed]

## May a Texas private school stock and give epinephrine, asthma rescue medication or naloxone, and what must its policy say? {#epinephrine-asthma-and-naloxone}

**Short answer.** Texas law permits, but does not require, a private school to adopt policies for maintaining and administering epinephrine delivery systems, medication for respiratory distress and opioid antagonists such as naloxone. [^tx-ed-38-208-a-optional-policies] [^tx-ed-38-222-b-private-opioid]

For these provisions, a private school is one offering instruction in any of prekindergarten through grade 12 that is not operated by a governmental entity, excluding a school whose students meet the definition in Section 29.916(a)(1), which defines a home-schooled student. [^tx-ed-38-201-private-school] [^tx-ed-29-916-home-schooled-medication] An adopted epinephrine policy must let authorized and trained school personnel and volunteers administer an epinephrine delivery system to a person reasonably believed to be experiencing anaphylaxis on campus, and may extend that authority to off-campus school events and travel to and from them. [^tx-ed-38-208-b-epinephrine] The school must then have at least one authorized and trained person present during all hours the campus is open, may not penalize an employee or volunteer for refusing to administer or be trained, and must store the supply in a secure, easily accessible location. [^tx-ed-38-208-d-e-staffing] Training must cover recognizing anaphylaxis, administration, follow-up emergency procedures and disposal. [^tx-ed-38-210-training] Within 10 business days after an administration, the school must report it to its governing body, the prescriber and the commissioner of state health services. [^tx-ed-38-209-report] Written notice of the policy goes to a parent or guardian of each enrolled student before the policy is implemented and before the start of each school year. [^tx-ed-38-212-notice]

An adopted respiratory-distress policy must let authorized and trained personnel and volunteers give the medication on campus or at a school-sponsored or school-related activity on or off school property, and a student whose parent has not told the school of an asthma diagnosis must be referred to a primary care provider that day. [^tx-ed-38-208-b-1-respiratory] A person who in good faith acts or fails to act under the epinephrine and respiratory-distress subchapter is immune from civil or criminal liability or disciplinary action. [^tx-ed-38-215-a-good-faith] A private school and its personnel and volunteers are also immune from suit for acts or omissions under that subchapter. [^tx-ed-38-215-immunity]

A private school that adopts an opioid antagonist policy may apply it only to campuses serving grades 6 through 12 or to every campus. [^tx-ed-38-222-b-private-opioid] The policy must authorize trained personnel and volunteers to administer an opioid antagonist to a person reasonably believed to be experiencing an opioid-related drug overdose, keep at least one such person present during regular school hours, set the number of doses each campus must have, and require secure, easily accessible storage. [^tx-ed-38-222-c-contents] The 10-business-day reporting duty and the good-faith immunity apply to opioid antagonists as well. [^tx-ed-38-223-report] [^tx-ed-38-227-immunity] By contrast, a school district must adopt an opioid antagonist policy for each campus serving grades 6 through 12, [^tx-ed-38-222-a-district] and the food-allergy anaphylaxis care policy in Section 38.0151 is imposed on school districts and open-enrollment charter schools. [^tx-ed-38-0151-public]

## Do Texas's student-athlete concussion removal and return-to-play rules apply to a private school's teams? {#concussion-rules-for-sports}

**Short answer.** Texas's concussion removal, return-to-play and annual acknowledgment rules apply only to interscholastic athletic activity sponsored or sanctioned by a school district, a public school or the University Interscholastic League, not to athletics sponsored only by a private school. [^tx-ed-38-152-applicability]

Where the subchapter applies, a student must be removed from practice or competition immediately if a coach, physician, licensed health care professional, school nurse, parent or certain other licensed persons believe the student might have sustained a concussion. [^tx-ed-38-156-removal] The student may not return until a treating physician has evaluated the student, the return-to-play protocol is complete, the physician has given a written statement that return is safe, and the student and parent have signed the required acknowledgments and consent, and a coach may not authorize the return. [^tx-ed-38-157-return] Before participating each school year, the student and a parent must sign a UIL-approved form acknowledging receipt of concussion information. [^tx-ed-38-155-form]

The oversight-team requirement is addressed to the governing body of each school district and open-enrollment charter school, [^tx-ed-38-153-oversight-team] and the broader concussion response policy that S.B. 2398 added in 2025, which covers a concussion believed sustained on school property or during a school-sponsored or school-related activity, is imposed on each school district. [^tx-ed-38-173-district-policy] This answer does not address the concussion rules of private athletic associations.

## What immunization and vision, hearing and spinal screening requirements must a Texas private school enforce? {#immunizations-and-health-screenings}

**Short answer.** Texas's school vaccine requirements and its vision, hearing and spinal screening requirements expressly apply to students attending private primary and secondary schools, subject to the statutory exemptions. [^tx-25-tac-97-61-private] [^tx-hs-36-004-private] [^tx-hs-37-001-private]
Immunization is not required for admission if the student submits a physician's affidavit or certificate that the immunization poses a significant risk to the health and well-being of the student or a member of the student's family or household, or an affidavit, signed by the student or a minor's parent or guardian, declining immunization for reasons of conscience, including a religious belief, or is on active military duty, and a student is exempt from vision, hearing or spinal screening that conflicts with the tenets and practices of a recognized church or religious denomination of which the student is an adherent or member. [^tx-ed-38-001-c-exemptions] [^tx-hs-36-005-compliance] [^tx-hs-37-002-compliance]

Education Code section 38.001 requires each student to be fully immunized against diphtheria, rubeola, rubella, mumps, tetanus and poliomyelitis, subject to the admission exemptions in Subsection (c). [^tx-ed-38-001-a-b-requirements] That list is not the whole requirement: the Department of State Health Services may modify the list or require immunizations against additional diseases for admission to any elementary or secondary school, so the department's current requirements govern admission. [^tx-ed-38-001-b-additional] [^tx-25-tac-97-61-private] A conscience affidavit must be on the Department of State Health Services form and submitted no later than the 90th day after it is notarized. [^tx-ed-38-001-c-exemptions] Since H.B. 1586 took effect on September 1, 2025, the department must post the blank affidavit form in printable format on its website, and a person may not be required to provide any information to obtain a blank form. [^tx-hs-161-0041-form] A student who has not been immunized for reasons of conscience may be excluded from school during an emergency or epidemic declared by the commissioner of public health. [^tx-ed-38-001-f-epidemic]

For screening, the chief administrator of each school must ensure that each admitted student complies with the vision and hearing screening requirements or submits an affidavit of exemption, and a parent may substitute a professional examination for the screening. [^tx-hs-36-005-compliance] The chief administrator must keep screening records on the department's form, open to inspection by the department or the local health department. [^tx-hs-36-006-records] The same chief-administrator duty and professional-examination substitute apply to spinal screening. [^tx-hs-37-002-compliance]

## Do Texas's criminal hazing laws, including the duty to report hazing, apply to a private high school? {#hazing}

**Short answer.** Texas's criminal hazing law applies to a private high school, and a person with firsthand knowledge of a planned or completed hazing incident commits a Class B misdemeanor by knowingly failing to report it to the dean of students or other appropriate school official or to law enforcement. [^tx-ed-37-151-definitions] [^tx-ed-37-152-offenses]

The hazing subchapter defines an educational institution to include "a public or private high school"[^tx-ed-37-151-definitions], and it reaches hazing directed against a student for the purpose of joining, affiliating with, holding office in or keeping membership in an organization, a term that covers clubs, bands, academic, athletic, cheerleading and dance teams and service or social groups whose members are primarily students. [^tx-ed-37-151-definitions] Hazing means an intentional, knowing or reckless act, on or off campus, that involves physical brutality, sleep deprivation, exposure, confinement or calisthenics creating an unreasonable risk of harm, harmful consumption of food, liquid, alcohol or drugs, a task that violates the Penal Code, or coerced consumption of a drug or intoxicating amounts of alcohol. [^tx-ed-37-151-hazing-definition]

A person also commits an offense by engaging in hazing, by soliciting, encouraging, directing or aiding it, or by recklessly permitting it; an offense that causes serious bodily injury is a Class A misdemeanor, and one that causes death is a state jail felony. [^tx-ed-37-152-offenses] [^tx-ed-37-152-penalties] An organization that condones or encourages hazing, or whose officers or members commit or assist in it, faces a fine of $5,000 to $10,000 or, where the offense caused personal injury, property damage or other loss, a fine of $5,000 up to double the loss. [^tx-ed-37-153-organization] The victim's consent is not a defense. [^tx-ed-37-154-consent] A person who voluntarily reports a specific hazing incident to the school, a peace officer or a law enforcement agency before being contacted about it or otherwise included in the investigation, and who cooperates in good faith with the school's process or the police investigation, is immune from civil or criminal liability that might otherwise result from the reported incident. [^tx-ed-37-155-reporter-immunity] The immunity does not cover a person who reports the person's own act of hazing or who reports in bad faith or with malice. [^tx-ed-37-155-reporter-immunity]
