# School Policy Compliance in New Jersey: Employee Leave, Benefits and Tuition Remission[^about]

The leave and benefit rules a New Jersey independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: earned sick leave, pregnancy and lactation accommodation, temporary disability and family leave insurance for school staff, the Family Leave Act and FMLA, SAFE Act leave, jury service, tuition remission and deductions from final pay.

## What earned sick leave must a New Jersey private school provide, and which employees does it cover? {#earned-sick-leave}

**Short answer.** New Jersey's Earned Sick Leave Law requires every employer, expressly including an educational institution and a nonprofit agency, to provide earned sick leave that accrues at one hour per thirty hours worked in the State or is front-loaded at the start of each benefit year. [^esl-employer-defined] [^esl-hourly-accrual-rate]

The employer definition sets no minimum number of employees, and the employee definition excludes only construction-industry employees under contract pursuant to a collective bargaining agreement, per diem health care employees and public employees who already receive full-pay sick leave, so teachers and staff at a private school are covered. [^esl-employer-defined] [^esl-employee-defined] An employer need not let an employee accrue, use or carry forward more than forty hours in a benefit year. [^esl-40-hour-cap] A paid time off policy satisfies the statute if the leave is fully paid, usable for the statutory purposes in the manner the Act provides and accrued at least as fast as the statutory rate. [^esl-pto-policy]

The permitted uses include the employee's or a family member's illness, injury, health condition or preventive care and absences arising from domestic or sexual violence. [^esl-purposes-health] They also include a closure of the workplace or of a child's school by order of a public official because of an epidemic or other public health emergency, and attending a school-related conference, meeting or event that the child's school requests or requires. [^esl-purposes-closure-and-school] An employer may require reasonable documentation for earned sick leave of three or more consecutive days, and for unforeseeable sick leave used on dates on which it has prohibited the use of foreseeable leave. [^esl-three-day-documentation] Information about an employee's or family member's health, or about domestic or sexual violence affecting them, must be kept confidential and disclosed only to the affected employee or with that employee's written permission. [^esl-health-info-confidentiality] An employee eligible for both earned sick leave and temporary disability or family leave insurance benefits may choose which to use and in what order, but may not receive more than one kind of paid leave at the same time. [^pl-2025-279-esl-order]

## What pregnancy and lactation accommodations must a New Jersey school provide to its employees? {#pregnancy-and-lactation-accommodation}

**Short answer.** New Jersey's Law Against Discrimination requires an employer to reasonably accommodate an employee's pregnancy-related needs requested on a physician's advice, and to give a breastfeeding employee daily break time and a private non-toilet space near the work area, unless the employer shows undue hardship. [^lad-12-s-accommodation]

The Law Against Discrimination defines an employer to include all persons unless another section specifically exempts them, so the accommodation duty is not limited by employee count. [^lad-5-employer-defined] The statute's examples of pregnancy accommodation include bathroom breaks, breaks for water, periodic rest, help with manual labor, job restructuring, modified schedules and temporary transfers to less strenuous or hazardous work, and the employer may not penalize an employee for requesting or using an accommodation. [^lad-12-s-accommodation] Accommodation and leave for pregnancy or breastfeeding may not be provided on terms less favorable than those given to other employees similar in their ability or inability to work, and the protected condition includes childbirth, expressing milk and related medical conditions, including recovery from childbirth. [^lad-12-s-equal-treatment]

Federal law adds two overlapping duties. The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act requires an employer with fifteen or more employees to reasonably accommodate the known limitations related to pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions of a qualified employee unless the accommodation would impose an undue hardship. [^usc-2000gg-1-pwfa-accommodation] [^usc-2000gg-pwfa-covered-entity] That Act is subject to Title VII's religious-employer provision in 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-1(a). [^usc-2000gg-5-religious] The federal PUMP Act provision requires reasonable break time each time the employee needs to express breast milk, for one year after the child's birth, and a private place other than a bathroom. [^usc-218d-pump-break-lactation] An employer with fewer than fifty employees is exempt from the federal provision if compliance would impose an undue hardship by causing significant difficulty or expense in relation to the size, financial resources, nature or structure of its business; the New Jersey duty applies its own undue-hardship test. [^usc-218d-small-employer-hardship] [^lad-12-s-accommodation] How the Law Against Discrimination's religious-association provisions bear on a religious school's employment decisions is addressed in the [New Jersey guide on discrimination law and religious employers](/practice-guides/school-policy-compliance/us/new-jersey-religious-employers).

## Do New Jersey temporary disability and family leave insurance cover staff at a nonprofit or church-run school? {#disability-and-family-leave-insurance}

**Short answer.** New Jersey temporary disability and family leave insurance cover only work that counts as employment under the Unemployment Compensation Law, which reaches staff of a secular nonprofit school but excludes service for a church or for a primarily religious school that a church operates, supervises, controls or principally supports. [^tdbl-covered-individual] [^ucl-nonprofit-and-church-exclusion]

The Temporary Disability Benefits Law makes an employer subject to the Unemployment Compensation Law a covered employer for family leave insurance, and defines a covered individual as a person in employment as the Unemployment Compensation Law defines it. [^tdbl-covered-employer-fli] [^tdbl-covered-individual] The Department of Labor and Workforce Development states that a New Jersey employer subject to the Unemployment Compensation Law is also subject to the Temporary Disability Benefits Law, apart from certain government entities. [^njdol-subject-employer-tdi] Service for a religious, charitable or educational organization that is excluded from federal unemployment tax only by section 3306(c)(8) of the Federal Unemployment Tax Act is employment under the New Jersey statute unless a listed exclusion applies, and such an organization is an employer once it pays $1,000 or more in remuneration in the current or preceding calendar year. [^ucl-nonprofit-and-church-exclusion] [^ucl-nonprofit-employer]

The same provision states that this category of employment does not apply to service in the employ of a church or convention or association of churches, or of an organization or school operated primarily for religious purposes and operated, supervised, controlled or principally supported by a church or convention or association of churches. [^ucl-nonprofit-and-church-exclusion] The exclusion is framed in two parts, a primarily religious purpose and church operation, supervision, control or principal support, so a school with a religious affiliation that is neither operated, supervised or controlled by a church nor principally supported by one does not fall within its words. [^ucl-nonprofit-and-church-exclusion] The provision also excludes service by an ordained, commissioned or licensed minister of a church in the exercise of the ministry, and by a member of a religious order performing duties the order requires. [^ucl-nonprofit-and-church-exclusion] The definition of covered individual does not separately exclude teachers. [^tdbl-covered-individual]

These exclusions describe automatic coverage. An employing unit whose services are not employment may file a written election to have those services in one or more establishments deemed employment for all purposes of the Unemployment Compensation Law for at least two calendar years, [^ucl-43-21-8-election] and employment under that law is the definition temporary disability and family leave insurance coverage follows. [^tdbl-covered-individual]

Where coverage applies, P.L.2025, c.279 entitles a covered individual who took temporary disability or family leave insurance benefits to be restored to the same or an equivalent position when the leave ends, and bars retaliation for requesting or taking those benefits. [^pl-2025-279-restoration] [^pl-2025-279-no-retaliation] The provision preserves the Family Leave Act's own restoration rights. [^pl-2025-279-restoration] The Department of Labor and Workforce Development states that from July 17, 2026 an employee on unpaid leave who receives temporary disability or family leave insurance benefits, and who does not qualify for job protection under the Family Leave Act or the FMLA, is entitled to job protection, with no minimum employer size or work history requirement. [^njdol-faq-tdi-fli-job-protection]

## Which job-protected family leave laws apply to a New Jersey private school? {#family-leave-act-and-fmla}

**Short answer.** Since July 17, 2026, the New Jersey Family Leave Act has covered an employer with fifteen or more employees for each working day in twenty or more calendar workweeks of the current or preceding year, giving an eligible employee twelve weeks of family leave in any twenty-four-month period. [^fla-employer-defined] [^pl-2025-279-effective-date] [^fla-twelve-weeks]

An employee becomes eligible after three months of employment and 250 base hours in the preceding twelve months. [^fla-employee-defined] The Act's family leave is leave to care for a child after birth, foster placement or adoption, to care for a family member with a serious health condition, or for listed public health emergencies such as an ordered closure of a child's school; it does not cover the employee's own health condition. [^fla-family-leave-defined] The employer definition contains no exception for nonprofit or religious employers. [^fla-employer-defined] Family leave may be paid, unpaid or a combination, [^fla-paid-or-unpaid] and an employer may deny leave to a salaried employee among the highest-paid five percent of its employees or its seven highest-paid employees, whichever is greater, only where denial is necessary to prevent substantial and grievous economic injury and the employer gives notice of its intent when it determines denial is necessary. The denial right does not apply to leave for an epidemic of a communicable disease, a known or suspected exposure or efforts to prevent its spread, during a declared state of emergency or when a public health authority indicates it is needed. [^fla-highest-paid-denial]

The federal Family and Medical Leave Act covers a private elementary or secondary school regardless of its size, but an employee is eligible only if the school employs at least fifty employees within seventy-five miles of the employee's worksite. [^cfr-825-600-school-coverage] [^usc-2611-fmla-75-mile] For other private employers the federal Act applies only at fifty or more employees in twenty or more workweeks, a threshold the school regulation displaces. [^usc-2611-fmla-employer] [^cfr-825-600-school-coverage] A federally eligible employee must also have worked for the employer for at least twelve months and 1,250 hours in the preceding twelve months, [^cfr-825-110-eligibility] and the federal regulations set special rules for instructional employees, such as teachers and coaches, on intermittent leave, reduced schedules and leave near the end of an academic term. [^cfr-825-600-instructional-employees] Wage replacement during state family leave comes from family leave insurance where the school's staff are covered, as explained in the [question on disability and family leave insurance](#disability-and-family-leave-insurance).

## What leave must a New Jersey school give an employee who is a victim of domestic or sexual violence? {#safe-act-leave}

**Short answer.** New Jersey's SAFE Act requires an employer with twenty-five or more employees to give an eligible employee up to twenty days of unpaid leave in one twelve-month period, used within the twelve months after domestic violence or a sexually violent offense against the employee or a family member. [^safe-employer-defined] [^safe-twenty-days]

Under the Security and Financial Empowerment Act, employees are counted for each working day during each of twenty or more calendar workweeks in the current or preceding calendar year, and an employee is eligible after twelve months of employment and 1,000 base hours in the preceding twelve months. [^safe-employer-defined] Each incident is a separate offense for which leave is available until the twenty days for the twelve-month period are used, and the leave may be taken intermittently, in intervals of no less than one day, for the activities related to the incident that the statute lists. [^safe-twenty-days] The employee may use accrued paid leave or family leave insurance benefits during the twenty days, and leave that also qualifies under the New Jersey Family Leave Act or the federal Family and Medical Leave Act counts against each entitlement at the same time. [^safe-paid-leave-concurrent] The employer may require documentation from a listed category, such as a restraining order, prosecutor's letter or medical record, and must keep the information and any information about the leave in the strictest confidentiality unless the employee authorizes disclosure in writing or a federal or State law, rule or regulation requires it. [^safe-act-leave-documentation] [^safe-act-leave-confidentiality] The employer must also display a conspicuous notice of employees' rights under the Act. [^safe-act-rights-notice]

Separately, earned sick leave, which every employer must provide without an employee-count threshold, may be used for absences resulting from domestic or sexual violence against the employee or a family member, as described in the [question on earned sick leave](#earned-sick-leave). [^esl-purposes-violence]

## Can a New Jersey school penalize an employee for missing work to serve on a jury? {#jury-service-leave}

**Short answer.** A New Jersey employer may not penalize an employee with respect to employment, or threaten or coerce the employee, because the employee is required to attend court for jury service. [^njsa-2b-20-17-protection]

A violation is a disorderly persons offense, and the employee may sue within ninety days after the violation or the end of jury service, whichever is later, for economic damages and reinstatement, with a reasonable attorney's fee if the employee prevails. [^njsa-2b-20-17-remedies] The section addresses penalties and coercion and contains no requirement that the employer pay wages for days spent on jury service. [^njsa-2b-20-17-protection] [^njsa-2b-20-17-remedies]

## Is tuition remission for employees' children at a New Jersey K–12 school excluded from federal income tax? {#employee-tuition-remission}

**Short answer.** Internal Revenue Code § 117(d) excludes from gross income an educational organization's tuition reduction for below-graduate education of an employee or the employee's dependent child, but a highly compensated employee's reduction qualifies only if offered on nondiscriminatory terms. [^irc-117-d-qualified-reduction] [^irc-132-h-dependent-child] [^irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination]

The exclusion is limited to tuition reductions given by an organization described in § 170(b)(1)(A)(ii), which is an educational organization that normally maintains a regular faculty and curriculum and an enrolled student body at the place where it teaches. [^irc-117-d-qualified-reduction] [^irc-170-b-educational-organization] A K–12 independent school that meets that description falls within the definition, and the education may be at the employer's own school or at another such organization. [^irc-117-d-qualified-reduction]

A reduction that does not qualify, such as one offered only to highly compensated employees, is not excluded by § 117(d) and falls within gross income as compensation for services, including fringe benefits. [^irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination] [^irc-61-compensation-fringe] The highly compensated employee test uses the definition in Code § 414(q), [^irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination] which covers a 5-percent owner in the current or preceding year and an employee whose preceding-year compensation exceeded an inflation-adjusted threshold, limited, if the employer so elects, to the top-paid group of employees. [^irc-414-q-hce-definition] IRS Notice 2025-67 states that the threshold remains $160,000. [^irs-notice-2025-67-hce] IRS Publication 970 describes a qualified tuition reduction as one for education below the graduate level, including primary and secondary school. [^irs-pub-970-primary-secondary] Neither the statute nor the publication addresses a standalone pre-kindergarten or child care program. [^irs-pub-970-primary-secondary] The Treasury regulation describes a § 170(b)(1)(A)(ii) educational organization as one whose primary function is the presentation of formal instruction, with a regular faculty, curriculum and enrolled student body, so a standalone pre-kindergarten or child care provider falls within it only if formal instruction, rather than custodial care, is its primary function. [^cfr-1-170a-9-c-1-formal-instruction] This answer addresses federal income tax only; the treatment of tuition remission under the New Jersey gross income tax is outside its scope.

## Can a New Jersey school deduct a signing bonus, tuition or training repayment from a teacher's final paycheck? {#repayment-from-final-pay}

**Short answer.** A New Jersey school may deduct a repayment from a teacher's wages only if the deduction fits a category the Wage Payment Law lists, such as an employer loan repaid on its original schedule, because the statute otherwise bars withholding or diverting any portion of wages. [^wpl-4-4-deductions]

The listed categories cover deductions that law requires or empowers, benefit and savings contributions, and, among others, payments for employer loans on the periodic schedule in the original loan agreement and payments to correct payroll errors, provided the employer approves them. [^wpl-4-4-deductions] [^wpl-4-4-loans-and-errors] The list does not name repayment of a signing bonus, tuition or training costs, and further deductions are permitted only as the Commissioner of Labor and Workforce Development authorizes by regulation. [^wpl-4-4-loans-and-errors] [^wpl-4-4-commissioner-regulation]

Final wages are due no later than the regular payday for the pay period in which the employment ended, whether the teacher resigned or was discharged. [^nj-wpl-final-pay-deadline] An employee may recover unpaid wages in a civil action plus liquidated damages of up to 200 percent, with costs and reasonable attorney's fees, but liquidated damages are not required for a first violation if the employer shows an inadvertent good-faith error made on reasonable grounds, acknowledges the violation and pays the amount owed within thirty days of notice. [^wpl-4-10-liquidated-damages] The Wage Payment Law governs deductions from wages; whether a separate repayment promise is enforceable as a contract claim against the former employee is a different question that this guide does not cover.
