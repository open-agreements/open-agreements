# School Policy Compliance in Virginia: Employee Leave, Benefits and Tuition Remission[^about]

The leave and benefit rules a Virginia independent or religiously affiliated K–12 school's policies must reflect: the paid sick leave law taking effect in 2027, pregnancy and lactation accommodation, the paid family and medical leave insurance program, the FMLA, organ donation leave, crime victim leave, jury duty, military and emergency responder leave, tuition remission and deductions from final pay.

## What paid sick leave must a Virginia private school provide, and when does the new paid sick leave law reach it? {#paid-sick-leave}

**Short answer.** From July 1, 2027, Virginia's new paid sick leave article requires an employer, as Title 40.1 defines it, to let each employee accrue at least one hour of paid sick leave for every thirty hours worked. [^va-lb-psl-2027-employer] [^va-lb-psl-2027-accrual] Coverage starts with employers of at least fifty employees and reaches employers of at least one employee on January 1, 2029. [^va-lb-psl-2027-employer] [^va-lb-psl-2027-accrual]

Chapters 1128 and 1129 of the 2026 Acts, which created the new article, each provide that their first enactment becomes effective on July 1, 2027. [^va-lb-psl-ch1128] [^va-lb-psl-ch1129] Until then, Virginia's existing paid sick leave article does not reach school staff, because it defines a covered employee as a home health worker providing personal care, respite or companion services to a person receiving consumer-directed services under the state medical assistance plan. [^va-lb-psl-current-scope] The new article borrows the Title 40.1 definition of employer, which covers any association or corporation doing business or operating in Virginia that employs another for wages, so it reaches an independent or religious school once the school meets the employee count for the phase then in force: at least fifty employees from July 1, 2027, at least twenty-five from January 1, 2028 and at least one from January 1, 2029. [^va-lb-title-40-1-employer] [^va-lb-psl-2027-employer] Its scope section carves out home health workers; licensed, registered or certified health professionals who work for a facility licensed by the Department of Health or for the University of Virginia Medical Center or VCU Health System Authority and average no more than thirty hours a week; employees working on an as-needed basis who are licensed, registered or certified by a health regulatory board, or who are employed by one of those health facilities or by an agency licensed by the Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Services; and railroad employers and employees under 45 U.S.C. § 351. The as-needed exclusion can reach a school nurse or counselor licensed by a health regulatory board who works only as needed; it contains no exception for nonprofit, religious or educational employers. [^va-lb-psl-2027-scope]

Accrued leave carries over to the next year, but an employer need not let an employee accrue or use more than forty hours in a year unless it selects a higher limit. [^va-lb-psl-2027-accrual] Employees exempt from overtime under the federal executive, administrative and professional exemption in 29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(1) are treated as working forty hours a week for accrual unless their normal workweek is shorter, and an employer that front-loads forty hours at the start of the year satisfies the accrual rule. [^va-lb-psl-2027-exempt-and-frontload] A paid time off policy satisfies the article if it provides enough leave and the leave may be used for the same purposes and under the same conditions, and the article does not require payment for unused leave at separation. [^va-lb-psl-2027-pto-and-payout]

The leave must be available for the employee's own or a family member's illness, injury, health condition, diagnosis, care, treatment or preventive care, and for absences related to domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking to seek medical care, counseling, legal services, relocation or other victim services. [^va-lb-psl-2027-uses] An employer that requires notice of the need for leave must give employees a written policy containing the notice procedure, and may not deny leave for noncompliance with a policy it never provided. [^va-lb-psl-2027-written-policy] The employer may set a minimum increment for using leave, but that minimum may be no greater than four hours, it may not require disclosure of the details of health information or of domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking, may not require the employee to find a replacement or work an alternate shift, and may require reasonable documentation only for leave of three or more consecutive work days, with an employee's own written statement counting as reasonable documentation for leave related to domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking. [^va-lb-psl-2027-increments-and-documentation] An absence control policy may not count paid sick leave as an absence that may lead to discipline or other adverse action. [^va-lb-psl-2027-absence-control] An employee may sue without first filing an administrative complaint, and a prevailing employee recovers twice the uncompensated sick leave and twice actual damages, with injunctive relief, other appropriate legal or equitable relief such as reinstatement, and lost wages and benefits with interest, attorney fees and costs. [^va-lb-psl-2027-civil-action]

## What pregnancy and lactation accommodations must a Virginia school provide, and does a religious school's exemption apply? {#pregnancy-and-lactation-accommodation}

**Short answer.** A Virginia employer with five or more employees in each of twenty or more calendar weeks must reasonably accommodate an employee's or applicant's known limitations related to pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions, including lactation, unless it shows undue hardship. [^va-lb-vhra-3909-employer-and-duty] [^va-lb-vhra-3905-religious] The count is of employees for each working day in those weeks of the current or preceding calendar year, and the Act's religious-employer exemption does not reach this duty. [^va-lb-vhra-3909-employer-and-duty] [^va-lb-vhra-3905-religious]

Section 2.2-3909 of the Virginia Human Rights Act lists as reasonable accommodations longer or more frequent bathroom breaks, breaks to express breast milk, a private non-bathroom location for expressing milk, seating or equipment changes, temporary transfer to less strenuous or hazardous work, help with manual labor, job restructuring, a modified schedule, light duty and leave to recover from childbirth. [^va-lb-vhra-3909-employer-and-duty] Undue hardship turns on the nature of the employer's operation and workforce, the size of the facility and the nature and cost of the accommodation, and providing a similar accommodation to other classes of employees creates a rebuttable presumption that the accommodation is not an undue hardship. [^va-lb-vhra-3909-hardship] The employer may not take adverse action against an employee who requests or uses an accommodation, including failing to reinstate her to her previous or an equivalent position when the need ends, and may not require leave if another reasonable accommodation can be provided; it must also engage in a timely, good-faith interactive process. [^va-lb-vhra-3909-adverse-action-and-process] The employer must post information about these rights, include it in any employee handbook and give it directly to new employees and to an employee within ten days after she gives notice of pregnancy. [^va-lb-vhra-3909-handbook-notice]

Section 2.2-3909 defines employer by employee count alone and contains no religious-employer exception. [^va-lb-vhra-3909-employer-and-duty] The Act's religious-employer provision, § 2.2-3905(E), provides that "The provisions of this section shall not apply"[^va-lb-vhra-3905-religious] to a religious educational institution's employment of individuals of a particular religion to perform work associated with its activities, so by its words it limits § 2.2-3905's discrimination rules where a religious school prefers members of its religion, and does not reach the separate accommodation duty in § 2.2-3909. [^va-lb-vhra-3905-religious] Section 2.2-3905 separately bars discrimination because of pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions including lactation, and defines lactation to include expressing milk. [^va-lb-vhra-3905-pregnancy-lactation] How the Act's religious-employer provisions bear on a religious school's other employment decisions is addressed in the [Virginia guide on discrimination law and religious employers](/practice-guides/school-policy-compliance/us/virginia-religious-employers).

Federal law adds two overlapping duties. The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act requires an employer with fifteen or more employees to reasonably accommodate a qualified employee's known limitations related to pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions unless the accommodation would impose an undue hardship. [^va-lb-pwfa-accommodation] [^va-lb-pwfa-covered-entity] That Act is subject to Title VII's religious-employer provision in 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-1(a). [^va-lb-pwfa-religious] The federal PUMP Act provision requires reasonable break time each time a nursing employee needs to express breast milk, for one year after the child's birth, and a private place other than a bathroom. [^va-lb-pump-break-lactation] An employer with fewer than fifty employees is exempt from the federal provision only if compliance would impose an undue hardship by causing significant difficulty or expense in relation to the size, financial resources, nature or structure of its business; the Virginia duty applies its own undue-hardship test. [^va-lb-pump-small-employer] [^va-lb-vhra-3909-hardship]

## Will Virginia's paid family and medical leave program cover staff at a nonprofit or church-run school? {#paid-family-and-medical-leave-insurance}

**Short answer.** Virginia's paid family and medical leave insurance program follows the unemployment compensation law's coverage rules, which exclude service for a church or for a school operated primarily for religious purposes and operated, supervised, controlled or principally supported by a church. [^va-lb-pfml-employer-definition] [^va-lb-pfml-employer-and-covered-individual] [^va-lb-ui-nonprofit-employment] [^va-lb-ui-church-exclusion] Its coverage otherwise reaches employment at a nonprofit school with four or more employees in each of twenty weeks of the current or preceding year, and each worker must still meet the individual eligibility rules. [^va-lb-pfml-employer-definition] [^va-lb-pfml-employer-and-covered-individual] [^va-lb-ui-nonprofit-employment] [^va-lb-ui-church-exclusion]

The Virginia Employment Commission must begin collecting contributions by April 1, 2028 and paying benefits by December 1, 2028, [^va-lb-pfml-dates] for up to twelve weeks in a benefit year. [^va-lb-pfml-twelve-weeks] The program's definition of employer is the unemployment compensation law's definition, [^va-lb-pfml-employer-definition] and a covered individual other than an electing self-employed person must meet that law's monetary eligibility test. [^va-lb-pfml-employer-and-covered-individual] That test counts wages paid "in employment for employers"[^va-lb-ui-monetary-eligibility] in the claimant's base period. [^va-lb-ui-monetary-eligibility] Service for a religious, charitable or educational organization that is excluded from federal unemployment tax solely by section 3306(c)(8) of the Federal Unemployment Tax Act is employment under the Virginia statute only if the organization had four or more individuals in employment for some portion of a day in each of twenty different weeks in the current or preceding year. [^va-lb-ui-nonprofit-employment] For that category the statute excludes service in the employ of a church or convention or association of churches, or of an organization operated primarily for religious purposes and operated, supervised, controlled or principally supported by a church or convention or association of churches, and service by ministers and members of religious orders performing their religious duties. [^va-lb-ui-church-exclusion] The church exclusion has two parts, a primarily religious purpose and church operation, supervision, control or principal support, so a school with a religious affiliation that meets only one of them does not fall within its words. [^va-lb-ui-church-exclusion] A school whose services are not employment may elect in writing, with the Commission's approval, to have them treated as employment for at least two calendar years. [^va-lb-ui-election]

Benefits are payable to bond with a new child in the first year after birth, adoption or foster placement, to care for a family member with a serious health condition, for the employee's own serious health condition, for military caregiving and qualifying exigencies, and for up to four weeks of safety services related to domestic violence, harassment, sexual assault or stalking. [^va-lb-pfml-purposes] An employer of more than ten employees must deduct half the per-employee contribution from wages, or a lesser percentage the employer and employee agree on, and remits the full contribution, while an employer of ten or fewer deducts half of the contribution that applies to larger employers and owes no additional share. [^va-lb-pfml-contributions]

A covered individual who receives benefits after at least 120 days with the current employer is entitled to restoration to the same or an equivalent position, and the employer must maintain health benefits during the leave; a violating employer owes lost compensation, interest and liquidated damages equal to that compensation plus interest, which a court removes if the employer proves good faith and reasonable grounds. [^va-lb-pfml-restoration] [^va-lb-pfml-liquidated-damages] Leave that also qualifies under the federal FMLA runs concurrently with it, [^va-lb-pfml-coordination] and an individual's agreement to waive the program's rights is void. [^va-lb-pfml-waiver-void] Each employer must give employees written notice of the program's rights at hiring, annually and when leave is requested or foreseen, and must display the Commission's poster. [^va-lb-pfml-notice]

## Does the federal Family and Medical Leave Act cover a small Virginia private school, and when are its teachers eligible? {#fmla-and-job-protected-leave}

**Short answer.** The federal Family and Medical Leave Act covers a private elementary or secondary school regardless of its size, but an employee is eligible only if the school employs at least fifty employees within seventy-five miles of the employee's worksite. [^va-lb-fmla-school-coverage] [^va-lb-fmla-eligibility] [^va-lb-fmla-75-mile] The employee must also have worked for the school for twelve months and 1,250 hours in the preceding twelve months. [^va-lb-fmla-school-coverage] [^va-lb-fmla-eligibility] [^va-lb-fmla-75-mile]

For other private employers the federal Act applies only at fifty or more employees in twenty or more workweeks, a threshold the school regulation displaces. [^va-lb-fmla-employer] [^va-lb-fmla-school-coverage] An eligible employee must also have worked for the employer for at least twelve months and 1,250 hours in the twelve months before the leave. [^va-lb-fmla-eligibility] The federal regulations set special rules for instructional employees, such as teachers and athletic coaches, on intermittent leave, reduced schedules and leave near the end of an academic term. [^va-lb-fmla-instructional-employees]

At a school too small for its employees to be FMLA-eligible, the paid family and medical leave insurance program supplies a state job-restoration right from December 1, 2028: a covered individual who receives benefits after at least 120 days with the current employer is entitled to restoration to the same or an equivalent position, with no minimum employer size in that section. [^va-lb-pfml-purposes-fmla] [^va-lb-pfml-restoration-fmla] Leave that qualifies under both the program and the FMLA runs concurrently. [^va-lb-pfml-coordination-fmla] Who is covered by that program is addressed in the [question on paid family and medical leave insurance](#paid-family-and-medical-leave-insurance), and a Virginia employer with five or more employees in each of twenty or more calendar weeks must also allow leave to recover from childbirth as a reasonable accommodation unless it shows undue hardship. [^va-lb-vhra-3909-employer-and-duty-fmla]

## Must a Virginia private school give unpaid leave to an employee who is donating an organ or bone marrow? {#organ-donation-leave}

**Short answer.** A Virginia employer with fifty or more employees must give an eligible employee up to sixty business days of unpaid leave in any twelve-month period to serve as an organ donor and up to thirty business days to serve as a bone marrow donor. [^va-lb-organ-definitions] [^va-lb-organ-leave]

The organ donation leave article uses the Title 40.1 definition of employer limited to employers of fifty or more employees, which reaches a private school of that size, and an employee is eligible after at least twelve months and 1,250 hours of service in the previous twelve months. [^va-lb-organ-definitions] [^va-lb-title-40-1-employer-organ] The employee must give written physician verification that the employee is a donor and that the donation is medically necessary, and organ donation leave may not be taken concurrently with FMLA leave. [^va-lb-organ-leave] The employee may use paid sick leave or other paid time off in addition to or in place of organ donation leave. [^va-lb-organ-leave]

The leave is not a break in continuous service for salary adjustments, leave accrual, seniority or other benefits, and the returning employee is entitled to the same or an equivalent position, although the employer may deny restoration for conditions unrelated to the exercise of rights under the article. [^va-lb-organ-restoration] The employer must maintain health benefit plan coverage during the leave as if the employee had continued working. [^va-lb-organ-health-plan]

## What leave must a Virginia school give an employee who is a crime victim or is affected by domestic violence? {#crime-victim-and-domestic-violence-leave}

**Short answer.** Every Virginia employer must let an employee who is a crime victim leave work to attend the criminal proceedings relating to the crime once the employee provides the law-enforcement victim form and any notice of the scheduled proceeding. [^va-lb-crime-victim-leave] The employer may limit the leave where it creates an undue hardship. [^va-lb-crime-victim-leave]

The section's operative words, "Every employer shall allow"[^va-lb-crime-victim-leave], carry no employee-count threshold, and Title 40.1 defines employer to include any association or corporation doing business or operating in Virginia that employs another for wages, which reaches a private school of any size. [^va-lb-crime-victim-leave] [^va-lb-title-40-1-employer-cv] Undue hardship means significant difficulty and expense, considering the size of the employer's business and its critical need of the employee. [^va-lb-crime-victim-definitions] The employer need not pay for the time, but may not dismiss the employee for taking the leave or discriminate in compensation or other terms of employment because the employee left work to attend a proceeding. [^va-lb-crime-victim-protection]

The paid sick leave article that takes effect July 1, 2027 adds paid leave for absences due to domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking to seek medical or mental health care, counseling, legal services, relocation or other victim services, for the employee or a family member, at employers then within its phased coverage. [^va-lb-psl-2027-uses-cv] [^va-lb-psl-2027-employer-cv] From December 1, 2028, the paid family and medical leave insurance program pays up to four weeks a year of benefits for safety services, which include legal assistance, protective order proceedings, medical treatment, counseling and relocation related to domestic violence, pervasive harassment, sexual assault or stalking. [^va-lb-pfml-purposes-cv] [^va-lb-pfml-safety-services]

## Can a Virginia school penalize an employee, or require use of vacation, for jury duty or a court appearance? {#jury-duty-and-court-leave}

**Short answer.** A Virginia employer may not discharge an employee, take adverse personnel action or require use of sick leave or vacation because of an absence for jury duty or a summoned or subpoenaed court appearance, if the employee gives reasonable notice. [^va-lb-jury-court-protection] The protection excludes a defendant in a criminal case and also covers a later hearing the court requires in writing. [^va-lb-jury-court-protection]

The protection covers any person summoned for jury duty and any person, other than a defendant in a criminal case, summoned or subpoenaed to appear in court or required in writing by the court to appear at a later hearing. [^va-lb-jury-court-protection] An employee who appears for jury duty for four or more hours in a day, including travel time, may not be required to start a work shift that begins at or after 5:00 p.m. that day or before 3:00 a.m. the next day, and an employer that violates the section commits a Class 3 misdemeanor. [^va-lb-jury-court-protection] The section addresses discipline, forced use of leave and shift scheduling and contains no requirement that the employer pay wages for days spent on jury service. [^va-lb-jury-court-protection]

## What leave must a Virginia school give employees who serve in the National Guard, the reserves or as volunteer emergency responders? {#military-and-emergency-responder-leave}

**Short answer.** A Virginia private employer must give unpaid leave to a member of the Virginia National Guard, the Virginia Defense Force or another state's National Guard employed in Virginia who is called to state active duty or to duty under Title 32. [^va-lb-guard-leave] The employer may not force the member to use vacation or other accrued leave for that service, and federal service and volunteer emergency response carry their own protections. [^va-lb-guard-leave]

On honorable release, the member must apply in writing for reemployment within fourteen days if the absence did not exceed 180 days, or within ninety days if it did, and is then restored to the former position or, if that position no longer exists, to a position of like seniority, status and pay or a comparable vacant position unless that would be unreasonable; the right ends when cumulative service absences from that employer exceed five years. [^va-lb-guard-reemployment] An employer may not deny initial employment, reemployment, retention, promotion or any benefit of employment because of that membership or service, and a violation is established if membership or service is a motivating factor unless the employer proves it would have acted the same way anyway. [^va-lb-guard-discrimination]

Federal service is governed by the Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act, which entitles a person whose absence is necessitated by service in the uniformed services to reemployment if the person gave advance notice, the cumulative absences from that employer do not exceed five years and the person reports or applies for reemployment on time, subject to the exceptions in the statute's other subsections. [^va-lb-userra-reemployment] That Act defines an employer as any person, institution, organization or other entity that pays salary or wages for work performed, with no minimum number of employees. [^va-lb-userra-employer]

Under § 40.1-27.5, added by 2026 Va. Acts chapter 330, which sets no effective date of its own and so took effect July 1, 2026, an employer may not discipline or retaliate against an employee solely because the employee misses work because the employee is serving as a recognized volunteer firefighter or emergency medical services volunteer actively responding to an emergency alarm or during a declared state of emergency, if the employee gives notice at least one hour before the scheduled start and later provides the incident report and a certification from the officer in charge; the time may be unpaid, the employee may use accrued paid leave, and the protection does not apply to an employee deemed essential by statute or contract. [^va-lb-emergency-responder] [^va-lb-ch330-responder] [^va-lb-const-art-4-13] A volunteer member of the Civil Air Patrol is entitled to leave without loss of seniority, accrued leave or benefits for up to ten workdays a federal fiscal year of emergency-mission training and up to thirty workdays responding to emergency missions, which the employer may treat as unpaid. [^va-lb-civil-air-patrol]

## Is tuition remission for employees' children at a Virginia K–12 school excluded from federal and Virginia income tax? {#employee-tuition-remission}

**Short answer.** Internal Revenue Code § 117(d) excludes from gross income an educational organization's tuition reduction for below-graduate education of an employee or the employee's dependent child, but a highly compensated employee's reduction qualifies only if offered on nondiscriminatory terms. [^va-lb-irc-117-d-qualified-reduction] [^va-lb-irc-132-h-dependent-child] [^va-lb-irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination] Because Virginia taxable income starts from federal adjusted gross income, a reduction excluded federally is also outside Virginia's starting point. [^va-lb-va-taxable-income]

The exclusion is limited to tuition reductions given by an organization described in § 170(b)(1)(A)(ii), which is an educational organization that normally maintains a regular faculty and curriculum and an enrolled student body at the place where it teaches. [^va-lb-irc-117-d-qualified-reduction] [^va-lb-irc-170-b-educational-organization] A K–12 independent school that meets that description falls within the definition, and the education may be at the employer's own school or at another such organization. [^va-lb-irc-117-d-qualified-reduction] The Treasury regulation describes such an organization as one whose primary function is the presentation of formal instruction and says the term includes primary, secondary and preparatory schools, so a standalone pre-kindergarten or child care program falls within it only if formal instruction, rather than custodial care, is its primary function. [^va-lb-cfr-1-170a-9-formal-instruction] IRS Publication 970 describes a qualified tuition reduction as one for education below the graduate level, including primary and secondary school. [^va-lb-irs-pub-970-primary-secondary]

A reduction that does not qualify, such as one offered only to highly compensated employees, is not excluded by § 117(d) and falls within gross income as compensation for services, including fringe benefits. [^va-lb-irc-117-d-3-nondiscrimination] [^va-lb-irc-61-compensation] The highly compensated employee test uses the definition in Code § 414(q), which covers a 5-percent owner in the current or preceding year and an employee whose preceding-year compensation exceeded an inflation-adjusted threshold, limited, if the employer so elects, to the top-paid group of employees. [^va-lb-irc-414-q-hce] IRS Notice 2025-67 states that the threshold remains $160,000 for 2026, and Notice 2026-66, published in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2026-48, raises it to $170,000 for 2027. [^va-lb-irs-notice-2026-66] [^va-lb-irs-notice-2025-67]

Virginia taxable income of a resident individual starts from federal adjusted gross income, with the modifications listed in §§ 58.1-322.01 through 58.1-322.04, so a tuition reduction excluded under § 117(d) is also outside the Virginia starting point. [^va-lb-va-taxable-income] The additions in § 58.1-322.01, which bring excluded amounts back into Virginia taxable income, list interest on other states' obligations, certain federal obligation income, unrelated business taxable income, certain lump-sum retirement distributions, accumulation distributions, first-time home buyer account losses and certain double-benefited donations, and do not list tuition reductions. [^va-lb-va-additions]

## Can a Virginia school deduct a signing bonus, tuition or training repayment from a teacher's final paycheck? {#repayment-from-final-pay}

**Short answer.** A Virginia employer may not withhold any part of an employee's wages, other than payroll or withholding taxes or as the law otherwise provides, without the employee's written and signed authorization, so a repayment deduction from a teacher's final pay needs that authorization. [^va-lb-wage-withholding]

The wage payment section borrows the federal Fair Labor Standards Act's definition of employer, [^va-lb-wage-employer] which includes any person acting in the interest of an employer, defines a person to include an association, corporation or organized group of persons, and treats the operation of a preschool, elementary or secondary school as covered enterprise activity whether the school is public or private and whether or not it operates for profit. [^va-lb-flsa-employer] [^va-lb-flsa-person] [^va-lb-flsa-school-enterprise] The section also bars requiring an employee other than executive personnel to sign an agreement forfeiting wages for time worked as a condition of employment, except as otherwise provided by law. [^va-lb-wage-forfeiture]

Final wages for work performed before termination are due on or before the date they would have been paid had employment not ended. [^va-lb-final-pay] An employer that fails to pay wages owes the wages plus an equal amount as liquidated damages and interest at eight percent a year, and a court that finds the employer knowingly failed to pay awards triple the wages due with attorney fees and costs; "knowingly"[^va-lb-wage-knowingly] includes deliberate ignorance and reckless disregard and requires no proof of intent to defraud. [^va-lb-wage-liquidated-damages] [^va-lb-wage-treble] [^va-lb-wage-knowingly] In an action to recover unpaid wages commenced on or after July 1, 2026, no additional damages or penalties are awarded if the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds, but that defense is available only if the employer pays all wages unlawfully withheld within fourteen days of notice of the violation. [^va-lb-wage-good-faith] Subsection P was added by 2026 Va. Acts chapter 1040, and its July 1, 2026 date is written into the subsection itself rather than into a separate enactment clause, so it turns on when the action is commenced, not on when the unpaid work was performed. [^va-lb-ch1040-good-faith] Whether a separate repayment promise is enforceable as a contract claim against a former employee is a different question that this guide does not cover.
