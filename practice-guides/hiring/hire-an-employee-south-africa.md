# Hire an employee in South Africa[^about]

Plan a first South African employee hire: the employing entity, written particulars, the 2026 minimum wage, hours, leave, PAYE, UIF and other payroll duties, contractors, fixed terms, probation, notice, restraints, IP, employee data and work visas.

## What should a founder settle before hiring a first employee in South Africa? {#prepare-the-hire}

**Short answer.** Before a first South African hire, a founder settles which entity will employ the person, because a foreign company that is party to a South African employment contract must register there as an external company within 20 business days. [^za-prepare-external-company-conducting-business] [^za-prepare-external-company-registration-deadline] The employer must also register with the South African Revenue Service (SARS) within 21 business days of becoming an employer, unless no employee is liable for normal tax. [^za-prepare-sars-employer-registration-deadline]

The hiring file records the facts the written particulars must state at commencement, including the full name and address of the employer, the employee’s name and occupation or a brief description of the work, the place of work and the start date. [^za-prepare-particulars-facts] Those facts, with the hours, pay and payroll arrangements, feed later questions in this guide: the [written particulars](#written-particulars), the [minimum wage](#pay-and-hours), [working time](#working-time), [employees’ tax](#paye-payroll-tax) and the other [payroll contributions](#uif-contributions).

The [foreign employer question](#foreign-employer-without-entity) covers employing without a local subsidiary, including through an employer of record. The [contractor question](#employee-or-contractor) covers when a contract engagement is employment. The [fixed-term question](#fixed-term-and-agency-workers) covers when fixed-term and agency arrangements become permanent employment.

The [South Africa hiring reviewer checklist](/checklists/hiring/south-africa) turns these points into review items.

Statistics South Africa reported an official unemployment rate of 33.6% in the second quarter of 2026, with 16.7 million people employed and 8.5 million unemployed. [^za-prepare-qlfs-unemployment-rate] [^za-prepare-qlfs-employment-levels]

## Can a foreign company employ someone in South Africa without a local subsidiary? {#foreign-employer-without-entity}

**Short answer.** A foreign company can employ someone in South Africa without a local subsidiary, but the employment contract counts as conducting business there, which requires registration as an external company within 20 business days. [^za-foreign-external-company-conducting-business] [^za-foreign-external-company-registration-deadline] A foreign company that is not tax resident in South Africa must, unless the Commissioner authorises otherwise, withhold South African employees’ tax only if it conducts business through a permanent establishment in South Africa or acts through a representative employer, while one that is tax resident withholds like any resident employer. [^za-foreign-paye-permanent-establishment-rule]

An external company must also continuously maintain at least one office in South Africa and register its address. [^za-foreign-external-company-office]

The permanent-establishment test replaced a narrower rule in December 2023. Commentary describes the earlier rule: a non-resident employer withheld employees’ tax for a South African tax-resident employee only through a representative employer, and otherwise the employee accounted for tax through the provisional tax system. [^za-foreign-cdh-history-provisional-tax] [^za-foreign-paye-permanent-establishment-rule] Commentary reads an employee’s home as not a fixed place of business of the employer where the employer is not free to use it for business purposes, so such remote working is unlikely to create a permanent establishment. [^za-foreign-werksmans-home-office]

Commentary describes permanent establishment as a largely factual test that seldom gives certainty, and reported in February 2026 that the Budget proposed adding a requirement that the employee be effectively connected to the permanent establishment. [^za-foreign-cdh-pe-uncertainty] [^za-foreign-cdh-budget-proposal] The conservative course is a written permanent-establishment assessment before the first payroll run.

Employees’ tax is not the only payroll duty. Practitioners note that a foreign employer can owe unemployment insurance contributions and the skills development levy even when it has no employees’ tax obligation, and that the levy cannot be paid without registering with the South African Revenue Service (SARS). [^za-foreign-bowmans-uif-sdl] A non-resident employer carrying on business in South Africa must also give the Compensation Commissioner its head-office address and the name of its chief officer in South Africa, who is then deemed to be the employer for compensation-fund purposes. [^za-foreign-coida-non-resident-employer]

Commentary describes an employer of record as a third party that becomes the legal employer so a foreign client can employ without its own South African entity, and warns that South African courts look past the contracts to the real relationship, so the client can be found to be a co-employer, and that the temporary-employment-service deeming rules can make lower-earning workers the client’s employees. [^za-foreign-cdh-eor-model] [^za-foreign-cdh-eor-risk] The [fixed-term question](#fixed-term-and-agency-workers) sets out those deeming rules.

## When is a South African contractor treated as an employee? {#employee-or-contractor}

**Short answer.** A person earning no more than the earnings threshold who works for or renders services to another is presumed to be an employee, whatever the contract says, if any one of seven listed factors is present, such as control or economic dependence, until the contrary is proved. [^za-contractor-presumption-factors] Under the 2014 amendment, the presumption applies for the purposes of the Labour Relations Act, any employment law and section 98A of the Insolvency Act. [^za-contractor-presumption-2014] The threshold has been R269 600.90 a year since 1 May 2026. [^za-contractor-earnings-threshold]

The seven factors are control over how the person works, control over the person’s hours, forming part of the organisation, an average of at least 40 hours a month over the last three months, economic dependence, being provided with tools of trade or work equipment, and working for only one person. [^za-contractor-presumption-factors] The presumption shifts the burden: until the contrary is proved, the person is an employee.

Above the threshold the general section 200A presumption does not apply, though the taxi and forestry sectoral determinations contain their own presumptions for people working in those sectors, and the label still does not decide the point. [^za-contractor-taxi-presumption] [^za-contractor-forestry-presumption] Commentary describes the courts’ approach as substance over form, looking at the real relationship, and as weighing supervision and control, integration into the organisation and economic dependence. [^za-contractor-cdh-substance-over-form] [^za-contractor-ww-dominant-impression] Commentary also reads earners below the threshold as more likely to be found employees whatever their contract says. [^za-contractor-cdh-below-threshold]

Commentary notes that classification decides access to the labour statutes: only an employee can claim unfair dismissal or the protections of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA). [^za-contractor-ww-employee-protections] The [intellectual property question](#ip-ownership) covers ownership of work made by employees and contractors.

## What must written employment particulars in South Africa contain? {#written-particulars}

**Short answer.** Where Chapter Four of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA) applies, the employer must give an employee written particulars when employment starts, covering the employer, occupation, workplace, start date, hours, pay, deductions, leave and notice or a fixed-term end date, among other items. [^za-particulars-role-and-workplace] [^za-particulars-hours-pay-leave] [^za-particulars-coverage-24-hours] Chapter Four does not cover an employee who works less than 24 hours a month for the employer. [^za-particulars-coverage-24-hours]

The list runs from the employer’s full name and address, the employee’s name and occupation or a brief description of the work, the place of work and the start date, to ordinary hours and days, the wage or its calculation method, the overtime rate, other cash payments, payments in kind and their value, pay frequency, deductions, leave, and the notice period or the end date of a fixed-term contract. [^za-particulars-role-and-workplace] [^za-particulars-hours-pay-leave] For an employer with five or more employees, it also covers any council or sectoral determination that applies, prior service that counts, and a list of other documents forming part of the contract with a reasonably accessible place to obtain copies. [^za-particulars-documents-and-changes]

An employer with fewer than five employees is exempt only from the items on applicable determinations, prior service and other contract documents, and from sections 30, 31 and 33; the 2002 amendment deleted the separate domestic-worker exception. [^za-particulars-small-employer-exemption] [^za-particulars-domestic-worker-deletion] When a listed term changes, the particulars must be revised and the employee given a copy, an employee who cannot understand them must have them explained in a language and manner the employee understands, and the employer keeps them for three years after the employment ends. [^za-particulars-documents-and-changes]

Where Chapter Four of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act applies, remuneration is due no later than seven days after the end of the period for which it is payable, and an employer with five or more employees also gives written information on each payday covering the employer, the employee, the period, the pay, each deduction and the amount actually paid, plus, where relevant to the calculation, the pay and overtime rates, the ordinary and overtime hours worked, the hours worked on a Sunday or public holiday and any averaging-period totals, given at the workplace or an agreed place during ordinary working hours or within 15 minutes of their start or end; the National Minimum Wage Act separately applies that payslip duty to the payment of the national minimum wage. [^za-particulars-payslip-information] [^za-particulars-payslip-hours] [^za-particulars-small-employer-exemption] [^za-particulars-nmw-payslip] A salary-only offer letter therefore leaves gaps that the hours and pay schedule, the leave schedule and the incorporated policies fill.

## What is South Africa’s national minimum wage in 2026? {#pay-and-hours}

**Short answer.** From 1 March 2026, South Africa’s national minimum wage is R30.23 for each ordinary hour worked, with a lower rate for expanded public works programme workers and separate allowances for learnerships. [^za-wage-2026-rate-and-exceptions] [^za-wage-2026-binding-date] Farm and domestic workers receive the same R30.23 rate, expanded public works programme workers R16.62 an hour, and learners the Schedule 2 allowances. [^za-wage-2026-rate-and-exceptions] The minimum cannot be waived and overrides any contract that pays less. [^za-wage-no-waiver]

Unless a section 15 exemption specifies a different wage for a period of up to one year, every employer must pay wages no less than the national minimum wage, and the minimum becomes a term of the contract unless the contract provides more. [^za-wage-no-waiver] [^za-wage-exemption] The comparison uses the amount payable in money for ordinary hours of work, excluding payments that enable the worker to work, such as transport, equipment, tool, food or accommodation allowances, and payments in kind such as board or accommodation, each unless a sectoral determination specifies otherwise, as well as gratuities such as bonuses, tips or gifts and any other prescribed category of payment. [^za-wage-calculation-excludes-allowances] [^za-wage-calculation-other-exclusions] Subject to section 9A of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act, a worker is entitled to the minimum for the hours worked on any day, and a worker paid on another basis may not receive less than the minimum for ordinary hours. [^za-wage-calculation-other-exclusions] A monthly salary therefore needs an hourly conversion against the agreed ordinary hours, and a contract, collective agreement or other law that provides a more favourable wage prevails over the minimum. [^za-wage-no-waiver]

The 2026 notice gives farm and domestic workers the same R30.23 rate, sets R16.62 an hour for expanded public works programme workers, and applies separate allowances to learnerships. [^za-wage-2026-rate-and-exceptions]

Statistics South Africa reported average monthly earnings of R30 611 in the formal non-agricultural sector in May 2026. [^za-wage-qes-average-earnings]

## What working hours and overtime limits apply to a South African employee? {#working-time}

**Short answer.** Under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA), the default limits for a covered employee are 45 ordinary hours a week and overtime by agreement, up to ten hours a week at one and a half times the wage, subject to agreed alternatives and any applicable sectoral or ministerial determination. [^za-hours-ordinary-limit] [^za-hours-overtime-2002-text] [^za-hours-overtime-rate] [^za-hours-small-business-scope] [^za-hours-public-works-determination] Those alternatives are an agreed extension of up to 15 minutes a day, capped at 60 minutes a week, so that an employee who serves members of the public can finish doing so, a written compressed week, collective averaging, a collective increase to 15 overtime hours for up to two months in 12, and agreed paid time off instead of overtime pay, either the ordinary wage plus at least 30 minutes off or at least 90 minutes off per overtime hour, granted within one month or, by written agreement, 12 months. [^za-hours-ordinary-limit] [^za-hours-compressed-week] [^za-hours-averaging] [^za-hours-overtime-2002-text] [^za-hours-time-off-agreement] [^za-hours-time-off-alternative] The ordinary-hours and overtime-agreement limits also do not apply to urgent work that unforeseeable circumstances require and ordinary hours cannot cover, although overtime pay still does. [^za-hours-urgent-work] Employees earning above R269 600.90 a year are excluded from these and related working-time provisions from 1 May 2026. [^za-hours-earnings-threshold-2026]

The daily ordinary limit is nine hours for an employee who works five days a week or fewer and eight hours for one who works more than five days. [^za-hours-ordinary-limit] Since 2002, an overtime agreement cannot require or permit more than 12 hours’ total work on any day, and a collective agreement may raise overtime to 15 hours a week for no more than two months in any 12. [^za-hours-overtime-2002-text] Two statutory alternatives vary the daily limits: a written compressed-week agreement may allow up to 12 hours a day, including meal intervals, without overtime pay, within 45 ordinary hours, ten overtime hours and five days a week, and a collective agreement may average ordinary hours and overtime over up to four months, within averages of 45 ordinary hours and five overtime hours a week, with the first two such agreements lapsing after 12 months. [^za-hours-compressed-week] [^za-hours-averaging] An overtime agreement concluded when the employee starts, or in the first three months, lapses after one year. [^za-hours-overtime-agreement-lapses] Instead of overtime pay at one and a half times the wage, an agreement may provide for the ordinary wage plus 30 minutes’ paid time off per overtime hour, or 90 minutes’ paid time off per overtime hour, with the time off granted within one month or, by written agreement, 12 months. [^za-hours-time-off-alternative]

A sectoral or ministerial determination made under the BCEA can vary these defaults for the employers and employees it covers, so a hiring review confirms whether one covers the hire and applies its terms where they differ. [^za-hours-small-business-scope] [^za-hours-public-works-determination] The small business determination applies to an employer whose business employs fewer than ten employees, but not to domestic workers, the public service, an employer that conducts more than one business or a business formed by dividing or dissolving an existing one, and a bargaining council agreement or sectoral determination takes precedence over it unless that agreement or determination provides otherwise. [^za-hours-small-business-scope] It allows up to 15 overtime hours a week, paid at least one and one third times the wage for the first ten hours and one and a half times beyond that, and lets a written agreement with the employee average ordinary hours and overtime over up to four months, within averages of 45 ordinary and ten overtime hours a week, lapsing after 12 months. [^za-hours-small-business-overtime] The determination for expanded public works programmes, by contrast, sets general limits of 40 hours and five days a week and eight hours a day, allows ten hours a day on an agreed four-day week, and lets a task-rated worker work up to 55 hours a week to complete tasks allocated on a 40-hour basis. [^za-hours-public-works-determination]

Apart from the earnings threshold, the working-time chapter, except section 7, does not apply to senior managerial employees, to sales staff who travel to customers and regulate their own hours, or to employees working less than 24 hours a month for the employer. [^za-hours-chapter-exclusions]

The threshold exclusion removes sections 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17(2) and 18(3) of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act. [^za-hours-earnings-threshold-2026] The threshold is measured on regular annual remuneration before deductions, excluding the employer’s pension, medical and similar contributions, overtime pay, achievement awards and subsistence and transport allowances. [^za-hours-earnings-threshold-2026] Commentary notes that each annual increase brings some employees back within these rights. [^za-hours-ens-threshold-effect]

## What paid leave must a South African employee receive? {#leave-schedule}

**Short answer.** Under South Africa’s Basic Conditions of Employment Act, an employee working 24 hours or more a month for the employer has statutory rights to paid annual leave and paid sick leave. [^za-leave-chapter-24-hours] [^za-leave-annual-21-days] [^za-leave-sick-six-weeks] The default entitlements are at least 21 consecutive days’ paid annual leave per 12-month cycle and paid sick leave of six weeks’ normal working days per 36-month cycle. [^za-leave-annual-21-days] [^za-leave-sick-six-weeks] Annual leave may instead accrue by agreement at one day per 17 days, or one hour per 17 hours, worked or entitled to be paid, sick leave accrues at one day per 26 days worked in the first six months, and a bargaining-council collective agreement may vary leave only within statutory limits, which include that it may not cut annual leave below two weeks, reduce maternity, parental, adoption, commissioning parental or sick leave, or conflict with Chapter Six. [^za-leave-annual-21-days] [^za-leave-sick-six-weeks] [^za-leave-bargaining-council-variation] [^za-leave-bargaining-council-2018] The employer may reduce annual leave by occasional leave on full remuneration granted at the employee’s request in that cycle, and may deduct sick leave taken in the first six months from the first sick leave cycle’s entitlement. [^za-leave-annual-occasional] [^za-leave-sick-six-weeks] Subject to section 23, sick pay is the wage the employee would ordinarily have received for the day, paid on the usual payday, unless an agreement reduces the daily rate to no less than 75% of the ordinary-hours wage while increasing the paid days at least commensurately and keeping total sick pay over the cycle at least equal to the six-week entitlement. [^za-leave-sick-six-weeks] Under the small business determination, a written agreement may also reduce annual leave by family responsibility leave granted on full remuneration at the employee’s request. [^za-leave-small-business-family-responsibility] The [working-time question](#working-time) explains how a sectoral or ministerial determination covering the hire can vary these defaults. Parental leave follows the interim regime the Constitutional Court ordered in *Van Wyk*. [^za-leave-van-wyk-order] [^za-leave-van-wyk-interim-allocation]

The employer must grant annual leave within six months after the cycle ends and may pay it out only on termination. [^za-leave-annual-timing-payout] Sick pay can be withheld for absences of more than two consecutive days, or more than twice in eight weeks, when the employee does not produce a medical certificate on request, but not from an employee living on the employer’s premises who cannot reasonably obtain one unless the employer gives reasonable assistance. [^za-leave-sick-certificate]

An employee employed for longer than four months who works at least four days a week is also entitled to three days’ paid family responsibility leave in each annual leave cycle, when the employee’s child is sick or on the death of a spouse or life partner or a listed relative, unless a collective agreement varies the days or circumstances, and the employer may require reasonable proof of the event before paying; the 2018 amendment repealed the separate ground for a child’s birth. [^za-leave-family-responsibility-coverage] [^za-leave-family-responsibility-death] [^za-leave-family-responsibility-birth-repealed] [^za-leave-family-responsibility-variation] [^za-leave-family-responsibility-proof]

Under the *Van Wyk* interim order, a single parent, or the only employed parent, is entitled to at least four consecutive months’ parental leave, and two employed parents share four months and ten days between them, including the six weeks after birth that a birth mother may not work without medical clearance. [^za-leave-van-wyk-interim-allocation] [^za-leave-van-wyk-sharing-notice] If two parents cannot agree on how to take the remainder, it is apportioned so that each parent’s total leave is as close as possible to half of four months and ten days, to be completed within four months of the birth or other relevant date. [^za-leave-van-wyk-sharing-notice] For parental leave on a birth, an employee notifies the employer in writing of the intended start and return dates, unless unable to do so, at least four weeks before the leave starts or, if that is not reasonably practicable, as soon as it is. [^za-leave-van-wyk-sharing-notice] Commentary reports that for adoption and commissioning parental leave the written notice of leave and return dates is due one month ahead, unless impracticable. [^za-leave-cdh-notice-adoption] The Court suspended its declaration of invalidity for 36 months to let Parliament legislate. [^za-leave-van-wyk-order]

Commentary reads statutory parental leave as protected but unpaid; any income during it comes from the Unemployment Insurance Fund or from a contractual employer benefit, and commentary notes that the Court left the unemployment-insurance benefit provisions unchanged during the suspension period. [^za-leave-ww-unpaid-protected] [^za-leave-bowmans-uif-unchanged] An employer that already pays enhanced maternity leave faces a policy choice, because commentary reads the judgment as requiring parental benefits to reach all categories of parents. [^za-leave-cdh-employer-implications]

## How does PAYE work for a South African employee? {#paye-payroll-tax}

**Short answer.** A South African employer deducts employees’ tax, known as Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE), from remuneration and pays it to the South African Revenue Service (SARS) within seven days after the end of the month of deduction, subject to statutory qualifications. [^za-paye-deduct-and-pay-within-seven-days] [^za-paye-sars-monthly-obligation] The Commissioner may authorise otherwise or allow a longer period, an employer that ceases to be one pays within seven days of ceasing, and the Employment Tax Incentive can reduce the amount payable. [^za-paye-deduct-and-pay-within-seven-days] [^za-paye-employment-tax-incentive] The employer registers with SARS within 21 business days of becoming an employer unless no employee is liable for normal tax. [^za-paye-registration-21-days]

The monthly employer declaration, the EMP201, carries PAYE together with the skills development levy and unemployment insurance contributions, and payment goes to SARS with the same declaration. [^za-paye-emp201-declaration] SARS states that in most instances the employer issues each employee with an employees’ tax certificate (IRP5 or IT3(a)) at the end of each tax period, and the employer also submits a reconciliation declaration, the EMP501. [^za-paye-sars-monthly-obligation] For 2026, SARS set the annual reconciliation window from 1 April to 31 May and the interim reconciliation, covering March to August, from 21 September to 31 October. [^za-paye-2026-annual-window] [^za-paye-2026-interim-window]

Unless the Commissioner authorises otherwise, a non-resident employer withholds PAYE only if it conducts business through a permanent establishment in South Africa or acts through a representative employer, which the [foreign employer question](#foreign-employer-without-entity) covers. [^za-paye-non-resident-employer-rule]

The Employment Tax Incentive lowers the cost of hiring younger, less experienced employees: an employer that qualifies reduces the PAYE it pays to SARS by the incentive calculated for its qualifying employees, without changing the employee’s salary. [^za-paye-employment-tax-incentive]

## What unemployment insurance contributions apply to a South African employee? {#uif-contributions}

**Short answer.** Every employer and employee covered by the Unemployment Insurance Contributions Act contributes monthly to the Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF): 1% of remuneration from the employee and a matching 1% from the employer. [^za-uif-duty-and-rate] Contributions are calculated on remuneration up to R17 712 a month, the ceiling the South African Revenue Service (SARS) has applied since 1 June 2021. [^za-uif-sars-ceiling]

An employee who works less than 24 hours a month for the employer does not contribute. [^za-uif-sars-exclusions] The employer deducts the employee’s share and pays the total of 2% within seven days after the end of the month, or any longer period the Commissioner determines, or by the last business day before a weekend or public holiday on which the seventh day falls, to SARS with the monthly employer declaration if it is registered with SARS. [^za-uif-sars-ceiling] [^za-uif-sars-payment-timing] [^za-uif-payment-deadline] An employer that is not registered with SARS for employees’ tax or the skills development levy registers with the Unemployment Insurance Commissioner instead and pays its contributions there. [^za-uif-sars-registration-route] [^za-uif-duty-and-rate]

Separately, every employer must, as soon as it starts activities as an employer, give the Unemployment Insurance Commissioner information about its employees, whatever they earn, including the street addresses of the business and its branches and each employee’s name, identification number, monthly remuneration and work address; an employer that is not resident in South Africa, or is a body corporate not registered there, must also supply the particulars of the authorised person required to carry out its duties under the Act. [^za-uif-employer-information-duty]

## When must a South African employer pay the skills development levy and register for workplace-injury compensation? {#skills-levy-and-compensation-fund}

**Short answer.** A South African employer pays a skills development levy of 1% of its leviable amount, its remuneration bill measured as for employees’ tax, unless a statutory exemption applies, most relevantly a leviable amount not expected to exceed R500 000 over the next 12 months. [^za-levy-one-percent-rate] [^za-levy-leviable-amount] [^za-levy-sars-exemptions] The Minister may instead announce a rate in the national budget, which applies from the announced date for 12 months subject to Parliament passing implementing legislation within that period, or, on a SETA’s request and in consultation with the Minister of Finance, fix a different rate and basis by notice in the Gazette for employers within that SETA’s jurisdiction, provided the notice collects no less than the standard rate would. [^za-levy-one-percent-rate] [^za-levy-leviable-amount] [^za-levy-seta-rate] The other exemptions cover public service employers, certain public entities, qualifying public benefit organisations and certain municipalities. [^za-levy-sars-exemptions] Every employer carrying on business in South Africa must also register with the Compensation Commissioner under the Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act (COIDA), unless the Commissioner has exempted it as an employer referred to in section 84(1)(a) or (b). [^za-coida-employer-registration]

The leviable amount is the total remuneration paid or payable to employees each month, as determined under the Fourth Schedule to the Income Tax Act, whether or not the employer must deduct employees’ tax, subject to listed exclusions, which since 1 September 2024 also cover amounts within paragraph (eD) of the Income Tax Act’s gross income definition; the levy is declared and paid monthly on the employer declaration, the EMP201. [^za-levy-leviable-amount] [^za-levy-2024-exclusion] [^za-levy-sars-emp201] An employer below the R500 000 level need not register for the levy on that ground. [^za-levy-sars-exemptions]

The Commissioner applies the compensation fund to pay compensation, medical costs and other benefits to or for employees under COIDA where no other person is liable. [^za-coida-compensation-fund] The employer furnishes the prescribed particulars of each business and notifies the Commissioner of any change within seven days. [^za-coida-employer-registration] A non-resident employer names its chief officer in South Africa, who is deemed to be the employer. [^za-coida-non-resident-chief-officer]

## Can a South African employer dismiss an employee during probation without a fair reason? {#probation-and-dismissal}

**Short answer.** A South African employer cannot dismiss a probationary employee without a fair reason and a fair procedure, because under the 2025 Code of Good Practice: Dismissal the employer must show both for any dismissal that is not automatically unfair. [^za-probation-code-fair-reason-procedure] Probation exists to evaluate the employee’s performance and suitability before the appointment is confirmed. [^za-probation-code-purpose]

The Code recognises three possible fair grounds: the employee’s conduct, the employee’s capacity and the employer’s operational requirements. [^za-probation-code-three-grounds] An offer-letter termination clause that describes employment as at will, lets the employer end it on notice whatever the reason, or ends it automatically when probation expires does not remove the employer’s burden to show a fair reason and a fair procedure for the dismissal. [^za-probation-code-fair-reason-procedure] A notice period sets how much warning is given; it does not supply the reason or the process, which the [notice question](#termination-notice) covers.

The probation period should be set in advance and be reasonable for the job and the time needed to judge suitability, and probation should not be used to deprive employees of permanent status. [^za-probation-code-duration] [^za-probation-code-not-to-deprive] During probation the employer should give reasonable guidance, appropriate to the nature and size of the employer and the job, which may include instruction, training or counselling. [^za-probation-code-guidance] Before dismissing or extending probation, the employer gives the employee an opportunity to make representations and considers them. [^za-probation-code-representations]

The 2025 Code allows less compelling reasons for a dismissal during or at the end of probation that relates to conduct or capacity, including poor performance. [^za-probation-code-less-compelling] Commentary notes that earlier guidance confined that allowance to poor work performance and that the 2025 Code also recognises the position of small businesses that lack formal human resources capacity. [^za-probation-werksmans-relaxed] [^za-probation-werksmans-small-business] Commentary reports that the Code took effect on 4 September 2025 and replaced the earlier dismissal and operational-requirements codes. [^za-probation-werksmans-replaced-codes]

## When does a fixed-term or agency worker in South Africa become a permanent employee? {#fixed-term-and-agency-workers}

**Short answer.** For an employee covered by section 198B of the Labour Relations Act, a fixed-term contract kept beyond three months without work of limited or definite duration or another justifiable reason is deemed to be of indefinite duration. [^za-fixed-term-three-month-rule] [^za-fixed-term-small-employer-exclusion] Section 198B does not cover employees earning above the earnings threshold, an employer with fewer than ten employees or with fewer than 50 employees whose business has operated for less than two years (in either case unless it runs more than one business or was formed by dividing or dissolving an existing business), or a fixed term that a statute, sectoral determination or collective agreement permits. [^za-fixed-term-small-employer-exclusion]

Where section 198B applies, an offer of a fixed-term contract, or of a renewal or extension, must be in writing and state the reason for fixing the term, and where it is relevant in any proceedings, the employer must prove the justifiable reason and that the term was agreed. [^za-fixed-term-writing-and-proof] The statute lists reasons that justify a fixed term, including replacing an employee who is temporarily absent, a temporary increase in work not expected to last beyond 12 months, a student or recent graduate trained or gaining work experience to enter a job or profession, exclusive work on a specific project of limited or defined duration, a non-citizen with a work permit for a defined period, and a position externally funded for a limited period. [^za-fixed-term-justified-reasons] Within the section’s scope, a fixed-term employee kept for more than three months may not be treated less favourably than a comparable permanent employee without a justifiable reason. [^za-fixed-term-writing-and-proof]

Commentary on a 2026 Labour Court ruling reads the section as rejecting vague formulations such as completion of tasks, and rolling or call-back contracts, as a basis for fixing the term. [^za-fixed-term-cdh-vague-reasons]

A worker placed by a temporary employment service, often called a labour broker, who earns no more than the threshold is deemed to be the client’s employee once the placement is no longer a temporary service, which means a placement of more than three months that is not a substitution for a temporarily absent employee and not in a category that a bargaining-council collective agreement, sectoral determination or ministerial notice treats as temporary service. [^za-agency-deeming-rule] That is the main risk in employer-of-record arrangements discussed in the [foreign employer question](#foreign-employer-without-entity).

## How much notice ends a South African employment contract? {#termination-notice}

**Short answer.** Under section 37 of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA), a covered contract terminable on notice needs at least one week’s notice in the first six months, two weeks until the first anniversary and four weeks thereafter, subject to statutory variations and exceptions. [^za-notice-2002-periods] The rules exclude employees working under 24 hours a month, give farm and domestic workers four weeks after six months, let a collective agreement cut the one-year four weeks to two, allow pay instead of notice, and preserve termination without notice for lawful cause. [^za-notice-24-hour-exclusion] [^za-notice-2002-periods] [^za-notice-payment-in-lieu] [^za-notice-without-notice-for-cause] The [working-time question](#working-time) explains how a sectoral or ministerial determination covering the hire can vary these defaults. Notice must be in writing, except when an illiterate employee gives it, and an employee who cannot understand a notice must have it explained orally, by or on behalf of the employer, in an official language the employee reasonably understands. [^za-notice-in-writing]

| Length of employment | Minimum notice under section 37(1), other than farm and domestic workers |
| --- | --- |
| Six months or less | One week |
| More than six months but less than one year | Two weeks |
| One year or more | Four weeks |

Apart from that reduction, a collective agreement may not shorten these periods. [^za-notice-2002-periods] No agreement may require an employee to give longer notice than the employer must give. [^za-notice-in-writing] An employer may pay the remuneration for the notice period instead of requiring work during it. [^za-notice-payment-in-lieu] Notice rules do not apply to an employee who works less than 24 hours a month for the employer, and they leave intact the right of either party to terminate without notice for any cause recognised by law. [^za-notice-24-hour-exclusion] [^za-notice-without-notice-for-cause]

On termination the employer pays out unused annual leave and, for an employee employed longer than four months, accrued leave for the incomplete leave cycle. [^za-notice-termination-leave-pay] The [probation question](#probation-and-dismissal) covers the reason and procedure a dismissal needs, and the [retrenchment question](#retrenchment) covers dismissals for operational requirements.

## What process applies when a South African employer retrenches employees? {#retrenchment}

**Short answer.** A South African employer that contemplates dismissing employees for operational requirements must consult, and where no collective agreement, workplace forum or registered union applies, it consults the employees likely to be affected or their nominated representatives. [^za-retrench-who-to-consult] Where the termination chapter of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act applies, a retrenched employee is entitled to severance pay of at least one week’s remuneration for each completed year of continuous service, unless the employee unreasonably refuses the employer’s offer of alternative employment. [^za-retrench-severance-pay] [^za-retrench-chapter-five] [^za-retrench-alternative-employment]

The consultation is a meaningful joint consensus-seeking process on measures to avoid or minimise the dismissals, change their timing and mitigate their effects, on how the employees to be dismissed are selected, and on severance pay. [^za-retrench-consensus-topics] Commentary notes that the 2025 Code of Good Practice: Dismissal adds an annexure with the form and content of the notice of possible retrenchment that opens the process. [^za-retrench-werksmans-notice-annexure]

An employee who unreasonably refuses the employer’s offer of alternative employment, whether with the employer or another employer, loses the statutory severance pay, and the termination chapter does not cover an employee working less than 24 hours a month for the employer. [^za-retrench-alternative-employment] [^za-retrench-chapter-five] The [notice question](#termination-notice) covers notice pay and leave pay due on termination.

## Can a South African employment contract include a restraint of trade? {#restraint-of-trade}

**Short answer.** A South African employment contract can include a post-employment restraint of trade, and practitioners read the law as treating it as valid and enforceable unless the party challenging it shows that it is unreasonable. [^za-restraint-bowmans-valid-unless-unreasonable] [^za-restraint-nrf-generally-enforceable] The restraint limits a constitutional right, because every citizen may choose a trade, occupation or profession freely, subject to regulation by law. [^za-restraint-constitution-section-22]

Commentary agrees that a restraint must protect a proprietary interest, such as confidential information or customer connections, and that preventing competition for skilled labour is not such an interest: a former employee may use the general knowledge and skills gained in the job. [^za-restraint-bowmans-protectable-interest] [^za-restraint-nrf-general-skills]

A restraint’s duration must match the interest. Practitioners report that, without convincing reasons for a long restraint, courts may cut it to the period that protects the interest; in two Labour Appeal Court decisions handed down on the same day, one restraint was reduced to one year and another was upheld because a 24- to 36-month product lead time justified it. [^za-restraint-bowmans-reduce-duration] [^za-restraint-bowmans-two-decisions] [^za-restraint-bowmans-lead-time] Commentary also reads the courts as holding that the way employment ends does not matter: a reasonable restraint remains enforceable after resignation, dismissal or retrenchment, unless the termination was fraudulent or in bad faith. [^za-restraint-cdh-termination-irrelevant]

The conservative drafting course is a restraint tied to a recorded interest, with each restriction, its duration and its territory justified by that interest, rather than a blanket non-compete.

## Does a South African employer own the copyright and inventions its employees create? {#ip-ownership}

**Short answer.** A South African employer owns the copyright in work an employee makes in the course of employment under a contract of service, unless an agreement excludes that rule or a narrower statutory rule applies. [^za-ip-employer-owns-course-of-employment] Unless an agreement excludes them, the narrower rules give a newspaper or periodical proprietor only the publication rights in an employee’s literary or artistic work made for publication, and give a person who commissions a photograph, portrait, gravure, film or sound recording, and pays or agrees to pay for it, the copyright in work made pursuant to that commission, subject to the newspaper rule. [^za-ip-author-owns-by-default] [^za-ip-commissioned-works] [^za-ip-employer-owns-course-of-employment] Outside those rules, copyright in a contractor’s work vests in the contractor as author. [^za-ip-author-owns-by-default] Any assignment of copyright, including from a contractor or from a South African employer to a foreign parent, has effect only if it is in writing and signed by or on behalf of the assignor. [^za-ip-assignment-in-writing]

Where copyright does not vest in the employer under section 21, an assignment to the employer has effect only if it is in writing and signed by or on behalf of the assignor. [^za-ip-employer-owns-course-of-employment] [^za-ip-assignment-in-writing]

For inventions, the Patents Act voids two kinds of employment term: a term requiring an employee to assign to the employer an invention made otherwise than within the course and scope of employment, and a term restricting the employee’s rights in an invention made more than one year after the employment ends. [^za-ip-patents-void-conditions]

## What does South Africa’s data protection law require for employee records and transfers abroad? {#employee-data}

**Short answer.** Under the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA), an employer collecting personal information directly from a candidate or employee must first take reasonably practicable steps to make the person aware of the listed matters, unless the person already knows them or a statutory exception applies. [^za-data-notice-contents] [^za-data-notice-timing] [^za-data-notice-exceptions] The exceptions cover repeat collection of the same kind of information for the same purpose after the employer has already taken the notification steps, and listed cases, such as the person’s consent to non-compliance or compliance not being reasonably practicable. [^za-data-notice-exceptions] Those matters include the information collected, the employer’s name and address, the purpose, whether supplying it is voluntary or mandatory, any intended transfer abroad and the person’s rights. [^za-data-notice-contents] A transfer to a recipient in another country, including a foreign parent or a payroll or human resources provider, also needs a ground listed in section 72(1), such as the employee’s consent, or a law, binding corporate rules or binding agreement giving adequate protection, meaning principles substantially similar to POPIA’s processing conditions and to its rules on onward transfer. [^za-data-transfer-grounds]

POPIA prohibits processing special personal information, including health, race, trade union membership, biometric information and criminal behaviour relating to alleged offences or proceedings about them, unless an exception applies, such as consent or processing necessary to establish, exercise or defend a right or obligation in law. [^za-data-special-information-prohibition] [^za-data-special-information-exceptions] Commentary reads POPIA as covering the whole employment relationship, from recruitment to after termination, and records such as employment history, references, disciplinary records and health and biometric information. [^za-data-ww-employment-scope]

Where a provider such as a payroll or human resources platform processes employee information for the employer as an operator, the employer must require it, by written contract, to establish and maintain the security measures POPIA requires. [^za-data-operator-written-contract] Transferring special personal information to a country without adequate protection also needs the Information Regulator’s prior authorisation, unless a sector code of conduct in force displaces that requirement; one authorisation covers later processing that does not depart from it. [^za-data-prior-authorisation-special]

For transfers within a group, commentary recommends a data protection agreement where concluding one is not onerous, unless another section 72 ground applies, and notes that the ground for transfers necessary to perform a contract concluded in the employee’s interest can cover an employment arrangement such as a secondment. [^za-data-cms-agreement-recommended] [^za-data-cms-employment-contract-ground]

## When do South African employment equity duties apply to a growing company? {#employment-equity}

**Short answer.** Every South African employer is bound by the prohibition on unfair discrimination in employment policy and practice, but affirmative action duties bind only designated employers, which for a private employer since 1 January 2025 means 50 or more employees or designation by collective agreement to the extent it provides. [^za-equity-unfair-discrimination] [^za-equity-chapter-three-designated-only] [^za-equity-original-definition] [^za-equity-turnover-limb-deleted] [^za-equity-commencement-small-employers]

Before the 2022 amendment took effect, an employer with fewer than 50 employees could still be designated by turnover; the amendment deleted that limb, so a private employer with fewer than 50 employees is designated only if, and to the extent that, a collective agreement makes it one. [^za-equity-original-definition] [^za-equity-turnover-limb-deleted] The Department of Employment and Labour announced that employers with fewer than 50 employees are no longer bound by Chapter III, including the reporting duty, from the 2025 reporting period. [^za-equity-commencement-small-employers]

A designated employer’s numerical goals must comply with any sectoral target the Minister sets for its sector under section 15A. [^za-equity-sectoral-targets] [^za-equity-goals-must-comply]

Two rules reach every employer from the first hire. The prohibition on unfair discrimination, as amended in 2013, applies on the listed grounds or on any other arbitrary ground, and the listed grounds include race, gender, sex, pregnancy, family responsibility, age, disability, religion, HIV status and culture. [^za-equity-unfair-discrimination] [^za-equity-arbitrary-ground] Medical testing of an employee is prohibited unless legislation permits or requires it or it is justifiable on the listed grounds, and testing of an employee’s HIV status is prohibited unless the Labour Court determines it justifiable. [^za-equity-medical-testing]

## Can a South African company hire a foreign national? {#foreign-national-hires}

**Short answer.** A South African company may hire a foreign national only if the person’s immigration status authorises employment by that company on the terms offered, and the employer must make a good faith effort to ascertain that status. [^za-foreign-national-employment-prohibition]

The main routes for skilled hires are the general work visa and the critical skills work visa, both assessed under the 2024 points-based system: an applicant must meet the other prescribed requirements and earn 100 points, and an occupation on the critical skills list alone supplies 100 points. [^za-foreign-national-points-notice] [^za-foreign-national-points-table] Without a listed occupation, an applicant can reach 100 points through a combination of the other criteria, which cover qualifications, a mandatory offer of employment banded by salary, work experience, an employment offer from a Trusted Employer and language skills, and may then be issued with a general work visa. [^za-foreign-national-points-table] [^za-foreign-national-points-notice] Commentary reports the same structure. [^za-foreign-national-ww-points-system] [^za-foreign-national-ww-100-points] Commentary also notes that a remote work visitor’s visa does not allow its holder to take up employment in South Africa, and that employers accredited under the Trusted Employer Scheme can use an accelerated visa process. [^za-foreign-national-ww-remote-visa] [^za-foreign-national-baker-trusted-employer]

A fixed term tied to a non-citizen’s time-limited work permit is a recognised justification for a fixed-term contract, which matters for lower earners covered by the [fixed-term rules](#fixed-term-and-agency-workers). [^za-foreign-national-fixed-term-permit]
