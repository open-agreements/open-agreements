# New York Asset Protection Trusts: The Self-Settled Trust Rule, Out-of-State Trusts and Spouses[^about]

Why New York creditors can reach a trust its creator can benefit from, how out-of-state trusts and bankruptcy fare, and open questions for spouses.

## Can a New Yorker create a trust for themself that creditors cannot reach? {#self-settled-trust-rule}

**Short answer.** A New Yorker cannot create a trust for their own benefit that keeps out their own creditors, because EPTL 7-3.1(a) makes a trust for the creator's use void against the creator's existing and future creditors. [^eptl-7-3-1-self-settled-void]
The creditor does not have to prove that the creator meant to defraud anyone or was insolvent, as the Second Department held in *Vanderbilt Credit Corp. v Chase Manhattan Bank*. [^vanderbilt-no-fraud-or-insolvency-needed] In April 2026 the Fourth Department applied the same rule in *Matter of Anthone v Carlo*, holding that a lower court erred in requiring a judgment creditor to establish a fraudulent conveyance before enforcing against the assets of the debtor's own trust. [^anthone-no-fraudulent-conveyance-burden]
The rule applies whether the trust is revocable or irrevocable, as the Third Department held in *State of New York v Coyle*. [^coyle-revocable-or-irrevocable] A discretionary trust gives no more protection: in *Vanderbilt*, the trustee could pay the creator the entire principal, so the creditor could reach the whole trust fund without proving a fraudulent conveyance. [^vanderbilt-trustee-power-reaches-corpus]
For a revocable trust, a second statute reaches the same result: a creator who keeps an unqualified power to revoke remains the absolute owner of the trust property as far as creditors are concerned. [^eptl-10-10-6-revocable-trust-owner]

"(a) A disposition in trust for the use of the creator is void as against the existing or subsequent creditors of the creator."[^eptl-7-3-1-self-settled-void]

## Does New York allow any exceptions for trusts that benefit their creator? {#statutory-exceptions}

**Short answer.** New York's self-settled trust statute excludes qualified retirement accounts and plans, and it also provides that a trustee's power to pay or reimburse the creator's taxes on trust income or principal does not by itself make a trust one for the creator's use. [^eptl-7-3-1-retirement-plans-excluded] [^eptl-7-3-1-tax-reimbursement-power]
**Retirement accounts and plans.** Individual retirement accounts qualified under Internal Revenue Code section 408 or 408A, and Keogh and corporate retirement plans qualified under section 401, are not treated as a disposition in trust for the creator's use. [^eptl-7-3-1-retirement-plans-excluded] Additions to those assets lose the exemption if they were made after the date ninety days before the interposition of the claim on which the judgment was entered, which includes additions made after the claim was interposed, or if they are voidable transactions under the Debtor and Creditor Law. [^eptl-7-3-1-recent-retirement-additions]
**Tax-reimbursement powers.** A trustee's authority to pay principal to the creator under EPTL 7-1.11, or to pay or reimburse the creator's tax on trust income or principal, does not make the trust one for the creator's use, and no creditor may reach trust property based on those powers. [^eptl-7-3-1-tax-reimbursement-power] Section 7-1.11 itself lets a trustee, unless the trust instrument provides otherwise, pay the creator principal equal to the income taxes charged to the creator on trust principal. [^eptl-7-1-11-tax-principal-payment] Read together, these provisions protect a trust whose only route back to its creator is tax reimbursement; on the statute's text they do not protect a trust that can pay its creator for other purposes, which remains subject to subdivision (a). [^eptl-7-3-1-tax-reimbursement-power]
**Medicaid trigger clauses.** A separate subdivision works against the creator rather than for them. A trust clause that suspends, ends or diverts the creator's or the creator's spouse's interest if either applies for medical assistance or needs nursing or custodial care is void as against public policy, unless the trust is a testamentary trust or meets the Social Services Law requirements the statute names. [^eptl-7-3-1-medicaid-trigger-void]
**Supplemental needs trusts.** A different statute, EPTL 7-1.12, allows the beneficiary of a supplemental needs trust to be the trust's creator if the trust meets those same Social Services Law requirements. [^eptl-7-1-12-self-settled-supplemental-needs] That provision defines when a trust qualifies as a supplemental needs trust; this guide does not address how it interacts with private creditors' claims.

## How long do creditors have to challenge a New York trust or a transfer into it? {#creditor-deadlines}

**Short answer.** A creditor's claim that a self-settled trust is void under EPTL 7-3.1 has been held subject to a six-year limitations period, while a claim to set aside a transfer as voidable under the Debtor and Creditor Law generally must be brought within four years. [^cetero-six-year-limitations] [^dcl-278-extinguishment-deadlines]
**Claims that the trust is void.** EPTL 7-3.1 sets no limitations period of its own. The Second Circuit observed in *Thea v. Kleinhandler* that New York courts have therefore required these claims to be brought within six years. [^thea-courts-require-six-years] In *State of New York v Cetero*, the Third Department applied the six-year period to a claim that would set aside trust provisions placing the trust assets beyond a creditor's reach. [^cetero-six-year-limitations] In *Christopher v Tomeck*, the same court held claims under EPTL 7-3.1 and the former fraudulent-conveyance sections time-barred where the last transfer to the trust occurred in 2001, the creditor was notified in 2002, and suit came in 2008. [^christopher-tomeck-claims-time-barred]
When the six years begin is less settled. In *Christopher v Tomeck*, the court placed accrual at the transfer to the trust or, at the latest, when the creditor learned of the allegedly fraudulent activity. [^christopher-tomeck-accrual-of-trust-claims] No decision found in our review addresses when the period begins for a creditor whose claim arises years after the trust was funded while the trust remains in place, so that timing is an open question.
**Claims against the transfer.** For a transfer made with actual intent to hinder, delay or defraud, the claim is extinguished unless brought within four years after the transfer or, if later, one year after the creditor discovered or reasonably could have discovered it. Claims based on a transfer for less than reasonably equivalent value carry a flat four-year limit. [^dcl-278-extinguishment-deadlines]
**Which statute applies.** Those deadlines belong to the Uniform Voidable Transactions Act, which Assembly Bill 5622 of 2019 enacted in place of the former fraudulent-conveyance provisions. [^a5622-enacts-voidable-transactions-act] The Second Department noted in *Rosenfeld v Brody* that the Act became effective April 4, 2020. [^rosenfeld-uvta-effective-april-2020] In *Palmerone v Staples*, the same court applied the version of the Debtor and Creditor Law in effect at the time of the conveyances, which was former section 274. [^palmerone-version-at-time-of-conveyance] Read together, a transfer made before April 4, 2020 is judged under the former provisions. On our reading, its limitations analysis then follows the CPLR 213 decisions above, such as *Christopher v Tomeck*, rather than section 278. [^christopher-tomeck-claims-time-barred]

## Does a Nevada, Connecticut or other out-of-state self-settled trust protect a New York resident from creditors? {#out-of-state-trust}

**Short answer.** No New York appellate decision found in our review decides whether another state's self-settled trust law protects a New York resident's trust from New York creditors, and the New York authority that exists points toward exposure. [^dcl-279-debtor-located-at-residence] [^portnoy-new-york-weightier-concern] A creditor has two separate lines of attack, and they turn on different law.
**Attacking the transfer.** In a New York court, a claim against an individual's transfer is governed by the law of the place where the individual has their principal residence when the transfer is made. [^dcl-279-debtor-located-at-residence] Read together, this means a New York resident's transfer to a Nevada or Connecticut trustee is tested under New York's Debtor and Creditor Law in a New York court, whatever law the trust instrument chooses. [^dcl-279-debtor-located-at-residence] Under that law, a transfer made with actual intent to hinder, delay or defraud any creditor is voidable by creditors whose claims arose before or after the transfer. [^dcl-273-actual-intent-present-future-creditors] The statute's listed signs of intent include a transfer to an insider, the debtor's keeping possession or control of the transferred property, and a transfer made shortly before or after a substantial debt was incurred. [^dcl-273-badges-of-fraud]
**Attacking the trust itself.** Whether a New York court would apply EPTL 7-3.1 to treat a trust validly formed under another state's statute as void is an open question. The legal cause is a gap in the statutes: New York's provision giving effect to a trust's choice of New York law covers only creators who are not domiciled in New York, [^eptl-7-1-10-non-domiciliary-choice] and no statute found in our review addresses a New York domiciliary's choice of another state's law.
The closest New York-forum decision is *In re Portnoy*, where a bankruptcy court applied New York law, despite a Jersey (Channel Islands) governing-law clause, to decide whether the creator of a self-settled offshore trust kept a property interest he had to disclose. [^portnoy-new-york-weightier-concern] The *Portnoy* court also held that a creator who is the only party to a trust's choice-of-law clause cannot use it to take property out of his own reach. [^portnoy-settlor-cannot-choose-favorable-law] The decision has two limits: it resolved a bankruptcy discharge dispute rather than a New York collection action, and the court noted that the debtor had waived any argument that New Jersey law applied instead. [^portnoy-new-jersey-residence-waived] In *In re Huber*, a Washington bankruptcy court found that Alaska, whose law the trust chose, had only a minimal relation to the trust, while Washington had a substantial relation and a strong public policy against self-settled asset protection trusts. [^huber-alaska-minimal-relation] The *Huber* court then held the debtor's transfers into the trust void under Washington's statute; that decision is nonbinding in New York. [^huber-transfers-void-washington-law]
**Reaching assets held elsewhere.** A New York court may also lack power over trust property located outside the state. In *Riechers v Riechers*, a Westchester County court held that it had no jurisdiction over the corpus of a Cook Islands trust, although it had personal jurisdiction over the husband who created it. [^riechers-no-jurisdiction-over-trust-corpus] That trial court also said that, even with jurisdiction, a cause of action would not lie to set aside a trust established for the legitimate purpose of protecting family assets; it said so while assuming a jurisdiction it held it did not have. [^riechers-legitimate-purpose-statement]
**Divorce is a separate route.** In *Riechers*, the court used its personal jurisdiction over the husband to treat the value of marital assets placed in the offshore trust as subject to equitable distribution. [^riechers-trust-assets-distributable] The Second Department affirmed the award of half the trust's value, increasing it to correct a computation error. [^riechers-appellate-affirms-half-value] New York's equitable-distribution statute lists, among the factors a court considers, the wasteful dissipation of assets and any transfer made in contemplation of a matrimonial action without fair consideration. [^drl-236-dissipation-and-transfer-factors]

## Can a bankruptcy trustee undo a New Yorker's transfer to a self-settled trust made years earlier? {#bankruptcy-look-back}

**Short answer.** A bankruptcy trustee can avoid a debtor's transfer to a self-settled trust or similar device made within ten years before the bankruptcy filing, if the debtor is a beneficiary and made the transfer with actual intent to hinder, delay or defraud a creditor. [^usc-548-e-ten-year-self-settled-trust]
Because section 548(e) is federal law, its ten-year reach-back does not depend on which state's trust law the trust chooses. [^usc-548-e-ten-year-self-settled-trust] The trustee can also use any avoidance right that an actual unsecured creditor holds under state law, which for a New York debtor includes the Debtor and Creditor Law. [^usc-544-b-state-law-avoidance] A trust's restriction on transferring a beneficial interest binds the bankruptcy estate only if the restriction is enforceable under applicable nonbankruptcy law. [^usc-541-c-2-enforceable-restrictions] Read together with EPTL 7-3.1(a), this suggests that where New York law governs the trust, its spendthrift clause gives the creator no protection in bankruptcy, because New York law makes the trust void against the creator's creditors. [^eptl-7-3-1-void-in-bankruptcy-context]
Two out-of-state bankruptcy decisions show how courts have applied the ten-year rule; both are nonbinding in New York. In *Battley v. Mortensen*, the Alaska bankruptcy court avoided an Alaska resident's transfer of land into his own Alaska asset protection trust. [^mortensen-transfer-avoided] The court reasoned that applying the state law that permits such trusts would be an odd result for a federal statute aimed at closing that loophole, [^mortensen-federal-loophole-reasoning] and it treated the trust's express purpose of protecting assets from creditors as evidence of intent. [^mortensen-trust-purpose-shows-intent] In *In re Huber*, a Washington bankruptcy court granted the trustee summary judgment under the same provision on evidence that supported an inference of actual intent. [^huber-548-e-summary-judgment]

"(1) In addition to any transfer that the trustee may otherwise avoid, the trustee may avoid any transfer of an interest of the debtor in property that was made on or within 10 years before the date of the filing of the petition, if— (A) such transfer was made to a self-settled trust or similar device; (B) such transfer was by the debtor; (C) the debtor is a beneficiary of such trust or similar device; and (D) the debtor made such transfer with actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud any entity to which the debtor was or became, on or after the date that such transfer was made, indebted."[^usc-548-e-ten-year-self-settled-trust]

## Is a trust someone else creates for a New Yorker protected from that New Yorker's creditors? {#trust-created-by-someone-else}

**Short answer.** Property held in trust for a New York judgment debtor is exempt from the debtor's money judgments when someone other than the debtor created the trust or supplied the trust fund, subject to the exceptions the statute lists. [^cplr-5205-c-1-third-party-trust-exempt] Those exceptions preserve rights under a qualified domestic relations order, and rights under any order of support, alimony or maintenance to enforce arrears or past-due support, whether or not the arrears have been reduced to a money judgment, [^cplr-5205-c-4-support-orders-preserved] and deny the exemption to retirement-plan additions made after the date ninety days before the interposition of the claim on which the judgment was entered, or that are voidable transactions. [^cplr-5205-c-5-recent-retirement-additions]
The income interest is also protected by a restraint on transfer: a beneficiary's right to receive trust income cannot be assigned or otherwise transferred unless the trust instrument gives the beneficiary that power. [^eptl-7-1-5-income-right-not-transferable] For a trust that is not one of the retirement arrangements the statute names, ninety per cent of the income or other payments from a trust whose principal is exempt is itself exempt, except such part as a court determines to be unnecessary for the reasonable requirements of the judgment debtor and dependents. [^cplr-5205-d-1-trust-income-exemption] For income or payments from an individual retirement account plan, or a Keogh (HR-10), retirement or other plan described in CPLR 5205(c)(2), that reasonable-requirements exception does not apply, and the ninety percent exclusion becomes a one hundred percent exclusion. [^cplr-5205-d-1-trust-income-exemption] Where the trustee has discretion over payments, a creditor of a beneficiary who did not create the trust cannot compel the trustee to pay the beneficiary anything, as the Second Department stated in *Vanderbilt Credit Corp. v Chase Manhattan Bank*. [^vanderbilt-third-party-discretionary-trust]
**Trusts funded partly by the beneficiary.** The exemption's text reaches property where the trust was created by, or the fund proceeded from, a person other than the debtor. [^cplr-5205-c-1-third-party-trust-exempt] When the beneficiary also contributed, *Fordyce v Fordyce* said that even in a self-settled trust creditors reach only the interest the settlor retained for himself. [^fordyce-only-retained-interest-reachable] No New York decision found in our review apportions a trust between a beneficiary's own contributions and contributions from others, so our review found no answer to how much of a partly self-funded trust the exemption protects; the conservative course is to hold the beneficiary's own contributions in a separate trust from property others supply.
In *Pangea Capital Mgmt., LLC v. Lakian*, the Second Circuit conditionally certified whether a settlor who holds title in a revocable trust for himself and another beneficiary remains the absolute owner as against his creditors. [^pangea-certified-mixed-beneficiary-question] The New York Court of Appeals did not answer that question in 2019, because its answer to the first certified question made the second immaterial, and no New York decision resolving that certified question was found in our review; the conservative course is to assume that creditors can reach property the settlor can recover by revoking the trust. [^pangea-court-of-appeals-did-not-reach]

## Can New York spouses protect assets by creating trusts for each other? {#spouses-trusts-for-each-other}

**Short answer.** On the statute's text, a trust one spouse creates solely for the other is not a disposition for the creator's own use under EPTL 7-3.1(a), but the transfer into it can still be challenged under New York's voidable-transactions law. [^eptl-7-3-1-creator-use-spousal-trusts] [^dcl-274-a-transfer-without-value-insolvent]
**The transfer.** A transfer is voidable by existing creditors when the debtor receives no reasonably equivalent value in exchange and is insolvent at the time or becomes insolvent as a result. [^dcl-274-a-transfer-without-value-insolvent] In our reading, a gift into a trust for a spouse returns nothing of equivalent value to the creator, so a spouse who is insolvent, or made insolvent by the gift, makes a transfer that existing creditors can avoid. [^dcl-274-a-transfer-without-value-insolvent] A spouse is a relative under the Debtor and Creditor Law, [^dcl-270-n-spouse-is-relative] a relative of an individual debtor is an insider, [^dcl-270-h-insider-includes-relative] and a transfer to an insider is one of the listed signs of actual intent to hinder, delay or defraud. [^dcl-273-b-insider-badge-spousal]
**Crossed trusts.** When each spouse creates a trust for the other at about the same time, a further question arises: whether a court would treat each spouse as the real creator of the trust that benefits them. The idea comes from the reciprocal-trust doctrine, which the Supreme Court applied in *United States v. Estate of Grace* under the federal estate tax; that doctrine requires only that the trusts be interrelated and leave the settlors in about the same economic position as if each had created a trust for themself. [^grace-reciprocal-trust-estate-tax] No New York or Second Circuit decision found in our review applies that doctrine to a creditor's claim, so whether a New York court would uncross spousal trusts under EPTL 7-3.1 is an open question. [^grace-reciprocal-trust-estate-tax] The legal cause is that *Grace* answered a federal estate tax question, [^grace-reciprocal-trust-estate-tax] while EPTL 7-3.1 asks whether a disposition was for the use of its creator. [^eptl-7-3-1-creator-use-spousal-trusts] If a court did uncross the trusts, each spouse would be treated as the creator of the trust that benefits them, and that trust would be void against that spouse's creditors; this is our inference, not a holding. [^eptl-7-3-1-creator-use-spousal-trusts]
**The closest New York facts.** In *Matter of Tomeck*, a wife created an irrevocable trust and, about a month later, her husband transferred his half interest in the marital home to her. [^tomeck-spousal-transfer-facts] The Court of Appeals did not reach the other issues the creditor agency raised and left them for the Surrogate on remand. [^tomeck-court-of-appeals-did-not-reach] In the later action against the trust, *Christopher v Tomeck*, the Surrogate's Court granted the defendants summary judgment on the merits, but the Third Department affirmed on statute-of-limitations grounds instead, [^christopher-tomeck-affirmed-on-limitations] holding the EPTL 7-3.1 and fraudulent-conveyance claims time-barred, so no appellate court ruled on the merits. [^christopher-tomeck-spousal-claims-time-barred]

## How do a joint trust and tenancy-by-the-entirety property affect creditor protection for New York spouses? {#joint-trust-and-entirety-property}

**Short answer.** A joint trust that both spouses fund and can benefit from is self-settled as to each of them, so on the text of EPTL 7-3.1(a) it does not protect either spouse's contribution from that spouse's own creditors. [^eptl-7-3-1-joint-trust-each-creator]
**Joint trust or separate trusts.** Separate trusts change the result only for a trust the creating spouse cannot benefit from, and that trust raises the transfer and crossed-trust questions covered in the previous answer. [^eptl-7-3-1-joint-trust-each-creator] How much of a jointly funded trust each spouse's creditors can reach is not settled. *Fordyce v Fordyce* said that creditors of a self-settled trust's creator reach only the interest the settlor retained, [^fordyce-retained-interest-joint-trust] but no New York decision found in our review apportions a trust between two contributing spouses.
**Property held as tenants by the entirety.** New York real property conveyed to spouses is held as tenants by the entirety unless the conveyance declares a joint tenancy or tenancy in common, and the same rule applies to cooperative apartment shares conveyed on or after January 1, 1996. [^eptl-6-2-2-entirety-real-property-coops] Each spouse may sell, mortgage or otherwise encumber their own rights in the property, subject to the other spouse's continuing rights. [^vrw-each-spouse-may-convey-own-rights] A grantee of one spouse's interest holds it as a tenant in common, not as a tenant by the entirety. [^vrw-grantee-holds-tenancy-in-common] The Court of Appeals in *V.R.W., Inc. v Klein* described the spouses' survivorship right as fixed: it cannot be destroyed without the consent of both spouses. [^vrw-survivorship-needs-both-spouses] A conveyance by one spouse without the other's consent cannot impair the nonconsenting spouse's survivorship interest, [^vrw-unilateral-conveyance-cannot-impair] and a grantee or mortgagee of one spouse takes subject to both spouses' reciprocal survivorship rights. [^vrw-grantee-subject-to-survivorship] The voidable-transactions article's definition of asset excludes an entirety interest only to the extent the interest is not subject to process by a creditor holding a claim against only one spouse. [^dcl-270-b-entirety-interest-excluded] The captured authorities do not say how far New York entirety property is subject to that process, so this guide states no rule on it.
**Moving entirety property into a trust.** No New York statute or decision found in our review says whether entirety property keeps its character, or its protection, after the spouses convey it into a trust. Two textual signals point against it, though neither is a holding: co-trustees take property as joint tenants, [^eptl-6-2-2-trustees-joint-tenants] and entirety status is reserved to co-owners who are married to each other. [^vrw-grantee-holds-tenancy-in-common] This suggests that a conveyance into a trust gives up the entirety form, and whether any protection survives then depends on the trust's terms and on EPTL 7-3.1.

> [!NOTE]
> **Practice note.**
>
> A deed that conveys both spouses' interests to two or more co-trustees on the assumption that the property stays held by the entirety can instead create a joint tenancy. [^eptl-6-2-2-trustees-joint-tenants]

## Can a surviving spouse's creditors reach property the other spouse's will leaves to the survivor's own trust? {#will-gift-to-survivors-trust}

**Short answer.** No New York decision found in our review decides whether a surviving spouse's creditors can reach a deceased spouse's bequest to the survivor's own trust, but the statutory text points toward protecting that principal, subject to limits for support claims, trust income and the survivor's powers over it. [^eptl-7-3-1-will-gift-creator-test] [^cplr-5205-c-1-fund-from-another-person] The limits are these: rights under a qualified domestic relations order, and under an order of support, alimony or maintenance to enforce arrears or past-due support, are not impaired [^will-gift-cplr-5205-c-4-support-arrears]; for a trust that is not one of the retirement arrangements the statute names, only ninety per cent of the income from a trust whose principal is exempt is itself exempt, and not any part a court determines to be unnecessary for the reasonable requirements of the judgment debtor and dependents [^will-gift-cplr-5205-d-1-trust-income]; and, on our reading, the protection fails if the survivor's power over the bequeathed property, such as a revocation or withdrawal power that would return it to the survivor, works as a presently exercisable general power of appointment. [^eptl-10-7-2-general-power-creditors]
The exemption therefore protects principal more fully than income: a creditor of the survivor may still reach part of the trust income, and a support creditor collecting arrears is not stopped by the trust exemption. [^will-gift-cplr-5205-d-1-trust-income] [^will-gift-cplr-5205-c-4-support-arrears]
The legal cause is the wording of EPTL 7-3.1(a), which asks whether a disposition in trust is for the use of its creator. [^eptl-7-3-1-will-gift-creator-test] For property passing under the deceased spouse's will, the deceased spouse makes that disposition. On the statute's text, then, the survivor's benefit from that portion is not a benefit from the survivor's own disposition; this is our inference, and no New York authority found in our review addresses it. CPLR 5205(c)(1) points the same way, because it exempts trust property where the fund held in trust proceeded from a person other than the judgment debtor. [^cplr-5205-c-1-fund-from-another-person] The survivor's powers over the bequeathed property can still expose it. If the survivor holds a presently exercisable general power over that property, EPTL 10-7.2 subjects the property to the survivor's creditors regardless of who created the power, except for a power exercisable solely for the survivor's support, maintenance, health and education. [^eptl-10-7-2-general-power-creditors] A power of appointment is general to the extent that it is exercisable wholly in favor of the donee, the donee's estate, the donee's creditors or the creditors of the donee's estate. [^eptl-10-3-2-b-general-power-defined] On our reading, a revocation or withdrawal power that lets the survivor take the bequeathed property for themself is exercisable in the survivor's favor and so is general to that extent. [^eptl-10-3-2-b-general-power-defined] [^eptl-10-7-2-general-power-creditors]
Two complications remain. The survivor's own contributions to the same trust stay within EPTL 7-3.1(a); *Fordyce v Fordyce* indicates that creditors reach the interest the settlor retained, [^fordyce-retained-interest-will-gift] and no decision found in our review says how a court would separate the two portions of a mixed trust. If the survivor reserved an unqualified power to revoke the trust, EPTL 10-10.6 also makes the survivor the absolute owner, as far as creditors are concerned, of the property the survivor disposed of into it. [^eptl-10-10-6-revocation-power-will-gift] Property that passes to the survivor outright and is later added to the survivor's trust is, on the statute's text, the survivor's own disposition for the survivor's use. [^eptl-7-3-1-will-gift-creator-test]

> [!CAUTION]
> **Drafting note.**
>
> A will clause that leaves property to the surviving spouse outright, intending it to fund the survivor's existing trust, can instead make the survivor's later contribution to that trust a disposition for the survivor's own use, which EPTL 7-3.1(a) makes void against the survivor's creditors. [^eptl-7-3-1-will-gift-creator-test]
